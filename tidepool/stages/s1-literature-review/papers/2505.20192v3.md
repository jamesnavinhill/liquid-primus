## Abstract

While Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is the prevailing method for equipping Large Language Models (LLMs) with function calling capabilities, its effectiveness is often compromised by two critical challenges: 1) Imbalanced Training Signals, where lengthy Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tokens dominate the training signals over concise function calls in the learning objective, and 2) Imbalanced Data Hardness, characterized by a scarcity of hard training examples. To overcome these limitations, we propose Balanced Supervised Fine-tuning (BalanceSFT), a novel framework incorporates two key components: a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss that employs a learnable hyperparameter to dynamically adjust the token contributions of CoT reasoning and function calls, together with a Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy that establishes a feedback loop to selectively generate new, high-quality complex data guided by model errors. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed BalanceSFT framework. With BalanceSFT, a 7B model achieves function calling performance on par with state-of-the-art giants like GPT-4o. Our code, models, and dataset are open-sourced.

## 1 Introduction

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a transformative era in natural language processing ouyang2022training; achiam2023gpt; touvron2023llama; bai2023qwen, demonstrating exceptional capabilities in text understanding, generation, and complex reasoning zhong2024evaluation. A pivotal direction in enhancing their practicality is equipping them with function calling abilities, enabling LLMs to interact with external tools and APIs to execute concrete actions and access real-world data wang2025function; singh2024llm. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) has emerged as the standard paradigm to instill this capability, typically by training models on demonstrations that combine a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process with the final function call lightman2023let; guo2025deepseek; team2025kimi.

Figure: Figure 1: Two challenges in LLM Function Calling. (a) Imbalanced Training Signals: The lengthy Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tokens dominate the learning signal, overshadowing the concise but critical function call. (b) Imbalanced Data Hardness: The training dataset is dominated by simple examples, with a scarcity of hard cases necessary for robust performance.
Refer to caption: x1.png

Despite the remarkable progress, conventional SFT approaches suffer from two inherent and critical imbalances that severely limit its effectiveness ju2024mitigating. On the one hand, as illustrated in Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S1.F1), the training signals are skewed due to a token-level imbalance. In a typical function-calling sample, the lengthy CoT reasoning tokens numerically dominate the concise, yet critical, function call tokens qian2025toolrl. This Imbalanced Training Signal causes the model to be optimized primarily for generating plausible reasoning, often at the expense of precision in the final tool execution. On the other hand, the data distribution is characterized by an Imbalanced Data Hardness li2023api; zhang2024xlam. Existing training datasets are naturally dominated by simple examples, while complex and challenging cases that are crucial for robust performance are scarce. This scarcity prevents the model from effectively learning to handle edge cases and intricate scenarios zeng2025toolace.

To tackle the dual challenges, we propose Balanced Supervised Fine-Tuning (BalanceSFT), a novel framework designed to recalibrate the SFT process. BalanceSFT incorporates two simple yet effective components: 1) A Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss function that introduces a learnable hyperparameter to dynamically balance the contribution of CoT tokens and function call tokens to the total loss, ensuring that the model does not overlook the critical execution step; 2) A Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy that establishes a feedback loop to generate new, high-quality complex data guided by model errors. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of our BalanceSFT framework. The results show that a 7B model trained with BalanceSFT achieves tool-calling performance competitive with state-of-the-art giants like GPT-4o hurst2024gpt on the comprehensive Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) patil2024gorilla and other specialized benchmarks chen2025acebench; li2023api.

Our main contributions are threefold:

- •
We identify two critical yet overlooked limitations in standard SFT for tool learning: the imbalance in training signals between lengthy reasoning and concise function calls, and the imbalance in data hardness.
- •
We propose BalanceSFT, a novel framework that introduces a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss and a Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy to calibrate the learning focus.
- •
We train a 7B model with the proposed BalanceSFT framework, achieving tool-calling performance comparable to the state-of-the-art model like GPT-4o.

## 2 Related Work

Function Calling for LLMs. The ability of LLMs to interact with external tools and APIs through function calling has emerged as a crucial aspect of their practical utility wang2024executable; singh2024llm; patil2024gorilla. This capability allows LLMs to transcend the limitations of solely processing and generating text, enabling them to ground their responses in real-world data and automate complex tasks wang2024gta; kavathekar2025small; 2025InSpire. Early explorations in this area focused on enabling LLMs to understand descriptions of functions and generate the necessary calls with appropriate parameters wang2023describe; zheng2023llm. By training LLMs on datasets specifically curated for function calling scenarios, these methods aim to instill a deeper understanding of function semantics and parameter requirements prabhakar2025apigen; zeng2025toolace. The quality and diversity of these fine-tuning datasets play a critical role in the resulting performance of the LLMs in function calling tasks. RL has also been explored as a means to optimize LLMs for function calling zhang2025nemotron; qian2025toolrl. RL-based approaches often involve defining reward functions that incentivize the generation of correct and executable function calls. While RL offers the potential to directly optimize for task success, it still severe sparse reward and the lack of effective data qu2025latent; zeng2025toolaceacer.

Improving Reasoning Capability via SFT. The integration of CoT with function calling mechanisms holds substantial promise for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of tool-use lu2024toolsandbox; kokane2025toolscan; chen2024towards. By reasoning through the user’s request and identifying the most appropriate course of action, including the selection of relevant tools and their parameters, the model can make more informed decisions about function invocation li2025review; shen2024llm. The explicit reasoning process provided by CoT can also improve the transparency and interpretability of the model’s actions wu2025joint; dong2025tool. However, the integration of CoT into the training process for function calling introduces a unique challenge related to the design of the loss function yang2025alleviating. Traditional training methodologies, primarily SFT, typically treat the entire generated sequence (including the reasoning process and the final function call) uniformly when calculating the loss lobo2025impact. This can lead to an imbalance, where the potentially lengthy sequences of reasoning steps dominate the loss calculation, potentially overshadowing the importance of the final, often shorter, function call. Consequently, the model might be incentivized to generate elaborate and seemingly plausible reasoning chains agarwal2024faithfulness; chen2025reasoning, even if they do not ultimately lead to a correct or executable function call. This inherent tension between the verbose reasoning process and the need for a succinct and precise function call necessitates a more nuanced approach to loss function design that can effectively balance these two critical aspects of the task. Our work addresses this challenge by introducing a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing approach that explicitly considers the different roles and importance of the reasoning process and the final function execution during training.

## 3 Method

In this section, we elaborate on our proposed Balanced Supervised Fine-Tuning (BalanceSFT) framework, which is designed to address the dual challenges of Imbalanced Training Signals and Imbalanced Data Hardness.

Figure: Figure 2: Overview of the BalanceSFT framework. It starts with a standard function call dataset, which is refined through a Base Quality Check and Answer Check to create initial training data and identify hard data. The model is first initialized via a Cold Start using the Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) Loss. Subsequently, the Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy creates a Self-evolving Loop where the model iteratively reasons on hard cases, generates new solutions, and undergoes quality-gated retraining.

### 3.1 Overview

The overall architecture of our BalanceSFT framework is illustrated in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S3.F2). It consists of two core components that work in synergy: 1) A Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss that dynamically balances the learning signals between lengthy Chain-of-Thought reasoning tokens and concise function call tokens during training, and a Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy that establishes a feedback loop to selectively generate and utilize high-quality complex data based on model errors. These two components address the signal imbalance and data hardness imbalance, respectively, forming a comprehensive solution for robust function calling capability.

### 3.2 Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) Loss

Motivation and Analysis.
We conduct an intuitive analysis of a CoT Dataset. As shown in Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S3.T1), a CoT Dataset distilled from QwQ-32B qwq based on the open-sourced function call dataset xlam-function-calling-60k zhang2024xlam, which exhibits a significant token imbalance: Chain-of-Thought reasoning segments contain approximately 10 times more tokens (mean=350.74) than function call results (mean=31.07).
This substantial disparity highlights the need to rebalance the training focus toward the short but critical function execution components.

**Table 1: Token Statistics of the CoT Dataset for function call. Note the approximately 10x higher token count for the CoT compared to the final execution.**
| Dataset | CoT Token Len. | Result Token Len. |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean | Median | Mean | Median |  |
| CoT Dataset | 350.74 | 248.00 | 31.07 | 27.00 |

Formulation.
The output of an LLM $\mathcal{M}$ can be decomposed into two components: chain-of-thought $t$ and the final function calling execution $f$. Let $N_{t}$ and $N_{f}$ denote the token counts for these components, respectively, with $N_{\text{all}}=N_{t}+N_{f}$ representing the total tokens, and $V$ represents the size of the vocabulary. The standard SFT loss can be expressed as a weighted sum:

$$ $\displaystyle L_{\text{SFT}}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{all}}\sum_{j=1}^{V}-p_{ij}\log p_{ij}}{N_{all}}$ (1) $$

We decompose the loss $L_{\text{STF}}$ as following:

$$ $\displaystyle L_{\text{STF}}$ $\displaystyle=\frac{N_{t}}{N_{\text{all}}}\cdot\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\sum_{j=1}^{V}-p_{ij}\log p_{ij}$ (2) $\displaystyle+\frac{N_{f}}{N_{\text{all}}}\cdot\frac{1}{N_{f}}\sum_{i=N_{t}+1}^{N_{t}+N_{f}}\sum_{j=1}^{V}-p_{ij}\log p_{ij}$ $\displaystyle=w_{t}\cdot L_{\text{think}}+w_{f}\cdot L_{\text{result}}$ $$

where $w_{t}=\frac{N_{t}}{N_{\text{all}}},w_{f}=\frac{N_{f}}{N_{\text{all}}}$, thus $w_{t}+w_{f}=1$.
Let $L_{\text{think}}$ and $L_{\text{result}}$ represent the loss for reasoning and function call components, respectively.

The statistical analysis from Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S3.T1) reveals that $N_{t}\gg N_{f}$, which implies $w_{t}\gg w_{f}$ during standard training. This naturally leads to the model being optimized primarily for generating plausible reasoning at the expense of precise function execution. To address this inherent imbalance, we propose the Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss:

$$ $L_{\text{SSB}}=\alpha\cdot L_{\text{think}}+(1-\alpha)\cdot L_{\text{result}}$ (3) $$

where $\alpha$ is a learnable parameter with $\alpha\in[0,1]$. The introduction of $\alpha$ allows the model to dynamically adjust its focus between reasoning depth and execution accuracy based on performance requirements. In practice, we set $\alpha$ as a trainable parameter to avoid extensive hyperparameter tuning.

### 3.3 Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) Strategy

The core objective of the HDR strategy is to address the Imbalanced Data Hardness issue through a carefully designed self-evolving data refinement loop. This process can be formally described as a sequence of iterative data filtration and augmentation operations. Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{initial}}$ denote the initial training dataset. Each data point $d_{i}=(q_{i},c_{i},f_{i})$ consists of a user query $q_{i}$, a CoT reasoning sequence $c_{i}$, and a function call $f_{i}$.

Data Filtration via Quality Checks.
The dataset undergoes a two-stage filtration process defined by boolean functions $\text{BQC}(\cdot)$ and $\text{AC}(\cdot)$ for Base Quality Check (including response, query, and CoT identification) and Answer Check (including function, parameter, and format verification), respectively:

$$ $\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{qualified}}$ $\displaystyle=\{\,d_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{initial}}\mid\text{BQC}(d_{i})\!\land\!\text{AC}(d_{i})\,\},$ (4) $\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}$ $\displaystyle=\{\,d_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{initial}}\mid\text{BQC}(d_{i})\!\land\!\neg\text{AC}(d_{i})\,\}.$ $$

Here, $\mathcal{D}_{\text{qualified}}$ is used for the Cold Start phase to initialize the model $\mathcal{M}_{0}$, while $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}$ is reserved for iterative self-evolution.

Self-Evolving Loop. The model at iteration $t$, denoted as $\mathcal{M}_{t}$, is used to reason on the current hard dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t}$, generating a new set of candidate responses $\mathcal{R}^{t+1}$:

$$ $\mathcal{R}^{t+1}=\{\mathcal{M}_{t}(q_{i})\mid\forall q_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t}\}$ (5) $$

Each candidate response $r_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{t+1}$ is evaluated by an ensemble of LLM judges $\mathcal{J}=\{J_{1},J_{2},\dots,J_{k}\}$ (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro comanici2025gemini, GPT-4o hurst2024gpt, Claude-3.5 sonnet TheC3) via majority voting. The scoring function $\text{Score}(r_{j})$ aggregates their judgments:

$$ $\text{Score}(r_{j})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\mathbb{I}\left(J_{m}(r_{j})\geq\tau\right)$ (6) $$

where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function and $\tau$ is a passing threshold. Responses meeting the quality standard form the new high-quality dataset:

$$ $\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}^{t}=\{r_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{t+1}\mid\text{Score}(r_{j})=1\}$ (7) $$

The model is then fine-tuned on this newly generated data:

$$ $\mathcal{M}_{t+1}=\text{SFT}\left(\mathcal{M}_{t},\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}^{t}\right)$ (8) $$

Simultaneously, the hard dataset is updated by retaining failed samples:

$$ $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t+1}=\{d_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t}\mid\text{Score}(\mathcal{M}_{t}(q_{i}))<1\}$ (9) $$

This iterative process continues until convergence criteria are met: $\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t+1}=\emptyset$ or $t=T_{\text{max}}$.

The entire HDR strategy establishes a virtuous cycle where model improvement and data quality enhancement reinforce each other, effectively resolving the data hardness imbalance through the iterative refinement process:

$$ $\mathcal{M}_{t+1},\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t+1}=\text{HDR-Iteration}(\mathcal{M}_{t},\mathcal{D}_{\text{hard}}^{t})$ (10) $$

### 4.1 Experiment Setting

Dataset. As shown in Table [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.T4), our training dataset comprises two components: xlam-function-calling-60k zhang2024xlam and Open-Agentic-tool-use Open-Agentic-tool-use. xlam-function-calling-60k contains 60000 samples from 3,673 APIs across 21 categories, while Open-Agentic-tool-use includes 30000 samples from 129 APIs across 8 categories. The former focuses on single-turn tool-use to strengthen the model’s basic capabilities, while the latter focuses on multi-turn tool-use to address complex scenario requirements. The training dataset we adopted covers thousands of APIs from multiple categories, helping the model learn cross-domain API matching logic and improve its generalization ability. The distribution of the diverse and comprehensive API resources is shown in Figure [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#A1.F5), and the details of the dataset are further illustrated and analyzed in Appendix [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#A1.F6).

Benchmarks.
To assess our methodology, we choose a highly representative benchmark, the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL)  patil2024gorilla. For BFCL, we conduct evaluations on BFCLv3, which contains single-turn and multi-turn function calling scenarios. Performance across all subsets of BFCL is reported in terms of accuracy (%). We further evaluate our model on two specialized tool-use benchmarks, ACEBench chen2025acebench and APIBank li2023api.

Backbones.
To demonstrate the effectiveness and general applicability of our BalanceSFT framework, we select Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst hui2024qwen2 as our primary base model. To demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, we also conduct additional experiments using Llama-3.2-3B-Inst touvron2023llama.

Baselines.
For comprehensive comparison, we include both open-source and closed-source models: open-source models include Moonshotai-Kimi-K2-Instruct team2025kimi, DeepSeek-R1-0528 guo2025deepseek, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 yang2025qwen3, ToolACE-MT (8B) zeng2025toolace, and BitAgent-8B Bitagent; closed-source models include GPT-5 openai2025gpt, GPT-4o hurst2024gpt, Gemini-2.5-Pro comanici2025gemini, and Grok-4 grok4.

Implementation Details.
Our implementations are based on the training framework LLaMA Factory zheng2024llamafactory. To ensure fair comparison, we maintain consistent hyperparameters across all experiments: batch size of 512, learning rate of $4\times 10^{-5}$, warm-up rate of 0.05. The SSB loss hyperparameter $\alpha$ is initialized to 0.7 and set as a learnable parameter. During inference, we set the number of inferences to 3 with a temperature 0.7. In HDR, for the LLM-as-judge phase, we use Gemini-2.5-Pro comanici2025gemini, GPT-4o hurst2024gpt,
Claude-3.5 Sonnet TheC3 as judges with the same temperature setting at 0.7, threshold $\tau=0.5$, and scoring range [0, 1]. The maximum iteration $T_{max}$ is 10. All trainings run on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

### 4.2 Data Preparation

We construct two CoT datasets: (1) Strategy CoT, generated by GPT-4o following prescribed reasoning strategies, and (2) Natural CoT, generated by QwQ-32B qwq based on its natural reasoning process (implemented with vLLM kwon2023efficient, temperature = 0.1).
We conduct standard SFT on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst using each dataset. As shown in Table [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.T3), we observe that Natural CoT consistently outperforms Strategy CoT. Therefore, we adopt Natural CoT for cold-start data generation and employ self-inference in the Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) stage to maintain model-consistent reasoning patterns.

### 4.3 Main Results

The performance of BalanceSFT-7B (built upon Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst) on the BFCL benchmark is summarized in Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.T2). We report accuracy across the main subsets using the official BFCL evaluation script.

Multi-Turn Evaluation. The Multi-Turn subset evaluates a model’s ability to handle complex, continuous conversations. As shown in Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.T2), BalanceSFT-7B demonstrates the strongest overall capability, leading the Overall score with 47.00. It also ensures optimal performance in the key failure mode categories: Miss Function at 47.00 and Miss Parameter at 41.00, indicating the ability to keenly detect the absence of functions and required parameters in the dialogue flow. In contrast, the Long Context metric, which tests memory and coherence over conversation history, is led by the closed-source model GPT-4o-2024-11-20 hurst2024gpt at 51.00, with BalanceSFT-7B being the second best at 46.50.

Single-Turn Evaluation. The Single-Turn subset focuses on isolated prompts under Non-Live and Live settings. As reported in Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.T2), BalanceSFT-7B maintains its competitive edge by achieving the highest Overall score of 84.00. ### 4.5 Further Analysis

Comparison with Reinforcement Learning. To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed BalanceSFT method, we compare it with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) guo2025deepseek method on BFCL shown in Table [4(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.F4.sf1), the 7B and 3B models are trained upon Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst and Llama-3.2-3B-Inst shao2024deepseekmath. We use the hyperparameter settings and reward design from the official GRPO baseline described in Appendix [B](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#A2). For Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Inst, the GRPO method achieves a higher score in the Single-Turn task (84.60 vs. 84.00), but the BalanceSFT approach outperforms it significantly in the Multi-Turn task (47.00 vs. 40.75). This suggests that while GRPO maintains an edge in simpler, Single-Turn interactions, the BalanceSFT method better enhances the model’s capability to handle more complex, Multi-Turn dialogues. A similar trend is observed with Llama-3.2-3B-Inst, where BalanceSFT outperforms GRPO in both tasks, marginally in Single-Turn (78.99 vs. 77.78) and more notably in Multi-Turn (36.12 vs. 34.00). The results indicate that the proposed BalanceSFT approach provides a more balanced performance boost, particularly excelling in the more challenging Multi-Turn setting compared to the GRPO method, thereby validating its advantage in enhancing model robustness across different interaction complexities.

Effectiveness of HDR Self-Evolving Loop. As shown in Figure [4(c)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.F4.sf3), the model’s ability in Multi-Turn function call grows with the Self-evolving Loop until convergence, providing strong evidence that the proposed HDR can effectively and continuously sample complex edge samples, driving the model performance toward a high-accuracy.

This improvement arises from the HDR Self-Evolving Loop, which forms a self-reinforcing training cycle. HDR leverages model errors to create harder edge samples that target its weak points, forming a focused feedback loop for continual improvement. Through iterative refinement, the model progressively enhances its accuracy, robustness across complex, multi-step tasks.

Effectiveness of Alleviating Catastrophic Forgetting.
To assess how BalanceSFT impacts the coder model’s original coding capabilities, we evaluate our model on widely recognized code generation benchmarks in Figure [4(b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.F4.sf2), HumanEval and MBPP (including their plus variants) austin2021program; chen2021evaluating; yu2024humaneval. The results, presented in Table [4(c)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20192v3#S4.F4.sf3), demonstrate a crucial advantage of our method. Specifically, BalanceSFT-7B maintains strong performance across all benchmarks, with pass@1 scores only marginally lower (within 4%) compared to the base Qwen2.5-Coder-Inst model. For instance, on HumanEval, BalanceSFT achieves 0.841 compared to the base model’s 0.866, and on MBPP, it reaches 0.794 versus 0.812. This minimal performance degradation stands in stark contrast to models fine-tuned using standard SFT. The SFT method shows dramatic drops in pass@1 scores on all the benchmarks, notably falling from 0.866 to 0.470 on HumanEval and from 0.823 to 0.445 on HumanEval+.

These results indicate that BalanceSFT effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, preserving the model’s original code capabilities across benchmarks. This is because the SSB loss dynamically rebalances the training signal, focusing updates on the concise, yet crucial function call tokens. By concentrating learning on these key tokens, the model avoids overfitting to lengthy reasoning and maintains its original coding capabilities.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we identify two critical yet overlooked challenges in standard SFT for function calling: the imbalance in training signals between lengthy reasoning and concise function calls, and the scarcity of hard training data. To address these issues, we propose BalanceSFT, a novel framework that integrates a Self-adjusted Signal Balancing (SSB) loss and a Hard Data Re-sampling (HDR) strategy. The SSB loss dynamically recalibrates the learning focus between reasoning and execution, while HDR iteratively enriches the training set with high-quality complex examples. Extensive experiments demonstrate that BalanceSFT enables a 7B model to achieve function calling performance competitive with state-of-the-art giants like GPT-4o on BFCL and other benchmarks. Our work provides a simple yet effective pathway toward more robust and generalizable LLM-based tool use.

## Limitations

Despite its strong performance, BalanceSFT has two major limitations. First, the effectiveness of the SSB loss depends on the initial quality and structure of the CoT data, which may limit its applicability in domains where high-quality reasoning annotations are scarce. Second, while HDR improves data hardness, it relies on an ensemble of LLM judges for quality control, which introduces computational cost and potential bias from the judge models.
