# s6.1 — putting every baseline row on the current instrument

## Why this had to happen before anything else in s6

`s5.3` recorded, in the sweep's own run log, that the replayed `B1` did not match the `B1` row
written at `s5.2`: composite 0.6649 against 0.6700, IFEval 0.8189 against 0.8170. The graders
were edited between the two, and the eight sweep arms were all scored by the current ones. The
conclusion drawn there was correct and narrow — the replayed figure is the comparable base, and
the `s5.2` row "is a measurement taken with a different instrument and should not be differenced
against anything in this stage."

What was never followed through is that `B1` was the only row replayed. `B2`, `B3`, `B4` and `B5`
were measured in the same window, by the same instrument, and were left there. The `s6` table is
the first place where the finalists get differenced against the *competitors* rather than against
the matched base, and `B5` (Nova) is named by hand in the pre-registered claim. Differencing a
finalist scored by the current graders against a competitor scored by the old ones is not a small
error; it is the same class of error the project already caught once and stopped short of fixing.

## What it cost, and why it was safe to pack

Nothing was regenerated. All four rows have their `completions_*.jsonl` retrievable from their
original jobs, so the eval task's `rescore_object` mode replays them: it loads no weights,
generates no tokens, joins on item id, re-renders every prompt and checks it against the recorded
prompt hash, then runs the current graders.

Four arms were packed onto one L4. `s5.6` measured that packing three llama.cpp *generation*
servers onto one L4 collapsed aggregate throughput to 0.28 tok/s and silently produced empty
completions on two of three arms, and that finding stands. It does not transfer here, because
what contended there was the GPU, and a replay never touches it: `generated_tokens` is 0 and
`peak_gpu_gb` is 0.0 on all four rows. The operator's standing note — pack each card with as many
runs as fit, and do not read an instance limit as a reason to serialize — is what made packing
the default; the `s5.6` measurement is what makes it a decision rather than an assumption.

Job `651c0193`, four arms, 0.02 GPU-h, 8.2 to 9.4 wall-seconds per arm, 0 assertion failures on
all four. The prompt-hash check passing on every item is the load-bearing part: it is what says
these are the same prompts the original run answered, re-graded, and not a different measurement
wearing the same name.

**The tokenizer is per-arm and had to be, in a replay.** `replay.load_tokenizer` reads
`base_model`, but a llama.cpp row rendered its prompts with `tokenizer_repo`. Reading each job's
`eval_summary.json` gave: `B2` and `B3` on `LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct` for both keys, `B4` on
`ibm-granite/granite-4.0-1b`, and `B5` on `NovachronoAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Nova-Function-Calling` — a
GGUF row whose `base_model` says Liquid and whose renderer says Nova. Each arm's replay was given
the repo that actually produced its text, via `pack_overrides`. A wrong choice here would have
failed the prompt-hash check rather than passing quietly, which is the property that made it
worth trusting the arrangement at all. `B5` alone also carried `clean_control_object` on its
original run (it was queued after the 138-item clean-corpus arm entered the harness at 17:19Z),
so its replay sets it too and the other three do not.

## What moved

| Metric | B2 (Q4_0, QAD) | B3 (Q4_K_XL, unsloth) | B4 (Granite, fp) | B5 (Nova Q4_K_M) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| native_tools composite, s5.2 | 0.6392 | 0.5837 | 0.7641 | 0.6821 |
| native_tools composite, replayed | 0.6392 | **0.5786** | 0.7641 | 0.6821 |
| ifstruct validity, replayed | 0.1370 | 0.1195 | 0.3205 | 0.0710 |
| ifeval strict, replayed | 0.8078 | 0.8022 | 0.7394 | 0.5379 |
| probe flag rate, replayed | 0.0222 | 0.0074 | 0.0000 | 0.0185 |
| probe false-flag, replayed | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |

One row moved, and by exactly the amount `B1` moved: −0.0051 of native tool-calling composite,
`B1` 0.6700 → 0.6649 and `B3` 0.5837 → 0.5786. Two rows shifting by an identical delta is not a
coincidence to wave through, and the reading that fits is that the grader edit reclassified the
same small set of items in the same category, which both of these two rows got wrong-then-right
(or right-then-wrong) together and the other three were unaffected by. `s6.4`'s error analysis
reads the per-item verdicts and can name those items exactly; nothing before then needs to.

Every other cell is byte-identical to what `s5.2` recorded. That is the useful outcome: the
competitor claims the paper leans on — Granite's 9.4-point native tool-calling lead over our
full-precision reference and its 18.5-point structured-output lead, Nova's 28.0-point instruction-
following collapse — were never at risk, and can now be *stated* as unaffected rather than
assumed to be.

## What was recovered beyond the composites

The `s5.2` table carried six numbers per row. The replay writes the full `scored_*.jsonl` set, so
the `s6` family now has, for every baseline: the 11-category BFCL breakdown under both calling
conventions, IFStruct partial credit alongside first-attempt validity, the instruction-level
IFEval pair beside the prompt-level one, and `probe_stack_idiom` — which was never in the `s5.2`
table at all and is the axis the whole specialization argument turns on. It reads B2 0.285,
B3 0.250, B4 0.326, B5 0.160. Nova, the competitor tuned hardest for function calling, is the
*worst* row on the operator's own stack idioms by a wide margin, which is the shape the project
predicted and had no baseline measurement of until now.

Promoted to `tidepool/s6.1/baselines/<arm>/`, deliberately not into `tidepool/s5.3/arms/`: those
are the sweep's arms and a baseline sitting among them would be picked up by a comparison that
never meant to include it.
