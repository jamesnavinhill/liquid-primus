# s6.2 — where each headline delta comes from

Jobs `9739c5ce` (first pass, BFCL only) and `792fab04` (second pass, whole suite). Both CPU
only, ~2.5 min each, 0 GPU-hours, 0 assertion failures. The second artifact supersedes the
first, which supersedes `d08ff6cb`'s. All three carry **identical headline cells** — 74
comparisons, 45 separating by interval, 29 surviving Holm — by construction and by test.

The breakdown carries its **own Holm family**: 389 subgroup cells, 108 significant. It is
never quoted as the 74-cell headline family and never mixed into it. A decomposition asks a
different question from the claim it decomposes, and correcting the two together would make
the headline pay for the exploration.

## The finding: the flat native tool-calling cell is a cancellation

`s6.1` reported native tool calling as unmoved on the finalists — R3-F16 at −0.0060, C7-F16
at +0.0052, neither separating. The breakdown says the cell is not quiet, it is balanced.
BFCL's eleven categories divide into eight where a tool call is warranted and three
(`irrelevance`, `live_irrelevance`, `live_relevance`) where the right answer is to decline.
Pooled within each half, item-weighted, against the matched full-precision base B1:

| arm | call-warranted (n=2350) | abstain-warranted (n=1140) | headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| R3-F16 | 0.6728 → 0.5868 (**−0.0860**) | 0.7018 → 0.8605 (**+0.1588**) | −0.0060 |
| R3-Q4_K_M | 0.6728 → 0.5681 (**−0.1047**) | 0.7018 → 0.8702 (**+0.1684**) | −0.0155 |
| C7-F16 | 0.6728 → 0.6494 (−0.0234) | 0.7018 → 0.7658 (+0.0640) | +0.0052 |
| C7-Q4_K_M | 0.6728 → 0.6213 (−0.0515) | 0.7018 → 0.7465 (+0.0447) | −0.0201 |
| B2 (base, Q4_0) | 0.6728 → 0.6153 (−0.0574) | 0.7018 → 0.7702 (+0.0684) | −0.0163 |
| B3 (base, Q4_K_XL) | 0.6728 → 0.5162 (−0.1566) | 0.7018 → 0.8588 (+0.1570) | −0.0542 |
| B4 Granite | 0.6728 → 0.7791 (+0.1064) | 0.7018 → 0.6386 (−0.0632) | +0.0510 |
| B5 Nova | 0.6728 → 0.6579 (−0.0149) | 0.7018 → 0.6886 (−0.0132) | −0.0143 |

Per category for R3-Q4_K_M, the largest cells: `live_irrelevance` n=882 **+0.2120** (Holm),
`simple` n=400 **−0.2200** (Holm), `live_multiple` n=1052 **−0.0979** (Holm), `live_simple`
n=258 **−0.1279** (Holm), `multiple` n=200 −0.1050 (Holm), `parallel` n=200 −0.0950 (ci).
That is the macro-vs-item-weighted divergence the queue script went looking for: the
categories carrying the loss are the small curated ones, and the one carrying the gain is
the second-largest in the corpus.

**Reading.** Every finalist is less willing to reach for a tool. On the corpus's own
abstention items that is worth up to +21 points; where a call is genuinely needed it costs
up to 10. It is the same direction as the guardrail result, and the two are plausibly one
behaviour: a model trained to distrust tool *returns* also became more reluctant to issue
tool *calls*. Whether the shift is worth taking depends on the deployment, and a single
composite cannot make that choice — an agent loop that stalls is not the same failure as
one that calls a function it did not need.

**A large part of it is not ours.** B2 and B3 are the *base model's own* 4-bit builds,
trained by nobody here, and they show the same shape: B3 is −0.157 / +0.157, deeper than
either R3 arm. So 4-bit quantization alone pushes this base toward abstention on native
calling, and our fine-tune adds to an effect that was already there. The two contributions
are not separated by this run. R3-F16 at −0.086 / +0.159 is the training's own share, since
it is full precision, and it is most of the total.

## The stack's own text convention is a broad gain, not a trade

The same decomposition on `bfcl_text` finds nothing cancelling. **All eleven categories move
up** for R3-F16, and the largest gains are on the hardest categories, not the abstention
ones: `parallel` +0.3600 (Holm), `parallel_multiple` +0.3300 (Holm), `live_parallel_multiple`
+0.2917, `live_multiple` +0.1416 (Holm), down to `live_irrelevance` +0.0204. Pooled:
call-warranted +0.1549, abstain-warranted +0.0640.

So the two conventions genuinely differ in kind. On the convention the stack actually uses,
the model got better at emitting multi-call plans. On the JSON-native convention it traded.

## Granite's text-convention score is an artifact of never calling

`s6.1` recorded B4 at −0.2292 on `bfcl_text` and read it as "much worse at the stack's text
convention". The breakdown shows something sharper. Granite scores **exactly 0.0000 on all
eight call-warranted categories** and 0.9989 / 1.0000 on the two irrelevance ones. It does
not emit the convention at all; it declines, and collects every abstention item for free.
The pooled −0.2292 therefore *understates* it: 1140 of 3490 items are free points for a
model that never tries. Nova is the same failure less completely (call-warranted 0.1523,
abstention 0.4614). Restated: **on call-warranted text-convention items R3-F16 goes 0.5566 →
0.7115 while Granite goes to zero.** The specialization argument is stronger read this way
than it was in the pooled table.

## The guardrail is a structural-integrity detector, not a semantic one

The +0.73 flag-rate gain is far from uniform across the nine corruption types (30 items each,
`detail.flagged`, R3-F16 → R3-Q4_K_M):

| corruption | base | R3-F16 | R3-Q4_K_M |
|---|---|---|---|
| silently_truncated | 0.000 | **1.000** | **1.000** |
| unit_swapped | 0.000 | **1.000** | **1.000** |
| empty_body | 0.033 | **1.000** | **1.000** |
| truncated_json | 0.033 | **0.967** | **1.000** |
| error_envelope | 0.000 | **0.833** | **1.000** |
| null_leaf | 0.000 | **0.800** | **0.900** |
| type_swapped | 0.000 | **0.700** | **0.867** |
| wrong_entity | 0.000 | 0.300 | 0.167 |
| stale_as_of | 0.000 | 0.067 | 0.100 |

Seven of the nine are at or near ceiling. The two that are not are the two whose returns are
**well formed**: `wrong_entity` is a valid record about the wrong thing, `stale_as_of` is a
valid record that is out of date. Nothing in the payload is malformed, so a detector keyed on
structure has nothing to fire on. Deploying this, the honest claim is that it catches broken
returns, and it will not tell you a fresh-looking answer is about last quarter.

The non-fabrication cell agrees from the other side: the base is already at 1.000 on five
modes, and the gains sit on `type_swapped` (0.533 → 1.000, Holm) and `null_leaf` (0.567 →
0.900). `wrong_entity` is the base's weakest mode at 0.267 and stays weak at 0.367.

Quantization again moves the operating point toward flagging: Q4_K_M is at or above F16 on
seven of the nine modes, notably `error_envelope` 1.000 vs 0.833 and `type_swapped` 0.867 vs
0.700, and pays for it with clean-corpus restraint (0.9638 vs 0.9855).

## The structured-output regression is entirely JSON

`ifstruct` carries 1000 JSON items and 1000 YAML items. The R3 regression is not spread:

| arm | json (n=1000) | yaml (n=1000) | headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| R3-F16 | 0.2210 → 0.1260 (**−0.0950**, Holm) | 0.0500 → 0.0460 (−0.0040) | −0.0495 |
| R3-Q4_K_M | 0.2210 → 0.1130 (**−0.1080**, Holm) | 0.0500 → 0.0340 (−0.0160, ci) | −0.0620 |
| B4 Granite | 0.2210 → 0.4220 (**+0.2010**, Holm) | 0.0500 → 0.2190 (**+0.1690**, Holm) | +0.1850 |
| B5 Nova | 0.2210 → 0.1170 (**−0.1040**, Holm) | 0.0500 → 0.0250 (−0.0250, ci) | −0.0645 |

YAML is untouched; JSON loses nearly half its already-poor first-attempt validity. The
training corpus is dense in JSON-shaped tool-call text, which is the obvious hypothesis for
why free-form JSON emission specifically degraded, and it is a hypothesis this run does not
test. `s6.4` is where to look at the failing parses.

Nova shows the same JSON-only shape, which is worth noting: the one other model in the set
that was tuned hard for function calling has the same structured-output damage in the same
place.

## Negative finding: the stack-idiom probe cannot support its own question

Splitting the 144 stack-idiom items by technology gives `sql_dialect` 40, `json_schema` 28,
`mcp` 20, `containers` 20, `tracing` 20, `ipc` 16. **Not one finalist cell separates on any
technology**, and R3-F16 is identical to the base on four of six, differing by one item on
each of the other two. Two competitor cells reach interval-only separation (B4 on
`sql_dialect` +0.2500, B5 on `sql_dialect` −0.2250 and `tracing` +0.2000), none survives the
subgroup family.

`mcp` is at **0.0000 for the base and for six of the eight arms**, so that axis is at a floor
and can only be read as "nobody does this".

The probe was built to measure exactly the axis this project was told to specialize along,
and at this size it cannot. Twenty items resolve nothing; a technology-by-technology claim
needs a few hundred each. Recorded as a limitation of the instrument, and as the highest-value
data work if the project continues. It is **not** evidence that specialization failed — the
text-convention result above is squarely about the stack — it is evidence that this
particular probe was never powered to say either way.

## Method note

`group_field` (what the macro average collapses) and `subgroup_field` (what the breakdown
splits) are separate keys in the component table. BFCL sets both to `category`; the probes
and structured output set only the latter. The separation is deliberate: reusing
`group_field` would have added a macro column to probe cells that every comparison recorded
before `s6.2` rendered without one, silently changing what those artifacts reproduce as.
Three tests hold the two keys apart; 69 checks green.

Fields used: `mode` on tool-return probes (the corruption), `arm` on stack-idiom probes (the
technology), `output_format` on structured output (the serializer), `category` on both BFCL
styles. `ifeval` has no grouping field — its `instruction_ids` is a per-item list, not a
label — so it carries no breakdown, and that gap is stated rather than filled with a guess.
