# s5.3 — the supervised sweep

The C row of the experiment matrix, authored as one task and queued one arm at a time.
Task `s5-sft-sweep`, id `37186b22-9fbc-4c63-8448-4a2d21d64384`, source in
`../jobs/s5-sft-sweep/`.

## What differs between arms, and nothing else

Every arm runs the same file. The only thing that changes at queue time is `-p arm=<id>`,
and each arm's delta from the reference lives in one table in `main.py`, so the sweep's
specification is a block of code rather than eight command lines that can drift apart.

| Arm | Delta from C1 | What it isolates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C1** | reference: LoRA r16 α32, LR 1e-4, role-balanced mix, cross-entropy, guardrail block at 2 passes | the recipe everything else is measured against |
| **C2'** | `guardrail_epochs: 0` | what the guardrail supervision buys on the probe flag rate |
| **C3** | `tuning: full`, LR 1e-5, micro-batch 1 × accumulation 16 | adapter against full-parameter |
| **C4** | `loss: entropy_weighted` | loss shaping on the guardrail and tool axes |
| **C5a** | `replay_frac: 0.01` | 1% self-distillation replay |
| **C5b** | `replay_frac: 0.05` | 5% self-distillation replay |
| **C6** | `lora_r: 64`, `lora_alpha: 128` | adapter capacity |
| **C7** | `mix: raw` | what role balancing buys, against uniform sampling |

`C2` as originally written ("public data only") was retired at `s5.2` and its cell reassigned;
the reasoning is in `../../s3-research-plan/plan.md`. `C5` was specified over three replay
fractions, and 0% is C1 itself, so it costs two cells rather than three. Eight arms total.

Three arms change two things at once, and each does so deliberately.

- **C3** moves the learning rate with the tuning method. LoRA and full-parameter fine-tuning
  have different optimal LR scales by construction, and holding 1e-4 across both would compare
  a tuned recipe against a detuned one. Its micro-batch drops to 1 and accumulation rises to
  16, so the effective batch stays at 16 while the optimizer states for 1.2B parameters fit
  beside the activations on the same card.
- **C6** moves α with r, keeping α/r at 2. Rank is the variable; the scaling factor is not.
- **C2'** returns the guardrail tokens to the base mix rather than shrinking the run, so the
  comparison is the guardrail block against an equal weight of ordinary supervision.

## Equal cost, enforced

Each arm trains on exactly **64,000,000 tokens**, which is 0.71 of an epoch over the
calibrated mix and **4.45 GPU-hours** at the 3,994.2 tok/s measured on an L40S at `s5.1`.
Eight arms is **35.6 GPU-hours**. The run asserts its own token count at the end and fails
the arm if it lands under 90% of budget, because an arm that quietly trained on less is an
arm whose result reads as a hyperparameter effect and is a compute effect.

The `s5.1` calibration derived the budget from the mix constraints, which was right for a
calibration and wrong for a sweep. The constraints now set the *shares* and the budget is
fixed, so the two roles with fixed doses come out of the budget rather than on top of it:

| Role | Dose | Tokens | Share of budget | Passes over the source |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tool calling | scaled | 24,900,000 | 38.9% | 0.61 |
| Guardrail block | 2 passes, fixed | 8,703,580 | 13.6% | 2.00 |
| SQL | scaled | 15,142,000 | 23.7% | 0.17 |
| Code | scaled | 12,506,000 | 19.5% | 0.17 |
| Structured output | scaled, capped at 3 passes | 2,746,000 | 4.3% | 1.83 |

Priority roles take **56.8%** against a 50% floor. C5a and C5b take their replay off the top
(0.64M and 3.2M tokens), which pulls the base roles down proportionally and leaves the total
unchanged.

**Why two passes over the guardrail block.** The block is 7,988 rows and 4.35M tokens, and the
amended gate asks the probe flag rate to move from 0.0074 to 0.35. Dosing it by share would
make its weight an artifact of how large the rest of the corpus happens to be, so it is dosed
in passes like structured output, which the same argument already allows three. Two sits under
that precedent and lands at 13.6% of the budget. The dose is the first knob at `s5.4` if the
flag rate falls short, and `C2'` is what tells us whether the block is doing anything at all.

## The entropy-weighted loss, and the trap it avoids

C4 weights each supervised token by `1 + β·(H − H̄)`, where `H` is the model's own normalized
predictive entropy at that position and `H̄` is the mean over the supervised tokens in the
micro-batch, clamped at 0.1 below. The centring is the whole point: it holds the mean weight at
1, so `β` cannot act as a hidden learning-rate multiplier and the arm measures loss shaping.
Entropy is computed under `no_grad` in chunks over the flattened token axis, because a float32
softmax over the full vocabulary for a whole micro-batch is a gigabyte-scale temporary.

Every arm is scored on **plain cross-entropy over the held-out split**, never on its own
training objective. C4 reweights what it optimizes, and an arm ranked on its own reweighted
loss cannot be compared to the others.

## What has to exist before every arm can run

**The replay set does not exist.** C5a and C5b are the only arms that need it. Task
`s5-selfdistill` (`f0f8801f`) passed its `s5.1` smoke at 64 prompts and has never been run at
size; the prompt pool it draws from is `tidepool/s4.4/prompt_pool.jsonl.gz`, 447,053 rows.
C5b needs 3.2M replay tokens, so the generation run is sized at roughly 8,000 prompts. Until
it lands, an arm queued with `replay_frac > 0` and no `replay_object` **fails loudly** rather
than silently training C1 under a C5 label.

## Local verification

`test_sampler.py` runs on CPU over gzip fixtures in about a second and covers the three ways
this sweep could be quietly invalid: arms costing different amounts, the guardrail-off arm
receiving guardrail rows or the guardrail-on arms receiving none, and selection order drifting
between arms. **25 checks, all passing**, including:

- every arm lands within 0.1% of the 64.0M budget, on the real measured corpus sizes
- C1 sees the guardrail block exactly twice and C2' sees none of it
- C2' returns the freed tokens to the base mix instead of shrinking the run
- a replay arm with no replay rows is flagged rather than run as C1
- a budget smaller than the fixed doses is flagged rather than silently starving the mix
- two arms differing only in a hyperparameter draw identical base rows in identical order
- centring holds the mean entropy weight at 1.0000 at β ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} while still spreading
  individual weights from 0.23 to 2.09

## Queue order

The account allows two GPUs at a time and both are occupied by `s5.2` rows. As they free:

1. the four remaining baseline evals, B1r first
2. a smoke of this task at `smoke: true, max_steps: 30`, on arm C4, which exercises the new
   multi-source sampler, the guardrail role and the entropy loss in one cheap run
3. the replay generation at size, alongside the first sweep arm
4. C1 and C7 first, then C2', C4, C6, C3, then C5a and C5b once replay exists

**No `sweeps:` block, deliberately.** A `sweep_config` fans out inside a single job. Two GPUs
at a time and 4.45 GPU-hours per arm would make that one job run for a day and a half, a single
failure would take the whole C row, and the ledger would lose the per-arm job ids it is written
against. Individual queues cost one extra command each and keep all three.

## The replay buffer, and the silent failure it was one field away from

`C5a` and `C5b` are the only arms with a prerequisite outside the sweep task. Both replay
self-distilled completions over the general-instruction prompt pool, and that buffer did not
exist: the generator had only ever run a 64-prompt smoke at `s5.1`.

Sizing it is arithmetic. The smoke produced 245 completion tokens per prompt, greedy, at 320
new tokens maximum. `C5b` replays 5% of a 64.0M-token budget, or 3.2M tokens, and a training
row costs its prompt as well as its completion, so 8,000 prompts land near 4M training tokens
and `C5b` makes a little under one pass over the buffer. The run asserts on that ratio rather
than trusting the estimate: if the buffer came back small enough for `C5b` to replay it more
than twice, the arm would be measuring memorization of a small set as much as it measures
replay, and the run fails instead of producing a number that looks like replay and is not.

The sample is drawn by hash rank against a fixed salt, so 8,000 prompts is a strict superset
of the smoke's 64, and those 64 have already been generated twice with byte-identical output.
Greedy decoding and one generation for the whole sweep are what make the buffer a constant
rather than a source of variance in the comparison it exists to stabilize.

**The defect worth recording is the one that would not have announced itself.** The sweep's
sampler reads every source through a single code path: rows are keyed by `role`, each role is
sized from an `n_tok` field, and a file is opened by its suffix. The replay writer emitted
neither. A buffer with no `n_tok` indexes as **zero available tokens**, and a role with zero
available tokens contributes nothing to the mix, so both replay arms would have trained the
reference recipe, reported a replay fraction of 1% and 5% in their run records, and produced
the result that replay changes nothing. Every guardrail in the sweep would have passed. The
writer now counts tokens with the training tokenizer and the training template, writes
`replay.jsonl.gz`, and places the file at `tidepool/s5.3/replay/`, because the arms load
replay from shared storage rather than from a job artifact. The generating run fails if the
placement fails.

Card: an L40S rather than the smoke's L4, chosen on throughput, pinned to aws per the
provider note. The run is priced at well under an hour.

## The gate passes, and the replay buffer fails on the defect it was rewritten to prevent

Both `s5.2` slots freed at once when `B4` and `B5` landed together, so the smoke and the replay
generation went out in parallel rather than in the serial order recorded above. The smoke was
also what forced a task fix: `s5-sft-sweep`'s registered `parameters:` block did not list `smoke`,
`smoke_rows` or `max_steps`, although `main.py` reads all three, and the queue validator rejects
unknown keys outright. All three were added at exactly `main.py`'s own defaults (`false`, `512`,
`0`), so the eight full arms are byte-identical to what was specified.

**The gate passed.** `2680e4a8`, arm `C4`, 30 steps over 238,195 tokens in 6m25s at 2,833 tok/s,
val loss 1.2552 → 0.3599. The multi-source sampler, the `tool_guardrail` role and the
entropy-weighted loss all ran together without incident. All eight arms are cleared.

**The replay buffer failed, and the assertion that caught it earned its place.** `62d88386`
generated cleanly: 7,945 of 8,000 sampled prompts usable inside the 1,024-token cap, 7,945
completions, 0 empty, **1,850,696 completion tokens** at 1,688.5 tok/s, file placed in storage.
Then it failed one check, because the `n_tok` written into every row was **2**.

The cause is one call. `row_tokens` did `len(tok.apply_chat_template(msgs, tokenize=True, ...))`.
On this `transformers` version that call returns a `BatchEncoding`, and `len()` of a
`BatchEncoding` is its **key count** — `input_ids` and `attention_mask` — not its token count. So
a 1.85M-token buffer indexed as 15,890 training tokens.

This is the same defect this generator was rewritten at 17:09Z to prevent, arriving through a
different door. The rewrite made sure `n_tok` was *written*; it did not make sure the number
written was *a token count*. What saved it is that the earlier reasoning also left a downstream
check with independent evidence behind it: `C5b`'s 3.2M-token dose over a 0.02M-token buffer reads
as **201.4 passes** against a 2.0 ceiling, so the run failed and no arm ever trained on it.

Three changes went up together:

- `_ids()` unwraps `BatchEncoding`, plain `dict`, and batched list-of-lists forms before counting.
- **An exact invariant**, not a heuristic: a training row carries its prompt *and* its completion,
  so the buffer's training-token total can never fall below the completion tokens it was built
  from. This run violated that by two orders of magnitude. The ratio check caught the symptom;
  this names the cause at the point of failure instead of leaving it to be inferred.
- `test_row_tokens.py`, 8 cases, covering every return shape and asserting the `len() == 2`
  regression itself still reads 2 so the test cannot quietly stop testing anything.

`C5a` and `C5b` are last in the arm order, so nothing is blocked. The requeue goes out when a slot
frees. 0.372 GPU-h was spent and is recorded: the generation really ran.

## Arms now share a GPU

*2026-08-25 21:40Z*

The operator pushed back, correctly, on running one arm per card. An L40S is 48 GB; a
1.2B model with a rank-16 adapter uses a fraction of it. Treating the account's two-GPU
cap as a cap on *parallel work* was my error: it caps how many cards are up, not how much
runs on them. Eight arms at one per card is four sequential rounds and most of both cards
idle throughout.

`pack.py` is the supervisor that fixes it. It runs `main.py` once per arm as a separate
child process on one card, which puts the isolation in the operating system rather than in
a convention:

| concern | how a packed arm is isolated |
|---|---|
| process | its own `python main.py`; an OOM, an assertion or a kill takes that arm alone |
| memory | a hard allocator ceiling of `pack_headroom / n`, so an arm growing into its neighbours hits its own limit first |
| filesystem | writes only to `out/<arm>/`; no two arms can touch one path |
| reporting | children never call the job API; the supervisor collects each `score.json` and uploads artifacts `<arm>__`-prefixed |
| inputs | the corpus is downloaded once per card, not once per arm |
| shutdown | SIGTERM forwarded to every child, escalated to SIGKILL after a grace period |

Starts are staggered by 30–45 s. Every child peaks its memory while loading the same base
model, so starting four together stacks four load peaks that the steady state never
reaches — the pack could die on a transient that the run it is trying to do never touches.

**Comparability is the thing packing must not cost, so it is enforced rather than argued.**
A packed arm runs the same `main.py` a solo arm runs, from the same directory, copied by
`sync_pack_task.sh`; the only differences are where it writes, that it takes its config off
the environment, and that it exits non-zero instead of reporting. Arm C1 ran solo before
packing existed and is still running solo now, which makes it the control: if packing moved
the numbers, C1-solo against a packed arm's tokens-seen, priority share and row order is
where it would show.

Two failure modes were easy to introduce and invisible once introduced, so both are caught
statically by `test_pack_isolation.py` over `main.py`'s own syntax tree — one unguarded
`lab.log` puts four writers on one progress stream, one unguarded `lab.storage_download`
pulls the corpus once per arm. The stub a child gets in place of the job API answers
reporting calls as no-ops but *refuses* capability probes, because
`getattr(lab, "storage_upload", None)` coming back as a no-op would have the caller report
a successful upload of nothing. Both checks were mutation-tested: breaking each rule on
purpose fails the test.

Runs now also record peak GPU memory whether packed or not, which is the number that sizes
the next pack.

### What the smoke measured

The four-arm smoke returned in nine minutes and settled every open question about sizing.

Three arms finished clean and one failed, which is the result worth having. C3 hit
`torch.OutOfMemoryError` inside Adam's second-moment update while **9.4 GB was still free on
the card**: its own per-process ceiling stopped it, its three siblings never noticed, and the
supervisor reported a partial pack with the failing arm named and its last forty console
lines in the job log. That is the isolation doing exactly the job it was built for, bought
for 0.15 GPU-hours instead of at hour six of a 900-minute run.

| | torch says allocated | torch says reserved | driver and siblings see |
|---|---|---|---|
| LoRA r16 arm | 6.07 GB | 6.98 GB | **7.52 GB** |
| LoRA r64 (C6) | — | — | **7.22 GiB** |
| entropy-weighted (C4) | — | — | **10.64 GiB** |
| full-parameter (C3) | — | — | **~20–22 GB** (OOMed inside 11.0) |

`max_memory_allocated` undercounts by about 1.5 GB per arm, so sizing on it overcommits a
card by 4–6 GB across a pack of four. `pack_summary.json` now sizes on
`max_memory_reserved` plus a 0.55 GB CUDA context allowance, which reproduces the observed
footprint to within a tenth of a gigabyte.

Two numbers justify the whole exercise. Aggregate throughput across three packed arms was
**4,283 tok/s against 2,833 tok/s for a single arm alone** at the same step count — 1.51×
per card, so the sweep costs about a third fewer card-hours and leaves the second slot free
for other work. And packed C4 consumed **238,195 tokens, identical byte for byte to the solo
C4 smoke**: same rows, same order, same priority share. Packing changed the schedule, not the
science.

Pack A (`7e8ca5f9`) is C3 + C7 + C2' on one L40S at 22 / 9.5 / 9.5 GB under a 0.86 headroom,
C3 first because full-parameter is the slowest arm and should start earliest. C1 stays solo
as the control. Pack B is C4 + C6 + C5a + C5b at 13 / 9.5 / 9.5 / 9.5, once the replay buffer
the C5 pair reads exists.

### An arm does not have to be a trainer

Two of the eight arms replay a buffer of the base model's own frozen answers, and that buffer
is generated by a different program. Run as its own job it is a card at a fifth of its
capacity for forty minutes, followed by a second queue-and-launch cycle before the two arms
that need it can start; and the account refuses a third GPU rather than queueing it, so the
two cycles are strictly serial.

The supervisor now takes three more parameters that turn that into one card-run:

- `pack_scripts` — an arm runs a named program rather than the sweep trainer. `replay.py` is
  the self-distillation generator, made packable the same way `main.py` was: same stub for the
  job API, same `storage()` resolver, same exit-code contract. Its generation path is
  untouched, and the contract test now compares the two files' packing preambles directly, so
  an edit to one that is not made to the other fails locally.
- `pack_after` — an arm is held until another exits clean. One level deep, deliberately: two
  waves cover producer-then-consumer, and a real scheduler inside a supervisor whose value is
  that it is simple to reason about is a bad trade.
- `pack_provides` — names the file that hands over. When the producer exits clean the
  supervisor registers `out/<arm>/<file>` as the local stand-in for the object its consumers
  were going to download, and uploads it there once, on their behalf. The consumers read local
  disk; shared storage still ends up with the buffer for every later stage.

Sizing had to change with it. Relative shares divide the card between every arm listed,
including arms that never coexist, so a consumer still pays for the producer it replaced.
`pack_gb` states each ceiling in gigabytes — the unit the measurements arrive in and the unit
the failures arrive in — and the ceiling is checked against the pack's worst *moment* rather
than the flat sum: for each choice of which producers have already finished, add up the arms
alive in that state, and take the largest. Pack B peaks at 41.5 GB of a 42.9 GB limit, and it
peaks after the generator has handed over, which is exactly what the flat sum would have
refused.

**Scheduling is the one part of this that static checks cannot reach**, because it is a loop
over live processes and its failures all look like an ordinary exit code from outside: an arm
that never starts, an arm that starts before its input exists, a consumer that runs on a stale
file after its producer failed. So `test_pack_schedule.py` runs the supervisor for real
against arm scripts that sleep for fractions of a second and a stand-in for the job API, and
checks the handover, a failed producer, a producer that exits clean having written nothing,
peak sizing, an over-committed pack, per-arm scripts and four malformed-wiring cases. It takes
about ten seconds and needs no GPU. Mutating the supervisor to start every arm at once, or to
let consumers run after a failed producer, fails it.

Pack B is therefore the whole rest of the sweep as one job on one card: `RB` generating the
buffer, `C4` and `C6` training beside it from the start, and `C5a` and `C5b` starting when the
buffer exists. The stand-alone `s5-selfdistill` job is no longer on the critical path.

One further parameter exists for the stage after this one. The sweep trainer recognises its
own arm — `arm` selects a recipe from a table inside `main.py`, so one shared config describes
all eight arms — but an evaluation arm is a checkpoint and a component rather than a name in a
table, and s6 is eight arms across four components. `pack_overrides` takes JSON and patches the
shared config for one arm only, which is how a future child script is configured without an
arm table of its own. It is JSON rather than a bare string so that values keep their types; a
string parameter turns every number into text and every `false` into a `true`. Mutating the
supervisor to apply every arm's patch to every arm fails the scheduling test.

### The evaluation harness now packs too

Scoring is where the same waste shows up next, and worse. `s6` puts eight checkpoints through
four components; each pass alternates a generation batch with a stretch of CPU-bound grading,
so a card running one of them is idle for a good part of its own job. Eight sequential eval
jobs would be eight card-hours to do maybe three cards' worth of work.

`jobs/s5-eval/main.py` is now a packable child under the same contract as the trainer and the
replay generator, and the contract test reads all three rather than the two in this directory.
Three things had to change that the trainer never exposed, all of them the same failure in
different clothes: a shared default that two children would fight over.

**Arms differ in their inputs.** Every sweep arm reads one corpus; every eval arm reads a
different checkpoint. An adapter arrives in `pack_overrides`, so the supervisor's input
staging now walks the per-arm patches as well as the shared config, and it finds objects by
the project's own naming rule (any config key ending in `_object`) rather than a list written
out in `pack.py` that a new input would quietly fall off. Objects two arms share are still
fetched once. An object no arm on the card actually reads is no longer fatal: the arm that
needs it says which one it was denied, which is a better error than the supervisor guessing.

**The 4-bit path serves on a port.** `llama.cpp` runs as its own process listening on 8080.
Two of those on one card is not an out-of-memory error, which would at least be loud: the
second server fails to bind and the first answers both arms, and the run produces two
plausible-looking score sets from one model. Each child now gets its position in the pack on
the environment and derives its port from it, and the backend, the weights and the server log
go under the arm's own directory instead of three shared ones. The contract test fails if
either the port or the directories go back to being fixed.

**A memory ceiling only binds what allocates through torch.** The full-precision path does,
so it takes the same per-process ceiling the trainers use. The 4-bit server does not, and
pretending otherwise would be the dangerous kind of wrong: a pack carrying one sizes it by
its context length and slot count and records in the summary that that is what happened.

The changed harness is deliberately **not** registered as a task yet. It is the most reused
artifact in the project, every number in the comparison table came through it, and a solo
rerun of it is what most of `s5` is built on. It gets a small two-arm packed run of its own
for a job id before anything depends on it, on the first slot that frees after the sweep.

### A buffer in storage that must not be used

Shared storage already holds `tidepool/s5.3/replay/replay.jsonl.gz`, and it is the wrong
file. Job `62d88386` generated it: 7,945 self-distilled completions, 1.85M generated tokens,
none empty, uploaded, and then the job failed its own assertion. The generation was fine. The
accounting was not: every row was written with `n_tok = 2`, because `apply_chat_template`
returns a `BatchEncoding` on this version and `len()` over one counts its two keys rather than
its ids. The file therefore indexes a 1.85M-token corpus as 15,890 tokens.

The generator has been fixed since (it unwraps the encoding, and it now refuses to finish if
its per-row token count is smaller than the tokens it just generated), so `RB` in Pack B
rebuilds the object correctly and overwrites it. What was missing is a guard on the other
side. Nothing about the bad file looks wrong to a consumer: the rows are tagged `replay`, so
the "no replay rows" check passes, and the sampler simply asks for a 3.2M-token dose from a
set it believes holds 15,890 — which it satisfies by replaying the same 8,000 rows two hundred
times. C5b would have run to completion and reported a number measuring memorization of a
small set, under the name replay.

`main.py` now checks the mean tokens per row of any replay source it is given and fails the
arm if it is under sixteen, naming the object. A self-distilled row is a couple of hundred
tokens; a single-digit mean is a field counting something other than tokens. The stale object
is left where it is rather than deleted: the guard is what makes it safe, and a guard is worth
more than a clean bucket, since the next miscount will not be this one.

That is an account-region validation on the AWS account Transformer Lab runs on. It is
not capacity, not the team's 2-GPU cap, and not the task: the job reported `progress 0`,

**Why the fix was to change provider rather than to retry.** The provider steering note
(`updated_at 2026-08-23T22:48:37Z`) says two things that settle it. AWS GPU quota exists

without stopping before the last entry. The launch had landed in a region that has no
quota to begin with, and the region is not a knob `lab task queue` exposes — there is
`--provider`, and no `--region`. Retrying AWS unchanged would have been a coin flip on
which region the scheduler picked next. Working the row is the documented move, so `C1`
went to **Nebius**, the second entry.

Attempt 3 is job `1728ed4a-ff04-4cdf-b1dc-c05f5df05de2`, queued 03:46Z on `nebius` with
the recipe unchanged in every parameter. It reached `launching_cluster` inside a minute,
past the point attempt 2 died.

**What this does to comparability.** `C1` is the reference every other arm is measured
against, and it is now the only arm that will have run on a different provider from its
siblings. The card is the same model either way — L40S:1, 48 GB — and the recipe,
tokenizer, seed and 64.0M-token budget are byte-identical, so the comparison is between
two runs of the same code on the same card from two vendors. That is a weaker guarantee
than the one packing has (packed `C4` consumed exactly the solo `C4` smoke's 238,195
tokens), and it is worth stating rather than assuming: if `C1`'s throughput or loss curve
sits oddly against the packed arms, the provider is a candidate explanation and re-running
it on AWS once the region clears is the check. The provider note's own narrow exception
points the same way — a number to be compared directly against numbers on specific
hardware should not silently change hardware. Here the hardware model does not change,
only who rents it, so the run proceeds and the caveat is recorded.

**Relaunch accounting for `s5.3`/`C1`:** three attempts, each with a distinct named cause
and a distinct fix — a silent stall with no identified cause (stopped, and answered with
checkpointing plus the watchdog), a launch refused by an account-region validation
(answered by moving to the next source in the card's row), and the attempt now running.
Two of the three are progress by the rule that matters: the failure signature changed each
time. Nothing about the experiment design has been weakened to get a job through.

### Nebius has its own ceiling, and it is not ours

If a later Nebius launch is refused with `ResourceExhausted ... compute.instance.count
(limit N, requested N+1)`, that is the account-wide cap Nebius applies across every
Transformer Lab tenant. It costs nothing, it clears when other tenants release instances,
and the response is to move to **RunPod**, the third entry in the L40S row, rather than to
retry Nebius unchanged.
