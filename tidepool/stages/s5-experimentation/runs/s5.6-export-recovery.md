# s5.6 — export and recovery

Row **G** of the s3 plan, tested against **H4**. The stop/go into s6 is pre-registered:
**97% or more average retention across the axis suite with no single axis below 93%, at
1.5 GB or less on disk.**

## What s5.5 hands over

R3 is the selected checkpoint, chosen on tool calling first under s5.4's fallback after the
replay ladder came back null on instruction following. C7 is retained as the fallback,
because R3 carries the ladder's only family-corrected regression, 5.15 points of IFStruct
first-attempt schema validity. Both adapters are in shared storage under
`tidepool/s5.3/arms/{R3,C7}/adapter.zip`.

| | BFCL composite | bfcl_text | IFEval | IFStruct | detection |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| R3 | 0.7136 | 0.6739 | 0.7560 | 0.0885 | 0.7148 |
| C7 | 0.7049 | 0.6456 | 0.7338 | 0.1400 | 0.7222 |
| B1 base | 0.6649 | 0.5504 | 0.8189 | 0.1355 | 0.0074 |

Retention is measured against **each checkpoint's own full-precision self**, not against the
base and not across checkpoints. That is what H4 is about and it is the only reading the
quantization floor criterion supports.

## The four rows, and why the order is what it is

| row | step | varies | est. |
|---|---|---|--:|
| G1 | merge, convert, quantize, measure size and decode throughput | Q4_0 and Q4_K_M, R3 and C7 | ~1.5 h |
| G4 | quantizer group size | 32 against 64, on whichever format G1 favours | ~1.0 h |
| G2 | PTQ warm start plus KL(T=1) self-distillation, attention and preceding recurrent layers at BF16, KV cache FP8, LR 1e-5 to 1e-6 | recovery on the losing axis | ~8 h |
| G3 | error-targeted stepwise preference pass, a few hundred examples | targeting the axis G1 shows is worst | ~2 h |

**G1 and G4 come before G2 and G3 because recovery is only worth buying where there is a
measured loss.** The plan writes G2 and G3 as recovery steps, and both are expensive; which
axis they should target is a fact G1 produces rather than one to assume. If Q4_K_M at the
right group size already clears 97/93, G2 and G3 are a smaller job or none at all, and the
saving goes to s6's seed replicates.

**G3 is where the IFStruct regression gets its one attempt.** An error-targeted preference
pass over a few hundred examples is the cheapest lever the plan contains for a specific
failure mode, and R3's specific failure mode is first-attempt schema validity. Whether it is
pointed there depends on G1: if quantization itself costs structured output, the same pass
serves both purposes; if not, the target is the quantization loss and the IFStruct gap goes
to s6 as a reported limitation of the delivered model.

## The tooling already exists, which is why G1 is cheap

`s5-llama-build` packages more than the runtime. Its tarball
(`tidepool/llama-b10622-sm89.tar.gz`, tag b10622, built for sm_89 and verified) carries
`bin/llama-server`, `bin/llama-cli`, `bin/llama-quantize`, `bin/llama-bench`, and alongside
them `convert_hf_to_gguf.py`, `gguf-py/` and the converter's requirements file, pinned to the
same tag as the runtime. `adapters.py` in the eval pack already merges a LoRA arm onto its
base and distinguishes an adapter from a full-parameter checkpoint by looking rather than by
a parameter. So the export path is merge, convert to F16 GGUF, `llama-quantize` to each
format, then serve through the same `gen_gguf.py` backend every published 4-bit baseline was
measured on. No new dependency and no second runtime.

The confound is the one the harness already documents: a Q4-against-FP delta taken across
`transformers` and `llama.cpp` is a delta between two deployed artifacts, weights and runtime
together. G1 therefore measures **three** points per checkpoint, not two: FP in
`transformers`, F16 GGUF in `llama.cpp`, and Q4 in `llama.cpp`. The middle one is what makes
the retention number about quantization rather than about the runtime, and the project has
already paid for that distinction once at s5.2, where it split B2's shortfall into a runtime
half and a quantization half.

## Cost, stated before it is spent

Ledger stands at **65.916 of 145 GPU-hours**, so 79.1 remain. s6 is pre-registered as the
full suite on three finalists at FP and Q4, three seeds on the single winner, the robustness
set and the decontamination re-check, which prices at roughly 25 GPU-hours with the training
seeds dominating. **s5.6 is therefore sized at 20 to 25 GPU-hours** and leaves s6 whole with
roughly 30 in reserve.

What a larger version would cost, per the operator's standing note: running G2 over both
checkpoints rather than the one that needs it adds about 8 hours; a full quantizer sweep at
G4 (group size 32, 64 and 128 across both formats and both checkpoints) rather than the two
cells the plan asks for adds about 3; and a seed replicate of G2 to put an interval on the
recovery adds another 8. None of the three is queued. The first would be worth buying only if
G1 shows both checkpoints losing on different axes, and that is a decision to take after G1
reports rather than now.

## Two harness changes that went in before G1, and one gap to resolve at G4

**`gen_gguf.py` could only serve a GGUF published on the Hub.** G1 produces files that live in
shared storage instead, so the resolution was extracted into `resolve_gguf()` and taught a
second source, `gguf_object`. The two sources are **mutually exclusive and raise** rather than
taking a precedence rule: a row that set both would serve one file and be labelled with the
other, and the label is what a retention number is attributed to. An object that resolves to a
directory holding two `.gguf` files raises for the same reason instead of taking the first
match. 7 new checks in `test_resolve_gguf.py`, wired into `sync_eval_pack.sh`; the whole
eval-pack suite is green and the patched file is uploaded to task `08b4e028`.

**The `d4a7d46b` storage-mirror defect is closed in the same work.** `put()` now tries the
SDK's observed call shapes in order and records which one worked, instead of assuming a
signature. A failed mirror is a recorded note; what must not happen again is a job that
believes it mirrored something and did not, which cost `d4a7d46b` every one of its mirrors and
had the adapters recovered from job artifacts by hand.

**Known gap, recorded now so it is not a surprise at G4.** The plan writes G4 as group size 32
against 64, citing 2604.07888v1 and 2407.11062v3. `llama.cpp`'s block structure is **fixed per
quantization type** and is not a quantizer knob, so G4 cannot be run as written: it has to be
re-expressed as a choice among types (Q4_0 against Q4_K_M against IQ4_XS, say) or run through a
different quantizer entirely. The decision is deferred until G1 reports, since G1's numbers
determine whether G4 is worth running at all.

## G1 attempt log

**Attempt 1 — `e7dd289e`, FAILED, 0.117 GPU-h, no artifacts.** Set up, pulled the pinned
serving path, merged R3's rank-16 adapter onto the base and wrote the merged checkpoint, then
died two seconds into `convert_hf_to_gguf.py`:

    ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'conversion'

**Failure signature:** `ModuleNotFoundError: conversion`, at the first conversion, after the
first merge.

The cause is packaging and it predates this stage. At tag b10622 `convert_hf_to_gguf.py` is a
thin front end that does `from conversion import ModelBase, get_model_class, ...`; every
per-architecture writer lives in a sibling `conversion/` package at the repo root, and
`lfm2.py` there is what would write an LFM2.5 GGUF (`@ModelBase.register("Lfm2ForCausalLM")`,
which is exactly what the base model's `config.json` declares). `s5-llama-build` copied
`convert_hf_to_gguf.py`, `gguf-py/` and the converter's requirements file out of the source
tree and not that package, so **the serving path in shared storage has never been able to
convert anything.** Nothing caught it earlier because nothing had tried: every 4-bit number in
the project before this one came from a GGUF published on the Hub, and the tarball's own
verification checked that the binaries run, not that the converter imports.

**Fix, and why it is not a rebuild.** Rebuilding the tarball would recover 1.2 MB of pure
Python at the cost of recompiling the CUDA runtime, which would move the backend under every
4-bit number already recorded (B2, B3, B5, and the s5.2 runtime-versus-quantization split).
So the package is carried as its own object, cut from the **same pinned tag**:

    tidepool/llama-b10622-conversion.tar.gz   192 KB, 89 writers, sha256 233af659…

`s5-export` now takes a `conversion_object` parameter and unpacks it into the serving root
**only when the root does not already carry `conversion/`** — a repair, never a substitution,
so a future tarball built with the package is left alone. It then runs `python -c "import
conversion"` from the serving root **before touching any checkpoint**, so the same defect
would now cost seconds instead of a merge. `FACTS["conversion_source"]` records which of the
two paths the run took.

`s5-llama-build`'s copy list is fixed in the project tree as well (`conversion` added
alongside `gguf-py`), so the next rebuild is self-contained. That fix is not retroactive and
the current tarball is unchanged.

**Attempt 2 — `22a2d914`, queued 2026-08-28T14:06:52Z**, same task (`6fcd7fb8`), same card
(L4:1 on aws), same parameters plus `conversion_object`. **FAILED at 14:15Z, 0.144 GPU-h, and
the conversion fix is proven.** The package imported cleanly from the serving root (89 writers),
R3's rank-16 adapter merged onto the base, and `convert_hf_to_gguf.py` wrote `R3-F16.gguf` at
rc=0 in 20 seconds, which reached `tidepool/s5.6/R3/` at 2.343 GB. So the run made real
progress and the no-progress count resets.

It then hit **a second defect in the same archive, independent of the first**: the serving
tarball carries the executables and no shared objects, and they are linked against the CUDA
runtime stack. On this L4 image `libnccl.so.2` is not on the loader path, so `llama-bench`
exited 127 (recorded as a failure and survivable, `check=False`) and `llama-quantize` exited
127 (fatal, `check=True`), stopping the run at R3's first quantization. The build host had those
libraries, which is why `s5-llama-build`'s own verification passed, and no consumer had ever run
these binaries anywhere else: s5.2's 4-bit work used the vendor's published GGUF through a
serving path launched on a different image.

**Fixed at consume time rather than by a rebuild**, for the same reason as the first defect.
`s5-export` now discovers this environment's CUDA library directories (`site-packages/nvidia/*/lib`
from the pip CUDA wheels that torch pulls in, plus `<serving-root>/lib` and `/usr/local/cuda/lib64`
if they exist), puts them on `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`, and falls back to installing `nvidia-nccl-cu12`
if that is still not enough. It then **load-checks both `llama-bench` and `llama-quantize`
before the first merge**, treating rc 127 and the loader's own message as the signal and any
other non-zero exit as the tool talking about its arguments. A host that cannot run the binaries
now costs seconds. `FACTS["loader_repair"]` and `FACTS["loader_path"]` record what was needed.

**A second, quieter fix went in with it.** The 1.5 GB ceiling was being applied to every format
including F16, so attempt 2 logged `R3 F16: 2.343 GB … ceiling BREACHED` and would have appended
a failure that fails the job at the end even on a flawless run. A 1.2B model at 16 bits is
2.3 GB by arithmetic and F16 is an intermediate on the way to the 4-bit builds; the
pre-registered criterion is about the delivered build. The ceiling is now judged on the
quantized formats only and F16's size is recorded without a verdict.

`s5-llama-build` carries a note for the next rebuild: a self-contained archive should copy the
resolved non-system `.so` files into `<root>/lib`, which is why `s5-export` looks there first.

**A third defect, and the first one that is mine rather than the archive's.** `llama-quantize`

### G1, quality half — queued, `af80ef62` (R3) and `f7ebf0a3` (C7)

H4 is a claim about retention, and retention needs the axis suite run on the exported files. Six
rows: two checkpoints × {F16, Q4_0, Q4_K_M}, full item counts on all four components, the
138-item clean-control corpus carried, prompts rendered by the full-precision tokenizer so a Q4
row and its FP reference see identical bytes.

Three serving points per checkpoint rather than two, for the reason this project already paid for

Packed three arms to a card and split by checkpoint: wall clock on a pack is the slowest arm

score identically across all four axes, the two merges produced the same weights and the fault is
in the export, not in the retention. That reading takes precedence over any retention conclusion
drawn from the same table.

### G1, quality half — attempt 1 failed at server startup; `af80ef62` and `f7ebf0a3`

Both jobs died with nothing generated: `0/3 arms completed`, 0 tok/s, no artifacts, no score.
All six arms carry one failure signature and it is not ambiguous.

```
llama-server: error while loading shared libraries: libnccl.so.2:
cannot open shared object file: No such file or directory
```

`gen_gguf.load_gguf` raised `the server exited during startup (rc=127)` for each arm in turn,
0.5–1.0 min apart, on two separate cards. Spend 0.147 + 0.122 = **0.269 GPU-h**, charged in
full to the ledger.

**The staging was correct and that is what localizes the fault.** Every arm logged
`weights: tidepool/s5.6/<CK>/<CK>-<FMT>.gguf (695.8 MB), quantized by this project` and the
right port for its pack index before dying, so the `pack_overrides` merge, the `gguf_object`
resolution, the pack arithmetic and the exported files are all exonerated. What failed is the
host: `s5-llama-build` copies `llama-server` out of its build tree **without** the CUDA runtime
it links against, on the assumption that whatever machine runs it already has those objects on
the loader path. The L40S image that scored the s5.2 4-bit rows did. The L4 image does not.

**The project had already solved this and the fix had not travelled.** `s5-export/main.py` hit
the identical wall at its attempt 2 (`22a2d914`), documented it at length, and repairs the
loader path there before it merges anything. The eval pack is the only other place in the
project that launches a binary out of that archive, and it never got the repair. Recording that
as the actual defect: not a new failure mode, a fix applied in one of the two places that needed
it.

#### What changed

`gen_gguf.repair_loader_path()`, called between extracting the archive and launching the server,
and carrying the same three moves in the same order as the export job's:

1. probe the binary — rc 127 or the loader's own message, so a tool complaining about its
   arguments is not misread as a linking fault;
2. put this environment's `site-packages/nvidia/*/lib` on `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` (the pip CUDA wheels
   torch pulls in almost always already hold `libnccl.so.2`, so the first move installs nothing);
3. fall back to the `nvidia-nccl-cu12` wheel, and **raise** if that still fails — before a server
   is started and before an item is generated, so an unserviceable host costs a startup rather
   than a row that looks scored.

Which move it took is recorded per row in the serving facts as `loader_repair`, alongside the
backend and weights provenance already there, so a retention number carries the host repair it
needed instead of leaving it in a log nobody reads.

#### A second fault, one line past the first

Reading that path also turned up a `NameError` waiting immediately behind it: `load_gguf`'s
facts dict reads `size_mb`, which is bound in `resolve_gguf` and nowhere in `load_gguf` — a
leftover from the refactor that split `resolve_gguf` out to give `gguf_object` its own tests.
It fires the moment the server reports healthy, which is exactly one line past where attempt 1
died, so repairing only the visible fault would have bought a second dead card. The size is now
computed where it is used.

#### Tests

`test_loader_repair.py`, 7 checks: the no-repair-needed path probes once and installs nothing;
the wheel directories are tried before pip; the pip fallback is reported in the returned
description; an unrepairable host raises with the missing object named and `Nothing has been
served`; a non-loader non-zero exit is not read as a missing library; the directory list holds
no duplicates and no paths that do not exist. Against the pre-fix module all 7 fail (6 errors,
1 failure), which is the point of writing them here rather than describing the fix in a comment.

The `size_mb` regression is covered by a **whole-function** unbound-name scan of `load_gguf`
rather than a check for that one identifier — the class, not the instance. Wired into
`sync_eval_pack.sh` beside the existing contract checks, so it runs before any future build is
staged. Full `s5-eval` suite green, 14 tests. Task `08b4e028` re-uploaded before queueing.

#### Mirroring, and why it waits for the gate

The operator's standing note asks for artifacts to be mirrored to their GitHub and Hugging Face
repositories as they land. The six GGUFs have landed, and they are held in shared storage rather
than pushed, for one reason: the retention gate has not been judged yet, so nobody knows whether
these are the builds worth publishing. Publishing is also hard to take back once an index has
seen it. The mirror runs when G1 is decided, on whichever formats pass, with the retention table
alongside them so a downloaded build carries the numbers it was accepted on. No push credential
is reachable from this environment either, which is a second reason the push cannot simply be
done in passing.

#### Attempt 2's real outcome: both jobs completed clean by every check in place, and two of
each job's three arms were corrupted anyway

`46bd54cb` (R3) and `fb6f4bb9` (C7) both reported `completion_status: success`, correct item
counts, and non-empty `lab job artifacts` for all three arms apiece — every check this substage
had specified passed. Direct inspection of the downloaded completion files found otherwise: for
R3, `R3-F16` and `R3-Q4_0` came back 0% non-empty on three of four components; for C7, `C7-F16`
came back 100% empty on three components and 72.8% empty on the fourth, `C7-Q4_0` the same
shape at 60.0%. Only the format that ran mostly alone in each job's window — `R3-Q4_K_M` and
`C7-Q4_K_M` — has real content, confirmed genuinely clean by the same inspection.

**Root cause:** three concurrent 8-slot `llama-server` processes sharing one L4's *compute*
(not its VRAM — `pack_gb`'s ceilings arbitrate memory and have no lever on compute contention).
Per-token throughput on the contended arms collapsed from roughly 18 tok/s to roughly 0.28
tok/s, requests timed out, and `gen_gguf.py` converted those timeouts into silent empty-string
completions rather than a retry or a raised error. "The artifacts are non-empty" was never a
sufficient check, and it took a second job hitting the identical failure shape to confirm this
is a property of packing three arms onto one card's compute rather than something specific to
either export.

**Fix, two-part:** (1) `queue_s56_gguf.sh` rewritten to queue one job per arm, so every
`llama-server` gets the whole card and this exact contention cannot recur; (2) `asserts.py` /
`main.py` gained `check_completions()`, run after every `generate()` call, which fails the job
loudly (exit 3) if more than 2% of any component's completions come back empty — covered by
`test_completion_gate.py`, 7/7 passing against a dependency-free extraction of the assertion
machinery. Every job queued from this point forward is trusted to fail loudly on its own if this
recurs; jobs that predate the gate (`46bd54cb`, `fb6f4bb9`) needed the manual byte-level check
this section describes and got it. Charged in full despite the corruption, since the compute was
genuinely spent: 0.556 GPU-h (`46bd54cb`) + 0.906 GPU-h (`fb6f4bb9`) = 1.462 GPU-h.

Two things are worth saying about that before the numbers get repeated anywhere.

**The axis that broke is the axis the literature said would break.** Nine papers in `s2` had
structured output degrading first under quantization, and `2608.20210v1` measured a Q4_0
penalty in this exact architecture family that *grew* with capability. The pre-registered
"why it might not" for H4 named this failure and it happened. That is a prediction confirmed
and it belongs in the paper as one, not as a surprise found in the results.

**The ratio on that axis is carrying more weight than the rate underneath it can bear.**
IFStruct validity is 0.086 at full precision, so 85.5% retention is an absolute drop of 1.25
points, 25 items in 2000. Stack-idiom is 0.236 over 144 items, where Q4_K_M's 85.3% is five
items. Both are inside the range where sampling noise and a real regression look identical
on a ratio, which is exactly what the paired comparison is for and why the verdict waits on
it. Note also that an IFStruct validity of 0.086 *at full precision* is a finding about the
checkpoint and not about quantization; it is `s6`'s problem, and it is flagged here so that
nobody later reads a retention ratio as if the underlying rate were healthy.

**Decision 1 (taken autonomously): the pre-registered bar is applied unchanged.** The
tempting amendment — exempt an axis whose reference rate is near the floor from the ratio
criterion — would have moved a goalpost after seeing which side of it R3 landed on, and the
two axes it would exempt are precisely the two R3 fails. So the criterion stays at >= 93%
per axis and >= 97% on the mean, R3 is recorded as failing it at G1, and the ill-conditioning
is handled by *reporting* rather than by exemption: every retention row now carries the
reference rate and the absolute delta beside the ratio, and a `ratio_is_well_conditioned`
flag keyed on the reference rate alone (floor 0.30, which is where a 7% relative tolerance
stops being under two absolute points). A flag keyed on the reference cannot be tuned by the
outcome, which is the property that makes it safe to have added it after seeing R3.

**Decision 2 (taken autonomously): C7-Q4_K_M gets a solo relaunch after all.** This section
previously recorded it as needing none, since its arm in the packed job `fb6f4bb9` was
confirmed genuinely clean by byte-level inspection. That was right about the data and wrong
about the comparison. R3-Q4_K_M's packed arm was equally clean and was re-run solo anyway
for isolation-footing consistency (`b928b8fc`), so promoting C7's packed score would leave
the study with five arms measured alone on a card and one measured under three-way
contention — and that one row sits in the numerator of a retention ratio whose denominator
(C7-F16) is being measured solo. Mixing serving conditions across a ratio is the kind of
confound that is cheap to remove now and impossible to remove later. Cost is roughly 0.5
GPU-h against 75 remaining, and it queues the next time a slot frees.

**The comparison is split by checkpoint, and that is the strict direction.** `queue_s56_retention.sh`
runs one job per checkpoint, because the task takes one `reference` and retention has to be
against the arm's own full-precision self. The Holm family is therefore 8 cells rather than
16. For a claim that quality was *held*, a smaller family makes each corrected p-value
smaller and a degradation easier to flag, so the split tests H4 harder rather than more
softly. The verdict rests on the per-axis floor and the mean with the intervals beside them,
never on failing to reject.

**Changes to the compare task** (`ecd12a36`, re-uploaded this wake): a retention table
computed inside the job so the H4 verdict carries a job id; the four axes fixed in code
rather than derived, so which axes count is not a property of whichever table was to hand;
the ratio always taken against the run's `reference`; and the whole block **off by default**,
because against any other reference — the `s5.3` comparison's C1, say — the same arithmetic
silently prices the fine-tune and labels it quantization damage. Covered by
`test_retention.py`, 32 checks, wired into `sync_compare_task.sh`'s build-time gate; the
existing 53-check driver suite and the statistics suite still pass unchanged.

So the two readings genuinely disagree, and the disagreement is informative rather than a

low for the ratio to be well conditioned or for the paired test to resolve the difference.**

That is a reason to fix the reference rate, not the bar. `ifstruct_validity` at 0.086 at full
precision is the checkpoint's own weakness, and it is what makes the H4 arithmetic fragile in
both directions. Carrying it into s6 unexamined would leave the headline retention number
resting on an axis where 25 items decide the verdict.
