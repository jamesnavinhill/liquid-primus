# s5.6 — export and recovery

Row **G** of the s3 plan, tested against **H4**. The stop/go into s6 is pre-registered:
**97% or more average retention across the axis suite with no single axis below 93%, at
1.5 GB or less on disk.**

## What s5.5 hands over

R3 is the selected checkpoint, chosen on tool calling first under s5.4's fallback after the
replay ladder came back null on instruction following. C7 is retained as the fallback,
because R3 carries the ladder's only family-corrected regression, 5.15 points of IFStruct
first-attempt schema validity. Both adapters are in shared storage under
`tidepool/s5.3/arms/{R3,C7}/adapter.zip`.

| | BFCL composite | bfcl_text | IFEval | IFStruct | detection |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| R3 | 0.7136 | 0.6739 | 0.7560 | 0.0885 | 0.7148 |
| C7 | 0.7049 | 0.6456 | 0.7338 | 0.1400 | 0.7222 |
| B1 base | 0.6649 | 0.5504 | 0.8189 | 0.1355 | 0.0074 |

Retention is measured against **each checkpoint's own full-precision self**, not against the
base and not across checkpoints. That is what H4 is about and it is the only reading the
quantization floor criterion supports.

## The four rows, and why the order is what it is

| row | step | varies | est. |
|---|---|---|--:|
| G1 | merge, convert, quantize, measure size and decode throughput | Q4_0 and Q4_K_M, R3 and C7 | ~1.5 h |
| G4 | quantizer group size | 32 against 64, on whichever format G1 favours | ~1.0 h |
| G2 | PTQ warm start plus KL(T=1) self-distillation, attention and preceding recurrent layers at BF16, KV cache FP8, LR 1e-5 to 1e-6 | recovery on the losing axis | ~8 h |
| G3 | error-targeted stepwise preference pass, a few hundred examples | targeting the axis G1 shows is worst | ~2 h |

**G1 and G4 come before G2 and G3 because recovery is only worth buying where there is a
measured loss.** The plan writes G2 and G3 as recovery steps, and both are expensive; which
axis they should target is a fact G1 produces rather than one to assume. If Q4_K_M at the
right group size already clears 97/93, G2 and G3 are a smaller job or none at all, and the
saving goes to s6's seed replicates.

**G3 is where the IFStruct regression gets its one attempt.** An error-targeted preference
pass over a few hundred examples is the cheapest lever the plan contains for a specific
failure mode, and R3's specific failure mode is first-attempt schema validity. Whether it is
pointed there depends on G1: if quantization itself costs structured output, the same pass
serves both purposes; if not, the target is the quantization loss and the IFStruct gap goes
to s6 as a reported limitation of the delivered model.

## The tooling already exists, which is why G1 is cheap

`s5-llama-build` packages more than the runtime. Its tarball
(`tidepool/llama-b10622-sm89.tar.gz`, tag b10622, built for sm_89 and verified) carries
`bin/llama-server`, `bin/llama-cli`, `bin/llama-quantize`, `bin/llama-bench`, and alongside
them `convert_hf_to_gguf.py`, `gguf-py/` and the converter's requirements file, pinned to the
same tag as the runtime. `adapters.py` in the eval pack already merges a LoRA arm onto its
base and distinguishes an adapter from a full-parameter checkpoint by looking rather than by
a parameter. So the export path is merge, convert to F16 GGUF, `llama-quantize` to each
format, then serve through the same `gen_gguf.py` backend every published 4-bit baseline was
measured on. No new dependency and no second runtime.

The confound is the one the harness already documents: a Q4-against-FP delta taken across
`transformers` and `llama.cpp` is a delta between two deployed artifacts, weights and runtime
together. G1 therefore measures **three** points per checkpoint, not two: FP in
`transformers`, F16 GGUF in `llama.cpp`, and Q4 in `llama.cpp`. The middle one is what makes
the retention number about quantization rather than about the runtime, and the project has
already paid for that distinction once at s5.2, where it split B2's shortfall into a runtime
half and a quantization half.

## Cost, stated before it is spent

Ledger stands at **65.916 of 145 GPU-hours**, so 79.1 remain. s6 is pre-registered as the
full suite on three finalists at FP and Q4, three seeds on the single winner, the robustness
set and the decontamination re-check, which prices at roughly 25 GPU-hours with the training
seeds dominating. **s5.6 is therefore sized at 20 to 25 GPU-hours** and leaves s6 whole with
roughly 30 in reserve.

What a larger version would cost, per the operator's standing note: running G2 over both
checkpoints rather than the one that needs it adds about 8 hours; a full quantizer sweep at
G4 (group size 32, 64 and 128 across both formats and both checkpoints) rather than the two
cells the plan asks for adds about 3; and a seed replicate of G2 to put an interval on the
recovery adds another 8. None of the three is queued. The first would be worth buying only if
G1 shows both checkpoints losing on different axes, and that is a decision to take after G1
reports rather than now.
