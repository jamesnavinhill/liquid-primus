## Attempt 1: both jobs FAILED on the last line, after passing every check

Both jobs completed all of their work, saved every artifact, and then crashed on the same
statement: `lab.finish("success", ..., score=score)`. The SDK's `finish` takes
`(message, score, ...)` and has no status argument, so `"success"` bound to `message`, the
description bound positionally to `score`, and the explicit `score=` was a duplicate.

    TypeError: Lab.finish() got multiple values for argument 'score'

**Failure signature:** `TypeError: Lab.finish() got multiple values for argument 'score'`,
raised at the final status call, after `update_progress(100)` and after every
`save_artifact`. Identical in both jobs.

What that cost and what it did not. Every artifact saves before the call, so no measurement
was lost: the supervised run's five artifacts (`adapter.zip`, `metrics.json`, `score.json`,
`train_history.json`, `mix_calibration.json`) and the replay run's three (`replay.jsonl`,
`replay_summary.json`, `score.json`) are all listed by `lab job artifacts` and were read back
to recover the numbers below. What was lost is the job record: both read `FAILED` with
`job_data.score` empty, so neither appears in a `lab job list --score-metric` dashboard.

## Attempt 2: the fix, and why it is being re-run rather than waived

`lab.finish(message=..., score=...)` on success and `lab.error(message=...)` on assertion
failure, in both `main.py` files, with the SDK's real signature read off the published
package rather than inferred. Failed runs now carry their headline figures in the message,
since `error` takes no score; the numbers were already in `score.json` either way.

The science had passed, so re-running is not about the result. It is about what happens next:
the projected reference run is 16 hours, the baselines and the sweep after it are ranked by
`lab job list --score-metric`, and a crash on the last line empties exactly the field that
ranking reads. Catching that before the expensive half is what this substage is for, and a
patch that has never executed is not yet a fix. Two more L4 smokes at roughly 0.1 GPU-hour
each buy a proven path.

## L40S calibration

Task `s5-sft-l40s` (id `d6a7d16f`), a sibling of `s5-sft` that differs in four values:
`accelerators: L40S:1`, `smoke_rows: 1024`, `max_steps: 60`, `run_tag: s5.1-cal-l40s`. There
is no accelerator override at queue time, so a second task directory is the only way to change
the card. Sixty steps rather than thirty because the L4 run's throughput was still climbing at
its last step (1,571 tok/s against a 1,435 average), and an average taken before the rate
settles understates the card.

### Attempt 1 (`be8dcfde`) FAILED at launch, 0 GPU-h

`Launch failed: Unsupported GCP accelerator spec 'L40S:1'.` GCP's allowed list runs T4, L4,
P100, V100, A100-40GB, A100-80GB and H100:8, with no L40S at any count. The provider guidance
had already said so: the L40S row reads `AWS (g6e), Nebius, RunPod`, and the row is the
complete source list. `lab task queue` picked `gcp` because nothing pinned it. Failed in 4
seconds during `launching_cluster`, before any instance existed, so nothing is charged.

Two things to carry forward. `--provider` on `lab task queue` is the fix, and every job in the
`C` sweep needs it, since they all want the same card and all inherit the same default. AWS
also has a quota shape worth knowing before the sweep runs two arms at once: GPU quota exists
only in `us-east-1` (96 vCPU on-demand) and `us-east-2` (48), and one pool covers T4, A10G, L4
and L40S, so concurrent L40S arms eat the headroom the L4 jobs need.

### Attempt 2 (`68635a5d`) launching

Same task, `--provider aws`. Provisioning through `us-east-1a`.

Purpose: the plan's 145 GPU-hour budget is priced from an assumed 6,000 tokens/s for a 1.2B
model on one L40S. The only measurement in hand is 1,398 tok/s on an L4, and scaling that by a
spec-sheet card ratio is exactly the substitution this project's protocol refuses between
paired numbers. So the number gets measured on the card the `C` sweep will run on. Expected cost around 0.15 GPU-h.

**The plan's assumption was optimistic by 50%.** `s3.3` priced the whole 145 GPU-hour budget
at 6,000 tok/s on this card. The measured rate is 3,994.2, a ratio of 1.502. The L4 smoke had
put the L40S somewhere in 3,600–5,000 tok/s; the true figure sits at the bottom of that band,
so the card ratio taken off spec sheets would have been wrong in the direction that costs
money.

**A second number moved, and it is the one nobody was watching.** This run's sampler put the
epoch at 83.09M tokens where the L4 smoke put it at 90.6M, a 9% spread on the single figure
every training row is priced from. The mix rules are deterministic, so the spread is not in the
rules: it is in the per-role mean-tokens-per-row estimate, which is measured from a subsample
whose size follows `smoke_rows` (512 on the L4 run, 1024 here). Available `tool` tokens read
37.49M here against 40.8M there, `sql` 85.2M against 87.2M. Neither is wrong; both are
estimates, and the estimator is noisier than the budget table implied. Every projection below
is therefore quoted against the **conservative 90.6M** figure, and the full-scale runs will
report the exact count rather than an estimate of it, since at full scale the sampler walks the
whole pool.

Determinism across the two cards holds where it should: the template resolved `native` on both,
`priority_share` hit 0.5 exactly on both, and validation loss fell further here (0.2824 against
0.4771) on twice the steps, which is the expected direction.
