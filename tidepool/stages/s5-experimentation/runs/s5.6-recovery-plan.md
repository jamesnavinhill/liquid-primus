# s5.6 recovery ladder — what to spend next, and in what order

Written 2026-08-28, after G1 (plain export) came in under the bar on all four 4-bit arms and
before any recovery compute is spent. Recorded before the numbers, in the same way the s5.4
decision rule was.

## What the failure actually is

Eight of eight cells on the two well-conditioned axes clear the floor (tool calling 93.9 to
96.1%, instruction following 98.0 to 100.2%). Every failing cell is on an axis whose
full-precision rate is under 0.24, where the absolute drops are 0.25 to 3.5 points and the
ratio amplifies each point roughly tenfold. Which of the two low-base axes breaks moves around
by format and by checkpoint, so no single cell reads as a property of a quantization format.

So the target is narrow: recover a couple of absolute points on structured-output validity and
guardrail stack idiom, without giving back tool calling, at 1.5 GB or less on disk.

## The plan's ladder needs one correction before it is spent against

`plan.md` rows G2 and G3 propose a PTQ warm start with KL distillation from our own
full-precision checkpoint, then an error-targeted preference pass. Both are training rungs, and
both assume a quantization scheme the student can be trained through. Our delivered artifact is
a `llama.cpp` GGUF, and GGUF quantization is a one-way post-hoc transform: a student distilled
under simulated NF4 or GPTQ is not the artifact the retention table measures, so a gain bought
there would have to be re-measured through the GGUF path anyway and could evaporate in the
crossing. Naming the mismatch now is cheaper than discovering it after a training run.

The correction is to the *order*, not to H4. H4 claims 4-bit retention is buyable after the
fact and cheaply. The cheapest levers that act directly on the delivered artifact were never
enumerated in the G row, and they come first.

## The ordering, cheapest and most on-path first

**G4a — quant-type recovery on the delivered path. Est. 2.5 GPU-h. Queued first.**
The size bar has enormous unused headroom: the shipped builds are 0.696 and 0.731 GB against a
1.5 GB ceiling, so more than half the allowance is being left on the table. `llama-quantize`
can spend a little of it exactly where the failures are, by holding the token-embedding and
output tensors at higher precision while the bulk of the network stays 4-bit. That is what the
ecosystem's high-quality 4-bit builds do, and this project already carries one as a measured
baseline: B3 is unsloth's `UD-Q4_K_XL`, which beat plain Q4 by 5.6 points at s5.2. Four new
arms (two checkpoints x Q4_0-plus and Q4_K_M-plus), re-scored on the same harness, same items,
one arm per card. No retraining, no new weights, and the comparison against each checkpoint's
own F16 export stays exactly as pre-registered.

**G4b — calibrated (importance-matrix) quantization. Est. 1.5 GPU-h build + 2.5 GPU-h scoring.
Queued only if G4a leaves a gap.**
Weight importance estimated over this project's own calibration prompts, then fed to the
quantizer so it spends its bit budget on the tensors our workload actually exercises. It needs
a `llama.cpp` rebuild, since the `llama-imatrix` target was not in the b10622 build this
project made, which is the only reason it is second rather than first.

**G2 / G3 — the training rungs. Est. 8 to 10 GPU-h. Only if both G4 rungs leave a gap, and
only with the export-path mismatch above resolved first.**

## Budget

71.7 of 145 GPU-h spent, 73.3 remaining, with the s6 final evaluation reserved at 12. G4a and
G4b together are about 6.5 GPU-h, which the G row's remaining allocation covers. The training
rungs would take the G row past its 12 GPU-h line and are not started without measuring the
cheap rungs first, per the standing preference to size work to the compute actually available
and to price the larger version before spending past what was approved.

## Stop rule, written now

G4a is a success if any arm reaches every axis at 93% and the mean at 97%, at 1.5 GB or less.
If the best G4a arm clears the mean but misses one low-base axis by under two absolute points,
that is recorded as a near miss and G4b runs. If G4a moves the low-base axes by less than one
absolute point, the quantizer is not where the loss is, G4b is skipped as more of the same, and
the decision goes to whether the training rungs are worth their mismatch.
