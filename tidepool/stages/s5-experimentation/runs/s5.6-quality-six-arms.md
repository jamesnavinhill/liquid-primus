# s5.6 G1 quality half — all six exported serving points, one arm per card

Six GGUF serving points (two checkpoints × F16 / Q4_0 / Q4_K_M) scored on the same harness,
the same items and the same card shape every full-precision row in this project was measured
on. This record covers the arms as *measured*; the retention verdict is read from the
per-checkpoint comparison jobs, one per checkpoint, in `s5.6-retention-R3/` and
`s5.6-retention-C7/`.

## The pre-registered export-validity check passes

The queue script registered this before the numbers existed: *if `C7-F16` scores identically to
`R3-F16` across all axes, the two merges produced the same weights and the export is wrong, not
the retention.* They do not score identically.

| axis | R3-F16 | C7-F16 | difference |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| bfcl_composite | 0.7238 | 0.7057 | −0.0181 |
| ifeval_strict | 0.7468 | 0.7394 | −0.0074 |
| ifstruct_validity | 0.0860 | 0.1380 | **+0.0520** |
| probe_stack_idiom | 0.2361 | 0.1944 | −0.0417 |
| probe_flag_rate | 0.7407 | 0.7444 | +0.0037 |

The structured-output axis alone separates the two full-precision exports by 5.2 absolute
points, a 60% relative difference on a low base. The export pipeline carries each merge's own
weights; identical file sizes and decode speeds across the two checkpoints are fixed by
architecture and quantization type and were never evidence of a collapsed export.

## What these six arms settled, and what comes next

G1 asked one narrow question: does plain export hold the bar on its own. It does not. Size and
speed pass with room to spare and every 4-bit arm misses the quality half, with the failures
sitting entirely on the two axes whose full-precision rates are under 0.24. The recovery ladder
and its costed ordering are in `s5.6-recovery-plan.md`; the first rung (G4a, export job
`d2ce6665`) re-quantizes both checkpoints as `Q4_K_L` and `Q4_K_XL`, holding the
token-embedding and output tensors above the 4-bit body, and is scored on this same harness
with these same six arms untouched as the comparison.
