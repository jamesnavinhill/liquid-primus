# s4.2 — Column characterization and leakage risks

_Written 2026-08-25. Two kinds of statement live here and they are labelled: **schema**
facts, which are the datasets' own declared structure and are read directly from the
Hugging Face dataset-info endpoint, and **scouting** observations, which come from small
samples read row by row over the network._

**Scouting numbers are not results and are not cited anywhere else.** Their job was to
tell the diagnostics job what to count. Every number that reaches the stage report,
the data card, or the paper comes from job `s4-data-diagnostics`, over the full corpora,
with a job id attached. Where a scouting number appears below, it carries its sample
size so the job's answer can be checked against it.

IFStruct has **no response or target column**. Every field except `prompt` is a
constraint the validator checks, and `json_schema` and the boolean flags together are the
scoring rubric. A model can be scored on it; nothing in it can be trained on. That is why
s4.1 reclassified it as eval-only, and it is the sharpest file-level separation rule this
stage has to enforce.

BFCL's repository holds v3 category files only. BFCLv4 is the plan's secondary headline
number and its prompt set is not in this repository, so locating it is carried into s5.1
harness work with the ToolSandbox harness.

## Scouting observations

### Synth-APIGen carries two distinct refusal families, and one of them is mislabelled

Sampled 300 rows at 12 offsets. Two canned strings account for every non-call answer:

| Family | Answer text | Share of the 300 sampled | `tools` field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Empty-toolset abstention | "The query cannot be answered, no tools were provided." | — | `tools == []` in 22 of 200 rows in an earlier scan |
| Missing-parameter abstention | "The given question lacks the parameters required by the function." | — | `tools` populated, and the target function is usually present in it |
| Tool call | JSON array of calls | 225 of 300 | populated |

Non-call answers were 75 of the 300 sampled rows. Reading the target schema for
individual rows in the second family shows the label is right sometimes and wrong
sometimes, and the difference is mechanical:

- Row 3 asks to convert Cartesian `(3, 4)` and `(5, 12)` to polar. The declared tool
  `cartesian_to_polar_2D` takes exactly one parameter, `y`. The tool schema is itself
  degenerate for the task it describes, so refusing is defensible.
- Row 2471 asks to normalize `'apple,banana,apple,orange'` and `'dog,cat,dog,fish'`. The
  declared tool `convert_string_to_list_and_back` requires one parameter, `s: string`, and
  the query supplies both values verbatim. The row is answerable in two calls, and the
  label refuses.

The second case is the dangerous one. It is a **mislabelled over-refusal**, and the
project has a pre-registered ceiling on exactly that behaviour: a false-flag rate at or
below 0.15. Training on rows where the parameters are present and the label refuses
teaches the model to decline work it can do. `2512.04597v1` makes the adjacent point that
abstention learned from a fixed string becomes lexical rather than epistemic, which is the
same failure seen from the other side.

The diagnostics job separates the two families by exact string, and inside the
missing-parameter family applies a decidable test per row: for the declared target
function, are all `required` parameter names satisfiable from the query? Rows that pass
that test while carrying a refusal label are counted and their `hash_id`s written to an
artifact, so s4.4 can drop or relabel them by id rather than by re-deriving a heuristic.

### The suspected tool-name mismatch does not reproduce

Two papers in the s2 corpus document trajectories in the xlam lineage whose calls name
functions absent from the declared toolset, and s3 carried that in as a mandatory
repair-and-validate pass. A preview of one row appeared to show it. It did not survive
measurement: across 300 sampled rows and **389 individual calls, 0 called a function
missing from its own `tools` list**, and 0 rows failed JSON parsing. The apparent
mismatch was a truncated field in the preview, not a defect in the data.

Recording the negative result matters twice over. The repair pass stays in the pipeline
because the job re-runs the same check over all 49,402 rows rather than 300, and the
defect is not attributed to a dataset that does not appear to have it.

### ToolACE does not have one format, it has five

This was the largest surprise of the stage and it changes s4.4.

ToolACE declares its tool schemas inside the `system` string, and it does so in **five
different serializations**. Across 110 sampled rows: JSON 98, markdown bullet list 3,
YAML-ish `tool_name:` block 2, XML-ish `<tool_name>` block 2, LaTeX `tabular` 2, and 3 the
parsers here still do not classify. The call format varies with it: the common form is a
pythonic bracketed list `[FuncName(arg="value")]`, but some rows use custom delimiters such
as `[api_name]-<"paramName1"==param_value1; ...>` and `[api_Name]|(paramName1~"value1")`.

The variation is deliberate on ToolACE's part and it is the single most useful property the
corpus has for this project: it teaches a model to read a tool contract out of whatever the
system prompt happens to look like, which is exactly the operator's standing instruction to
specialize to the underlying technology and not to one vendor's spelling of it. It is also
a hard requirement on the preprocessing pipeline. A parser written for the JSON form
silently reports "no tools" on roughly one row in eight, and a naive reader then treats
those rows as empty-toolset abstention examples, which is precisely the mislabel this stage
is trying to avoid elsewhere. Five parsers are in the diagnostics job, and the job reports
the unclassified remainder as a number rather than dropping it.

Two more findings from the same sample:

- **Tool names are frequently not valid identifiers.** ToolACE's names include spaces:
  `Market Trends API`, `SEC Filings`, `Get Cars Information`. Of 188 sampled calls, about
  half carried a name no Python parser can read as an identifier. BFCL's pythonic
  categories score by parsing an abstract syntax tree, so training on these names as-is
  teaches output that the benchmark's own parser rejects. The fix belongs in s4.4:
  normalize names to identifiers in the training target, or convert those rows to a JSON
  call format, and say which in the data card.
- **The leading-space defect is not present.** The literature reports a whitespace form
  after `[` that breaks AST parsing. Zero of the sampled bracketed turns had it, and zero
  had a trailing space before `]`. Recorded as a negative result; the job re-checks all
  11,300 rows.

The first attempt at this analysis reported ToolACE as JSON-only with zero name
mismatches, because the regex reading a call name captured only the last word before `(`
and the schema reader looked only for a JSON array. Both were wrong, and both produced
clean-looking numbers. The corrected parsers find 4 name mismatches in 188 sampled calls,
which the job will resolve over the full corpus.

### Hermes json-mode rows are the structured-output training signal

`json_mode_agentic` (1,342) and `json_mode_singleturn` (1,241) carry an explicit `schema`
column, which is what makes them the replacement training signal for structured output now
that IFStruct is eval-only. Together 2,583 rows, against IFStruct's 2,000 held-out prompts
they are scored on.

## Leakage risks

Listed worst first. Each names the mechanism, not just the worry.

1. **IFStruct trained on by accident.** It was classified as "train + eval" until s4.1.
   Any path that lets its 2,000 prompts into a training mix inflates a headline number
   directly. Mitigation: it is on the eval side of the file-level separation, it is never
   in a mix manifest, and the diagnostics job reports its overlap against every training
   corpus so the separation is measured and not merely asserted.
2. **Group leakage on shared tool schemas.** Synth-APIGen rows are generated per function,
   and many rows share a function signature. A random split puts the same API schema in
   train and test, so the eval measures memorized signatures. Mitigation: the split key is
   the tool-schema signature, set in s4.3, and the job reports the group-size distribution
   so a small number of enormous groups cannot silently dominate a split.
3. **Group leakage on source repository file.** The in-house corpus is mined from 11
   repositories, and one file yields many examples. Mitigation: the split key is the source
   file path, and forks are excluded so a vendored copy of another project cannot appear on
   both sides.
4. **Benchmark prompt overlap.** The public training sets and the benchmark suites share
   ancestry: sql-create-context descends from WikiSQL and Spider, Clinton's text-to-SQL
   overlaps it, CodeFeedback overlaps HumanEval and MBPP idioms, and glaive-derived
   function-calling data circulates widely enough to touch BFCL's live categories.
   Mitigation: a 13-gram overlap pass from every eval prompt set against every training
   prompt, with the hit list written out by row id for removal in s4.4.
5. **Duplicate rows within and across corpora.** The Hermes config pair is the visible
   case; Clinton's text-to-SQL and sql-create-context are the likely cross-corpus case.
   Duplicates inflate an epoch's effective weight on whatever is duplicated and can
   straddle a split boundary. Mitigation: exact and normalized-hash dedupe counts per
   corpus and pairwise across corpora.
6. **Contamination through the replay fraction.** The replay mix exists to protect general
   quality, so it is the one component most likely to be drawn from a general instruction
   corpus that has itself seen benchmark data. Mitigation: the replay source goes through
   the same overlap pass as everything else, with no exemption for being "just replay".
7. **Model-generated data with a teacher in the eval loop.** Synth-APIGen rows carry
   `model_name` (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct observed). No teacher here
   is an LFM2.5 model and none is a judge in this project's eval, so there is no
   self-preference path. Recorded because it would be a leak if a later rung introduced a
   judge from the same family.

## What the diagnostics job measures

Job `s4-data-diagnostics`, one CPU job in experiment `tidepool`, produces every number the
stage report will carry:

- Per corpus and per config: rows, field-level missingness and empty-string rates, exact
  and normalized duplicate counts.
- Tool-schema distributions: tools per row, parameters per tool, required-parameter counts,
  nesting depth, and the declared type vocabulary including the `dict`/`object` split.
- Call distributions: calls per row, arguments per call, and the tool-name mismatch rate
  over every row rather than a sample.
- Refusal accounting: the two families by exact string, and the mislabelled over-refusal
  count with its row ids.
- Tokenization under the LFM2.5 tokenizer: tokens per row, tokens per tool call, and tokens
  spent on the tool-schema block, which is the diagnostic the 65k-vocabulary question needs.
- Group keys for s4.3: the tool-schema signature per row and the size distribution of the
  resulting groups.
- Decontamination: 13-gram overlap of every training prompt against IFStruct, IFEval,
  IFBench, Multi-IF, HumanEval, MBPP, MMLU-Pro and the BFCLv3 category files, with hit
  lists by row id.
