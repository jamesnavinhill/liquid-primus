# 2504.13932v3 — Enhancing Ultra-Low-Bit Quantization of Large Language Models Through Saliency-Aware Partial Retraining

**Relevance:** low
The paper targets 1–3 bit quantization of LLaMA-2-7B/13B evaluated on perplexity and commonsense QA, whereas this project needs 4-bit GGUF quantization of a 1.2B model evaluated on tool-calling/structured-output and instruction-following, so the bit-width regime, model scale, and eval axis all miss the target.

## TL;DR

The authors take ApiQ (a block-wise, LoRA-based PTQ method that jointly optimizes quantization clipping parameters and LoRA initialization) as a baseline for 2-bit LLaMA-2 quantization, and add a saliency-weighted L2 regularizer (Hessian-approximated via squared gradients, à la SqueezeLLM) that penalizes deviation of the most-influential weights from their original values. This closes a modest fraction of the accuracy gap to full precision (10.85% of the gap on LLaMA-2-7B, 7.54% on LLaMA-2-13B) with negligible extra memory but ~40% more training time. Two earlier attempts — swapping in BinaryMoS's or DB-LLM's more expressive quantization formulations under the same frozen-weights, LoRA-only regime — both failed to beat plain ApiQ.

## Techniques to try

- Saliency-aware weight-preservation regularizer for quantization fine-tuning: add $\sum_i \alpha_i (w_i - Q(w_i))^2$ to the reconstruction loss, with $\alpha_i$ from squared gradients (SqueezeLLM-style Hessian approximation) computed on ~100 calibration samples ("We calculated $\alpha_{i}$ in the same manner as SqueezeLLM [18], which approximates the Hessian matrix using squared gradients." — Section 4.2). Only relevant if a QAT/PTQ-with-LoRA step is ever added to the ladder; overhead is a calibration pass plus ~40% longer training with no extra memory.

## Baselines & datasets used

- ApiQ (block-wise joint optimization of quantization clipping params + LoRA init) as the foundation method, replicated on LLaMA-2-7B/13B.
- Calibration/eval datasets: WikiText-2 and C4 (perplexity), plus zero-shot accuracy on WinoGrande, PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge — none of these are structured-output, tool-calling, or instruction-following benchmarks.
- Hyperparameters used throughout: LoRA rank 64, block-wise optimization, 128 calibration samples of length 2048, AdamW, 20 epochs per layer (Table 3) — all at 2-bit on 7B/13B models, not transferable as-is to a 1.2B model at 4-bit.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Combining ApiQ (LoRA-only, frozen weights) with BinaryMoS's more expressive 1-bit quantization formulation did not beat plain ApiQ, and underperformed even BinaryMoS's own simpler predecessor OneBit: "this observation highlights a fundamental limitation in the ability of LoRA weights to counteract the accuracy loss induced by quantization, especially under the stringent constraint of 1-bit quantization" (Section 3.1) — i.e., LoRA-only adaptation cannot substitute for full-weight retraining at very low bit-widths.
- Combining ApiQ with DB-LLM's dual-binarization formulation caused severe overfitting with only 128 calibration samples (PPL 8.26 vs ApiQ's 7.56 on LLaMA-2-7B, WikiText-2), and only became competitive once training data was increased to 512 samples — "applying the DB-LLM quantization formulation augments the model's expressive power. However, it also heightens its susceptibility to overfitting" (Section 3.2).
- A naive (non-saliency-weighted) weight-preservation regularizer never beat plain ApiQ at any coefficient tested, "suggesting that the naive weight preservation approach is ineffective for regularizing the loss function" (Appendix 0.G) — uniform L2-to-original-weights penalties are not a free win.
- ApiQ itself shows a widening WikiText-2/C4 perplexity gap relative to the full-precision model, flagged by the authors as overfitting to the calibration set (Section 4, referencing Fig. 3).
- General finding relevant to any calibration-data-driven quantization step: small (128-sample) calibration sets are consistently insufficient once the quantization formulation is expressive — the paper's own ablation shows accuracy recovering further with more diverse/larger calibration data (PTB, and a mixed WikiText-2+C4+PTB corpus, and 512 vs 128 samples in Appendix 0.H).

## Metric / eval choices

- Perplexity on WikiText-2 and C4, plus zero-shot accuracy averaged over 5 commonsense-reasoning multiple-choice tasks — none measure schema validity, tool-call correctness, or instruction-following, so none of these numbers transfer directly to this project's BFCLv3/IFEval/structured-output targets.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper never tests 4-bit precision directly (it targets 2-bit, with a 3-bit ablation); it is unclear whether the saliency-aware regularizer's ~1-point accuracy gain would even be measurable at the much milder 4-bit compression this project ships, where ApiQ-style methods already report near-lossless perplexity in the paper's own comparison tables (Table 7).
- All results are on LLaMA-2-7B/13B; there is no evidence here about whether Hessian-based saliency weighting behaves the same on a 1.2B hybrid-architecture model with a 65K vocabulary, or interacts with GGUF's own quantization scheme (llama.cpp import-matrix quantization is a different implementation from ApiQ's LoRA-based block-wise training).
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a standard research paper.
