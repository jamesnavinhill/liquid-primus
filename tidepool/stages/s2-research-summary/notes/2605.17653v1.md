# 2605.17653v1 — LLMForge: Multi-Backend Hardware-Aware Neural Architecture Search with Infinite-Head Attention for Edge Language Models

**Relevance:** low
The paper is a hardware-aware neural-architecture-search framework for pretraining new sub-billion-parameter Transformers from scratch across accelerator substrates; our project post-trains and quantizes a fixed, already-chosen architecture (LFM2.5-1.2B) for tool-calling reliability, so nothing here changes a decision on our candidate ladder, metrics, or quantization plan.

## TL;DR

LLMForge searches Transformer architectures from scratch (via NSGA-II) so that per-layer attention shape and width/depth adapt to the hardware substrate (GPU, systolic accelerators, or a multi-chip ring edge accelerator), using a learned encoder surrogate ("Forge-Former") to avoid training every candidate. It reports Pareto-front ~100M–360M models that beat SmolLM2/Qwen-0.5B baselines on validation loss, energy per token, and latency at matched or fewer parameters, but everything is trained from random initialization on MiniPile/FineWeb-Edu — there is no post-training, instruction-tuning, or quantization step of an existing model.

## Techniques to try

- None directly applicable. Our project does not run NAS or pretrain from scratch — it post-trains and quantizes a fixed pretrained architecture (LFM2.5-1.2B), so IHA, Forge-Former, and Forge-DSE are out of scope for our candidate ladder.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Architectures pretrained from scratch on "MiniPile [14], a 1.6B-token subset of The Pile [10], under a fixed token budget of 655M tokens" for surrogate-label generation, and on "FineWeb-Edu-10BT [22]" for "13.1B tokens" for scaled Pareto-front validation — both much smaller-scale, non-instruction corpora than anything in our post-training pipeline.
- Baselines re-trained from scratch under a matched recipe rather than loaded from upstream checkpoints: SmolLM2-135M/360M, Pythia-160M, Qwen-0.5B, evaluated zero-shot on "ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge [7], BoolQ [6], HellaSwag [34], and SciQ [31]" — general knowledge/commonsense benchmarks, not BFCL, IFEval, or any tool-calling/structured-output metric.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "SmolLM2 baselines retain leads on ARC-Challenge and BoolQ at both scales, the multi-choice question-answering cluster where canonical baselines tend to hold their ground" — a reminder that architecture/training changes optimized for one objective (here, energy/latency) can trade away specific benchmark categories, a dynamic worth watching for analogously when our post-training could trade general knowledge (MMLU-Pro, GPQA) for tool-calling gains.
- "Forge-Former is trained on the IHA design space of Appendix A and reliably ranks only architectures within that support. Predictions for designs outside the training corpus... would require retraining the surrogate on a matching labeled set" — a generalization-boundary caveat for learned surrogates/proxies, tangentially relevant if we ever consider a learned proxy metric, but not used in our plan.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses ranking-fidelity metrics (Spearman ρ, Kendall τ) and "top-X% recovery (k@X%)" to validate a cheap surrogate against ground-truth validation loss — a general pattern for validating cheap proxies against expensive ground truth, but the paper's proxy is validation loss, not schema validity or tool-call correctness, so it doesn't inform our flag-rate/false-flag-rate eval design.
