# 2307.00331v2 — Quantization Variation: A New Perspective on Training Transformers with Low-Bit Precision

**Relevance:** medium
Directly targets the "quantization-aware distillation / QAT" rung at the bottom of our candidate ladder with concrete sensitivity findings and techniques, but all results are on encoder-only classification models (ViTs, BERT-base on GLUE) rather than decoder-only generative LLMs doing tool calls, so nothing here is validated on our prediction unit or on weight-only PTQ (the GGUF path we'd try first).

## TL;DR

The paper argues transformers (vs. ConvNets) suffer three linked problems under low-bit quantization-aware training: uneven module sensitivity (attention is fragile), heavier-tailed weight/activation distributions, and a weight-oscillation instability during training. It proposes module-dependent scale learning (finer granularity + gradient scaling for MHSA), a distillation-only training loss, and an oscillation-aware regularizer, and shows these help 1–4 bit QAT on small ViTs and BERT-base.

## Techniques to try

- Protect/attention-aware quantization: "The results across various models and bitwidth settings indicate that MHSA, particularly the value weight matrices, are highly susceptible to quantization." — Section 4.1 (leave-one-out study on DeiT-T/Swin-T/SReT-T)
- Keep first/last layers at higher precision: "due to the fact that the first (patch embedding) and the last (classification) layer are more sensitive to perturbation compared to intermediate layers, we fix their bitwidth to 8-bit" — Section 5.1
- Distillation-only objective (no hard-label loss) to stabilize QAT: "Here we only employ KD loss as the sole objective to optimize the model, which is more effective with adequate supervision signal in KD" — Section 4.4.2
- Oscillation-aware Bin Regularizer (penalize variance of real-valued weights within a quantization bin, with a cosine-annealed weight so it doesn't fight early scale learning) — Section 4.4.3, Eq. 10

## Baselines & datasets used

- Models: DeiT-T, SReT-T, Swin-T/S (ImageNet-1K, ~5–50M params) and BERT-base (GLUE benchmark, ~110M params) — all ≤110M, well under our 1.2B scale but at least in a "small model" regime worth reading numbers from.
- Baseline quantizer throughout is LSQ+ (Bhalgat et al., 2020); comparisons also include BinaryBERT, BiBERT, BiT and other 1-bit BERT methods on GLUE (Table 3).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Oscillation regularization actively hurts at higher bitwidth: "penalizing the oscillation during QAT will harm the normal optimization of latent weights, which is more prominent in higher bitwidth. Accordingly, we only apply OBR to the 2-bit and 3-bit quantization." — Appendix B. A regularizer tuned for 1–3 bit is not free to apply blindly at 4-bit.
- The authors explicitly say their three techniques do **not** transfer to ConvNets and, by their own reasoning, are specific to transformer structure (module-dependent quantization needs per-head MHSA structure; oscillation regularization targets a problem ConvNets barely have) — Appendix G. This cuts both ways: it suggests these techniques could transfer to our decoder-only transformer, but the paper gives zero evidence for decoder-only, autoregressive, or generation/instruction-following tasks — everything shown is classification (ImageNet top-1, GLUE).
- Full QAT here is compute-heavy even at small scale: "The total training time for our DeiT-T with 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs is 57.3 hours" (Table 5, after a claimed 50% reduction over baseline) for a ~5M-parameter model over 150 epochs with a teacher model. Scaled qualitatively to 1.2B, full QAT-from-scratch-style training would be a heavy, likely disproportionate cost for "quantization-aware training if needed" — this is worth flagging as a reason to prefer PTQ/GGUF or lighter QAT-distillation variants first.
- Mixed-precision quantization (assigning different bitwidths per component) is presented as a tempting direct fix for the sensitivity variation but is shown to cost more in hardware area/power than their module-dependent scheme (Table 8) — a data point against per-layer mixed-precision as a shortcut.

## Metric / eval choices

- SDAM (Standard Deviation of the Absolute Mean of activation magnitude) used to quantify how much more "outlier-prone" transformer activations are than ConvNet activations (Table 2) — a candidate diagnostic metric if we want to characterize which of our model's modules are most distorted by 4-bit quantization.
- "Oscillation percentage": weights whose quantized integer value flips sign of update direction, tracked via an EMA (Eq. 11–12) — a training-time diagnostic for QAT instability, not applicable to our weight-only PTQ path unless we actually do QAT.

## Open questions for our project

- All evidence is on classification (ImageNet top-1/top-5, GLUE accuracy); there's no evidence in this paper about whether these QAT techniques preserve structured-output/schema-validity behavior specifically, which is our actual quantization concern (does 4-bit hurt tool-calling more than knowledge?). This paper cannot answer that question directly.
- The paper's setting is full weight+activation QAT trained from scratch/fine-tuned over many epochs with a teacher; our plan is weight-only 4-bit GGUF, likely via PTQ or lightweight quantization-aware distillation. It's unclear how much of the module-dependent-quantization and oscillation-regularization machinery is even applicable to a weight-only, llama.cpp-style GGUF quantization pipeline versus the general weight+activation QAT setting studied here.
- No instructions-to-the-reader or suspicious embedded directives were found in the paper text; it reads as a normal research paper throughout.
