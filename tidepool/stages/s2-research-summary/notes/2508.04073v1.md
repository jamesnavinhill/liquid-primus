# 2508.04073v1 — Efficient Strategy for Improving Large Language Model (LLM) Capabilities

**Relevance:** medium
Reports a concrete quantization-order effect (quantize-then-LoRA beats LoRA-then-quantize) relevant to our 4-bit shipping decision, but at a different scale/model (LLaMA-3.2-1B, rank-2 LoRA), narrow domain-QA task, and a weak LLM-as-judge ranking eval rather than the benchmark suite (BFCLv3, IFEval, etc.) this project relies on.

## TL;DR

A master's-thesis-derived paper builds a family of nine LLaMA-3.2-1B variants (base, LoRA fine-tuned, 4-bit quantized, and combinations, plus RAG) on a narrow academic-thesis QA dataset, then ranks them with GPT-4o as an LLM-as-a-judge over 100 questions. The main actionable finding: quantizing the base model first and then applying LoRA preserves fine-tuning gains, while fine-tuning first and quantizing afterward measurably degrades quality — relevant context for our own precision-order decision when shipping a 4-bit GGUF of a fine-tuned model.

## Techniques to try

- Quantize the base model before LoRA fine-tuning rather than after, so the adapter is learned/retained at higher effective precision: "The best approach is to first quantize the base model and then apply fine-tuning with LoRA, as this allows the adapter to retain full precision, preserving the benefits of fine-tuning without performance degradation." — Section 2.1
- Combining fine-tuning with a lightweight retrieval layer gave a further small bump in their ranking: "connecting a RAG system provides a slight further improvement in performance." — Section 2.1 (low weight for us since RAG is out of scope for a single fine-tuned on-device model, but corroborates that retrieval and fine-tuning are complementary rather than substitutes)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base model: LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct (GGUF, from lmstudio-community), quantized to Q8_0 and Q4_K_M.
- Fine-tuning data: a custom question-answer dataset built from 1,920 UNAL (Universidad Nacional de Colombia) academic theses/dissertations, split 75/25 train/test, "average length of prompts was 143 characters, while the average length of responses was 149 characters" (Section 1.4.1) — a narrow closed-domain QA task, not tool-calling or structured output, so limited direct transfer to our JSON/tool-schema target.
- LoRA config used: rank 2, alpha 8, dropout 0.2 (Table 2) — notably very low rank for a 1B model; no ablation given for why rank 2 was sufficient.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Quantizing after fine-tuning degrades the adapter: "quantization applied to an already fine-tuned model tends to degrade its performance. This occurs because quantization reduces the numerical precision across the entire model, including the adapter trained during fine-tuning, leading to a drop in performance." — Section 2.1. In their ranking, the fine-tuned-then-quantized model ("LLM-ft-q") was the worst performer of all nine variants (average position 8.56 of 10, zero first-place rankings).
- LoRA-to-GGUF conversion required merging into full precision first, not just converting the adapter, and was resource-heavy: "it was insufficient to convert only the adapter. It was necessary to download the full base model weights, merge them with the fine-tuning weights, and then convert the combined model, which required substantial RAM and often caused runtime crashes. As a result, the conversion process had to be adapted specifically for each model to complete successfully." — Section 3.1. Worth planning for in our own LoRA→GGUF pipeline.

## Metric / eval choices

- Evaluation was purely comparative ranking via LLM-as-judge, not fixed benchmarks: "For this evaluation, the GPT-4o model from OpenAI was used with an LLM-as-a-judge prompt. A total of 100 questions were presented to each model, and the ranking process was executed accordingly." — Section 2.1. No BFCL/IFEval/MMLU-style scores are reported anywhere, so none of the numbers here are directly comparable to our pre-registered targets; the paper is useful only for its qualitative ordering effect, not for any number to carry over.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the quantize-then-LoRA ordering effect they observed (at LoRA rank 2, 1B LLaMA, 4-bit) generalize to our setting, where we plan quantization-aware distillation/training on top of a full SFT+DPO/GRPO pipeline rather than a single LoRA pass? Their result argues for baking quantization in earlier rather than treating it as a pure post-hoc step, which lines up with our candidate ladder's move to quantization-aware training if plain PTQ underperforms — but their evidence is a single 1B model, one narrow QA task, and an LLM-judge ranking, not a controlled ablation, so it should be treated as a hypothesis to test rather than an established result.
- No instruction-like text directed at a reader/agent was found in the paper; it reads as a straightforward descriptive thesis writeup.
