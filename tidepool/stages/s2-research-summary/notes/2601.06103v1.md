# 2601.06103v1 — The Impact of Post-training on Data Contamination

**Relevance:** medium
Not about tool-calling, quantization, or structured output at all — it studies benchmark contamination — but its central finding (SFT entrenches narrow memorization while GRPO with verifiable rewards produces gains that generalize to held-out tasks, and this gap widens with scale) speaks directly to this project's SFT-vs-GRPO step on the candidate ladder and its "keep general ability from eroding" question, at a model scale (0.5B–4B) close to the 1.2B target.

## TL;DR

The authors inject 5 copies of GSM8K/MBPP test items into pretraining for Qwen2.5 (0.5B/1.5B) and Gemma3 (1B/4B), then apply SFT or GRPO. SFT's performance gains from the leaked data stay local to the contaminated benchmark and don't transfer to an uncontaminated sibling benchmark (GSMPlus/HumanEval), while GRPO's gains show up on both — and this SFT-memorizes / GRPO-generalizes split gets stronger as model size increases.

## Techniques to try

- Use a paired contaminated-vs-uncontaminated benchmark (e.g. GSM8K vs GSMPlus, MBPP vs HumanEval) to distinguish real generalization from narrow overfitting when comparing SFT and GRPO checkpoints — directly transferable idea for checking whether this project's tool-calling improvements generalize beyond the exact schemas trained on ("we compare the performance of the contaminated and clean model's performance on benchmarks that aim to measure the same underlying ability" — Section 4.1).
- GRPO with rule-based/verifiable rewards is reported to convert training signal into more generalizable capability than SFT, and more so at larger scale ("GRPO shows the opposite trend: bigger models channel additional capacity toward broad generalization and thus *dilute* relative over-estimation" — Section 5, "Scale amplifies the contrast.").
- Zero-shot evaluation was deliberately chosen over few-shot to avoid confounds ("We opted for zero shot evaluations as the few shot examples can impact how we have contaminated the data as well." — Appendix B.3) — a reminder to keep eval prompting fixed/matched when comparing recipes.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B and Gemma3 1B/4B, non-instruction-tuned checkpoints, extended-pretrained on a 25B-token mix of FineWeb-Edu, CodeParrots, and OpenMath-Instruct, then SFT or GRPO'd.
- Benchmarks: GSM8K and MBPP (contaminated tasks) vs GSMPlus and HumanEval (uncontaminated, same-ability probes); LM Evaluation Harness plus the math-verify library for answer parsing.
- GRPO setup: single epoch over GSM8K train, 8-bit AdamW, 16 candidate generations per prompt, rule-based/heuristic correctness rewards summed without weighting (Appendix A).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Analyzing only pre-trained models can mask the true effect of contamination. Continued pre-training on clean data can drive the apparent gap between contaminated and clean models to nearly zero, but the leaked information is merely submerged not erased and is readily rediscovered during post-training." (Introduction / Findings) — a matched base-model rerun taken right after pretraining may look clean even when post-training would resurface an effect; relevant caution for this project's "matched rerun of the base model" comparisons.
- "SFT's gains are almost purely local: the contaminated model answers GSM8K or MBPP questions better compared to the base model, but exhibits no extra competence on GSMPlus or HumanEval." (Discussion) — i.e. SFT gains that look like real capability improvement can be pure memorization of the exact training distribution, not generalized skill.
- Data-mix pitfall: "Preliminary experiments using only web text even with high-quality sources like books resulted in significant performance drops on math and coding tasks, rendering some experiments ineffective." — pure-web continued pretraining tanked task performance, forcing a more task-heavy mixture; not directly applicable to a fine-tuning-only pipeline but a reminder that off-distribution continued training can regress target-task performance.
- Scope limits stated by the authors themselves: model sizes capped at 4B, only two open-weight families, only rule-based RL rewards, and a synthetic 5-copy/late-injection contamination scenario — the authors flag that "larger proprietary models, different architectures, or alternative RLHF algorithms could behave differently" (Section 5.1), so the SFT/GRPO generalization contrast should be treated as suggestive, not settled, at any scale this project would use it for.

## Metric / eval choices

- Confidence intervals on (Contaminated − Clean) score differences via standard error propagation from per-dataset accuracy SEs (Appendix C) — a usable pattern for reporting statistically-grounded deltas between two model variants on a shared benchmark, relevant to this project's own "matched rerun" delta claims (e.g. BFCLv3 +3.0, guardrail metrics within 2.0 points).
- The paper explicitly caps SFT and GRPO training at "approximately the same number of update steps to ensure comparability" when comparing the two recipes — useful methodological note if this project runs its own SFT-vs-GRPO ablation on the candidate ladder.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the SFT-memorizes/GRPO-generalizes pattern (found here for math/code correctness) hold for structured-output and tool-calling correctness, where the "test set" analog would be specific tool schemas seen during training vs novel ones at eval time? This bears on the project's standing constraint to specialize to *underlying technology* rather than vendor-specific SDK idiosyncrasies — GRPO with the schema-validity + execution-correctness reward on the candidate ladder might generalize across schemas better than SFT alone, per this paper's pattern, but that is not tested here.
- The paper's scale range (0.5B–4B) brackets the project's 1.2B target, but its GRPO reward is a simple rule-based correctness check on math/code, not a schema/JSON validity check — it does not address the project's reward-hacking concern for schema-checking rewards.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard empirical study.
