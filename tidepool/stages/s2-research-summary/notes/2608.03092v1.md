# 2608.03092v1 — SMOPD: Multi-Reward Reinforcement Learning via Specialize-and-Merge Online Policy Distillation

**Relevance:** high
Directly diagnoses and fixes a failure mode of GRPO-style multi-reward RL — a sparse binary reward (their "format" reward) getting drowned out by a denser reward — which is precisely the shape of the schema-validity + execution-correctness reward pair on our GRPO rung of the candidate ladder.

## TL;DR

The paper shows that under standard multi-reward RL (GRPO/GDPO), a sparse binary reward (structural format compliance) is essentially unlearnable when combined at equal weight with a denser reward (tool-call accuracy), because most rollout groups show no within-group variation on the sparse dimension. Their fix, SMOPD, trains one RL "teacher" per reward with that reward's priority cranked way up, then merges the teachers into a single student via on-policy token-level distillation (forward KL) plus a balanced-reward anchor, recovering both capabilities in one policy. Tested at 1.5B/3B/7B on Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2, closer in scale to our 1.2B target than most RL papers.

## Techniques to try

- Diagnose whether a candidate reward pair is "complementary-but-imbalanced" (like format vs. accuracy) before committing to a single scalarized GRPO run — check per-dimension within-group variance, since "In such groups, per-reward normalization cannot create an informative signal where no response-level distinction exists" (Section 1).
- If schema-validity and execution-correctness rewards turn out to have this dense/sparse mismatch, consider the two-stage specialize-then-merge recipe rather than assuming equal-weight GDPO/GRPO will balance them: "we set $w_{k}\gg w_{j}$ for $j\neq k$" to train a reward-specialized teacher, one per reward ("Method: SMOPD Framework", Section 3.1).
- For the merge/distillation step, forward KL (mode-covering) was preferred over reverse KL (mode-seeking) for combining multiple reward-specialized teachers on the same prompt: "Forward KL is mode-covering ... encouraging the student to place mass on *all* high-probability tokens of the teacher mixture ... different teachers may be confident about different tokens ... at the same position" (Section 3.2, Distillation loss).
- Uniform teacher mixing (no learned gating) was the strongest and simplest choice in their ablations — a useful prior against over-engineering a routing/gating mechanism: "The adaptive mechanisms therefore add no consistent benefit over the parameter-free mixture, motivating uniform mixing as SMOPD's default" (Appendix E, Results).
- Their top-κ (κ=16) truncated-mixture approximation for on-policy distillation is a cheap way to get teacher signal without full-vocab logprobs, and was shown not sensitive to κ: "Forward-KL performance remains highly stable across support sizes, with Overall varying by only $0.007$ between κ=16 and κ=64" (Section 4.3, Ablating OPD Design Choices).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Complementary-reward setting: Qwen2.5-{1.5B,3B}-Instruct trained on the RLLA-4K split from ToolRL, with accuracy reward (function-name/parameter match, range [-3,3]) and binary format reward (correct `<think>`/`<tool_call>`/`<response>` XML structure) — this reward pairing is structurally close to our schema-validity + execution-correctness idea.
- Evaluated generalization on BFCL-v4 (AST categories) and API-Bank (LLM-judge protocol), both held-out tool-use benchmarks — BFCL is the same family of benchmark named in our pre-registered claim (though our target is BFCLv3 overall, not the AST subset used here).
- Conflicting-reward setting: Qwen2.5-{3B,7B}-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on Alpaca prompts with dual reward models for "useful" and "harmless" (HH-RLHF, PKU-SafeRLHF, Alpaca eval sets) — less directly relevant to us.
- Baselines compared: GRPO, GDPO (liu2026gdpo), GD²PO (hard sign-conflict filtering variant).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Core negative result: "Under balanced priorities, GDPO behaves similarly to GRPO, with format compliance remaining below $9\%$" — i.e., naive equal-weight multi-reward GRPO/GDPO essentially fails to learn a sparse binary reward at all (8.8% format compliance at 1.5B), even though the model can occasionally produce correct format.
- Pure on-policy distillation without the task-level anchor plateaus at (roughly) the imitation ceiling of its teachers and cannot exceed them: "the OPD-only student reaches $5.544$ Overall, barely above the teachers it imitates ($5.500$/$5.522$)" — merging teachers is not sufficient by itself; a policy-gradient anchor was needed to push past teacher quality.
- Strict exact-match evaluation can understate real tool-calling ability once the model is more capable: on API-Bank, "the more capable backbone tends to produce richer argument strings—more specific values, fuller context, or extra optional fields—which deviate from the short reference strings more often, so the strict matcher penalizes exactly the additional capability" — a concrete caution for us since our schema-validity / execution-correctness grading needs to avoid punishing valid-but-differently-formatted output.
- Teacher specialization does not always transfer cleanly across backbones: on Llama-3.2-3B "teacher specialization is the most challenging, both individual teachers degrade substantially" before merging recovers most of the loss — a caution that this recipe's teacher-specialization step may behave differently on our LFM2.5 hybrid architecture than on the dense transformer backbones tested here.
- The method requires training M full RL runs (one teacher per reward) plus a distillation run, all on "8 H100 GPUs" per run — this is a heavier compute footprint than a straightforward single GRPO run, worth weighing against our managed-hardware budget.

## Metric / eval choices

- They separate BFCL's full-suite "Overall" (which averages in categories like Multi-Turn/Web-Search that these single-turn tool models were never trained for) from an "AST" metric restricted to Non-Live/Live AST categories, on the grounds that "the full-suite Overall average is dominated by categories these single-turn tool callers never see" — relevant if our BFCLv3 overall target ends up mixing in similarly out-of-scope categories.
- For API-Bank they apply the original exact-match scorer first, then send only exact-match *failures* to an LLM judge (qwen3.7-plus) to rescue functionally-equivalent-but-differently-formatted calls, reporting both the judged and the original strict scores side by side (Appendix F) — a two-tier eval design worth considering for our own tool-call correctness grading, though note it uses a judge model at *eval* time only, not as a training reward.

## Open questions for our project

- Does our schema-validity reward (likely close to binary pass/fail like their format reward) and execution-correctness reward (likely more graded, like their accuracy reward) have the same dense/sparse imbalance this paper diagnoses? If so, a single equal-weighted GRPO run may badly under-train schema validity, echoing our own open question "Which training signal actually instils 'flag rather than assert'."
- Is the full specialize-then-merge SMOPD pipeline (multiple RL teachers + online distillation) proportionate for a 1.2B model on our compute budget, or is a lighter fix (e.g., simply up-weighting the sparse reward within one GRPO run, which their own ablation in Appendix D suggests works almost as well — "moderate SMOPD attains 2.757 ... beating both of its own teachers") sufficient without the full merge machinery?
