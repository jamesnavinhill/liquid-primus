# 2511.17473v1 — Masked-and-Reordered Self-Supervision for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

**Relevance:** medium
The paper is a GRPO/RLVR reward-design study at a comparable model scale (1.5B–3B) that could inform how we shape denser rewards for our GRPO candidate-ladder step, but it works entirely in text math/proof reasoning and never touches tool calls, JSON schemas, or quantization.

## TL;DR

MR-RLVR adds two auxiliary, automatically-computable process-level rewards on top of standard outcome-only RLVR/GRPO: "masked-then-fill" (mask key steps/formulas, reward the model for reconstructing them) and "step reordering" (shuffle reasoning steps, reward correct re-ordering). On Qwen2.5-3B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B it improves math benchmark Pass@k over vanilla GRPO, with the largest gains on the hardest benchmarks and in low-data regimes, at the cost of a two-stage training pipeline and extra data curation using large teacher models.

## Techniques to try

- Two auxiliary process-level rewards for a GRPO stage, computed automatically without human labels: "we design two process-aware tasks on proof-style and computation-style trajectories: (1) Masked-Then-Fill, which masks key formulas, reasoning steps, or theorem invocations and requires the model to reconstruct the missing content given surrounding context; and (2) Step Reordering, which shuffles reasoning steps and asks the model to recover a coherent logical order." (Section 1, Introduction)
- Two-stage curriculum for GRPO: shape the policy with dense process rewards first, then switch to sparse outcome-only reward for fine-tuning — "In Stage I, we use only the process-level rewards described above to perform RLVR updates... In Stage II, starting from the Stage I checkpoint, we fine-tune the model on computational math problems with programmatically verifiable final answers, using only terminally verifiable rewards." (Section 4, Methodology)
- MR-RLVR's largest relative gains show up exactly where the base model is weakest and data is scarcest, suggesting auxiliary process rewards are most useful as a sample-efficiency lever rather than a ceiling-raiser: "process-level self-supervision in MR-RLVR provides more sample-efficient learning signals than standard GRPO, enabling better generalization with limited training data." (Section 5.3.1)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base models: Qwen2.5-3B-Base and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, trained with GRPO in the verl framework on 8x A100/A800 80GB GPUs (Section 5.1, Model Configuration).
- Training corpora: DeepTheorem (theorem-proving) and DeepMath (computational reasoning), 10k problems sampled from each; DeepMath traces were re-generated with GPT-o3-mini to remove leakage from embedded self-verification, and DeepSeek-R1-0528 was used to annotate mask positions and step boundaries (Section 5.1, Training Data Curation).
- Eval benchmarks: AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, all math competition/benchmark sets — none at or reflecting structured-output/tool-calling tasks.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Gains can reverse under sampling variance even with more training: "We also observe minor performance degradation on AIME24 Pass@5/Pass@8 for Qwen2.5-3B, which we attribute to the inherent variance in the sampling process with n=64 samples." (Section 5.2)
- Auxiliary process rewards give shrinking returns once the base model has already saturated the benchmark: "both models show relatively limited improvements on MATH500 (0.13%-4.06%)... the problems in MATH500 are relatively simple and the models have largely mastered their solution patterns, resulting in minimal marginal gains from multi-round verification." (Section 5.2) — a caution that dense process rewards may buy little once a metric (e.g. our guardrail suite) is already near ceiling.
- A concrete reward-hacking/spurious-correctness case: the masked-then-fill task's automatic verifier can be satisfied by wrong reasoning that happens to produce the right value — "This coincidentally correct situation reveals speculative behavior: the model may directly apply the same pattern after seeing XOR operators multiple times in the preceding text, or infer from context that the result should be 0xFFFFFFFF and then reverse-engineer a seemingly reasonable formula, rather than strictly following the required reasoning procedure. Such errors are more difficult to detect than obvious computational mistakes." (Section 5.4) — directly relevant to our "how is reward hacking avoided when the reward is a schema check" question, as an example of a schema/format-style check passing despite wrong process.
- General caution on token-level SFT for chain-of-thought data, cited as motivation for using RL instead: "token-level supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on chain-of-thought data often degenerates into imitating specific solution templates rather than learning transferable reasoning strategies." (Section 1, Introduction)

## Metric / eval choices

- Unbiased Pass@k estimator (Chen et al. formula) with n=64 generated samples per problem, k ∈ {1, 5, 8}; nucleus sampling at temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, max generation length 4096 tokens (Section 5.1, Evaluation Setup).
- Final-answer verification via MathRuler (symbolic/numeric matching) plus text matching; the same MathRuler tool is reused inside the masked-then-fill process reward for entity-level match scoring (Sections 4.2, 5.1).

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's process-level tasks (masking spans, reordering steps) are defined over natural-language proof/computation text. It is unclear whether an analogous construction (e.g. masking tool-call arguments, or reordering multi-step agent/tool-call plans) would produce a comparably clean, automatically-verifiable reward signal for structured tool-call output, or whether schema structure is too rigid/low-entropy for a masked-fill task to add signal beyond what schema-validity reward already provides.
- Their data curation pipeline depends on large teacher models (GPT-o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1-0528) to identify mask positions and step boundaries; replicating this for our tool-call domain would need an equivalent large-model annotation pass over agent traces, and the paper gives no cost/quality analysis of that step.
- No quantization, on-device throughput, or small-vocabulary tokenization effects are discussed anywhere in the paper; it offers no evidence either way on our 4-bit/GGUF or 65K-vocabulary questions.
