# 2602.05988v1 — Layer-wise LoRA fine-tuning: a similarity metric approach

**Relevance:** medium
A concrete, cheap technique for choosing which layers get LoRA modules (and possibly for limiting how much of the model general-ability training touches), but it is validated only on dense transformer encoders/decoders (125M–8.5B) and one multimodal model, never on tool-calling/structured-output tasks, quantization, or a hybrid conv+attention architecture like LFM2.5.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes ranking transformer layers by 1 − CKA(input representation, output representation) and applying LoRA only to the top-N most "active" layers, cutting LoRA trainable parameters up to 50% with roughly neutral-to-positive effects on GLUE, math (GSM8K/MATH), and code (HumanEval/MBPP) benchmarks for RoBERTa/DeBERTa, LLaMA 2-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, and LLaVA-1.5-7B. It's a pre-training-time (inference-only, no extra backprop) selection step that is orthogonal to and composable with LoRA/PiSSA, which is relevant to our "LoRA SFT" rung of the candidate ladder, but it's an efficiency/preservation trick, not a fix for structured-output reliability itself.

## Techniques to try

- Rank transformer blocks by layer importance $I_{L_i} = 1 - \text{CKA}(R_{i-1}, R_i)$ (dissimilarity between a layer's input and output representations on the target task's data) and LoRA only the top-N layers, rather than all layers — cheap (inference-only, done once before training) and "orthogonal to and readily compatible with existing low-rank adaptation techniques" (Abstract). Could be tried at the LoRA-SFT rung to cut trainable params and possibly protect general-purpose layers from drift while the tool-calling-specific layers adapt.
- Exclude the first layer from the candidate set for encoder-only architectures: "the first layer input and output representations consistently display a near-zero similarity, which implies an $I_{L_1}$ close to its maximum value" due to CKA's centering artifact on the untouched embedding output — a metric quirk to watch for if reusing CKA layer-importance directly ("Appendix A The first layer of encoder-only models").
- The method composes with rank-doubling to hold total trainable-parameter count constant while concentrating capacity on fewer, more important layers — but the paper finds this redistribution "does not effectively improve task performance" (Table 5, RoBERTa GLUE, +0.09 pts only), so simply moving budget to fewer layers isn't a free win.

## Baselines & datasets used

- GLUE benchmark (MNLI/SST2/MRPC/CoLA/QNLI/QQP/RTE/STSB) for RoBERTa-base and DeBERTa-v3-base, LoRA rank 8 (matching Hu et al. 2022 / Zhang et al. 2023 setup).
- MetaMathQA (train) → GSM8K, MATH (eval); Code-Feedback (train) → HumanEval, MBPP (eval); for LLaMA 2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, Gemma-7B, LoRA rank 128 (matching PiSSA/Meng et al. 2025 setup).
- ScienceQA (via LMMs-eval) for LLaVA-1.5-7B, half the language-tower layers selected.
- Baselines compared against: full fine-tuning, standard LoRA (all layers), PiSSA (all layers), FIM-score layer selection (Lodha et al., 2023), and naive fixed heuristics — Last / First / Middle / Extremes / Alternate layer subsets (Figure 3).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Effects are model-specific and not uniformly positive: "applying our method together with both LoRA and PiSSA on Gemma-7B leads to small predictive performance drops in all tasks. On the other hand, on LLaMA 2-7B and Mistral-7B-v0.1, ... most tasks had an improvement" — i.e., the same layer-selection recipe can help or hurt depending on the base model, with no a priori way to know which before running it.
- On RoBERTa-base the gain over the FIM baseline is much smaller than on DeBERTa-v3-base; the authors attribute this to "the large amount of parameters outside of the layers in RoBERTa_base," speculating the technique matters less when transformer blocks are a smaller share of total parameters — worth flagging since LFM2.5's hybrid architecture (short-convolution blocks + few GQA blocks) has a very different parameter distribution across "layers" than a standard transformer, so this caveat may bind harder for our model.
- The naive "middle layers" heuristic is a strong, free baseline and sometimes *wins* outright (e.g., Gemma-7B, and on GSM8K/MRPC in general), but it can also fail catastrophically: "the naive strategy reaches a catastrophic 7.57 percentage points gap with respect to the predictive performance of our method on HumanEval with Mistral-7B" — so fixed layer-subset heuristics are unreliable and task/model-dependent, not just a slightly worse shortcut.
- Rank redistribution (doubling LoRA rank on the selected subset to match baseline total parameter count) gave essentially a wash (+0.09 pts avg on GLUE), contradicting the intuition that concentrating more expressive capacity on the "important" layers should help — the authors' own explanation is that CKA-selected layers respond to "coarse-grained patterns" that don't need extra rank.
- The paper's own listed open question is directly relevant to us: whether every model has an "ideal subset to fine-tune that leads to improvements or negligible degradation... over fine-tuning all layers" is explicitly stated as unknown by the authors themselves.

## Metric / eval choices

- GLUE: Matthews correlation for CoLA, overall (matched+mismatched) accuracy for MNLI, Pearson correlation for STSB, accuracy elsewhere — standard GLUE protocol, not something new to borrow.
- GSM8K/MATH: accuracy; HumanEval/MBPP: pass@1 — standard code/math benchmarks, all reported as average of three runs with std dev, which is a useful practice for any of our own LoRA ablations (variance across seeds is non-trivial: e.g., LLaMA 2-7B PiSSA+Ours HumanEval 25.63±2.80).
- Compute-side metrics also measured directly and could be reused as secondary axes for our own LoRA ablations: fine-tuning wall-clock speedup (up to ~1.4x on RoBERTa, ~1.25x on LLaMA-2-7B) and peak GPU memory reduction (~20% RoBERTa, ~15% LLaMA-2-7B) from skipping backprop through unselected layers.

## Open questions for our project

- Does a CKA-based (or similar internal-representation-dissimilarity) layer-importance signal transfer to LFM2.5's hybrid architecture, where most blocks are gated short-convolutions rather than attention+MLP transformer blocks? The paper's method and its "layer = normalization + skip + attention + MLP" definition assumes a standard transformer block throughout; it is untested on conv-based token-mixing layers.
- If layer-selective LoRA leaves some layers completely untouched, could that be a lever for the "keep general ability from eroding while training a narrow one" problem the brief raises — e.g., select layers by CKA dissimilarity on the tool-calling/structured-output data, and check whether the untouched layers are exactly the ones responsible for retaining IFEval/MMLU-Pro/GPQA performance? The paper never measures forgetting/regression on unrelated tasks — it only measures in-domain task performance — so this hypothesis is unverified even within the paper's own scope.
- No instructions addressed to the reader were found in the paper; it is a standard research paper describing a method and experiments.
