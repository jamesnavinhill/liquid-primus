# 2509.20645v3 — Anticipatory Evaluation of Language Models

**Relevance:** skip
The paper studies whether LLMs can forecast benchmark scores from text-only task descriptions before any experiments are run; it has no methods, metrics, or pitfalls relevant to post-training a 1.2B tool-calling model or shipping a 4-bit GGUF build.

## TL;DR

The paper introduces Precog, a corpus of 2,290 description-performance pairs scraped from arXiv, and shows GPT-5 can predict a benchmark's numeric score (e.g., accuracy) from a natural-language description of the task/dataset/eval protocol alone, reaching 14.6 MAE overall (9.9 MAE on high-confidence predictions). This is a meta-research tool for planning which benchmarks to build, not a technique for training or evaluating a specific model like LFM2.5-1.2B.

- None. The paper's contribution (an LLM-as-forecaster for benchmark difficulty) does not map onto any step of the candidate ladder (LoRA/SFT/DPO/GRPO/quantization) for this project.

## Baselines & datasets used
