# 2412.10246v2 — Detecting LLM Hallucination Through Layer-wise Information Deficiency: Analysis of Ambiguous Prompts and Unanswerable Questions

**Relevance:** low
This is a test-time hallucination-detection method for ambiguous/unanswerable QA prompts using layer-wise information-theoretic scores, with no connection to structured tool-call generation, quantization, or the BFCL/IFEval/schema-validity metrics this project targets.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes layer-wise usable information ($\mathcal{L}$I), a test-time metric that sums $\mathcal{V}$-usable-information changes across all transformer layers (rather than only the final layer) to detect when a model is hallucinating due to ambiguous prompts or unanswerable questions. It requires no additional training and is evaluated on QA datasets (CondaQA, CoQA, QuAC) rather than on tool-calling or structured-output tasks.

## Techniques to try

- (Not directly applicable to this project's tool-calling/schema-validity focus; the core idea — aggregating usable-information signal across all layers instead of just the final layer to flag low-confidence/unanswerable cases — could in principle inspire a lightweight, training-free "flag rather than assert" signal for malformed tool returns, but the paper itself only tests this on open-domain QA, not structured output.) "$\mathcal{L}$I integrates easily with pretrained LLMs without requiring additional training or architectural modifications."

## Baselines & datasets used

- CondaQA, CoQA, and QuAC — QA datasets used to compute $\mathcal{V}$-usable information across layers, compared under no-instruction vs. binary-instruction ("Is this answerable?") prompting.
- Baseline compared against: final-layer-only $\mathcal{V}$-usable information ($\mathcal{V}$I) from Xu et al. (2020).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "The quantity of $\mathcal{V}$I is not monotonically increased or decreased across depth. By the final layer (32), the relative effect of prompts becomes inconsistent across datasets, highlighting the value of analyzing all layers." — this is a critique of relying on final-layer signals alone, which is worth flagging as a general caution if this project ever leans on final-layer logits/confidence for the "flag rather than assert" mechanism.
- The paper reports that final-layer $\mathcal{V}$I "shows no significant correlation with the model prediction" in their case study, i.e., a commonly-used single-layer confidence metric failed to track correctness/answerability.

## Metric / eval choices

- Predictive $\mathcal{L}$-information / conditional $\ell$-entropy, an information-theoretic aggregate across all layers (defined via $I_{\mathcal{V}}(X\rightarrow Y)=H_{\mathcal{V}}(Y|\varnothing)-H_{\mathcal{V}}(Y|X)$), distinct from BFCL/IFEval-style task accuracy metrics used in this project.
