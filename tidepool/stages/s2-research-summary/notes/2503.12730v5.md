# 2503.12730v5 — TinySQL: A Progressive Text-to-SQL Dataset for Mechanistic Interpretability Research

**Relevance:** low
This is a mechanistic-interpretability paper using text-to-SQL as a toy task to study circuits (EAP, SAEs, logit lens) in 33M–1B models; it shares surface topic (SQL generation, small-model fine-tuning) with this project but has no method, metric, or pitfall for post-training a 1.2B tool-calling model for reliable structured output under quantization.

## TL;DR

The authors build TinySQL, a synthetic dataset of progressively harder text-to-SQL tasks, fine-tune 33M/0.5B/1B models on it, and use it as a testbed to compare interpretability techniques (Edge Attribution Patching, Sparse Autoencoders, logit lens) for finding the circuits that generate SQL. The contribution is entirely about circuit discovery methodology, not about training models for deployment-grade reliability.

## Techniques to try

- None transfer directly to this project's candidate ladder (LoRA/full SFT, DPO, GRPO, quantization-aware training). The paper's "techniques" are interpretability tools (EAP, SAE feature selection, logit lens), not training methods.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Fine-tuned TinyStories-33M, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct on synthetic text-to-SQL data (CS1–CS5 complexity levels plus synonym/natural-language variants), each 100k examples, single epoch, "AdamW... learning rate used is $1\times 10^{-5}$... We employ 100 warmup steps" ("Training Details.", Appendix D) — not a benchmark this project would reuse, since it's synthetic single-table SQL rather than tool-call/JSON-schema output.
- Compared TinySQL's linguistic difficulty (rarity, lexical density, Flesch readability) against Spider, WikiSQL, MIMICSQL, BIRD, etc. (Table 1) — a dataset-construction reference only, not applicable to JSON/MCP/tool-schema data.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- On the question of whether narrow fine-tuning erodes general capability (one of this project's open questions), the paper reports a small positive data point at much smaller scale: "For the command sets CS1, CS2 and CS3, the refined models, especially the LLMs, perform much better, while retaining most of their general capability" ("Working Note on Over-training", Appendix B), with BM2 (Qwen2.5-0.5B) going from 24% SQL / 92% general to 81–92% SQL while general capability only drops to 79–87%. This is a weak analogy at best — "general capability" here is a generic held-out task eval, not MMLU-Pro/IFEval/GPQA, and the scale (0.5B–1B, single-epoch SFT) doesn't map cleanly onto the guardrail-metric regime this project cares about.
- "EAP performed best on smaller BM1 models... In larger models (BM2/BM3), top edges often involved MLPs, leading to few head selections and low recovered accuracy (5–10%)" — an interpretability-tool limitation, not a training pitfall.
- Mean-ablation circuits were "comparable to random circuits of the same size, suggesting poor alignment with ground-truth task structure" — again an MI-methodology finding, not something that bears on this project's training decisions.

## Metric / eval choices

- Exact-match accuracy on generated SQL strings (no partial credit reported in main results) and "Recovered Accuracy" (fraction of clean-vs-corrupted performance gap recovered by an ablated circuit) — both specific to the circuit-discovery framing and not usable as-is for this project's schema-validity / flag-rate / execution-correctness metrics.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the pre-registered claims (BFCL, IFEval, quantized 4-bit retention, first-attempt schema validity, malformed-tool-return flagging) are addressed by this paper at all; it operates in a different research area (interpretability) with different models, scale, and objectives.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
