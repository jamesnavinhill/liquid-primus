# 2506.09104v1 — Unifying Block-wise PTQ and Distillation-based QAT for Progressive Quantization toward 2-bit Instruction-Tuned LLMs

**Relevance:** high
Directly demonstrates, with numbers, that naive quantization degrades instruction-following far more than knowledge on an instruction-tuned model in our size class, and proposes a progressive PTQ→distillation-QAT recipe that is a near-drop-in analog for our 4-bit GGUF quantization step.

## TL;DR

The paper (UPQ) shows that pushing an instruction-tuned LLM straight to INT2 with standard next-token-prediction QAT wrecks instruction-following (IFEval) even when MMLU partially survives, and fixes this by first doing a cheap INT4 block-wise PTQ warm-start and then QAT that distills the FP16 teacher's token distribution (generalized JSD) into the INT2 student instead of training on raw pretraining text. This is directly relevant to our worry that 4-bit GGUF quantization could hurt structured-output/instruction-following more than raw knowledge, and suggests a concrete recipe (PTQ warm-start + distillation loss, not plain NTP loss) for our quantization-aware distillation/QAT stage.

## Techniques to try

- Progressive quantization FP16→INT4 (PTQ)→INT2 (QAT): "we propose *UPQ*$-$a novel progressive quantization framework (FP16$\xrightarrow{\text{PTQ}}$INT4$\xrightarrow{\text{QAT}}$INT2)" — Section 1. Even though our target is 4-bit (an easier target than their 2-bit), a cheap PTQ pass before any QAT/distillation step is worth trying as a warm start to reduce the error the QAT stage has to fix.
- Distillation loss instead of next-token-prediction loss for the QAT stage: "we introduce INT2 distillation-based QAT, which minimizes the generalized JSD between an INT2 quantized model (student) and its original FP16 counterpart (teacher)" — Section 1. This is the mechanism that recovers instruction-following; worth using JSD-based (not just forward-KL) distillation against the FP16 checkpoint during our quantization-aware distillation stage.
- Block-wise PTQ as a cheap warm-start is far cheaper than QAT: "block-wise PTQ can be executed with just one to two million tokens sampled from a pre-training dataset such as C4 ... taking only a few hours with just a single GPU" versus QAT needing "several hundred million to billions of tokens ... around one to two days with a single 8-GPU node for models in the 3B parameter range" — Section 3.2. Useful cost/benefit reference for budgeting our own quantization stage.
- Zero-excluding balanced integer sets for extreme low-bit quantization (Stretched Elastic Quant, and a modified {-15,...,15} INT4 set): Section 3.1–3.2. Less directly applicable at 4-bit (standard GGUF quant schemes already include a zero/asymmetric point), but worth knowing the rationale if we hit similar bin-imbalance issues.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Llama 3.2 3B Instruct as the base instruction-tuned model for all reported ablations (close to our 1.2B target scale).
- Calibration/training data: "one to two million tokens from a general English corpus like C4" for block-wise PTQ; pretraining-style tokens (not proprietary SFT data) for QAT — explicitly chosen because "the details of which$-$including the dataset itself as well as its construction process$-$are often proprietary and undisclosed."
- INT4 PTQ baselines compared: FlexRound vs. OmniQuant (FlexRound wins slightly and is adopted as default).
- QAT baselines compared: NTP-QAT (next-token-prediction loss) vs. Distill-QAT (JSD-based), with and without the INT4 PTQ warm start.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Plain NTP-QAT (no distillation) on open pretraining data cannot restore instruction-following even after an INT4 PTQ warm start: on Llama 3.2 3B Instruct, "INT4 PTQ → NTP-QAT" reaches MMLU 49.73 but IFEval only 20.51 (FlexRound variant), versus the INT4-only PTQ checkpoint's MMLU 58.60 / IFEval 52.57 — i.e. instruction-following collapses much harder than knowledge under naive low-bit QAT (Table 2). This is direct evidence for one of our open questions.
- Qualitative failure mode of NTP-QAT models: they degenerate into repetitive/looping text ("Do not use commas in your response. ... (repeated)") rather than following the actual instructions, even though they may still get some IFEval sub-checks right by accident (Table 1).
- Distillation only helps to a point: "INT4 PTQ → Distill-QAT" recovers IFEval to 45.19 and MMLU to 53.20 (FlexRound), still short of the INT4-only baseline (52.57 / 58.60) — so instruction-following is not fully restored even with UPQ at INT2; there is a residual accuracy gap the paper does not close.
- The approach needs FP16 teacher logits available during the QAT/distillation stage (compute/memory cost of running the FP16 teacher alongside the student), which is a real cost to budget for if we adopt this for our 4-bit stage.

## Metric / eval choices

- MMLU (knowledge/task ability) and IFEval (instruction-following) used as the two primary axes, explicitly justified as "two of the most representative benchmarks for evaluating instruction-tuned LLMs" — the same knowledge-vs-instruction-following split our project cares about.
- WikiText2 perplexity and a commonsense-reasoning (CSR) average are reported as secondary/sanity metrics alongside MMLU/IFEval (Table 2).

## Open questions for our project

- All quantitative results here are at INT2 on a 3B model; we need 4-bit GGUF on a 1.2B model, which is a much less aggressive target — it's unclear how much of the "NTP-QAT destroys IFEval" effect still applies at 4-bit, where PTQ alone already gets much closer to FP16 (their INT4-PTQ-only row is already close to baseline quality). Worth checking whether distillation is even necessary at 4-bit or whether PTQ/light QAT suffices.
- Would generalized JSD distillation (student vs. FP16 teacher on general corpus text) integrate cleanly with our planned DPO/GRPO stages, or should it be a separate quantization-aware distillation step run after those, as our candidate ladder already assumes?
- The retrieved text for this paper cuts off after Section 3.2 / Table 2 (no Section 3.3 Distill-QAT loss formulation, no Section 4 main results, no conclusion or appendices were present in the supplied file) — the full JSD loss definition and the paper's headline main-results table were not available to verify here; worth re-pulling the complete paper if this method is adopted.
