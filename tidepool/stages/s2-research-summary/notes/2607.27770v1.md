# 2607.27770v1 — Beyond the Best Teacher: Expanding and Compressing the Reasoning Solution Manifold

**Relevance:** medium
Directly touches GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards (one rung on our candidate ladder) and uses IFBench as its instruction-following benchmark (one of our guardrail metrics), but the paper's core contribution is a multi-teacher expand-then-compress pipeline that requires training several separate GRPO models and then distilling them — which collides with our "ensembling is out of scope" constraint, so nothing here is directly adoptable as-is.

## TL;DR

The paper argues a single GRPO run only amplifies part of the valid "reasoning solution manifold," so it trains several GRPO teachers from the same initialization, each pointed at examples the existing teacher union doesn't yet cover ("Residual GRPO"), then distills all of them into one student via reliability-gated on-policy distillation plus a residual term that preserves a winner teacher's specialist token preferences. On Qwen3-1.7B/4B this student beats the single best teacher on math, code, and instruction-following (IFBench) while shipping as one deployable model.

## Techniques to try

- On-policy distillation restricted to the student's own sampled token, weighted by which teachers are "reliable" for that example, is cheap to compute ("It requires only the student and teacher log-probabilities of the sampled token." — Sec 3.3) — relevant if we ever add a distillation step against a stronger teacher for schema/tool-call behavior, without needing full-vocab KL.
- Coverage-gating a training subset by per-example success rate ("An example is removed from subsequent residual rounds once its teacher-union coverage exceeds τ_drop=0.5" — Sec 4.1) is a simple recipe for curriculum/data-filtering during our own GRPO step (schema validity + execution correctness rewards), independent of the multi-teacher framing.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Math/code training pools built from Skywork-OR1-RL-Data; instruction-following training data from the verifiable set released with IFBench (Pyatkin et al., 2025) — same benchmark family as one of our own guardrail metrics (IFBench 47.33 target), though used here as *training* data, not just eval.
- Compares "Residual GRPO" against a "Parallel GRPO" baseline (four teachers trained on four random disjoint data partitions) to isolate whether coverage-aware allocation beats naive data-splitting for diversity.
- All training done with VERL + vLLM rollout, binary rule-based outcome rewards only ("we use binary outcome rewards R(x,y)∈{0,1} throughout training and data construction" — Sec 4.1), consistent with our own "no model-scored stored reward" constraint.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Core premise is itself a pitfall to keep in mind for our GRPO stage: "A single RL run may amplify one discovered basin while leaving other valid basins under-explored... an RL-trained model can become a strong but incomplete teacher: reliable on the solution modes it has captured, yet brittle on regions of the training distribution that require alternative modes." (Sec 1) — worth remembering when judging whether our schema-validity/execution-correctness GRPO run has actually generalized or just overfit one mode of valid tool calls.
- The method fundamentally requires training M≥2 separate GRPO teachers per task domain (four used here) plus a distillation pass — a compute multiplier that is hard to justify at 1.2B scale on a single-model ladder, and squarely the kind of ensembling our project has ruled out of scope.
- Naive uniform-weight teacher aggregation is worse than quality-weighting, and both are worse without the residual term: "Replacing uniform teacher aggregation with per-example quality weights improves the seven-benchmark math mean from 15.81 to 15.99... Adding the residual objective... raises the mean from 15.99 to 16.70" (Qwen3-1.7B, Sec 4.3) — i.e. naive averaging of multiple signals suppresses specialist behavior, a caution if we ever combine multiple reward signals or teacher judgments in one loss.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports Avg.@8 and Pass@8 (8 sampled responses per problem) across AIME/HMMT/AMC math sets, LiveCodeBench v6 for code, and IFBench loose Avg.@8 for instruction following — all at Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B scale, below/near our 1.2B target but not measuring anything tool-call or schema related.
- Uses a "Teacher Envelope" (best-of-teachers per benchmark, not a deployable model) purely as an upper-bound diagnostic — a useful analysis pattern (measuring how much of the achievable ceiling a single model captures) that could translate to our own BFCLv3/tool-call evals when comparing candidate rungs.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the reported numbers are on tool-calling, structured-output, or malformed-input-flagging tasks, so nothing here transfers as a benchmark number for our BFCLv3/schema-validity targets.
- Given "ensembling is out of scope" for us, is there a cut-down, single-teacher version of this on-policy-distillation objective (Sec 3.3's L_base with one reliable teacher) worth considering if we later add a stronger-teacher distillation rung, without the multi-teacher construction machinery?
