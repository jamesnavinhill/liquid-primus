# 2508.03332v2 — Exploring Layer-wise Information Effectiveness for Post-Training Quantization in Small Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Gives useful, scale-matched evidence that quantization damage is layer-heterogeneous and worse in smaller models, but the method targets ~2-bit mixed-precision on pure-Transformer architectures with perplexity/reasoning evals, not the 4-bit uniform GGUF + structured-output setting this project needs.

## TL;DR

The paper (LieQ) shows that a small, cheap geometric signal — how much a layer's singular-value entropy drops relative to a random-init baseline ("Compactness Shift") — predicts which Transformer layers are most damaged by aggressive quantization, without needing per-layer perplexity probing. Using this signal to keep ~1 layer at 4-bit and the rest at 2-bit recovers most of the accuracy lost to naive uniform 2-bit PTQ on Qwen3/LLaMA3.x models from 0.6B–8B. It's evidence that quantization sensitivity concentrates in specific (often shallow) layers rather than being uniform, which is a useful prior when deciding how to allocate precision in a 4-bit GGUF build, but the paper itself never touches 4-bit-only regimes, tool calling, or hybrid conv/attention architectures like LFM2.

## Techniques to try

- Use a cheap, data-free per-layer sensitivity proxy (SVD-based "Compactness Shift" between trained and randomly-initialized projections) instead of drop-one-layer perplexity probing to decide which layers deserve extra bits in a GGUF quant scheme ("This selection is data-free in the sense that it requires no validation set inference, relying only on the intrinsic weight statistics, i.e., via SVD on a single representative forward pass." — Section 3.2, Geometry-Driven Layer Selection).
- If experimenting with mixed-precision GGUF (not just flat Q4), protect only the single most "geometrically irreplaceable" layer at higher precision rather than a large subset — the paper found this yields most of the recoverable accuracy: "This confirms that protecting even the smallest subset (one layer) of geometrically critical layers yields a significant recovery in accuracy." (Section 4, Sensitivity to Bit Budget).
- The diagnostic is described as backend-agnostic: "LieQ is orthogonal to the choice of PTQ back-end, providing a plug-and-play guideline for exist PTQ methods. In our implementation, mixing the proposed diagnostic strategy with GPTQ-4bit or AWQ-4bit yields further compression while maintaining high accuracy." (Section 3.2, Integration with Existing Quantization Methods) — could inform whether to non-uniformly weight layer precision within an otherwise-4-bit GGUF build.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Perplexity on WikiText-2 and C4; zero-shot reasoning on PIQA, ARC-e/c, BoolQ, HellaSwag, Winogrande, MMLU (Section 4, Evaluation Datasets and Metrics) — general knowledge/reasoning benchmarks, not instruction-following or structured-output metrics.
- Compared against GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, PB-LLM, SliM-LLM, QuIP/QuIP#, AQLM as PTQ baselines (Section 4, Models and Baselines).
- Models: Qwen3 (0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B), LLaMA3.x (1B/3B/8B), LLaMA1&2 (7B/13B) — all standard decoder-only Transformers, sizes bracketing this project's 1.2B target.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Naive uniform low-bit PTQ collapses badly at sub-8B scale: "2-bit quantization increasing perplexity by two orders of magnitude. *This problem is especially severe in compact models (sub-8B parameters) due to their limited redundancy to absorb quantization noise.*" (Section 1, Introduction) — e.g. Qwen3-0.6B FP16 WikiText2 perplexity 20.9 vs. GPTQ-2bit 2.38E+04 (Table 1).
- "Finding 1. Smaller models (e.g., Qwen-0.6B) exhibit lower robustness under extremely low-bit quantization(∼2bit), with certain important layers standing out as significantly more critical than others" (Section 3.1) — directly relevant to the project's concern that a 1.2B model may be more fragile under compression than larger peers, though this paper's evidence is at 2-bit, not the project's 4-bit target.
- Existing finer-grained/rotation/codebook methods (APTQ, LLM-MQ, QuIP#, AQLM) that achieve strong low-bit accuracy "sacrifice hardware efficiency with heterogeneous data formats" or "require additional process or runtime transforms and irregular memory access, complicating deployment" (Section 2) — a caution against adopting exotic quantization formats if the deployment target (llama.cpp/GGUF on a consumer GPU) needs standard kernels.
- Author-stated limitation: end-to-end throughput gains are only estimated, not fully engineered: "significant room remains for optimization in practical engineering. End-to-end inference throughput optimization will depend on specific system-level implementations." (Limitations) — the paper's Table 4 decode-speed numbers (e.g., L1-7B: 38.85→50.89 tok/s) come from a simple end-to-end estimate, not a fully optimized kernel, so should not be treated as production throughput guarantees.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses Spearman correlation (ρ>0.8) between the drop-one-layer perplexity metric (ΔPPL) and the geometric proxy (Δr) as validation that the proxy is a reasonable substitute for expensive functional-saliency probing (Section 3.1, Supporting Metric: Top-k Energy).
- All evaluation is perplexity + zero-shot reasoning accuracy; no structured-output, tool-calling, or instruction-following metric is used anywhere in the paper, so it cannot directly answer whether quantization damages structured output more than knowledge.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the "shallow layers are most critical" finding (also tied to "super weights" outlier literature) hold for LFM2's hybrid gated-short-convolution + GQA architecture, or is it specific to pure-attention Transformers where the method's per-projection SVD analysis was defined?
- The paper's mixed-precision scheme (1 layer at 4-bit, rest at 2-bit) is far more aggressive than this project's target (uniform ~4-bit GGUF retaining ≥97%/93% quality floors) — would a similar layer-sensitivity ranking be worth using just to decide which llama.cpp GGUF quant type (e.g., higher-bit K-quant for a few sensitive layers) to apply within an otherwise uniform 4-bit build, or is the effect only visible at the much harsher 2-bit regime this paper tests?
- No test of whether quantization sensitivity by layer correlates with degradation specifically in JSON/tool-schema output vs. general perplexity — this would need to be checked directly against the project's own BFCL/schema-validity metrics rather than inferred from this paper.
