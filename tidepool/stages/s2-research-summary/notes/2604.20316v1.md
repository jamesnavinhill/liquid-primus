# 2604.20316v1 — R2IF: Aligning Reasoning with Decisions via Composite Rewards for Interpretable LLM Function Calling

**Relevance:** high
Directly targets the project's GRPO rung of the candidate ladder — a composite reward for function-calling RL, tested at 1.5B–7B (close to the 1.2B target) on BFCL, with ablations that bear on reward hacking, SFT warm-start necessity, and inference-cost tradeoffs.

## TL;DR

R2IF adds two reward terms on top of a binary format/correctness GRPO reward for tool-calling RL: a Chain-of-Thought Effectiveness Reward (CER) that scores whether the reasoning prefix actually helps a student model produce the right tool call, and a Specification-Modification-Value (SMV) reward that grades whether the reasoning explicitly names the argument's spec, describes the needed transformation, and instantiates the value. Tested on Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B and Llama3.2-3B against Raw/SFT/Binary-reward/ToolRL baselines on BFCL and ACEBench, with ablations showing each component is load-bearing.

## Techniques to try

- Structured `<reason></reason><tool></tool>` output format enforced as a hard binary gate before any softer reward applies: "$\mathcal{R}_{\text{format}}=1$ if and only if the output satisfies all of the following conditions: (i) it contains exactly one <reason></reason> block and one <tool></tool> block; (ii) the <reason> block precedes the <tool> block; (iii) response should not include any other text." — Section 3.1.1
- Weight the hard correctness/format gate higher than the soft reasoning-quality terms to keep optimization from drifting toward verbosity: "the binary reward is weighted by 3 to avoid the optimization focus shifting excessively toward the softer reward terms." — Section 3.1.1
- SMV reward evaluated only on exact-matched tool calls, avoiding credit assignment to reasoning attached to the wrong tool/schema: "We compute SMV only on strictly aligned calls... This prevents SMV credit for mismatched tools or schemas." — Section 3.1.3
- Parameter-level SMV combines a hard executability check (is the arg actually instantiated) with a semantic-similarity-gated score against ground-truth spec/modification text: $r_{\mathrm{val}}(p)=\mathbb{I}[p\in a_i.\mathrm{arg}]$ and $r_{\mathrm{sem}}(p,t)=\mathbb{I}[\mathrm{sim}(R_p,t)\geq\tau]\cdot\mathrm{sim}(R_p,t)$ — Section 3.1.3, Eqs. 3-4

## Baselines & datasets used

- Training data: a filtered/balanced 2,500-sample subset of ToolACE (Liu et al. 2024a), capped at 500 instances per case type to fix class imbalance — Section 4.1
- Baselines compared: Raw instruct model, SFT (reasoning distilled from GPT-4o), Binary-reward-only GRPO (Zhang et al. 2025), and ToolRL (Qian et al. 2025) — Section 4.2
- Benchmarks: BFCL (single-turn subset) and ACEBench, both at model scales ≤7B (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B, Llama3.2-3B) — Section 4.2, 4.3

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Binary/outcome rewards can produce high accuracy with shallow, non-load-bearing reasoning: "some methods achieve high accuracy but relatively low ACE scores, such as Qwen2.5-3B with the Binary Reward method... its reasoning is shallow and lacks depth, relying on simple rules rather than thorough analysis." Directly relevant to the project's "which signal instils flag-rather-than-assert" question — a schema/correctness-only reward alone does not guarantee the model's stated reasoning is doing any work.
- SFT warm-start is more important the smaller the model: "SFT warm-start is more critical for smaller models... binary rewards often act as a hard gate: malformed outputs receive little to no learning signal, pushing small models more frequently into a near-zero-reward regime and further impairing exploration efficiency." This is a concrete argument for including an SFT stage before GRPO at 1.2B scale, matching the project's candidate ladder ordering.
- Candidate "student model" evaluators for CER can degenerate: "The Qwen student models frequently default to the rejection response even when tool invocation is required, causing continuation-based success rates on non-irrelevance cases to degenerate to near-zero constants. Under this behavior, CER becomes largely uninformative." — a caution that a model's own rejection/refusal bias can silently break a reward or eval signal built on top of it.
- Reasoning-length inference overhead is non-trivial and scales with model/task: "+71 tokens for Llama3.2-3B and +131 tokens for Qwen2.5-3B" versus ToolRL, i.e., roughly 60-90% more reasoning tokens than a leaner outcome-reward baseline — relevant to the project's throughput-floor constraint if a similar reasoning-heavy reward is adopted.
- Explicitly out of scope / untested: "the current evaluation of R2IF is limited to single-turn function query tasks... the model's ability to maintain coherent reasoning and accurate tool calls [across turns] remains untested" (a 50-sample multi-turn appendix probe exists but is small). Also: "the reward design of R2IF is highly tailored to specific function calling tasks, which may hinder its applicability to tasks that require different forms of reasoning."
- No quantization or on-device deployment discussion at all — the paper only trains/evaluates full-precision models on 6 RTX PRO 6000 GPUs via Verl; nothing here transfers to the 4-bit GGUF / throughput-floor part of the project.

## Metric / eval choices

- Average CoT Effectiveness (ACE): mean over test cases of the improvement in a fixed student model's success rate when conditioned on the candidate's reasoning prefix versus a no-reasoning baseline — a way to score whether reasoning is causally useful rather than just present. Could be adapted as a proxy for "the model's stated reasoning actually determines its structured output" in this project's own eval, though it requires a separate student model and multiple sampled continuations per instance (added inference cost, see Appendix D: "Enabling CER increases per-step training time by 11.4% on Llama3.2-3B and 2.9% on Qwen2.5-7B").
- Irrelevance-rejection accuracy tracked alongside overall accuracy specifically to check that gains aren't just from a conservative "reject everything" strategy: "R2IF maintains strong irrelevant tool-rejection performance (≥96% accuracy) while boosting Atomic and Single-turn task gains" — a useful sanity check pattern for the project's own flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair.
- Human evaluation of reasoning interpretability along four dimensions (Predictability, Comprehensibility, Interpretability, Faithfulness) with Cronbach's α > 0.7 reliability, using a published framework (Bhambri et al. 2025) — a possible template if the project wants qualitative interpretability sign-off beyond automatic metrics.

## Open questions for our project

- ACE requires a separate "faithful student model" fine-tuned with a distinct faithfulness-enhancing method (Akter et al. 2025) as the effectiveness evaluator — unclear how this maps onto a 1.2B target model with no obvious larger/separate student available in-project, and the paper's own experiment shows this evaluator choice is fragile (Qwen students degenerate to rejection).
- The paper only reports results at 1.5B-7B and never at sub-1.5B scale, so it doesn't directly answer whether SMV-style parameter-level reasoning supervision is learnable at 1.2B, only that the smaller (3B) models needed more SFT warm-start than the 7B ones — suggests the trend (smaller = more SFT-dependent) likely continues down to 1.2B but is not measured there.
- No discussion of malformed/garbage tool-return handling (the project's "flag rather than assert" sub-criterion) — R2IF's "irrelevance" case is about queries with no applicable tool, not about tool results that come back malformed after a call, so its reward design would need to be extended, not directly reused, for that requirement.
