# 2505.23854v1 — Revisiting Uncertainty Estimation and Calibration of Large Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
The paper never touches tool-calling, structured output, or JSON schemas, but its findings on how post-training, reasoning mode, model scale, and quantization each move uncertainty/calibration quality are directly relevant background for the project's "flag rather than assert" sub-criterion, even though none of its numbers are measured on anything close to a 1.2B model.

## TL;DR

The authors benchmark three black-box, single-pass uncertainty-estimation methods (token-probability, numeric self-report, and linguistic hedging judged by another LLM) across 80 LLMs from 0.6B to 671B on MMLU-Pro. Linguistic Verbal Uncertainty (LVU, scoring hedge words like "probably"/"might" with a judge LLM) beats the other two methods, and several training/architecture factors — post-training, reasoning mode, scale, MoE vs dense, quantization — measurably shift calibration and selective-classification quality, but accuracy itself is a poor proxy for whether a model's uncertainty is trustworthy.

## Techniques to try

- Judge-scored linguistic hedging (LVU) as an uncertainty signal: "using LLaMA-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct as a separate evaluator to interpret and score linguistic uncertainty" — Section 2.1. Could inform how to score/reward "flag rather than assert" behavior in preference or GRPO data (a judge model rating whether the model appropriately hedges/flags vs. blindly asserts), though this project's constraint against model-scored stored reward as a training signal limits how directly this transfers.
- Track calibration (ECE) and selective classification (AUROC) as two distinct axes rather than one: "the correlation between calibration (ECE) and selective classification (AUROC) is generally low or even close to zero, especially for LVU" — Section 3.7. Suggests the flag-rate/false-flag-rate pairing in this project's sub-criteria is the right instinct (two orthogonal numbers), and a single combined score would hide failure modes.

## Baselines & datasets used

- MMLU-Pro (reasoning- and knowledge-heavy multiple choice, June 2024 release) as the sole evaluation dataset — not a tool-calling or structured-output benchmark, so none of the absolute numbers transfer to BFCL-style targets.
- 80 models spanning 0.6B–671B, both dense and MoE, reasoning and non-reasoning variants, several quantization variants (FP8, AWQ) of the Qwen3 family.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Smallest models produce unreliable uncertainty signals: "lightweight models such as Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B struggle to produce meaningful uncertainty scores, often approaching random guessing" — Section 3.1. This is a caution sign for a 1.2B target model: general-purpose uncertainty/calibration capability may not scale down well, even before considering the narrower "flag malformed tool return" task.
- LVU's apparent strength in small models is an artifact, not a real signal: "smaller models tend to generate responses that poorly follow instructions or omit uncertainty cues altogether. The judge model tends to assign high LVU uncertainty to such non-compliant outputs... not because their uncertainty estimates are intrinsically better, but because instruction violations correlate strongly with failure" — Section 3.1. Directly cautionary for this project's flag-rate metric: a model that is simply bad at following the output format could look like it has a high "flag rate" when it's actually just failing to comply, which would inflate false-flag-adjacent behavior in ways the 0.80/0.10 target pair might not catch cleanly.
- High accuracy does not imply good calibration: "several top-performing models—including LLaMA-4-Maverick, LLaMA-3.1-405B, and GPT-4.1—exhibit strong accuracy but poor uncertainty estimation under TPU" — Section 3.5. Relevant caution against assuming BFCL/IFEval gains automatically carry over to reliable malformed-tool-return flagging.
- Quantization degrades uncertainty quality somewhat, more so at smaller scale: "quantization generally leads to mild degradation in uncertainty estimation, with effects more noticeable in smaller models... the impact of quantization remains modest, typically within a 5% margin" — Section 3.9. Note this is FP8/AWQ on the Qwen3 series (not 4-bit GGUF, and not tested at 1.2B scale), so it is suggestive rather than a number to carry over directly for the "does 4-bit quantization damage structured output more than knowledge" question.

## Metric / eval choices

- Calibration via Expected Calibration Error (ECE) with 10 uncertainty bins, and discrimination via AUROC for selective classification — Section 2.2. These are the paper's two headline metrics; conceptually parallel to (but not the same construction as) this project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair.
- Explicit exclusion of multi-generation/consistency methods (e.g., semantic entropy) and internal-state methods as infeasible or too costly for black-box, single-pass deployment — Section 2. Matches this project's own single-forward-pass, no-repair, no-grammar-constraint framing for schema validity.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the "instruction non-compliance masquerading as high uncertainty/flag rate" failure mode (Section 3.1) apply to the malformed-tool-return probe? A 1.2B model that garbles its output format under quantization could look like it's "flagging" when it's actually just breaking format — worth checking that the flag-rate metric distinguishes a genuine flag from a malformed non-answer.
- Would judge-model-scored linguistic hedging be usable at all as a signal here, given the project's constraint that "no model-scored stored reward may be reused as a training signal" — this paper's core method (LVU) is exactly that kind of judge-scored signal, so it's informative context but not a directly adoptable technique under this project's rules.
