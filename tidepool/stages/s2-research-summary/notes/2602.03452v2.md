# 2602.03452v2 — Beyond Variance: Prompt-Efficient RLVR via Rare-Event Amplification and Bidirectional Pairing

**Relevance:** medium
Not about tool-calling, structured output, or quantization at all, but it directly touches the GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung of our candidate ladder: it gives concrete, transferable guidance on shaping binary-outcome rewards (schema-valid/invalid is exactly this kind of reward) and on avoiding degenerate advantage groups.

## TL;DR

The paper shows that for GRPO-style RLVR with binary verifiable rewards, training on just two well-chosen prompts (one hard-but-solvable, one easy-but-brittle) with a reweighted advantage scheme (WGRPO) beats a two-prompt baseline chosen by training-variance heuristics, and even rivals training on a 1209-prompt pool, on Qwen2.5-Math-7B math benchmarks. The core mechanism is that group-normalization on binary outcomes already "amplifies" rare tail events (rare success on a hard prompt, rare failure on an easy prompt) into large-magnitude advantages, and their method just leans into that instead of fighting it.

## Techniques to try

- Weight incorrect/negative outcomes asymmetrically before group-normalizing, rather than using raw {0,1} or {-1,1} rewards: "$y_{i}=\begin{cases}+1,&s_{i}^{\text{raw}}>\tau,\\ -\lambda_{\text{neg}},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}$" and empirically "we recommend using a reasonably large default (we use $\lambda_{\mathrm{neg}}{=}100$) rather than optimizing $\lambda_{\mathrm{neg}}$ per dataset" (Appendix B.3) — relevant if we design a binary schema-validity reward for our GRPO rung and want a principled default rather than per-run tuning.
- Select training/probe examples by targeting a success-rate window under the current policy rather than by raw difficulty or historical variance: "$q^{+}=\arg\min_{q\in\mathcal{C}^{+}}\left|\bar{p}(q)-p_{\mathrm{hard}}\right|$" with $p_{\mathrm{hard}}\approx 1/G$, done via "a simple probing stage on two candidate pools with different expected difficulty under the same base model" (Section 3.2) — could translate to how we curate the ~300 malformed-tool-return probe set: pick items near the model's current flag-rate boundary rather than uniformly.
- Explicit statement that "high variance of historical accuracy is a weak proxy for informative training signal" (Section 5) — a caution against a data-selection heuristic we might otherwise reach for when picking hard cases for GRPO/DPO pair mining.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Models: Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (both larger than our 1.2B target; no smaller-scale results reported).
- Datasets: AIME 2025, AMC23, MATH500 for eval; training prompts drawn from AIME 2025 and DeepScaleR-sub (1209-prompt subset of DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset).
- Framework: verl (Sheng et al., 2024), GRPO with group size $G=8$, learning rate $1\times10^{-6}$, 500 max steps, ≤8 H100 GPUs.
- Main baseline: GRPO trained on two prompts selected by the historical-training-accuracy-variance heuristic from prior "one-shot RLVR" work (Wang et al., 2025).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Degenerate groups (all-correct or all-incorrect within a group of $G$ rollouts) produce **zero** advantage and thus no gradient update: "Thus, degenerate groups contribute no policy-gradient update (up to the KL term in our objective)" (Appendix B.2) — worth checking for our schema-validity reward: if a batch of rollouts on one prompt/tool-turn is uniformly valid or uniformly malformed, that example contributes nothing to the GRPO update, which could silently waste rollout budget on "easy" turns.
- A too-small negative weight hurts: "performance is consistently lower with $\lambda_{\mathrm{neg}}=1$" across all three benchmarks (Appendix B.3, Table 3), i.e. naive symmetric {+1,-1} binary rewards underperform an asymmetric weighting under this normalization scheme.
- Ablation II: substituting the "easy-but-brittle" prompt with a merely high-variance prompt (still using WGRPO) "generally hurts performance" relative to the easy+hard pairing (Section 5) — the specific selection criterion (targeted success-rate window), not just the reweighted loss, matters.
- Explicit limitations acknowledged by the authors: "we focus on the 7B ... and do not run full RLVR training for larger models, like 14B/32B/72B models" and "We focus on mathematical reasoning and do not evaluate other RLVR tasks with deterministic outcomes, such as code generation" (Appendix E) — so transfer to a 1.2B model or to tool-calling/schema-validity tasks is untested by this paper.

## Metric / eval choices

- Pass@$k$ with the unbiased estimator from Chen et al. (2021): $\mathrm{Pass@}k=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{C}}\left[1-\frac{\binom{n-c}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}}\right]$, $k\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64\}$, $n=64$ samples per problem — not directly reusable for our single-shot structured-output metric (first-attempt schema validity), but the underlying idea (dataset-level replication to stabilize small-eval-set variance: "128× for AIME 2025/AMC23 and 8× for MATH500") is relevant if our ~300-example malformed-tool-return probe set turns out to have high evaluation variance.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the rare-event-amplification effect (and the need for asymmetric $\lambda_{\mathrm{neg}}$ weighting) hold the same way when the binary reward is schema/execution validity on tool calls rather than exact-match math correctness, and at 1.2B instead of 7B?
- Would a "positive-negative pairing" analog — e.g., always including one hard-but-usually-malformed probe and one easy-but-occasionally-malformed probe per GRPO update — help stabilize our flag-rate/false-flag-rate training, given our constraint that no model-scored reward may be reused as a training signal (this paper's reward is a hard exact-match verifier, which is compatible with that constraint)?
