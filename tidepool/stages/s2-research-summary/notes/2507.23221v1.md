# 2507.23221v1 — A Single Direction of Truth: An Observer Model's Linear Residual Probe Exposes and Steers Contextual Hallucinations

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap with the "flag rather than assert" question, but the method needs a separate full-precision observer model with residual-stream access, is validated only on Gemma-2 (2B-27B) news summarization / logical-contradiction tasks, and gives no recipe transferable to training or quantizing a 1.2B tool-calling model.

## TL;DR

The paper trains a simple logistic-regression probe on a frozen "observer" LLM's mid-layer residual stream to detect whether a continuation contradicts its source context, and shows this single linear direction generalizes across model sizes/domains and can causally steer hallucination rate up or down by activation patching. It's a detection/interpretability result for summarization-style contextual hallucination, not a training method for tool-calling or structured output, and its smallest tested model (GPT-2-small) still underperforms the larger observers.

## Techniques to try

- Gradient-times-activation attribution to localize which MLP layers drive a probe score, potentially reusable if we ever want to inspect *why* our model flags a tool return as malformed: "Gradient‑times‑activation localises this signal to sparse, late‑layer MLP activity." (Abstract)
- Unsupervised domain adaptation of the observer via plain SFT on correct-only in-domain text (no new labels) before retraining the probe, which improved detection F1 without needing hallucination labels for the new domain — an idea for adapting a "flag garbage tool output" detector to new tool schemas cheaply: "this process improved F1 scores for all models: by +0.10 for GPT-2-small, +0.17 for Gemma-2-2B, and +0.14 for Gemma-2-9B" (§4.5)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Lexical overlap, entity verification (spaCy), semantic similarity (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small), and Lookback Lens (attention-based) as detection baselines — all beaten by the linear probe by "5–27 points" (Abstract).
- Datasets: CNN/DailyMail and XSum (summarization, hallucinated continuations from gpt-4.1-mini) and their own released ContraTales (2,000 examples, purely logical contradictions, generated with Claude Opus prefixes + o4-mini continuations) — none are tool-calling or structured-output datasets.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- On the logically-hard ContraTales set (closer in spirit to "is this tool result nonsense" than the news datasets), the probe's F1 dropped well below its news-domain performance: "The linear probe's F1 score on ContraTales, while higher than baselines, did not reach its news-domain performance levels" (§4.2), reaching only 0.75 F1 vs 0.97–0.99 on news — surface/attention-based cues degrade even further on logical (vs. lexical) inconsistency, a caution against assuming any single flagging signal transfers from easy to hard failure modes.
- Cross-domain transfer of surface-cue baselines was poor even though the linear probe transferred well: "lexical overlap F1 decreased from 0.78 (in-domain cnn/dm) to 0.42 when transferred to xsum. Semantic similarity performance was near chance levels out-of-domain" (§4.3) — a reminder that our malformed-tool-return flag detector must be validated out-of-distribution, not just in-domain.
- Attention-pattern attribution was noisy and inconsistent across datasets ("Per-head attention attributions showed fluctuations around zero, lacking layer-consistent signs"), so attention-based flagging heuristics (Lookback Lens style) are called out as unreliable relative to residual-stream probing.
- The method fundamentally requires white-box access to a full-precision transformer's hidden activations at a specific layer — inapplicable to a deployed 4-bit GGUF served through an inference runtime without exposing internal residual-stream activations, and untested at anything near our 1.2B target scale (smallest tested is GPT-2-small/Gemma-2-2B, and even 2B trails 9B/27B).

## Metric / eval choices

- F1 via 5-fold cross-validation with a fixed seed, and 95% bootstrap CIs on bars — a reasonable robustness pattern for a detector paper, but the paper only ever measures overall F1/flag detection, not a paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate design like our ≥0.80 flag / ≤0.10 false-flag target, so it offers no direct precedent for that specific eval framing.
- Causal steering was evaluated with an LLM judge (gpt-4.1) for hallucination rate plus RapidFuzz for repetition rate — the paper itself flags this as a limitation: "LLM-based evaluations can be subject to noise, bias, and variability, which may affect the precision of the quantitative steering results" (§5 Limitations), which is a relevant caution against relying on model-scored judges as ground truth (though not literally the same "no model-scored stored reward" constraint, since here the judge is only used for eval, not training).

## Open questions for our project

- Could a lightweight residual-stream probe (on the full-precision LFM2.5-1.2B, before quantization) be trained as an auxiliary "malformed tool return" detector, separate from the main generation head, to help hit the 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate targets — or is this infeasible given the model must ship as a 4-bit GGUF with no exposed activation access at inference time in the target runtime?
- The paper's own limitations note that its hallucination labels are synthetically generated by other LLMs (gpt-4.1-mini, o4-mini) and may carry generator-specific artifacts inflating apparent detector performance — the same risk applies if we synthesize malformed-tool-return examples for our own held-out probe set.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
