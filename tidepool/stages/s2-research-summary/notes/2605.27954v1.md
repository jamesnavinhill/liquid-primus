# 2605.27954v1 — Cyclical Entropy Eruption: Entropy Dynamics in Agent Reinforcement Learning

**Relevance:** high
Directly diagnoses a training-collapse failure mode of GRPO-style agent RL with a format/schema-validity reward component — the exact regime our candidate ladder's GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards stage would use — at sub-8B scale, and proposes a cheap, no-inference-cost fix (SEAL).

## TL;DR

The paper shows that agent RL (GRPO/GIGPO) does not simply collapse entropy like single-turn reasoning RL; instead it cycles through entropy descent, a sharp "eruption" (caused by correct/incorrect trajectories clustering too closely in representation space, which creates gradient interference that suppresses valid-trajectory likelihood), and subsidence — and this cycle can degrade into full training collapse (0% success). They propose SEAL, an auxiliary binary-classification loss on token hidden states that separates correct from incorrect trajectories in representation space, which stabilizes training and improves final performance with zero inference-time cost.

## Techniques to try

- SEAL auxiliary loss: attach a lightweight MLP classifier on top of per-token hidden states predicting whether the token's trajectory was correct/incorrect (binary, trajectory-level label), added to the RL loss with weight α: "The overall training objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RL}}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SEAL}}$" (Section 4.1). Directly applicable if we pursue the GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung of our ladder (schema validity + execution correctness reward), since our setup shares the same "format-valid vs. not / correct vs. not" structure this paper studies.
- Reward-penalty shaping for format errors used in their agent RL recipe: "we also apply a reward penalty of -0.1 to punish the format errors" (Section C.1, AlfWorld/WebShop setup) — a concrete, simple pattern for penalizing malformed tool calls inside a GRPO reward, relevant to our schema-validity reward design.
- Monitoring policy entropy, valid-action ratio, and a sentence-duplication ratio as training-health diagnostics during RL, not just downstream benchmark score — cheap early-warning signals for the kind of collapse this paper documents.
- SEAL is reported as robust to its hyperparameter: "SEAL remains effective over a broad range of α... its gains are robust rather than dependent on delicate hyperparameter tuning" (Section 4.3, Ablations) — low tuning burden if adopted.

## Baselines & datasets used

- AlfWorld (multi-step embodied household tasks, 3,827 instances) and WebShop (1.1M products, 12k instructions) as the two main agent RL benchmarks; also a search-augmented QA task (NQ, HotpotQA, TriviaQA, PopQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboo).
- Backbones at project-relevant scale: Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct and Llama3.2-1B/3B-Instruct — all ≤7B, directly comparable to our 1.2B target.
- RL algorithms: GRPO and GIGPO, built on the VeRL framework, following the Agent-RL/GIGPO implementation.
- Compute used: "2 NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs" for the 1.5B/1B runs and "2 NVIDIA H200 GPUs" for the 7B/3B runs — modest, not a large-cluster requirement, so the recipe itself is feasible at our scale even though our project runs on different managed hardware.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Llama3.2-1B instruct "cannot sample valid trajectories throughout the RL training" without mid-training first — they had to mid-train on a subset of OpenManus before RL could even proceed. Relevant warning: a small, under-tuned-for-tool-use base model may need a format/mid-training pass before RL, not jump straight to GRPO.
- Plain GRPO on Llama3.2-1B/WebShop collapsed entirely: Table 1 reports Score 00.00 / Succ. 00.00 for GRPO baseline, while "adding SEAL recovers training and boosts performance to 79.69%" — a concrete case of GRPO training fully failing at ~1B scale on a tool-use-like task, the same scale class as our target model.
- Degenerate patterns (sentence duplication, hallucinated tool/product attributes) acquired during an entropy eruption "can persist and accumulate across cycles," and "if these degenerate trajectories receive positive reward, the policy inadvertently learns and reinforce such garbage patterns" (Section 3.4 / Appendix B.6) — a concrete reward-hacking mechanism when the reward signal doesn't penalize degenerate-but-correct outputs, directly relevant to our schema-validity reward design (a reward that only checks "is this parseable/executable" can still reinforce sentence duplication or hallucinated content if it happens to pass).
- "Agent trajectories cluster together in the representation space" far more tightly than trajectories from a non-agent alignment task (UltraFeedback) — i.e., agent/tool-call outputs are inherently harder to separate in representation space than general instruction-following outputs, a structural reason agent RL is more failure-prone than general-purpose RLHF.

## Metric / eval choices

- Entropy curve over training steps as the primary training-health diagnostic, decomposed into three named phases (descent / eruption / subsidence).
- Valid-action ratio (fraction of sampled trajectories meeting format constraints) tracked over training steps, not just at the end.
- Sentence-duplication ratio: "we split each response into sentence-level chunks and measure the fraction of sentences that are repeated within the same response" — a cheap, automatic degeneracy metric usable during our own RL runs.
- Hallucination rate for WebShop defined narrowly as "the rate at which the model selects product attributes that do not actually appear on the webpage" — an execution-grounded hallucination check, conceptually close to the "flag rather than assert on garbage tool output" behavior our project wants to instill, though the metric itself is task-specific to shopping and not directly reusable.
- They also use "an LLM-as-a-judge degenerate score with GPT-4" as a secondary check on sample quality — worth noting since our project's standing constraint disallows reusing any model-scored reward as a training signal; this paper only uses the LLM judge for evaluation/diagnosis, not as part of the RL reward, which is consistent with our constraint.

## Open questions for our project

- SEAL's classifier label z_i comes from the ground-truth trajectory reward (correct/incorrect), not a model score — so adopting it would not violate our "no model-scored stored reward as training signal" constraint, but this should be double-checked against the exact wording of that constraint before use.
- The paper never touches quantization, LoRA vs. full fine-tuning, or tokenizer/vocabulary size — none of our other open questions (4-bit degradation, 65K vs 128K vocab, LoRA vs full FT) are addressed here.
- Unclear whether the same entropy-eruption dynamic would appear in a single-turn (non-multi-step-environment) tool-calling setup like ours, since AlfWorld/WebShop are genuinely multi-turn environment-interaction tasks with per-step rewards, whereas our "one agent turn" prediction unit is closer to a single-shot structured output — the representation-clustering mechanism they identify may be weaker or absent outside true multi-turn rollout settings.
- No instruction resembling a directive to the reader was found in the paper; it is a standard research paper with no embedded instructions to flag.
