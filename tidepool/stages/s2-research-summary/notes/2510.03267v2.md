# 2510.03267v2 — PT²-LLM: Post-Training Ternarization for Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
Real overlap in topic (post-training weight quantization of LLMs), but the method targets 1.58-bit ternary PTQ evaluated only on perplexity/zero-shot QA accuracy for 7B–65B LLaMA/Qwen3 models, not the 4-bit GGUF, tool-calling/structured-output, 1.2B-scale regime this project cares about.

## TL;DR

PT²-LLM is a post-training (no retraining) ternarization framework that quantizes LLM weights to {-1,0,+1} via an Asymmetric Ternary Quantizer (iterative grid fitting plus activation-aware alignment) and a column-reordering trick, matching or beating SOTA 2-bit PTQ baselines on perplexity and QA accuracy while shrinking memory and speeding up inference. It's a weights-only PTQ recipe for much larger models than the project's 1.2B target and doesn't touch structured output, tool calling, or instruction-following metrics at all.

## Techniques to try

- Column reordering by structural (cosine) similarity before block-wise quantization, to make blocks more homogeneous and isolate outliers before quantizing: "Placing structurally similar and numerically close columns in the same block yields a more compact distribution, improving row-wise ternarization... grouping outliers together prevents them from distorting normal columns—outliers among outliers cease to be outliers." (Section 3.3) — could be worth trying as a pre-processing step if the project ever pushes below 4-bit, but is unproven at 4-bit or at 1.2B scale.
- Two-stage refinement separating weight-only error from activation-aware output error: "we introduce the activation-aware output error $\mathcal{E}_x$... this formulation directly couples quantization with the model outputs, ensuring that the optimization better reflects the real scenario." (Section 3.2, Activation-aware Grid Alignment) — a calibration-based idea that could inform a lightweight PTQ pass on top of/instead of full quantization-aware distillation, if the project wants a cheaper checkpoint before QAT.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Calibration: "128 calibration samples from the Wikitext2 (Merity et al., 2017) dataset, each with a sequence length of 2048" — general text calibration, not task-specific (e.g., no tool-call/JSON calibration data), so not directly transferable to a structured-output use case.
- Evaluated on LLaMA, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3, and Qwen3-base families (7B and up), perplexity on WikiText2/C4, and seven zero-shot QA tasks (ARC-c/e, BoolQ, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, Winogrande) — no BFCL, IFEval, or any tool-calling/structured-output benchmark.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Updating the ternary assignment matrix T using the activation-aware objective caused overfitting to the calibration set: "Ideally, we would update T to further reduce the output error $\mathcal{E}_x$, but unlike ITF, no optimal solution exists. Greedy search is possible, yet in practice we observe that updating T leads to severe overfitting on the calibration set. Therefore, we freeze T and update only $(\alpha,\mu)$ once." (Section 3.2) — a concrete warning that calibration-driven quantization refinement can overfit to the calibration distribution rather than generalizing; relevant caution if the project calibrates its own 4-bit GGUF quantization on a narrow tool-call dataset.
- Calibration set choice matters: "using WikiText2 or C4 leads to comparable average accuracy, while PTB performs significantly worse, likely due to its lower data quality" (Section 4.3) — supports the general point that calibration data quality/domain match affects PTQ outcomes, though this paper never tests calibrating on structured-output/JSON data.
- Naive blockwise ternarization without reordering "causes severe performance degradation" due to within-block variance and column-wise outlier bias (Section 3.3) — a reminder that low-bit PTQ is highly sensitive to weight distribution shape, though this is at 1.58-bit, more extreme than the project's 4-bit target.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses perplexity (WikiText2, C4) plus average zero-shot QA accuracy across seven commonsense/reasoning benchmarks as its only quality metrics — no instruction-following, schema-validity, or tool-calling metric is used anywhere in the paper, so it cannot answer this project's core question of whether quantization damages structured output more than knowledge.
- Also reports compression time, model size reduction, and prefill/decode/end-to-end throughput via llama.cpp on A800 GPUs (Section 4.5) — throughput methodology (llama.cpp benchmark across prefill/decode/end-to-end) is a reasonable pattern to mirror for the project's own 4-bit throughput-parity check, but numbers are for 7B–65B models on datacenter GPUs, not a 1.2B model on an 8GB consumer GPU.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper only quantizes weights to 1.58-bit ternary or compares against 2-bit baselines; it gives no signal on how well its techniques (ITF, AGA, SSR) would hold up at 4-bit, where the project's quality bar is much tighter (≥97% retention, no axis below 93%).
- No result here addresses tool-calling, JSON/schema validity, or instruction-following degradation under quantization — the project's central open question (does quantization hurt structured output more than knowledge) remains unanswered by this paper.
- All experiments are on 7B+ models; nothing here is measured at or near the project's 1.2B scale, so magnitude of any transferable effect is uncertain.
