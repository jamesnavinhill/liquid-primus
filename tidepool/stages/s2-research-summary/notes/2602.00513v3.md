# 2602.00513v3 — Minerva: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards for Cyber Threat Intelligence LLMs

**Relevance:** high
The paper is off-domain (CTI identifier/schema mapping, not tool-calling) and off-scale (3B–8B backbones, not 1.2B), but its core contribution — MinervaRL, a hardness-gated self-training add-on to GRPO that fixes sparse-reward stalls under verifiable rewards — targets exactly the failure mode this project's GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung of the candidate ladder is likely to hit, and it reports general-capability preservation numbers under RLVR that bear directly on the "does RL erode general ability" question.

## TL;DR

MinervaRL augments plain GRPO: when a rollout group for a prompt contains no fully-verified completion ("hard" prompt), an EMA teacher is briefly shown the gold label to generate a rationale, that rationale is verifier- and quality-filtered, and the accepted trace is distilled back onto the *original label-free prompt*. Across four 3B–8B backbones and 12 CTI benchmarks this lifts the mean score from 48.2 (GRPO) to 52.5, beats SFT/rejection-sampling/off-policy-RLVR baselines, and reduces the "zero-solve fraction" (fraction of prompts whose whole rollout group scores 0) more than just raising the rollout budget does.

## Techniques to try

- Hardness-gated answer-conditioned rationale (ACR) generation + periodic distillation as a GRPO add-on for prompts where the rollout group never gets a verifier reward of 1: "when the current policy cannot solve a prompt within the available rollout budget, we temporarily reveal the ground-truth label during training to elicit a short rationale that justifies it... Accepted traces are then distilled back into the actor using the original task prompt, without any label hint." (Section 4.2) — directly reusable idea for the GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung: reveal the correct tool call / schema during training only to seed a distillation trace, never at inference.
- Track a "zero-solve fraction" (fraction of prompts whose rollout group max reward = 0) as a training-time diagnostic for reward sparsity, not just final eval accuracy: "we track this failure mode using the zero-solve fraction, defined as the fraction of prompts whose rollout group has maximum verifier reward equal to 0." (Section 7.1) — cheap signal to add to our GRPO training dashboards to catch schema/tool-call prompts the model can't yet solve at all.
- Detectability threshold as a way to reason about rollout budget vs. required per-prompt success probability: "$\varepsilon_{k,\zeta}:=1-\zeta^{1/k}\approx\frac{-\log\zeta}{k}$" — "If $p_\theta(a^\star\mid x)<\varepsilon_{k,\zeta}$, then the correct answer is not reliably observed under the available rollout budget" (Section 4.3) — a formula for estimating what rollout count N is needed before a given tool-schema difficulty is even learnable via plain GRPO.
- No separate format/schema reward was needed on top of the correctness verifier: "Unless otherwise stated, we do not add a separate format reward." (Section 4.1) — worth testing whether a single strict correctness verifier (parses AND matches) suffices for our schema-validity reward rather than a compound reward.

## Baselines & datasets used

- STaR-CTI (iterative bootstrap SFT on verifier-correct traces), DART-CTI (rejection-finetuning on a fixed verifier-filtered corpus), and LUFFY-CTI (off-policy RLVR with one guidance trace per prompt) — all built on the same Minerva-CTI data/verifiers, giving a controlled comparison of SFT vs. rejection-sampling vs. off-policy RL vs. MinervaRL. ("STaR-CTI, which iteratively bootstraps verifier-correct traces and retrains with SFT... DART-CTI, a rejection-finetuning baseline with a fixed verifier-filtered trace corpus... LUFFY-CTI, an off-policy RLVR baseline using one accepted guidance trace per prompt", Section 5.2)
- Backbones: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base — all larger than our 1.2B target; Llama-3.2-3B is the closest scale tested and shows the largest relative GRPO gain (33.1→42.1) and largest MinervaRL-over-GRPO gain (+6.2 pts), suggesting the technique may matter more, not less, at smaller scale.
- Transfer check on text-to-SQL using Qwen2.5-Coder-3B and the SQL-R1 setup: "MinervaRL obtains the best average score (60.8) and leads on four of seven datasets" (Section 7.2) — a structured-output domain closer to our own (SQL dialect is explicitly in our standing constraints).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Reward sparsity is real and budget alone doesn't fix it: "increasing the GRPO rollout budget from N=8 to N=12 also reduces zero-solve frequency, but does not close the gap to MinervaRL" (Section 7.1) — for our GRPO rung, simply sampling more completions per prompt is not a substitute for a sparsity-mitigation mechanism.
- Plain answer-only SFT overfits/doesn't generalize: "Direct answer-only SFT improves Minerva-Dev but performs much worse on AthenaBench-Mini, suggesting that final-answer imitation is less robust than verifier-guided RL augmented with trace distillation." (Section 7.3) — a caution for our SFT rungs earlier in the ladder if evaluated only on in-distribution held-out data.
- MinervaRL adds real training-time cost: "a 39.3% increase in a 10-step Llama-3.1-8B timing study" (Section 8, referencing Appendix I.4) — the ACR generation, EMA teacher forward passes, and periodic distillation are not free; budget for this if adopting the technique.
- Ablations show every component matters: "removing the EMA teacher, removing all filtering, or removing only the ML filter each reduces the average score" (Section 7.3) — the quality-filtering step (heuristic + learned TextCNN filter) is load-bearing, not incidental; a naive "always distill whatever the teacher generates" version underperforms.
- Distillation learning-rate scale is sensitive and non-monotonic: best at γ=0.05, with both γ=0.01 (under-uses traces) and γ=0.10 (degrades both validation splits) worse (Table 4b) — this hyperparameter needs its own small sweep, not a one-shot guess.
- Gains are backbone-dependent and sometimes small: MinervaRL over GRPO is only +0.4 pts average for Qwen3-4B despite +4.2 to +6.4 for the other three backbones — the method is not guaranteed to help uniformly across model families/sizes.

## Metric / eval choices

- Verifier reward is task-typed rather than a single scheme: "instantiated as exact identifier matching for single-label outputs, structured partial credit for hierarchical labels such as ATT&CK techniques and sub-techniques, or set-based overlap for multi-label targets" (Section 4.1) — a template for designing our own graded (not just binary) schema/tool-call correctness reward.
- Held-out general-capability check alongside the domain RL: on Llama-3.1-8B, MMLU-Pro moved 44.1 (base) → 45.6 (GRPO) → 43.6 (MinervaRL), and IFEval moved 72.3 → 72.3 → 73.8 (Table 3a) — i.e., domain RLVR training left IFEval unchanged-to-improved and MMLU-Pro within ~2 points, which is the same kind of guardrail check (though different benchmarks) our project's ≤2.0-point guardrail criterion demands; worth noting the paper only checked this at 8B, not at anything near 1.2B.
- LLM-judge (GPT-5.2) used only for a secondary response-quality preference evaluation, validated against two human annotators and a second LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 4.6): "GPT-5.2 agrees substantially with the human annotator average (QWK 0.6313, Spearman 0.7722), close to human–human agreement (QWK 0.6514, Spearman 0.6461)" (Section 6.2) — notably the judge was never used as a *training* reward, only for offline eval, consistent with our project's constraint against reusing model-scored reward as a training signal.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's ACR mechanism reveals the gold label to an EMA teacher for rationale generation; our target model is asked to emit structured output directly (tool calls / flags), often with limited or no chain-of-thought budget at 1.2B — does the "generate rationale, then distill the final answer" pattern still make sense when the deployed format has little room for rationale, or would we need to distill straight to the structured output only?
- All empirical results are at 3B–8B; the paper gives no signal on whether hardness-gated distillation still helps (or is even necessary) at 1.2B, where base capability and rollout diversity may be much lower — this is a real scale-transfer gap for us to test rather than assume.
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader/agent; it is a standard research paper describing methods and results only.
