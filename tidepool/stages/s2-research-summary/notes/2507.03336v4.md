# 2507.03336v4 — Disambiguation-Centric Finetuning Makes Enterprise Tool-Calling LLMs More Realistic and Less Risky

**Relevance:** medium
Useful SFT-recipe and eval-methodology lessons for tool-calling models (loss masking, reasoning-trace ablations, BFCL-based forgetting checks), but its core problem — disambiguating near-duplicate tools and eliciting missing user-side arguments at 3B–70B scale — is a different failure mode from this project's schema-validity/malformed-tool-return-flagging target at 1.2B, and it never touches quantization.

## TL;DR

DiaFORGE is a three-stage pipeline (synthetic multi-turn dialogue generation with injected near-duplicate "distractor" tools → LoRA SFT with reasoning traces → dynamic agentic evaluation) that teaches Llama/Gemma/Nemotron models (3B–70B) to ask clarifying questions before calling a tool, instead of guessing among similar APIs or asserting missing arguments. On their proprietary enterprise benchmark DiaBENCH it beats GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet by large margins, and a BFCL v3 parity check shows the disambiguation-focused fine-tuning doesn't degrade general function-calling ability.

## Techniques to try

- Mask loss on everything except the final assistant turn when turn-slicing multi-turn dialogues for SFT, rather than training on the full unmasked history: "the overwhelming volume of unmasked system & user tokens skews the training signal and noticeably degrades assistant performance at inference" (Appendix B) — directly contradicts a single-turn-only finding they cite from Shi et al. 2024, so worth confirming on our own multi-turn tool-call transcripts.
- Keep chain-of-thought/reasoning traces as part of the SFT target for tool-calling turns, not just the structured output: the "w/o Thinking Traces" ablation shows dynamic Acc dropping from 0.89 to 0.77 and FTR rising from 0.03 to 0.16 on Gemma-3-DiaFORGE-27B when reasoning traces are stripped from training and inference (Table 2, §4.1).
- Use a same-benchmark base-vs-tuned "parity check" (not just an absolute score) to certify no catastrophic forgetting from specialization: they re-ran BFCL v3 on Gemma-3-27B vs. Gemma-3-DiaFORGE-27B under both FC and prompt modes and reported near-identical scores (e.g., Overall Acc 59.26% vs 59.20% FC) plus a 0.50 MT-Bench pairwise win rate as their forgetting/overfitting check (Appendix C.5) — a template directly reusable for our own guardrail-metric (IFEval/MMLU-Pro/GPQA) comparisons against a matched base rerun.

## Baselines & datasets used

- BFCL v3 (Yan et al., 2024) used as the general/static function-calling parity benchmark, run in both native-FC and prompt-optimized modes.
- MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023) used as a pairwise LLM-judged general-capability regression check.
- DiaBENCH, their own dynamic evaluation benchmark (119 seed tools, proprietary/production-derived, not reproducible outside SAP) — not usable directly, but construction guidelines are given in Appendix A.5 for building an analogous benchmark.
- They release an open corpus of "$\sim$5,000 production-grade enterprise API specifications paired with rigorously validated, disambiguation-focused dialogues" under CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 (non-commercial share-alike), which limits direct reuse in a shipped product.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Dropping the validation cascade admits schema-invalid or tool-absent turns into training, regressing performance and inflating TAR" — removing their rule-based + LLM validator gate dropped dynamic Acc from 0.89 to 0.56 and pushed abstention (TAR) from 0.03 to 0.35 on Gemma-3-27B (Table 2) — a strong quantified argument for validating synthetic SFT data before training, at 27B scale.
- "Our post-training setup assumes the ground-truth tool is present in the retrieved candidate set: an assumption that does not always hold in production. Future work will incorporate hard negatives and explicit 'no-tool' dialogues" (Limitations) — they explicitly flag that they have not trained the model to recognize "none of these tools apply" or bad/absent options, which is the same gap this project needs to close for malformed-tool-return flagging; this paper doesn't solve it.
- Model scale is not monotonic with tool-calling quality in their results: "the compact Llama-3.2-DiaFORGE-3B and the mid-sized Llama-3.3-Nemotron-DiaFORGE-49B both surpass the much larger Llama-3.3-DiaFORGE-70B" (§C.2) — a caution against assuming bigger backbone automatically wins on this kind of task.
- Static, fixed-script evaluation "cannot capture how the assistant's outputs would influence subsequent user behavior in an interactive setting" and is called out as having "comparatively limited diagnostic value" versus their dynamic protocol — relevant if we ever build an agentic/interactive eval loop rather than relying purely on static BFCL-style scoring.

## Metric / eval choices

- Their headline triplet is Accuracy Rate (Acc, first tool call matches gold tool + exact required-argument set), False-Positive Tool-call Rate (FTR, wrong/hallucinated/extra tool calls), and Tool-call Abstention Rate (TAR, no tool call issued when one was needed) — reported together because "FTR and TAR directly quantify failures in tool disambiguation" and neither is meaningful alone, structurally similar to this project's paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate design.
- They report bootstrap 95% CIs (N=2000 resamples, percentile method) over their N=119 test-case benchmark, explicitly noting "the CI width reflects the small test-set size common to proprietary enterprise benchmarks" (Table 7) — directly applicable practice for our own ~300-example malformed-tool-return probe, which is a similarly small held-out set.
- Their dynamic evaluation uses an LLM user-proxy with a multi-sample-and-vote scheme (n=3 candidates, m=3 voters, mode pooling) to suppress user-proxy hallucination, "we observe a user-proxy hallucination rate below 1% across all samples" after mitigation (§4, Appendix C.3) — a concrete recipe if we ever need a simulated multi-turn agentic evaluator rather than static prompts.
- Their conversational-quality metric (ConvRel) is itself LLM-judged and they caution "its values should be interpreted as heuristic estimates rather than precise measurements" — worth remembering given this project's constraint that no model-scored stored reward may be reused as a training signal; the same caution about LLM-judge metrics as *training* signals (as opposed to reporting) applies.

## Open questions for our project

- Nothing in this paper addresses quantization, on-device deployment, or throughput; it offers no evidence either way on whether 4-bit quantization would damage the disambiguation/reasoning-trace behaviors it trains.
- It targets ambiguity in the *user's request* (which of several near-duplicate tools, which missing argument) rather than garbage/malformed *tool return values* — the project's "flag rather than assert" problem is a different direction of uncertainty (post-call, not pre-call) and isn't studied here; we'd need to look elsewhere for that.
- All reported models are 3B–70B; nothing here speaks to whether these techniques (loss masking, reasoning traces, LoRA r=16) hold up at 1.2B, or interact with a 65K-vocabulary tokenizer.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a normal research report throughout.
