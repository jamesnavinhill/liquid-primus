# 2605.03677v1 — Uni-OPD: Unifying On-Policy Distillation with a Dual-Perspective Recipe

**Relevance:** medium
On-policy distillation recipe details (data balancing, teacher-signal calibration) and a general-ability-retention result are useful context for the GRPO/distillation rungs of the ladder, but the paper never touches tool calling, structured output, quantization, or GGUF, and its teacher models require domain-specific RL training on 16 GPUs — nothing here settles a decision for this project outright.

## TL;DR

Uni-OPD is a recipe for on-policy distillation (student samples its own trajectories, a teacher scores them, reverse-KL loss) that fixes two failure modes: the student not exploring enough of the difficulty spectrum, and the teacher's token-level scores not tracking whether a trajectory was actually correct. It's evaluated on math reasoning, code generation, and multimodal tasks with Qwen3 students (1.7B–4B) and RL-trained domain-expert teachers, not on tool-calling or schema-validity tasks.

## Techniques to try

- Offline difficulty-aware data balancing: instead of filtering out always-easy/always-hard prompts (standard RL practice), upsample "mid-difficulty" prompts (correct on only some of several rollouts) to preserve diversity — the paper reports filtering "leads to substantial performance degradation in OPD" ("Our empirical findings show that such filtering leads to substantial performance degradation in OPD." — §3.3). Relevant if constructing malformed-tool-return / schema training data by rollout difficulty rather than hard-filtering.
- Online correctness-aware balancing: enforce a fixed correct/incorrect ratio within each rollout group during training rather than letting groups collapse to all-same-outcome ("we explicitly enforce a balanced composition of correct and incorrect trajectories within each rollout group during training." — §3.3). Directly applicable to GRPO training with schema-validity/execution-correctness rewards, where all-pass or all-fail rollout groups give zero-variance gradients.
- Outcome-guided margin calibration: use the binary outcome reward as a check on trajectory-level teacher-derived returns; if the worst "correct" trajectory doesn't outscore the best "incorrect" one by a margin δ, either mask the group ("margin mask") or additively shift correct-trajectory scores up ("margin shift") ("Margin shift instead repairs an unreliable group with the smallest additive correction." — §3.4). This is a distillation-specific fix (needs a teacher's log-probs), not directly usable for GRPO without a teacher, but the underlying idea — sanity-check a soft/aggregated signal against the hard outcome label before trusting it — is a transferable pattern for the project's "flag rather than assert" reward design.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Students: Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-1.7B (text), Qwen3-VL-2B/4B-Instruct (multimodal) — the 1.7B student result is the only one at a scale close to this project's 1.2B target.
- Teachers: domain-specific RL-tuned Qwen3-4B / Qwen3-VL-4B (same-size teachers via GRPO), and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 for strong-to-weak distillation.
- Training data: 57K DeepMath samples (difficulty ≥6) for math, 25.3K Eurus-2-RL-Data Code subset, ~15K-sample slices of OpenMMReasoner-RL-74K for multimodal math/logic/document tasks.
- Rewards are rule-based verifiers (DeepMath verifier for math, PRIME verifier for code, OpenMMReasoner verifier for multimodal), not model-scored — consistent with this project's "no model-scored stored reward" constraint, though the domain (answer correctness) differs from schema validity.
- Baselines compared: vanilla OPD, SFT, ExPO (weight-space extrapolation), ExOPD (reward-scaled extrapolation).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Naive RL-style filtering of always-easy/always-hard prompts "can substantially reduce data diversity and limit exploration of informative student-generated states" and measurably hurt OPD performance for small models, whose training data show "mirrored J-shaped or U-shaped difficulty distributions" (§3.3) — a caution against over-aggressive dataset curation for a small (1.2B) student.
- Named teacher-supervision failure modes worth designing around even outside distillation: "(a) OOD degradation: when student rollouts enter sparse or out-of-distribution regions relative to the teacher... may become noisy... (b) Overestimation of incorrect trajectories... (c) Underestimation of correct trajectories" (§3.1) — i.e., a soft/model-derived score can silently disagree with the ground-truth outcome, especially off-distribution; this is a generic argument for always checking a learned or aggregated signal against a hard verifier, relevant to reward-hacking concerns for schema-validity rewards.
- The method's infrastructure is heavy: teacher models are RL-trained separately (GRPO, 16× NVIDIA H20 GPUs per teacher) and served as stateless SGLang endpoints during distillation — not something transferable to this project's simpler ladder without a large compute budget; the paper itself notes only the marginal Uni-OPD overhead is cheap ("enabling all three components adds less than 1% wall-clock overhead per iteration relative to vanilla OPD"), not that OPD as a whole is cheap.

## Metric / eval choices

- General-capability retention check: evaluates the distilled model on 8 non-target benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8K, CommonsenseQA, IFEval) via lm-evaluation-harness, reporting IFEval as `inst_level_loose_acc`. Result: after specializing a Qwen3-4B student on math+code via multi-teacher OPD, the 8-benchmark average moved from 73.5 (base) to 73.9 (Uni-OPD/OPD), with IFEval essentially flat (88.9 → 89.2) — a comparable-shape result (different model, different narrow domain) for this project's own guardrail-metric-must-stay-within-2.0-points requirement, suggesting on-policy distillation-style training need not erode instruction-following even while a narrow skill is being pushed up substantially.
- Uses contamination-conscious/contest benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, HMMT, LiveCodeBench v6) for the target skill — good practice reference, though not applicable to a BFCL/tool-calling target metric.

## Open questions for our project

- Nothing in the paper addresses tool-call schema validity, malformed-tool-return flagging, JSON Schema/MCP formats, or GGUF/4-bit quantization — none of the project's core claims (BFCLv3, schema validity, flag-rate/false-flag-rate, 4-bit retention, throughput) are informed by this paper's numbers.
- The general-ability-retention result (IFEval 88.9→89.2, MMLU flat) is at 4B, on math/code, with a full OPD teacher pipeline — it is suggestive but not evidence at the project's 1.2B scale or for structured-output tasks; would need to be validated directly rather than assumed to transfer.
- The margin-calibration idea requires a teacher's per-token log-probabilities, which presupposes a distillation setup with an available teacher — not applicable to the GRPO-only step of the project's ladder unless a stronger teacher model is introduced.
- No prompt-injection or instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in this paper.
