# 2607.10738v1 — To Answer or to Abstain: Mitigating Search-Agent Hallucinations via Abstention-Aware Reinforcement Learning

**Relevance:** medium
Not about tool-schema validity, quantization, or GGUF, but its GRPO reward-shaping method for teaching a policy to abstain rather than fabricate — and its documented reward-hacking failure mode — directly informs the "flag rather than assert" and GRPO-reward-design questions this project is trying to settle.

## TL;DR

The paper trains a 7B search agent with GRPO to abstain ("not enough information") instead of hallucinating when retrieval fails, using a refusal reward that is dynamically calibrated per-query to the model's own estimated prior success probability rather than a fixed bonus. Naive approaches (SFT on refusal examples, mixing in unanswerable queries, or a flat static refusal reward) either barely move the refusal rate or collapse catastrophically into always-refusing.

## Techniques to try

- Query-specific, capability-calibrated refusal reward instead of a flat bonus: estimate each query's prior correctness probability `pi` from a pre-trained checkpoint, clip it, and set the base refusal reward to `1 - p*_i` (with a "courage" exponent γ shrinking the effective confidence) so easy queries are discouraged from abstaining and hard queries are not punished for it ("Rbase(qi) establishes a refusal boundary based on the model's estimated capability" — Section 2.3). This is directly analogous to shaping a "flag" reward per malformed-tool-return case by estimated difficulty rather than a uniform bonus.
- Batch-level dynamic penalty term that continuously corrects the refusal reward during RL based on the gap between the empirical and expected refusal rate, self-stabilizing against drift ("Et acts as a dynamic stabilizer to correct empirical over-or under-refusal during the RL rollout" — Section 2.3).
- EMA smoothing (α=0.5) plus step-wise quantization of the excess-refusal signal before it feeds the reward, to stop noisy per-batch fluctuations from producing a jittery, unstable reward landscape — a concrete recipe for stabilizing any GRPO run with a dynamically-adjusted auxiliary reward ("the dynamic penalty is calculated as P(Ẽt) rather than P(Et)... providing a much more stable optimization target for the GRPO algorithm" — Appendix B.1).
- Filtering training queries by rollout difficulty before RL: drop queries the model gets right in every sampled trajectory or wrong in every trajectory, since they contribute zero relative advantage to GRPO ("Such queries provide zero relative advantage and contribute little to policy learning" — Appendix C.1). Directly reusable for constructing the GRPO training set for the schema/execution-correctness reward stage.
- Removing the KL penalty entirely and instead enforcing a policy-entropy floor (threshold 0.4, per Skywork-OR1) to keep exploration alive during RL — an alternative to KL regularization worth considering if GRPO training on schema rewards collapses entropy too fast ("we completely remove the standard KL divergence penalty in GRPO... strictly keeping the policy entropy above a threshold of 0.4" — Appendix C.2).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base model: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (above this project's 1.2B target scale — treat magnitudes as directional, not transferable as-is).
- Cold-start SFT on 5,000 search trajectories distilled from GPT-oss-120B, then GRPO RL on 5,000 filtered queries from 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue training splits; evaluated on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue (1,000 sampled queries each).
- Baselines compared: SFT-Mixed (10-30% injected refusal trajectories), RL-Unanswerable (10-30% unanswerable queries mixed into RL), Static Reward (fixed rref of 0.05-0.9).
- Hardware: 2 nodes × 8×H100 GPUs — well beyond this project's single-GPU managed-hardware setting; the training recipe is directionally useful but not resource-matched.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Static refusal rewards are dangerously unstable: "even a marginal rref = 0.05 causes catastrophic reward hacking (99.9% refusal)" in from-scratch RL — a flat "reward the flag" signal can make the policy refuse/flag on everything. Directly relevant to the risk that a naive schema-validity or malformed-input reward in this project's GRPO stage could collapse into always-flagging.
- SFT alone is weak at instilling abstention: "SFT alone struggles to internalize abstention boundaries (refusal rates stagnate around 10-13%)" even with 10-30% refusal trajectories injected — evidence against relying on SFT alone for "flag rather than assert" behavior; some form of RL/verifiable-reward shaping appears necessary in this paper's setting.
- Naively mixing unanswerable queries into RL training produces unpredictable, non-monotonic effects on refusal rate and can trigger a "lazy refusal" collapse (52.2% refusal from scratch at 30% ratio) or leave the cold-start model stubbornly overconfident (near-zero refusal) depending on init — data-ratio tuning alone is not a reliable lever.
- Any gain in precision/reliability trades off against raw accuracy/recall in every method tested, including AWA-RL itself (e.g., cold-start RA-F1 optimum of 0.585 came with accuracy dropping from 0.552 to 0.529) — a reminder that the project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair should expect a similar capability-reliability tension, not a free lunch.
- The method depends on an LLM-as-judge (GPT-oss-120B) for both outcome correctness and a dense citation-quality reward feeding the GRPO advantage. This is exactly the kind of model-scored reward this project's standing constraints prohibit reusing as a training signal — the paper's core reward-shaping technique is portable, but its dense auxiliary "citation reward" machinery is not, without a non-model-scored substitute.

## Metric / eval choices

- Introduces RA-F1 (harmonic mean of Precision and Accuracy/Recall) as a single number capturing the capability-reliability trade-off: "RA-F1 = 2·Prec·Acc/(Prec+Acc)" (Section 3) — Precision here means correctness given the model chose to answer (Nc/(Nc+Nw)), Accuracy means overall correct/total. This maps reasonably well onto this project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pairing as a candidate way to combine them into a single scalar for tracking, though the project's own paired-metric definition (flag rate ≥0.80, false-flag ≤0.10) is stricter and asymmetric rather than symmetric like RA-F1.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the capability-calibrated reward-shaping idea (reward abstention proportional to estimated per-example difficulty, not a flat bonus) transfer to a 1.2B model and to malformed-tool-return detection rather than open-domain retrieval failure? The paper's own "Limitations" section flags this as unresolved even for their own domain: "scaling the validation to larger foundational models and a wider spectrum of task modalities remains an important next step" (Limitations).
- The paper's difficulty-estimation step requires "a preliminary inference pass over the training set to calculate the prior success rate" (Limitations) — is this affordable within this project's queued-job constraints at 1.2B scale, and is a similar prior estimation feasible for the ~300-example malformed-tool-return probe?
- Given the standing "no model-scored stored reward" constraint, what would replace this paper's LLM-judge correctness/citation rewards with a verifiable, non-model-scored analogue for the flag/schema-validity task in this project's GRPO stage?
