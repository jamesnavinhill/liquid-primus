# 2311.06233v7 — Data Contamination Quiz: A Tool to Detect and Estimate Contamination in Large Language Models

**Relevance:** skip
This paper is about detecting/estimating verbatim benchmark contamination in black-box LLMs via a multiple-choice quiz; it offers no technique, metric, or pitfall relevant to post-training LFM2.5-1.2B for tool-calling/structured-output reliability or to 4-bit GGUF quantization.

## TL;DR

The authors propose DCQ (Data Contamination Quiz), a black-box method that asks an LLM to pick the verbatim original of a dataset instance among GPT-4-generated word-level paraphrases, using bias-detection and bias-compensation quiz variants to estimate contamination levels without access to training data or model weights. It's an evaluation-integrity tool for benchmark trustworthiness, not a training or inference technique.

## Techniques to try

- (None. The quiz-construction method (BDQ/BCQ, non-preferred-option placement, permutation to bound over/underestimation) is designed for auditing whether a *benchmark dataset* leaked into a model's pretraining corpus — it has no application to instilling tool-calling behavior, schema validity, or quantization robustness in this project.)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Datasets used for contamination probing: AG News, WNLI, MeetingBank, AuTexTification, HumanEval, DROP, GSM8k, IMDB, Yelp Full Reviews, SAMSum, XSum, RTE — none of these are tool-calling/agentic-turn benchmarks (no BFCL, IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, or malformed-tool-return data appears anywhere in the paper).
- Models tested: GPT-3.5 (`gpt-3.5-turbo-0613`), GPT-4 (`gpt-4-0613`), Llama 2-Chat 70B — all far larger than the project's 1.2B target, and none quantized.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- The paper notes the replication-based baseline method (Golchin and Surdeanu 2024) "is ineffective for detecting contamination in short- or non-generative tasks such as classification and summarization" and "incorrectly flags nearly all samples as contaminated" when output diversity is low — a methodological caution specific to contamination-detection research, not to this project.
- Not applicable to sub-8B or quantized models: all reported numbers are for 70B+/proprietary models with full-precision API access.

## Metric / eval choices

- Contamination is reported as a min/max range per dataset partition, using Cohen's Kappa for chance-adjusted "minimum" contamination and highest BCQ accuracy for "maximum" contamination — a metric machinery specific to contamination estimation, with no analog among this project's BFCLv3/IFEval/schema-validity/flag-rate targets.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper does raise a generally relevant caution (in its Limitations section) that private/held-out evaluations are "neither a definitive nor a long-term solution" against contamination, citing the ARC-AGI-1 vs ARC-AGI-2 performance gap as evidence contamination can inflate benchmark scores over time. This is worth remembering only as a generic reminder that the project's own BFCLv3/IFEval/matched-base-rerun numbers assume clean, uncontaminated evaluation sets — the paper itself provides no method applicable at 1.2B scale or to tool-calling data to verify this.
- No instructions-to-the-reader text or other prompt-injection-like content was found in the paper body.
