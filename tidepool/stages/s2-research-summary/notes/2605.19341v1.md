# 2605.19341v1 — HalluWorld: A Controlled Benchmark for Hallucination via Reference World Models

**Relevance:** medium
Not a training method or a number we can adopt directly (all evaluated models are frontier/open-weight LLMs, none near 1.2B, and the domains are gridworld/chess/terminal-QA, not tool-schema output), but its central finding — that abstention under uncertainty is hard and is *not* reliably fixed by more reasoning — bears directly on our "flag rather than assert" sub-criterion.

## TL;DR

HalluWorld is a benchmark suite that defines hallucination as an observable claim contradicting an explicit, programmatically-known "reference world" (state, history, rules), and tests models across gridworld, chess, and real terminal-agent trajectories using five probe categories: Perceptual, Memory, Causal, Uncertainty, Compound. Its main result is that perception is nearly solved by frontier models but multi-step memory/causal reasoning and — most relevant to us — knowing when to abstain are not, and extended thinking often does not help abstention and can even hurt causal hallucination.

## Techniques to try

- Rule-based, no-LLM-judge scoring for structured probe answers, which matches our own constraint against model-scored reward: "Scoring is fully rule-based and no LLM judge is used." (§3.3, HalluWorld-Terminal evaluation protocol) — a template worth mirroring for our own malformed-tool-return flag/no-flag probe design.
- The paper's explicit separation of "view function" (what the model can observe) from "conflict policy" (how it should resolve contradictions) is a useful conceptual frame for designing our malformed-tool-return probes: define exactly what the model can and can't verify before scoring it as over- or under-confident.

## Baselines & datasets used

- HalluWorld-Terminal probes are derived from real agent trajectories: "We derive 529 probes from 132 Terminal-Bench [46] trajectories spanning 110 software-engineering tasks (e.g., kernel builds, ML framework configuration, etc.), executed by a gpt-4o-mini agent (to elicit diverse error modes instead of optimized solutions from stronger models)" (§3.3) — a candidate source of realistic degraded/garbage tool-output-like contexts, though built for QA-style probes, not tool-call schema validity.
- HalluWorld-Chess draws from curated Lichess FEN positions with randomized legal continuations and an "incorrect FEN" (transposed-piece) condition to test robustness to corrupted auxiliary metadata — a useful adversarial-input pattern (a base fact that a tool-like source contradicts) analogous to a malformed/garbage tool return.
- All evaluated models are large: GPT-4o(-mini), GPT-5.4/5.5(-mini), o3/o4-mini/o3-mini, Claude Sonnet/Opus 4.6, DeepSeek-V3.1, GLM-5, Qwen-3-30B, Kimi K2.6 — none at or below our 1.2B scale, so absolute numbers don't transfer, only qualitative patterns.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Extended thinking does not reliably improve the behavior we most care about (correctly flagging vs. asserting): "Extended thinking slightly improves perceptual accuracy ... but *worsens* causal hallucination" and "reasoning interferes specifically with epistemic abstention rather than with factual retrieval or causal inference" (§5.3, §7). This is a warning against assuming more inference-time reasoning (or GRPO-induced longer chains) will automatically raise our flag rate without hurting the false-flag rate.
- Abstention/uncertainty is the single hardest category even for frontier models in the realistic terminal domain: "Uncertainty probes are the hardest: even GPT-5.5 and o3 cap at 76.9% accuracy, meaning the best models hallucinate ≈ 1/4 answers when the correct response is 'cannot determine'" (§7) — a sobering data point suggesting our target of flag rate ≥ 0.80 with false-flag rate ≤ 0.10 is an aggressive bar relative to what frontier models achieve on a structurally similar abstention task, even before considering our much smaller model.
- Models over-trust textual/testimony-style information over direct observation even when it's wrong: "Opus 4.6 hallucinates *more* with an *accurate* notice board present (42.0% vs. 28.4% without), suggesting that written rules (whether accurate or not) can derail implicit causal reasoning rather than support it" (§5.5) — relevant if our malformed-tool-return probes include any auxiliary text/metadata alongside the tool output, since models may over-index on it regardless of correctness.
- Serialization/format of the same underlying content is itself "an independent driver of hallucination" (§5.2) — implies our own tool-return / schema serialization format (JSON vs. flattened text, etc.) could materially change flag/false-flag rates independent of content, worth ablating rather than assuming format-invariance.

## Metric / eval choices

- Formal hallucination definition: reference world $\mathcal{W}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{R})$, a view function $V$ controlling observability, and a conflict-resolution policy $\mathcal{P}$, with ground truth computed automatically rather than human/model-annotated (§3, Table 1) — a clean way to argue our malformed-tool-return labels are automatically/deterministically generated rather than model-scored.
- Five cross-domain probe categories (Perceptual, Memory, Causal, Uncertainty, Compound) used to decompose a single aggregate hallucination number into distinct failure modes, explicitly to avoid the trap of "overall hallucination rate conflates different failure modes" (§5.1) — analogous argument for why our single flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair should possibly be broken down by malformed-return type (truncated JSON, wrong type, semantically wrong-but-well-formed, etc.).
- Terminal probes are pre-filtered by two LLM-assigned scores, answerability and difficulty, keeping only "answerability_score = 5 and difficulty_score ≥ 4" (§3.3) — a filtering technique to keep a probe set hard and unambiguous, though note the filtering itself uses an LLM judge (gpt-5.4) at construction time, not at scoring time.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's central "reasoning helps perception but hurts abstention/causal reasoning" pattern is only shown on much larger frontier and mid-size open models (30B+ and closed frontier); it's unclear whether a 1.2B model trained via GRPO with a flag-vs-assert reward would show the same tradeoff, a different one, or none at all given its much smaller reasoning capacity — worth flagging as untested at our scale.
- No instructions addressed to the reader were found embedded in this paper's text; it reads as a standard benchmark paper.
