# 2605.09015v1 — LLiMba: Sardinian on a Single GPU — Adapting a 3B Language Model to a Vanishing Romance Language

**Relevance:** medium
Directly informs the "LoRA or full fine-tuning at 1.2B?" and adapter-rank questions with matched-condition evidence at a nearby model scale (3B), and shows a general pitfall (surface metrics hide qualitative divergence) relevant to schema-validity scoring, but the task itself is language adaptation/translation, not tool-calling or quantization.

## TL;DR

The paper adapts Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct to Sardinian via continued pretraining (CPT, full fine-tune) followed by SFT, comparing full fine-tuning against LoRA r64, rsLoRA r128, rsLoRA r256, and DoRA r256 under matched data/hardware/hyperparameters. rsLoRA r256 wins on translation metrics and is the only SFT variant whose qualitative factual outputs consistently verify, while DoRA r256 has higher rank than LoRA r64 yet underperforms it and produces the most confident fabrications of any variant tested.

## Techniques to try

- At the SFT stage (after a domain/language shift has already been absorbed by an earlier full-fine-tune CPT stage), a higher-rank rank-stabilized LoRA can beat both full fine-tuning and a low-rank LoRA: "all three of our LoRA adapters outperform full fine-tuning on every into-Sardinian translation direction" — Section 6.1. Worth testing whether an SFT-after-something-else adapter regime at 1.2B shows the same pattern for tool-calling.
- Use rsLoRA's alpha/√r scaling rather than conventional alpha/r scaling when going to higher adapter rank, since conventional scaling "causes gradient norms to collapse as rank grows" — Section 6.1 (citing Kalajdzievski 2023). Relevant if the candidate ladder's "higher-rank or full SFT" step uses LoRA rather than full fine-tuning.
- Reserve full fine-tuning for the stage with the largest distribution shift and use adapters only for later, smaller-shift stages: "We use full fine-tuning for this stage rather than a parameter efficient adapter based on Biderman et al. (2024) [2], who report that LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning when the target domain is far from the pretraining distribution" — Section 4.1.
- Do not trust loss/perplexity-only model selection for a specialization project: "training loss tracks BLEU but not factuality" and the paper recommends "evaluation beyond loss curves" (native/task-specific probes) — Section 6.3. Directly analogous to checking BFCL/schema-validity gains don't mask degraded tool-result-flagging behavior.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base/comparison model: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (not the target model family, but same general size class as the project's 1.2B target).
- FLORES-200 (997 parallel sentences, 6 translation directions) scored with BLEU and chrF via lm-evaluation-harness — not applicable to this project's task but the harness itself (lm-evaluation-harness) is a possible shared tool for standardized eval.
- SFT data built from Capybara (machine-translated), translation pairs, Claude-assisted synthesized instructions (native-reviewed), and song-related pairs — an example of a small (~14K pairs, ~12.8M token) mixed-quality SFT pool with heavy native-speaker review for a subset upsampled 5x.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- DoRA at 4x the rank of a plain LoRA baseline still underperformed it on most translation directions and produced the worst factual grounding of any SFT method tested: "DoRA r256 trails LoRA r64 on four of six directions" and "DoRA r256 is even worse [than LoRA r64]: it places his birth in Casteddu (Cagliari) in 1947 ... reports 308 goals in 526 matches, and concludes that the Cagliari stadium was renamed 'Stadio Giulini' in his honour. No such stadium exists" — Section 5.2. A caution against assuming DoRA's directional-preservation premise helps factuality/reliability.
- An intermediate-rank rsLoRA (r128) beat a lower-rank LoRA (r64) on every translation metric while introducing a categorically new failure — cross-script token leakage — that no other configuration produced: "Two of the eleven probes contain non-Latin script tokens embedded in Sardinian sentences ... No other model run produces script leakage of this kind" — Section 5.2. Shows metric-based model selection can pick a worse model.
- Higher-rank/higher-capacity adapters (rsLoRA r256, DoRA r256) produced "morphological hallucination" — fluent, phonotactically-plausible but non-attested output tokens — concentrated on long, open-ended generation prompts, distinct from factual fabrication: "the model has learned Sardinian phonotactics well enough to compose new Sardinian-shaped words" — Section 5.2. Mitigation reported: "Deployments that wrap broadly framed user queries in structured response templates can therefore mitigate the issue at inference time" — Section 7.3.
- Loss-based / perplexity metrics can be badly distorted by tokenization/script effects, illustrated via a cited Tibetan paper reporting perplexity 1.54 with near-zero BLEU (0.261) on translation: "Predicting structurally determined bytes deflates loss without measuring linguistic competence" — Section 6.3. Not directly applicable (project uses Latin-script JSON/tool-call tokens) but a reminder to sanity-check any loss/perplexity-based proxy metric against task-level outcomes.
- Training artifacts reduce confidence in some comparisons: "the LoRA r64 training metrics were lost, and the held out eval loss for the rsLoRA and DoRA runs was recovered from the latest checkpoint rather than from a true evaluation at the final step" — Section 7.2.

## Metric / eval choices

- Standard MT metrics (BLEU, chrF) plus a small hand-curated qualitative probe set (11 prompts) reviewed by a single native speaker, explicitly to catch failures the automatic metrics cannot: "The probes complement the BLEU and chrF figures and surface failure modes that translation metrics cannot capture" — Section 3.3. Analogous in spirit to the project's own combination of BFCL/IFEval-style automatic metrics plus a held-out malformed-tool-return probe.
- Authors explicitly flag their own qualitative eval as weak evidence: "reviewed by a single native speaker (the author himself)... A larger panel of speakers ... would provide stronger evaluation signal" — Section 7.2.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's central lesson — adapter method/rank can diverge sharply on task-relevant qualitative behavior (factual grounding, leakage) even when standard metrics (BLEU, loss) rank them smoothly or oppositely — raises the question of whether BFCL/IFEval score gains in this project could similarly mask degraded "flag rather than assert" behavior on malformed tool returns. Worth designing an analogous small qualitative probe set for that specific behavior, not just relying on the 300-example held-out probe's aggregate flag-rate/false-flag-rate numbers.
- All adapter comparisons here are at 3B and for a single base model/language; the paper itself flags that findings on DoRA underperformance and rsLoRA r256 dominance "may not generalize to other base models" — Section 7.1. Given the project's target is 1.2B (LFM2.5, a different architecture family with hybrid conv/attention blocks), this paper's specific rank/method rankings should be treated as a prior to test, not a settled conclusion.
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction addressed to an AI assistant; it is a standard research paper describing methods and results.
