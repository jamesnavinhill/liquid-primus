# 2601.14888v1 — What Makes Low-Bit Quantization-Aware Training Work for Reasoning LLMs? A Systematic Study

**Relevance:** high
Directly targets the project's last-resort rung (quantization-aware distillation/training) with a concrete PTQ→KD→RL recipe validated on reasoning models as small as 0.6B, close to our 1.2B target and quantization goal.

## TL;DR

The paper systematically studies why quantization-aware training (QAT) works for reasoning LLMs at extreme low-bit (3-bit/2-bit weight-only), and lands on a recipe called Reasoning-QAT: initialize from a PTQ checkpoint, recover accuracy with knowledge distillation (KD) rather than plain SFT, then optionally continue with RL from that KD checkpoint as a cold start. This is directly relevant to our project's plan to fall back to QAT if quantization-aware distillation alone doesn't hold the 4-bit GGUF quality bar.

## Techniques to try

- Use PTQ as the initialization point for any QAT/distillation pass rather than training from the bf16 checkpoint: ("PTQ provides a strong initialization that effectively saves the training cost and stabilizes the QAT training, especially in early stages." — §1, restated as finding in Abstract)
- Prefer knowledge distillation over supervised fine-tuning as the QAT training objective: ("Knowledge distillation is a robust objective for reasoning models trained via either supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning" — Abstract)
- If layering RL (e.g., our GRPO rung) onto a quantized model, use a KD-recovered checkpoint as the cold start rather than applying RL to the raw degraded quantized policy: ("Reinforcement learning remains feasible for quantized models given a viable cold start and yields additional gains" — Abstract)
- Align the QAT/distillation training data domain with whatever domain was used for PTQ calibration: ("Aligning the PTQ calibration domain with the QAT training domain accelerates convergence and often improves the final accuracy" — Abstract)
- Overall workflow order to mirror: ("Reasoning-QAT is structured in the following way: PTQ-based initialization $\rightarrow$ KD-based recovery $\rightarrow$ Cold-start RL" — §1)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (SFT-trained reasoner), Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-4B (RL-trained reasoners) — all ≤4B, in range for our 1.2B target.
- Quantization settings studied: 3-bit and 2-bit weight-only, group size 128 (appendix reportedly also covers weight-activation quantization, but that section was not present in the extracted text).
- Reasoning benchmarks: AIME-120, MATH-500, GSM8K, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench; non-reasoning benchmarks used for contrast: Winogrande, Hellaswag.
- PTQ baseline compared against: GPTQ.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Applying RL directly to a quantized policy without a recovery step fails: ("our preliminary attempts show that applying reinforcement learning (RL) directly on a severely degraded quantized policy often fails to explore valid reasoning trajectories under quantization noise" — §1). This is a direct warning for our GRPO rung if we ever apply it post-quantization instead of pre-quantization.
- Reasoning tasks degrade far more than non-reasoning/knowledge tasks under low-bit PTQ, on the same models: ("the performance degradation on reasoning tasks (e.g., 11.67%$\downarrow$ on AIME-120 and 12.80%$\downarrow$ on MATH-500) is much larger than that on non-reasoning tasks (e.g., 1.03%$\downarrow$ on Winogrande, 3.13%$\downarrow$ on Hellaswag)" — §2.1, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and Qwen3-4B, 3-bit GPTQ). This is suggestive evidence (not proof) for our open question of whether quantization hits structured-output/reasoning-like skills harder than general knowledge, though the paper measures math/code reasoning, not schema validity or tool-call formatting specifically.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports headline gains as absolute point deltas on specific benchmarks rather than averaged deltas, e.g.: ("on Qwen3-0.6B, it surpasses GPTQ by 44.53% on MATH-500 and consistently recovers performance in the 2-bit regime" — Abstract) and ("on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, it outperforms representative QAT baselines by up to 4.75% on average" — §1).

## Open questions for our project

- The extracted text of this paper cuts off partway through §2.1 (before §2.2's RQ2–RQ4 details, all of §3's experiments, §4's Reasoning-QAT workflow specifics, and the appendix). We do not have the actual QAT hyperparameters, RL algorithm details, data-mix composition, or the weight-activation and general-domain results tables that the abstract references — these would be needed before adopting the recipe wholesale.
- The paper studies 3-bit/2-bit *weight-only* quantization; our target is 4-bit GGUF. Unclear whether "PTQ init helps most in early stages" and "KD beats SFT" findings hold as strongly at 4-bit, where the PTQ starting point is already much closer to full precision.
- The paper's degradation evidence is about math/code reasoning vs. general knowledge tasks, not specifically about structured tool-call output or JSON schema validity — it's suggestive but not a direct answer to whether quantization disproportionately damages our first-attempt schema validity metric.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the available portion of the paper.
