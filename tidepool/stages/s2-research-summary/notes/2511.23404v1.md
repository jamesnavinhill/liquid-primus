# 2511.23404v1 — LFM2 Technical Report

**Relevance:** high
This is Liquid AI's own technical report on the LFM2 architecture family and post-training pipeline that the project's target model (`LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct`) descends from — the released LFM2-1.2B config matches the project's stated architecture exactly (hybrid gated-conv + GQA, 65,536 vocab, 2048 hidden), so it grounds architecture and recipe decisions even though every number in it is vendor-reported for the *predecessor* generation, not the exact base model this project fine-tunes.

## TL;DR

Liquid AI describes LFM2: a family of edge-first dense (350M–2.6B) and MoE (8.3B-A1B) models built on a hardware-in-the-loop-searched hybrid backbone (mostly gated short-convolution blocks plus a minority of GQA blocks), plus VL/Audio/ColBERT extensions. It documents the exact 1.2B config (16 layers, d_model=2048, 65,536-vocab BPE tokenizer) and a three-stage post-training pipeline (SFT → length-normalized direct preference alignment → parameter-space model merging) with concrete hyperparameters. All quality numbers come from an internal evaluation harness and are not third-party verified.

## Techniques to try

- Parameter-space model merging as a post-SFT/DPO step, not currently in the project's candidate ladder: "We use multiple parameter-space merging techniques to combine fine-tuned models without requiring additional training... The techniques we test (model soup, task arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE, and DELLA)" (Section 4.4). Appendix B gives runnable formulas for each — TIES (magnitude trim + sign election + disjoint merge) and DARE/DELLA (stochastic parameter dropout + rescale) are cheap to try if multiple SFT/DPO checkpoints are produced (e.g. different data mixes or seeds).
- Length-normalized, margin-based direct alignment objective that generalizes DPO/SimPO/APO-zero into one loss with tunable weights, used for their preference stage: "we recover length-normalized versions of DPO {ω=1, f(x)=log σ(x), m=0, λ=0, g(x)=0}... and APO-zero... as special cases. We can further construct joint objectives... to leverage the strengths of both objectives" (Section 4.3.2). Reference hyperparameters given in Table 5 (β=5.0, cosine LR 8e-7→8e-8, batch 2048, context 1024, 2 epochs) — usable as a starting point for the project's DPO stage at 1.2B scale.
- Preference data construction recipe: sample N=5 responses from the SFT checkpoint per prompt, score with an LLM jury, take highest as chosen / lowest as rejected, and "favor on-policy responses over instruction / preference samples in case of a tie to mitigate distribution shift" (Section 4.3.1) — directly relevant to building the malformed-tool-return preference pairs called for in the project's DPO stage.
- Tokenizer was deliberately trained to help structured-format tokenization: "We additionally include JSON and other code-like data to improve tokenization of structured formats" (Section 2.2, "Tokenizer and special tokens") — a concrete, checkable design choice bearing on the project's open question about whether the 65K vocab handicaps JSON/tool-call tokenization.
- Difficulty-ordered curriculum for SFT: rank examples by empirical solve-rate across a 12-model ensemble (350M–235B, dense and MoE) and train easy-to-hard (Section 4.2.1, "Curriculum learning") — a candidate technique if the project wants to sequence SFT data by difficulty for schema-validity or tool-call tasks.

## Baselines & datasets used

- SFT mixture (dense 350M/700M/1.2B/2.6B): ~5.39M samples from 67 sources; "Tool use" is 10.1% of the mixture, alongside "RAG" (13.2%), "Instruction following" (17.1%) (Table 4) — gives a rough sense of how much tool-use data a comparable vendor pipeline allocates, though the exact datasets are not named.
- Preference dataset: ~1M source conversations → ~700K final preference pairs after LLM-jury scoring and quality/length filtering (Section 4.3.1).
- Evaluation suite for the dense/MoE models: MMLU, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF, GSM8K/GSMPlus/MATH 500/MATH Lvl 5, MMMLU, MGSM (Tables 6–7) — same metric family the project's guardrails use (IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF, MMLU-Pro, GPQA), but measured on LFM2 (predecessor generation), not LFM2.5, and via Liquid's own internal harness, so the actual numbers (e.g. LFM2-1.2B: IFEval 74.89, IFBench 20.70, MMLU-Pro 19.71, GPQA 31.47) are far below the project's pre-registered guardrail targets (IFEval 86.23, IFBench 47.33, MMLU-Pro 44.35, GPQA Diamond 38.89) and must not be treated as the "matched base rerun" figures — those must come from the actual LFM2.5 base model.
- Inference throughput benchmarked with llama.cpp Q4_0 quantization on Samsung Galaxy S25 (Snapdragon 8 Elite) and AMD Ryzen HX 370, batch=1, at 1K/4K prompt lengths (Tables 2–3) — methodology template the project could reuse for its own 4-bit throughput-floor check, though this paper reports no quality-vs-quantization retention numbers, only speed.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- The paper's own evaluation methodology runs counter to the project's "no retry, no repair" first-attempt scoring philosophy: "Small models often fail evaluations because they struggle to output answers in the expected format... To measure their true capabilities more accurately, we build a custom evaluation harness with robust parsing logic" (Section 4.5), and Appendix C confirms MMLU parsing "falls back to constraint-based prediction... if the stripped token is not a valid choice." Liquid AI's own numbers are inflated relative to a strict, no-fallback parser — a concrete illustration of why the project's stricter first-attempt/no-repair protocol will likely produce lower and more diagnostic numbers than vendor-reported benchmarks for same-size models.
- Architecture search finding that could bound expectations for further hybridization: "under identical on-device performance budgets, augmenting these stacks... with linear-attention, state-space, or additional convolution operators does not improve aggregate quality on the evaluation suite and typically worsens device metrics" (Section 2.1) — a negative result for adding more exotic sequence-mixing components at this scale/latency budget, not something the project controls but useful context for why the backbone is what it is.
- Their own academic prototype's proxy objectives failed to transfer: "Our earlier academic prototype (STAR)... explored a specific design space... optimized on proxy signals (i.e., perplexity for quality, cache size for efficiency). In practice, these proxies do not transfer reliably to downstream task scores or device-level latency and memory" (Section 2.1) — a caution against using perplexity or cheap proxy metrics as stand-ins for the project's actual target metrics (BFCL, schema validity, flag rate).
- No results at all are given for structured output / schema validity / malformed-tool-return handling, nor for quantized-model quality retention — the two central sub-criteria of this project are simply absent from this report.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses an internal, non-public evaluation harness for language benchmarks and explicitly states scores "may differ from other reported scores" (captions of Tables 6, 7, 8, 9) — self-reported and explicitly flagged by the vendor itself as non-comparable to standard implementations.
- Multilingual transfer is measured as a retention ratio against English performance (e.g. "LFM2-1.2B retains 94.4% of its English GSM8K performance on MGSM" — Section 4.5) — a retention-ratio framing analogous to the project's own ≥97%/93%-per-axis quantization retention targets, worth reusing as a reporting convention.
- Decode/prefill throughput is measured at batch=1 on real target hardware (phone SoC, laptop CPU) rather than on datacenter GPUs, which matches the spirit of the project's single-8GB-GPU deployment target, though the actual devices differ (no RTX 2080 Super numbers here).

## Open questions for our project

- This report is for the LFM2 generation, not LFM2.5 — it is unclear from this document alone how much the architecture, tokenizer, or post-training recipe changed between LFM2 and LFM2.5; none of this paper's benchmark numbers should be used as the "matched base rerun" baseline for the pre-registered claims.
- The paper asserts the 65,536-token tokenizer was trained with extra JSON/code data "to improve tokenization of structured formats" but gives no benchmark isolating tokenizer vocabulary size as a variable (e.g., no ablation against a 128K-vocab tokenizer) — the project's own question about whether 65K vocab handicaps JSON/tool-call tokenization remains open even after reading this.
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a standard technical report and was treated purely as descriptive data.
