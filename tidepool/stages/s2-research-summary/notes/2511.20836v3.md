# 2511.20836v3 — Structured Prompts Improve Evaluation of Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Not a training method or quantization result for this project, but its central finding — that MMLU-Pro/GPQA scores swing by 15-21 points on a small model purely from prompt/CoT choice — is a concrete pitfall for how this project must run its "matched base rerun" guardrail comparisons.

## TL;DR

The authors bolt DSPy prompt-optimization methods (Zero-Shot Predict, Zero-Shot CoT, BFRS, MIPROv2) onto the HELM benchmarking framework and show that reported LM scores and leaderboard rankings depend heavily on prompt choice, not just model capability. Almost all of the gain comes from simply adding chain-of-thought; fancier few-shot/instruction optimizers add little once CoT is present, but cost 10x more prompt tokens.

## Techniques to try

- None of the prompting methods here are training techniques for this project's ladder (LoRA/DPO/GRPO/quantization); this paper is purely about eval-time prompting for benchmark leaderboards, not something to adopt for post-training LFM2.5.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Benchmarks directly overlap two of this project's guardrail metrics: MMLU-Pro and GPQA (general, not the Diamond subset), plus GSM8K and four medical QA/error-detection sets (MedCalc-Bench, Medec, HeadQA, MedBullets).
- Models span Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0 Flash, GPT-4o, o3 Mini, Llama 3.3 70B, and Qwen3 4B — Qwen3 4B is the only model near this project's 1.2B scale, and results are not reported below 4B.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Large, benchmark-specific swings from prompt format alone, at a model scale close to this project's: on MMLU-Pro, Qwen3 4B goes from "44.9% ± 3.1" (HELM baseline, no CoT) to "66.2% ± 3.0" (Zero-Shot CoT) — a >21-point swing (Table 3). On GPQA (general), Qwen3 4B moves from "34.3% ± 4.3" to "50.0% ± 4.7" (Table 3) — a ~16-point swing purely from adding a CoT instruction, no weight changes at all.
- "Moving from HELM's baseline to Zero-Shot Predict yields minimal improvement (60.5% → 61.4%). In contrast, introducing CoT reasoning and moving from Zero-Shot Predict to Zero-Shot CoT results in statistically significant gains (61.4% → 66.2%)." — i.e. the CoT trigger, not the optimizer, does almost all the work (Section 3.1).
- "Once CoT is introduced, moving to more sophisticated optimizers (BFRS and MIPROv2) does not lead to additional improvements (66.2% → 66.3%)" — expensive few-shot prompt optimization (BFRS/MIPROv2 add "1,741" and "1,816" extra prompt tokens per query respectively) buys almost nothing once a bare CoT instruction is present (Section 3.4).
- Benchmark-dependent: "HeadQA and Medec exhibit smaller gains," attributed to baseline saturation and knowledge-base limits respectively — not every eval is equally prompt-sensitive, so sensitivity found on MMLU-Pro/GPQA does not automatically generalize to e.g. IFEval or BFCL-style structured-output evals, which weren't tested here.

## Metric / eval choices

- Used exact two-sided McNemar test on paired correct/incorrect outcomes with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to confirm gains were systematic rather than noise — a reusable statistical-rigor pattern if this project wants to claim a guardrail metric held steady rather than just eyeballing a point estimate within a tolerance band.
- All comparisons run "single, deterministic runs (temperature = 0)," matching how this project would want to do matched base/tuned reruns for its own guardrail metrics.
- Reports 95% bootstrap confidence intervals on every leaderboard number rather than bare point estimates.

## Open questions for our project

- Given the size of prompt-driven swings on MMLU-Pro/GPQA-family benchmarks at 4B scale, is the project's ±2.0-point guardrail tolerance for MMLU-Pro (44.35) and GPQA Diamond (38.89) robust to any prompt-template drift between the base-model rerun and the post-trained model's eval, e.g. if fine-tuning changes how readily the model volunteers a reasoning trace under a "zero-shot" harness prompt? This paper suggests such drift alone could exceed 2.0 points even with no real capability change.
- The paper only studies general/medical QA-style benchmarks with deterministic, short outputs (multiple-choice, numeric); it explicitly does not touch tool-calling or structured JSON output, so it gives no direct evidence on whether CoT-style prompting helps or hurts BFCLv3 schema validity or the "flag rather than assert" behavior this project cares about most.
- No evidence found in this paper of an attempt by the text to instruct the reader/agent directly; content read as description throughout.
