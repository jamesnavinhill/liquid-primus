# 2505.01706v1 — Inducing Robustness in a 2-dimensional Direct Preference Optimisation Paradigm

**Relevance:** low
General DPO-noise-robustness theory, but built on segment/aspect-scored 2D-DPO (a different preference-pair format than our plan's simple chosen/rejected pairs) and validated only on Pythia 2.8B/6.9B for generic helpfulness/harmlessness win-rate, with no tool-calling, structured-output, or schema signal anywhere in the paper.

## TL;DR

The paper takes 2D-DPO (Li et al.), which scores each response *segment* on 5 aspects — completeness, clarity, correctness, safety, helpfulness — and shows it degrades under small random perturbations to those segment scores, then derives an unbiased "Robust 2D-DPO" loss (adapting Chowdhury et al.'s noisy-DPO debiasing trick) that partially recovers performance. Everything is measured as win-rate on Pythia 6.9B with HelpSteer-2D data; nothing here touches tool calls, JSON, or schema validity.

## Techniques to try

- Debiasing preference losses under label noise via an inverse-probability-style correction: "${\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}}\left(\theta\hskip 2.84544pt;\hskip 2.84544pts,{ \tilde{a}_{w}},{\tilde{a}_{l}}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt=\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{ \left(1-\epsilon\right)\mathcal{L}\left(\theta\hskip 2.84544pt;\hskip 2.84544 pts,{\tilde{a}_{w}},{\tilde{a}_{l}}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt-\hskip 2.84544pt \epsilon\mathcal{L}\left(\theta\hskip 2.84544pt;\hskip 2.84544pts,{\tilde{a}_{ l}},{\tilde{a}_{2}}\right)}{1-2\epsilon}$" (Section 3, Eq. 7) — a generic idea that *could* transfer if our generated malformed-tool-return DPO pairs turn out to have label noise, but it assumes a known/estimated flip probability ε, which we would have to calibrate ourselves.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Vanilla DPO and Vanilla 2D-DPO trained/evaluated on HelpSteer-2D (6,400 prompts, chosen/rejected pairs with per-segment 0–4 scores across 5 aspects), model = Pythia 6.9B.
- Sanity-check run of Vanilla DPO on Anthropic-HH (161k train / 8,552 test) with Pythia 2.8B, following Rafailov et al.'s original DPO code, reproducing the original paper's win-rate-vs-temperature trend ("We observe that the findings are in-line with the work done by Rafailov et al.").

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Vanilla 2D-DPO's win rate drops sharply when segment-level score noise is introduced only at evaluation time: "the Vanilla DPO algorithm's win rate experienced a sharp decline from 43.281% to 37.188%" — i.e., a DPO variant that looked fine noise-free can look meaningfully worse once its scoring signal is perturbed, a generic warning for any project relying on noisy/generated preference scores.
- Their own Robust 2D-DPO only partially recovers: win rate goes from 37.188% (vanilla, noisy) to 45.625% (robust, noisy) — still well below the 43.281% vanilla-2D-DPO/no-noise number, so the "robust" fix does not fully undo the noise's damage even in their own controlled setup.
- 2D-DPO underperforms Vanilla DPO on win-rate in their runs even without noise (31.25%/43.281% train/eval for 2D-DPO vs. 58.333%/58.854% for Vanilla DPO), though the paper does not explain this gap or claim 2D-DPO is worse in general — worth noting the added granularity did not obviously pay off at this scale.

## Metric / eval choices

- Sole metric is pairwise win-rate (percentage of eval prompts where the trained policy's implicit reward ranks the "preferred" response above the "rejected" one) — no BFCL-style task accuracy, no schema/format metric, no throughput or quantization consideration anywhere.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's noise model (uniform perturbation δ ∈ [0,1] added/subtracted from segment scores) is specific to 2D-DPO's per-segment aspect scoring; it is unclear this maps cleanly onto our simpler chosen/rejected DPO pairs for malformed-tool-return detection, where the "noise" we'd actually worry about is more like mislabeled flag/no-flag pairs than continuous score perturbation.
- All experiments are on Pythia 2.8B/6.9B base models with generic helpfulness/harmlessness data — no evidence at 1.2B scale or in a structured-output/tool-calling setting, so it's an open question whether label-noise robustness matters as much for our narrower, more verifiable (schema-checkable) reward signal.
