# 2607.25451v1 — Bits and Memories: Measuring Verbatim Extraction Across LLM Quantization

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap (quantization's effect on a language model's capabilities) but the paper measures verbatim-memorization privacy leakage, not structured-output/tool-calling quality, so nothing here settles or informs this project's specific 4-bit retention or tool-reliability questions.

## TL;DR

The paper quantizes Pythia models (160M/410M/1B) down to 4-bit and asks whether they still reproduce training text verbatim, using perplexity as the "general capability" control. It finds quantization erases memorized verbatim sequences faster than it erases general capability, but at 1B parameters the model still leaks 72% of memorized data while losing only ~4% of capability — so quantization is not a privacy fix, and the authors argue extraction (not membership inference) is the right thing to measure.

## Techniques to try

- The "selectivity" ratio construction — comparing how fast one capability decays vs. another under quantization via a log-ratio of retained fractions — is a reusable pattern for asking "does 4-bit quantization damage structured-output/tool-calling faster than it damages general knowledge?", which is one of this project's open questions. ("$s(q)=\frac{\log m(q)}{\log c(q)}$" — Section 3, "Selectivity")
- Always pairing a targeted metric with a capability control at the *same* precision point, so a drop can be attributed to "forgot this specific thing" vs. "model is just broken" — directly analogous to this project's plan to check BFCL/tool-call accuracy alongside guardrail metrics (IFEval, MMLU-Pro, etc.) at each quantization level. ("we measure general capability (perplexity on held-out text) at every single point, so that we can tell "the model forgot this passage" apart from "the model is simply broken."" — Section 1)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Pythia 160M/410M/1B (deduplicated), with the public Biderman et al. (2023b) ground-truth memorized-sequence set from the Pile — not applicable to LFM2.5-1.2B, which has no such public memorization ground truth.
- Quantization ladder: FP32/FP16, INT8 (LLM.int8()), and 4-bit NF4/FP4 (bitsandbytes), cross-checked against a self-written group-wise round-to-nearest (RTN) quantizer — all pre-GGUF/llama.cpp quantization schemes, not the target deployment format for this project.
- Capability measured via perplexity on WikiText-2 and Pile text, not via task benchmarks like BFCL, IFEval, or MMLU-Pro.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Quantization is not a privacy defense despite being "selective": at the 1B model, 4-bit NF4 "costs only about four percent of capability, yet the model still reproduces 72% of the memorized sequences it knew at FP16" — a warning against assuming any single retained-average number (like this project's ≥97% full-precision retention target) tells the whole story about what specifically survives or fails at 4-bit.
- The fraction of memorized data surviving quantization *increases* with model size (0.39 at 160M → 0.27 at 410M → 0.72 at 1B under NF4), the opposite of what one might hope; the authors explicitly flag this as an extrapolation risk since "real deployed models are far larger than the ones we can run."

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses strict greedy exact-match extraction (32-token prompt → 32-token greedy continuation, exact match against a 64-token known-memorized sequence) rather than probabilistic/discoverable extraction, chosen as "the most conservative and the least ambiguous" — a reminder that strict exact-match style metrics (relevant if this project ever needs an exact-match component for schema/tool-call strings) can undercount partial degradation; the paper supplements with mean correct-prefix length to catch partial forgetting.

## Open questions for our project

- Does an analogous "selectivity" analysis (BFCL/tool-call accuracy vs. IFEval/MMLU-Pro under 4-bit) show structured-output ability decaying faster or slower than general knowledge for LFM2.5-1.2B? This paper only establishes the pattern exists for memorization vs. perplexity on a different model family — it does not answer the project's actual question.
- None of the paper's numbers are directly transferable: different model (Pythia, not LFM2.5), different quantization backend (bitsandbytes/RTN, not GGUF/llama.cpp), different metric (verbatim memorization, not tool-call schema validity or task accuracy).
