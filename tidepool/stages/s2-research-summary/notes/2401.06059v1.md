# 2401.06059v1 — Investigating Data Contamination for Pre-training Language Models

**Relevance:** low
The paper studies pre-training-corpus contamination effects (GPT-2-small/large, from-scratch pre-training) and its evaluation-detection methods; it says nothing about post-training (SFT/DPO/GRPO), tool-calling, structured output, or quantization, so it does not settle any decision on this project's ladder, though it does bear on how much to trust benchmark numbers.

## TL;DR

The authors pre-train GPT-2-small/large from scratch with deliberately injected evaluation-set contamination (text-only vs. text+prompt+ground-truth) and find that ground-truth contamination inflates downstream scores more than text-only contamination, that the inflation is U-shaped in the number of repetitions (helps up to ~10 repeats, then hurts, even falling below the uncontaminated baseline at 20), and that the n-gram-based contamination definitions used in PaLM/Llama 2 reports are unreliable for both filtering and post-hoc "clean vs. dirty" evaluation splits. None of this touches post-training or quantization; it is entirely about what leaks into a pre-training corpus and how badly current contamination heuristics detect it.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Public GPT-2-small/medium/large checkpoints used as baselines against models pre-trained from scratch on Pile subsamples ("As baselines, we further evaluate all datasets on the public checkpoints for GPT-2-small, medium, and large variations").
- Eval sets: SST-2, MMLU, CNN/DailyMail (ROUGE + UniEval), SQuAD v1 — chosen because "the more difficult datasets are likely too challenging for GPT-2 series models," i.e. these are not representative of the agentic/tool-calling evals this project cares about.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- N-gram-based contamination filters (PaLM 8-gram/70% rule, Llama 2's per-token n-gram rule) are unreliable for cleaning a corpus: "in certain instances where token removal exceeded 30%, the model's performance remained comparable to that of the original model... documents excluded based on n-gram and Llama 2's definitions are not always genuinely contaminated."
- The standard "evaluate on clean vs. dirty eval subsets" methodology (used by PaLM/Llama 2 reports to argue robustness to contamination) fails to detect contamination that is known by construction to be present: "the performance for the four categories is similar to each other... there was no clear indication that the 'dirty' category outperformed the non-dirty categories," even on a model deliberately ground-truth-contaminated — i.e. this common eval-integrity check can give false reassurance.
- Effect of repeated contamination is non-monotonic/U-shaped, not "more contamination = more inflation": "as the contamination factor increased, performance initially improved but started to decline when the factor reached around 10 repetitions... at 20 repetitions, performance in some instances dropped below the baseline."
- One dataset (AG News) didn't follow the pattern at all — contamination "does not significantly enhance performance," plausibly because the base (uncontaminated) model already scored above public checkpoints from pre-existing corpus overlap, undercutting the generality of the paper's own main findings.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses MMLU accuracy as one of its four eval tasks, but only at GPT-2 scale (124M–774M) where MMLU accuracy is barely above chance (~23%) — not informative for this project's MMLU-Pro guardrail target (44.35) since the paper's models are far too weak on this task for any contamination signal to be meaningful.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's core warning — that "clean vs. dirty" benchmark-split arguments for robustness are not trustworthy — is relevant to how much weight to put on a "matched base rerun" as ground truth if the base LFM2.5-1.2B's own pre-training corpus has undetected overlap with BFCLv3, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, or IFEval items; this paper offers no method to check that, only evidence that the standard n-gram checks wouldn't catch it.
