# 2503.16416v2 — A Survey on Evaluation of LLM-based Agents

**Relevance:** medium
It is a landscape survey of agent-evaluation benchmarks (not a training-method paper), but it directly maps the BFCL/tool-use benchmark family the project's headline metric comes from and surfaces design gaps (robustness, guardrail metrics) relevant to the malformed-tool-return flag/false-flag design.

## TL;DR

This is a survey (no original experiments) cataloguing how LLM agents are evaluated: core capabilities (planning, tool use, self-reflection, memory), application-specific benchmarks (web, SWE, scientific, conversational agents), generalist-agent benchmarks, and cross-cutting dimensions (data curation, environment, interface, metric, safety). For this project the most relevant thread is its account of the BFCL lineage and adjacent tool-calling benchmarks, plus its discussion of what current benchmarks fail to measure (fine-grained progress, safety/guardrail compliance, robustness).

## Techniques to try

- Treat repeated-run robustness (their "pass^k") as a design pattern for the schema-validity / flag-rate metrics: "Robustness is often quantified via pass^k, the fraction of tasks where the agent succeeds across all k independent runs." — §5, Safety and Robustness Metrics. Could motivate running the ~300-sample malformed-tool-return probe across multiple seeds rather than a single pass, to guard against the flag rate being a lucky single-run number.
- Consider explicit "guardrail" metrics that penalize task success achieved via non-compliant behavior, as the survey recommends: "Future benchmarks must integrate 'guardrail' metrics, penalizing agents that achieve task success via non-compliant actions" — §5, Safety and Robustness Metrics. This is conceptually close to the project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pairing and could be cited as motivation for scoring it as a pair rather than a single number.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Traces the BFCL lineage directly relevant to the project's headline metric: "Benchmarks such as ToolAlpaca ..., ToolBench ..., and the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard v1 (BFCL) ... represent this early stage, relying on synthetic data and rule-based matching" then "Later versions of BFCL (v2 and v3) introduced multi-turn interactions, organizational tools, and multi-step logic, emphasizing continuous state management." — §2, Function Calling & Tool Use.
- Names adjacent/harder tool-use benchmarks worth knowing about even if out of scope: NESTFUL (dependent multi-call chains), ComplexFuncBench (implicit parameter inference, long-context), Scale's MCP Atlas and Tool-Decathlon (real MCP-server-sourced, agentic, multi-domain) — §2, Function Calling & Tool Use.
- Notes τ-Bench and τ²-Bench as tool-use-plus-policy-adherence conversational benchmarks with state-matching metrics, and AppWorld as a Tools-interface benchmark with schema adherence — §3.4 and Table 1.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- General warning about benchmark validity that generalizes to BFCL: "recent work suggests it [WebVoyager] exhibits over-optimistic performance estimates" — §3.1, Web Agents. Worth keeping in mind that leaderboard-style benchmarks can overstate real capability, though the survey doesn't make this claim about BFCL specifically.
- Flags a structural blind spot the project's malformed-tool-return probe is trying to avoid: "SWE-Lancer does not inherently penalize risky behaviors unless they interfere with replicating the target behavior" — §5, Safety and Robustness Metrics — i.e., a benchmark can reward "success" that papers over unsafe or non-compliant intermediate actions.
- "Notably, such binary outcome metrics are insufficient to understand the intermediate agent's progress, and call for fine-grained evaluation solutions" — §5, Metric. Argues pass/fail task-completion metrics (which BFCL-style scoring largely is) under-report where and how a model fails, relevant when diagnosing why schema validity or flag rate misses target.

## Metric / eval choices

- Categorizes benchmark metrics into task completion, execution-based unit testing (SWE), state matching (τ-Bench-style), and answer matching (GAIA-style) — §5, Metric. The project's own metrics (schema parse success, execution correctness, flag rate) map onto this taxonomy as a hybrid of task-completion and state/execution matching.
- Categorizes interaction interfaces into Code/Terminal, Tools (schema-constrained function calls, e.g. τ-Bench, AppWorld), and GUI — §5, Interaction Interface — useful vocabulary for framing this project's "Tools interface" evaluation surface (BFCL, structured output) versus other interface types it is explicitly not targeting.

## Open questions for our project

- The survey gives no results at model scales anywhere near 1.2B, no quantization discussion, and no data on malformed/adversarial tool-return handling specifically — it does not help answer the project's core questions about quantization damage, "flag rather than assert" training signals, or realistic flag/false-flag targets. Treat it purely as background/vocabulary, not as an evidentiary source for any of the pre-registered numeric targets.
- No embedded instructions to the reader were found in the text; it reads as a standard academic survey throughout.
