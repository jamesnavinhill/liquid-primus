# 2501.02342v1 — Optimizing Small Language Models for In-Vehicle Function-Calling

**Relevance:** medium
Close in scale (1.8B–3.8B Phi-3 variants) and shares the LoRA-vs-FFT, function-calling-fine-tune, and 4-bit-deployment concerns of this project, but it prunes down to size rather than post-training an already-small model, uses a different eval stack (no BFCL, no schema-validity or malformed-tool-return probes), and its quantization comparison is confounded by switching evaluation tools.

## TL;DR

Mercedes-Benz prunes Phi-3-mini (3.8B) down to 2.8B/1.8B via depth- and width-wise structured pruning, "heals" it with continued pretraining, instruction-tunes it, then fine-tunes (LoRA or full FFT) on a synthetic 25,500-example in-vehicle function-calling dataset with functional tokens (Octopus v2 style), and ships it as 4-bit GGUF via llama.cpp for on-device ARM CPU inference. Function-calling accuracy (exact-match) stayed high (0.84–0.88) after 4-bit quantization across model sizes, and function-calling fine-tuning barely moved general-benchmark scores, but general capability had already been badly damaged by pruning/healing before that point (MMLU near-halved from 0.70 to as low as 0.32–0.42).

## Techniques to try

- LoRA fine-tuning config for function-calling on a small dense model: "Rank 96 | Alpha 16 ... Target Modules embed_tokens, qkv_proj, o_proj, mlp (up/down), lm_head" with "at least two epochs of training without any weight decay and with a larger learning rate" (5e-5) — Table 2, Section 3.3. Contrast with FFT: "just one epoch and used a smaller learning rate along with a weight decay of 0.1" (5e-6) — Section 3.3.
- Negative/out-of-scope sampling to teach refusal: "500 irrelevant queries were generated using a negative sampling strategy. These queries were plausible but unsolvable by the provided functions (e.g., \"Can you teleport the car to Hawaii?\"). The assistant responds by politely declining the request." — Section 3, "Negative Examples." This is refusal-on-unsupported-request, not flag-on-malformed-tool-return, but the synthetic-negative-generation pattern is directly reusable for our malformed-tool-return probe construction.
- Functional/special tokens mapped to actions: "Eight MB tokens were defined for specific vehicle functions, such as set_ambient_light_color_program mapped to <MB_1>" (Octopus-v2-style) — Section 3.3. Worth knowing as an alternative to plain-JSON tool-call emission, though it trades off against our "specialize to the underlying technology, not vendor idiosyncrasies" constraint since it's a bespoke non-JSON encoding.
- Quality-control recipe for synthetic function-calling data: "we manually curated a subset of examples derived from common user questions and included them in the prompt to the LLM... enforced an even distribution of function calls... combined with de-duplication and post-processing" — Section 3.3.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Healing/continued-pretrain corpora: fineweb (short healing, 5000 QLoRA steps) and fineweb-edu (long healing, "45000 steps/15B tokens") — Section 3.2.
- Instruction tuning: OpenHermes-2.5, one epoch — Section 3.2.
- General benchmarks: Winogrande, TruthfulQA, MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC via lm-evaluation-harness (safetensors) and llama.cpp's own tool (gguf) — Sections 4.1–4.2.
- Function-calling eval: custom synthetic dataset (25,000 positive + 500 negative examples across 8 vehicle gRPC functions), scored by exact match on function name and arguments — Sections 3.3, 4.2.
- Production baseline they compare against: "the current production speech system for function calling, which achieves an accuracy of 0.75 across various vehicle functions" — Section 4.2.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Width-wise pruning is much more damaging than depth-wise: "width-wise pruning on top of the 2.8B model caused significant degradation in model capabilities across all benchmarks regardless of the healing and alignment strategy applied to it" — Section 4.1. Depth pruning also gives 2x faster token generation than width pruning at similar size — Section 4.3.
- Short healing is not enough even when text becomes fluent again: "the factual knowledge of the original Phi3-mini was almost entirely lost" after only 5000 QLoRA healing steps, requiring 45000 more steps (15B tokens) to partially recover — Section 3.2.
- Even with long healing and SFT, general-benchmark average score barely moved and "remains close to 0.50 for most configurations" — Section 4.1 — i.e. pruning damage to general ability was only partially reversible, and improvements were "task-specific and not universal across all benchmarks."
- Benchmark scores and qualitative impression diverged: "the benchmark results ... indicate that longer training and instruction tuning ... lead to only slight improvements in benchmark performance, direct interaction with the model revealed noticeable enhancements" — Section 4.1, a caution against trusting only the numeric harness scores.
- Their own quantization-impact claim is not a clean before/after comparison: safetensor models were scored with lm-evaluation-harness and gguf models with "llama.cpp's own evaluation tool for running standard benchmarks" (Section 4.2) — different tools, so "no significant performance drop" claims across the 4-bit boundary are confounded by measurement-tool change, not a controlled ablation.
- Function-calling accuracy held up under 4-bit quantization across sizes (0.86–0.88 for LoRA, 0.84–0.88 for FFT — Table 4), but this uses a coarse exact-match metric on their own narrow 8-function task, not a stress-tested schema-validity or malformed-input check.

## Metric / eval choices

- Function-calling metric is a simple exact-match on function name + arguments over 8 predefined functions — much coarser than BFCL or a first-attempt schema-validity check; no retry/repair ambiguity is discussed because the task space is tiny and closed.
- General-capability suite (Winogrande/TruthfulQA/MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC) is used as the "did we damage general ability" proxy, analogous to our IFEval/IFBench/MMLU-Pro/GPQA guardrail set, but at a coarser resolution (no per-axis floor, no matched-rerun baseline methodology).
- Token throughput reported only as raw tokens/sec on an ARM CPU vehicle head unit (6.76 → 9.44 → 11.02 t/s base→2.8B→1.8B, Table 5), not as a ratio to a matched base rerun, and on completely different hardware than our RTX 2080 Super target — not transferable as a number, only as a methodology reminder to profile the actual deployment class of hardware.

## Open questions for our project

- Their "quantization doesn't hurt much" conclusion is confounded by evaluating safetensors and gguf checkpoints with two different harnesses (Sections 4.1 vs 4.2) — this is a trap to avoid in our own 97%-retention claim; we must keep the eval tool fixed across full-precision and 4-bit runs.
- Does the paper's finding that "pruning beyond ~30% of original parameters collapses the model" (Section 3.1, citing Gromov et al.) have any bearing on us, given we are not pruning at all, only post-training an already-1.2B model? Likely not directly transferable, but it's a caution if quantization-aware distillation later reduces effective capacity.
- The paper's negative-sampling approach teaches "decline the unsupported request," a different skill from our "flag the malformed tool return" — worth checking whether these two refusal/flagging behaviors interfere or reinforce each other during joint training.
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a standard research write-up.
