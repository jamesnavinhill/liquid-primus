# 2505.06461v1 — Challenging GPU Dominance: When CPUs Outperform for On-Device LLM Inference

**Relevance:** low
Real overlap in tooling (llama.cpp/GGUF, quantization) and vocabulary, but the paper studies CPU-vs-GPU tokens/sec on an iPhone 15 Pro across six models — nothing here bears on structured-output quality, training signals, or the RTX 2080 Super desktop-GPU deployment target this project cares about.

## TL;DR

The authors benchmark llama.cpp inference speed (tokens/sec, not quality) for six 0.5B–8B models on an iPhone 15 Pro under F16/Q8/Q4 precision, CPU-thread counts, and GPU acceleration, finding that for sub-1.5B models, multi-threaded CPU-only execution can beat the phone's GPU due to kernel-launch and memory-transfer overhead. They also try a custom graph/tensor-parallel scheduler and profile GEMM as the dominant cost. None of this touches instruction-following, tool-calling, or quantized *accuracy* — only raw throughput on mobile hardware.

## Techniques to try

- None directly applicable. The paper's "graph-level + tensor-level parallelism" scheduler modification to llama.cpp raised CPU throughput from 11.5 to 15 tk/s on Llama-3.2-1B-F16 ("Version 2... reaching 15 tk/s" — Section 7.2), but this project's deployment is a single 8 GB GPU where GPU offload is already the plan, not CPU-thread scheduling on a phone.

## Baselines & datasets used

- No benchmark suite (BFCL, IFEval, MMLU, etc.) is used at all; the only metric is throughput on a fixed 7-token prompt ("The meaning of life is") — irrelevant to this project's accuracy/reliability targets.
- Models tested: Qwen2-0.5B, Qwen2-1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, Llama-3.2-8B, at F16/Q8/Q4 on iPhone 15 Pro (A17 Pro, 2P+4E CPU, 8GB RAM, iOS 18.2.1).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Attempting to split the compute graph across CPU *and* GPU simultaneously backfired badly: "Version 3... the throughput dropped significantly to ∼6 tk/s" versus ∼15 tk/s for CPU-only graph+tensor parallelism, attributed to "Memory Transfer Overheads," "Thread Scheduling Conflicts," and "Underutilized GPU for Small GEMMs" (Section 7.2–7.3). Relevant caution if this project ever considers CPU/GPU hybrid execution for the shipped GGUF — heterogeneous scheduling can regress rather than help, especially at small batch/single-token decode.
- 7B/8B models could not even be loaded at F16/Q8 on an 8GB device ("mmap failures") and small thread counts timed out at 4-bit ("1–2 CPU threads timed out for Q4") — a reminder that on-device memory ceilings bite hard, though the numbers are for an 8GB iPhone, not the project's 8GB desktop GPU.
- The paper explicitly could not explain its own headline result: "This counterintuitive behavior, despite repeated verification, remains without a detailed low-level explanation" (Section 5.4 Remark), due to lack of iOS-level profiling access — an unresolved mystery, not a technique.

## Metric / eval choices

- Sole metric is tokens/sec throughput averaged over five runs per configuration; no perplexity, task accuracy, or output-quality metric is reported anywhere in the paper, so it offers nothing on whether quantization damages structured output vs. knowledge — the exact question this project's notes are trying to answer.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper only measures speed, never quality — it cannot inform whether Q4 quantization damages structured-output/tool-calling more than knowledge (one of this project's central open questions); a citation gap worth flagging rather than a source of an answer.
- All findings are device- and engine-specific (iPhone 15 Pro Metal/ARM backend in llama.cpp); the authors themselves caution the results "may not generalize to other ARM-based mobile platforms" (Section 8.3), and by extension almost certainly don't generalize to a discrete desktop GPU (RTX 2080 Super, CUDA backend), which is this project's actual target.
- No instruction-addressed-to-reader text was found in the paper; it reads as a normal empirical systems paper throughout.
