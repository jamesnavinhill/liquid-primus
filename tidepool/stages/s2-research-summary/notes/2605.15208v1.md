# 2605.15208v1 — Quantization Undoes Alignment: Bias Emergence in Compressed LLMs Across Models and Precision Levels

**Relevance:** high
Directly evidences this project's central quantization risk — that low-bit compression selectively erodes a narrow instilled behavior ("say unknown / withhold judgment") while aggregate quality metrics stay flat, which is the same failure mode this project must rule out for "flag rather than assert."

## TL;DR

Three 3.8B–7B instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3.5-mini), quantized to BF16/8/6/4/3-bit with MLX weight-only PTQ, were run on 12,148 BBQ ambiguous-condition bias items across 5 seeds (911,100 records total). At 3-bit, 6–21% of items that were never stereotyped at full precision started producing stereotyped answers, and the models' rate of answering "unknown" fell 17.4%, even though perplexity barely moved. The paper's framing is that compression doesn't add uniform noise — it selectively knocks out the specific alignment-trained behavior of recognizing ambiguity and withholding an answer, and this is invisible to perplexity or other aggregate benchmarks.

## Techniques to try

- Item-level transition analysis: track per-item behavior at BF16 vs. each quantized level (not just aggregate scores) to catch regressions that a population-level metric dilutes away — "An item-level transition analysis methodology that detects quality regressions invisible to aggregated metrics" (Introduction, contribution 2). Directly applicable to auditing the flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair before and after 4-bit conversion.
- Dose-response sweep across multiple bit-widths rather than a single before/after comparison, to find the threshold where degradation accelerates — "The dose-response evaluation across multiple compression levels, rather than comparison of only two conditions, is essential for identifying critical thresholds" (VI-A).
- Quantization-aware alignment as a mitigation: "incorporate simulated quantization noise during alignment, analogous to quantization-aware training (QAT) but applied during instruction tuning or RLHF to produce models whose safety behaviors are resilient to subsequent compression" (VI-C) — relevant to the candidate ladder's final rung (quantization-aware distillation/training).
- Multiple random seeds per item at low but nonzero temperature to separate systematic behavioral shift from sampling noise: "the use of 5 seeds per item allows us to estimate per-item response consistency under this setting" (IV-B).

## Baselines & datasets used

- BBQ (Bias Benchmark for QA), ambiguous condition only, 5 categories (Age, Gender Identity, Race/Ethnicity, Religion, SES), 12,148 items, sourced from HuggingFace `Elfsong/BBQ`.
- Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct (3.8B) — all ≤8B, in range for this project's numbers to be trusted at face value, though still ~3–6x larger than the 1.2B target.
- Quantization: MLX framework, weight-only PTQ, group size 64, at BF16/Q8/Q6/Q4/Q3.
- Perplexity baseline measured on the Tulu-3 SFT mixture, 256 samples, 512-token sequences.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Perplexity is a false-assurance metric for quantization safety: "perplexity increases by less than 0.5% at 8-bit and under 3% at 4-bit across all three models, yet 2.5–5.6% of items already develop new biases at 4-bit" (Abstract). At Mistral-7B/3-bit specifically, "perplexity increases by only 10.2% yet 17.7% of previously unbiased items develop new stereotypical biases, a 173× disparity between the aggregate quality signal and the item-level fairness signal" (IV-C6). Directly warns against trusting a single aggregate perplexity or benchmark-average number to certify the 4-bit GGUF build.
- Model-family variation is large and not predictable from parameter count alone: Qwen2.5-7B transitioned only 6.0% of previously-unbiased items at Q3 vs. Mistral-7B (also 7B) at 17.7% and Phi-3.5-mini (3.8B) at 21.1% — "Mistral (7B) shows 17.7% transition, substantially worse than Qwen (also 7B)... architectural choices, pretraining data composition, and the specifics of instruction tuning all interact with quantization resilience in ways that cannot be predicted from model size alone" (V-A). Implies LFM2.5's specific architecture (hybrid conv/GQA, 65K vocab) could behave unpredictably under 4-bit and needs its own empirical check rather than extrapolation from other model families.
- The paper never measured parse-failure rate by quantization level and flags this as an unaddressed risk to its own results: "If compressed models produce more unparseable responses, this could introduce selection bias in our metrics. Future work should analyze parse failures as an additional signal of compression-induced capability degradation" (V-B) — this is essentially the schema-validity axis this project cares about, left untested here.
- Findings are specific to weight-only PTQ via MLX on Apple Silicon; the paper explicitly disclaims transfer to GGUF-style or GPU quantization approaches: "GPU-based quantization using frameworks like bitsandbytes or GPTQ may yield different numerical behavior" and "Other quantization approaches (GPTQ, AWQ, SqueezeLLM) use different calibration strategies and may produce different bias profiles" (V-B). The qualitative lesson (aggregate metrics mask targeted capability loss) likely transfers to 4-bit GGUF; the specific percentages do not.
- Quantization does not degrade uniformly toward the mean: items that were already fully biased at BF16 (SRS=1.0) actually became less biased under 3-bit ("0.958→0.706"), while unbiased items became biased — "compression disrupts all learned patterns indiscriminately but because far more items transition from unbiased to biased than from biased to unbiased, the net effect is an increase" (IV-C2). Suggests any narrow post-training behavior (like "flag on garbage") could shift in either direction under quantization, not just decay monotonically.

## Metric / eval choices

- Stereotype Reliance Score (SRS) and Unknown Selection Rate (USR), computed per-item across seeds to get a discrete distribution in {0, 0.2, ..., 1.0}, not just an aggregate mean — enables the bimodal-distribution finding ("71–92% of items show SRS=0.0... 8–27% show SRS=1.0", IV-C2).
- Chi-squared test on BF16-vs-compressed 2×2 contingency tables per model×category, with Cohen's h as effect size, plus a logistic regression with bit-width as a continuous predictor (coefficient β₁ = -0.0205, p<0.0001) to confirm dose-response rather than just eyeballing a trend.
- All proportions reported with 95% Wilson score confidence intervals.
- A four-part "unified evaluation protocol" is proposed for compressed models: aggregate metrics + transition analysis + epistemic-calibration metrics + per-item multi-seed consistency (VI-A) — a template worth adapting for the guardrail-metric checks (IFEval/IFBench/MMLU-Pro/GPQA) at 4-bit.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the same epistemic-calibration collapse (losing the ability to say "I don't know" / "unknown") transfer to this project's "flag rather than assert" behavior on malformed tool returns under 4-bit GGUF? This paper studied a structurally similar but not identical behavior (BBQ ambiguous QA, not tool-output validation), so it's suggestive, not conclusive, for the 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate targets.
- The paper only tested weight-only MLX quantization on 3.8–7B dense models; whether LFM2.5's hybrid short-conv + GQA architecture at 1.2B shows the same or a different degradation profile under llama.cpp-style 4-bit GGUF quantization is untested here and would need a project-specific check.
- No embedded instructions or directives to the reader were found in the paper text; it reads as a standard empirical study.
