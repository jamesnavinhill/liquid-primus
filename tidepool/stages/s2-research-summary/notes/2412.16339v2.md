# 2412.16339v2 — Deliberative Alignment: Reasoning Enables Safer Language Models

**Relevance:** low
Frontier-scale safety-alignment paper about jailbreak/overrefusal behavior in o1/o3-mini via CoT-over-specifications training; it shares only a loose conceptual thread ("reason before answering, flag when something is off") with this project's tool-calling/malformed-return work, with no transferable method, dataset, or number at the ~1.2B on-device scale.

## TL;DR

OpenAI trains o1-series reasoning models to explicitly recite and reason over safety specifications in their chain-of-thought before answering (SFT on context-distilled CoTs, then RL with an LLM judge that sees the spec), which improves jailbreak robustness and reduces overrefusals simultaneously. Everything is built and measured on o1/o1-preview/o3-mini-scale reasoning models with long inference-time CoT, not on small non-reasoning instruct models, so it is only tangentially useful for a 1.2B tool-calling project.

## Techniques to try

- None directly transferable: the core mechanism depends on a model with extended chain-of-thought reasoning at inference time ("we provide reward signal using a judge LLM that is given our safety specifications" — Section 2.1) and on context-distillation from a much larger/more-capable helpfulness-only model to generate training CoTs (Section 2.3.1) — infrastructure this project does not have for a 1.2B target model on the candidate ladder (LoRA/SFT/DPO/GRPO).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Internal-only eval sets (Challenging Refusal Evaluation, internal Policy Overrefusals) plus public benchmarks: StrongREJECT (jailbreak goodness@0.1), XSTest (overrefusal), WildChat toxic subset, SimpleQA, BBQ — none of these test structured output, tool calling, or schema validity, so they don't map onto BFCLv3/IFEval/schema-validity metrics this project cares about.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- The RL reward signal is produced by "a 'judge' reasoning model $\mathcal{G}_{RM}$ ... with access to our safety policies" (Section 2.4) — i.e., a model-scored reward is the primary training signal for the RL stage. This is exactly the kind of signal this project's standing constraint rules out ("No model-scored stored reward may be reused as a training signal"), so the paper's central RL recipe is not usable here as described.
- Providing full specifications at inference time instead of training on them underperforms training-time internalization, even though it seems like it should generalize as well: "Despite having access to the full spec, this baseline appears to learn less safety behavior than the model trained with deliberative alignment (and in many cases, even the model only trained with safety in the SFT stage)" (Section 4.1) — a mild caution against assuming a big system-prompt spec (e.g., pasting full JSON Schema/tool docs in-context) is a substitute for training the behavior in.
- o1-preview showed markedly worse response-style adherence than o1 on regulated-advice and self-harm safe completions (0.04 and 0.28 vs 0.65 and 0.92 for o1) because it was evaluated against a style standard introduced after its training — a reminder that eval/training-target drift between snapshots can make a model look like it regressed when the target itself moved.

## Metric / eval choices

- "goodness@0.1" (StrongREJECT): safety score against the top 10% most-effective jailbreak techniques per prompt, rather than an average over all techniques — a stress-test framing that could be worth borrowing conceptually for a malformed-tool-return "hard cases" slice, though the underlying technique/domain is unrelated.
- Human validation of an autograder: the paper cross-checks its main jailbreak metric with a 35-reviewer human review process on 2,100 adversarial prompts because "the compositional jailbreaks in the evaluation sometimes also confused the autograder" (Section 3.2) — a general reminder that autograders need human spot-checking, applicable in spirit to grading first-attempt schema validity or flag/false-flag rates.

## Open questions for our project

- Does anything like "explicit reasoning over a spec before acting" transfer to a non-reasoning 1.2B model asked to flag a malformed tool return, or does that require inference-time CoT budget this project's throughput/latency constraints don't allow?
- The paper's ablation methodology (drop safety data from SFT only / RL only / both) is a clean template for isolating which training stage teaches "flag rather than assert" — worth reusing the ablation *design* even though the safety-training content itself doesn't apply.
