# 2505.13249v1 — RN-F: A Novel Approach for Mitigating Contaminated Data in Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
The paper is about detecting benchmark/backdoor data contamination via quantization-residual anomaly signals on tiny non-LLM-scale models (1.2M–13M params, tabular/vision/text), not about training a model for tool calling or preserving structured-output/instruction-following quality under 4-bit quantization, so it offers topical overlap (quantization noise, edge inference) but no method or number this project would adopt.

## TL;DR

RN-F flags contaminated or backdoored inputs by comparing an fp16 model's activations to its int4-quantized counterpart: on "clean" in-distribution inputs the rounding error averages out, but memorized/backdoored inputs land in sparse activation regions and produce a biased, larger residual, which a calibrated per-layer threshold can catch. It's evaluated only on tiny tabular/text/image toy models (TabPFNGen 1.2M params, TinyStories-GPT2-XS 13M params, SD-lite 6M params) on the M5Product e-commerce dataset, not on instruction-following or tool-calling LLMs.

## Techniques to try

- None directly transferable — this is a training-data contamination/backdoor detector, not a technique for preserving structured-output quality under quantization or for training "flag rather than assert" behavior on malformed tool returns. The closest conceptual link is the general idea of using a cheap int4-vs-fp16 activation discrepancy as an anomaly signal ("RN-F ... leverages the quantization residual—the per-layer difference between full-precision and int4 activations—as an anomaly signal" — Section 1), but it detects *training-set* contamination/backdoors in inputs, not malformed *tool-call outputs* at runtime, so it does not map onto this project's flag/false-flag sub-criterion.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Dataset: M5Product (tri-modal e-commerce corpus: 64×64 product image crop, ≤128 WordPiece tokens of title/description, 50 tabular attributes) — "All three workloads draw their inputs from the M5Product corpus [19]" (Section 5.1, Dataset).
- Models: TabPFNGen (1.2M params), TinyStories-GPT2-XS (13M params), SD-lite (6M params), all PTQ'd to 4-bit with bitsandbytes — none is an instruction-tuned or tool-calling LLM, and all are far below the 1.2B scale of this project's target model.
- Baselines compared against: CDD (logit-distribution based, [8]), BAIT (backdoor scanning via target inversion, [10]), ConStat (performance-based contamination detection, [11]).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Limitations include the assumption of a mostly clean calibration buffer—above 10% contamination, residuals shift and false negatives increase." (Section 5.2) — the method degrades once the assumed-clean calibration set is itself dirty.
- "extreme sparsity (<5% density) limits residual accumulation, causing performance to collapse. This sets a practical lower bound on model capacity for RN-F-based detection." (Section 5.2) — an aggressively pruned/sparse model breaks the signal RN-F relies on.
- "Additionally, quantisation noise can leak membership information [25]." (Section 5.2) — flagged as an open privacy concern, not resolved in this paper.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports Accuracy, macro-F1, and ROC-AUC for contamination-instance detection, averaged over 3 seeds (Section 5.1, Evaluation Metrics) — a binary anomaly-detection framing (clean vs. contaminated instance), not applicable to this project's schema-validity or tool-call-correctness metrics.
- Reports overhead directly: "RN-F adds just 3.5–4.3% latency and 3.6–4.4% energy via one extra int4 pass (Table 2)" — a useful reminder that even a "free" extra int4 forward pass has a non-trivial (single-digit percent) latency cost, relevant context for this project's 5% throughput-floor requirement on the shipped 4-bit build, though RN-F's forward pass is additive/optional and not part of normal inference.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the pre-registered claims (BFCLv3, IFEval, schema validity, flag/false-flag rates, GGUF throughput) are addressed by this paper; it operates on a different problem (contamination/backdoor detection in training data) and at a different scale (≤13M-parameter tabular/vision/text toy models vs. our 1.2B tool-calling model), so this note settles nothing for the project's open questions.
- The paper's own claimed improvement figures are inconsistent in places (abstract says "up to 10.5%" detection-metric improvement; the conclusion says "surpassing state-of-the-art methods by up to 11.2 points"; Table 1's actual deltas for TinyStories are described in-text as "+7.9/+11.1/+10.0 pp") — worth treating the paper's headline numbers with some skepticism rather than citing them.
