# 2603.26603v2 — Sustainability Is Not Linear: Quantifying Performance, Energy, and Privacy Trade-offs in On-Device Intelligence

**Relevance:** medium
Directly studies llama.cpp/GGUF 4-bit quantization schemes and their effect on decode throughput and memory, which touches this project's shipping format, but the hardware (unrooted Android phone, CPU-only) and task (summarization quality via BERTScore/G-Eval) are far from the project's RTX 2080 Super GPU target and tool-calling/structured-output goal.

## TL;DR

The paper profiles energy, latency, memory, and generation quality for eight open LLMs (0.5B–9B) on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra running llama.cpp, comparing the Q4_K_M and IQ4_XS 4-bit GGUF quantization formats. Its central finding is a "quantization-energy paradox": the importance-aware IQ4_XS format shrinks memory but adds runtime codebook-reconstruction overhead that erases any energy/speed benefit, so Q4_K_M wins on generation speed, latency, and energy while IQ4_XS only wins on peak memory. It also finds a sparse MoE model (OLMoE-1B-7B) tracks the energy/speed profile of a 1–2B dense model despite 7B resident parameters.

## Techniques to try

- None directly transferable as a training technique — this is a measurement/benchmarking study, not a modeling method. The one actionable idea for this project is quantization-format selection: if choosing between GGUF 4-bit schemes for the shipped 1.2B model, favor a block-wise mixed-precision scheme (Q4_K_M-style) over a codebook/importance-aware scheme (IQ4_XS/AWQ-style) when the goal is preserving decode throughput, since "the structurally simpler Q4_K_M achieves significant victories across all critical dynamic operational metrics, including generation speed, overall latency, and total physical energy consumption" (Section IV-B) — relevant to the project's "4-bit decode throughput must land within 5% of the base's" requirement, though this was measured on ARM mobile CPU, not the project's target RTX 2080 Super GPU.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Eight instruction-tuned models 0.5B–9B (Qwen2-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Phi-2, Qwen2.5-3B, OLMoE-1B-7B-0125, Qwen2.5-7B, Meta-Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B), each quantized to Q4_K_M and IQ4_XS GGUF and run via llama.cpp on-device (Table I). None of these is at or near the project's 1.2B target scale, and none is the LFM2.5 architecture.
- Single fixed task: document summarization, greedy decoding, temperature 0, 512-token context, 100-token max output, 30 repetitions per configuration (Section III-C).
- Quality measured with BERTScore (reference-based) and a G-Eval-style LLM-judge protocol (reference-free) — no structured-output, tool-calling, or JSON/schema task is evaluated anywhere in the paper.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "While IQ4_XS reliably reduces peak memory, it confers no meaningful advantage in energy or speed... Quantization format therefore matters when fitting a model into RAM, but the choice between these two 4-bit schemes has negligible consequences for battery life or generation throughput" (Answer to RQ2) — a negative result for anyone assuming importance-aware/codebook quantization is a free win on latency.
- Reference-based metric bias: "Smaller models frequently achieved higher semantic similarity scores than larger counterparts, even when human inspection revealed summaries of lower quality... the metric favored extractive behavior over abstractive summarization, biasing the evaluation toward less capable models" (Section III-E / IV) — a caution about trusting embedding-similarity metrics (BERTScore) as a sole quality signal, relevant by analogy to not trusting a single automatic metric for structured-output correctness either.
- MoE models trade memory for speed, not the other way: "all experts must remain resident in memory at all times, placing pressure on the unified memory shared across CPU, GPU, and background processes" (Section V-B) — not applicable to LFM2.5 (dense/hybrid, not MoE) but a reminder that architecture-level efficiency claims often hide a memory-footprint cost.
- Large dense models (8B–9B) were "technically feasible to execute... but... practically unusable for real-time, interactive digital assistant applications" due to latency and energy (Answer to RQ1) — an on-device feasibility ceiling that doesn't transfer directly to a GPU deployment but reinforces that scale should be sized to the interaction budget, not the leaderboard rank.

## Metric / eval choices

- Non-parametric statistics throughout (Shapiro-Wilk to confirm non-normality, Friedman omnibus test, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm-Bonferroni correction) for comparing models/quantization schemes across 30 repeated runs (Section III-E, Tables II–IV) — a reasonable statistical-rigor template if this project ever needs to compare many quantization or fine-tuning variants across repeated runs with noisy metrics.
- Energy measured via on-device BatteryManager API telemetry with baseline-power subtraction and trapezoidal integration to get Joules and Joules/token (Section III-D) — not applicable to a GPU deployment target, where nvidia-smi/NVML power draw would be the analogous instrument if energy mattered, but it does not appear in this project's success metrics.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's quantization comparison (Q4_K_M vs IQ4_XS) is CPU/ARM-specific; it is unclear whether the same "codebook overhead cancels memory savings" effect holds on a CUDA GPU like the RTX 2080 Super, where dequantization kernels are optimized differently. This project should not assume the paper's throughput ranking transfers without checking on the actual target GPU.
- The paper never touches structured output, tool calling, JSON Schema, or instruction-following degradation under quantization — it offers no evidence either way on this project's central question of whether low-bit quantization damages structured output more than knowledge.
- No conflicting instructions or embedded directives were found in the paper text; it is a standard empirical measurement report.
