# 2502.17507v2 — C2-DPO: Constrained Controlled Direct Preference Optimization

**Relevance:** high
Directly bears on the DPO stage of the candidate ladder (DPO on preference pairs including malformed-tool-return pairs): it identifies a specific DPO pathology — probability collapse of even the "correct" response — and proposes a near-zero-cost fix.

## TL;DR

The paper proves that vanilla DPO (and IPO/CDPO/RPO/Distilled DPO, which it shows are all instances of the same classification framework) can drive down the probability of *both* the preferred and the rejected response, not just the rejected one — a phenomenon called likelihood displacement. It proposes C2-DPO, a regularizer added to the DPO loss that constrains how much probability mass can move between winner and loser relative to the reference policy, using only quantities DPO already computes.

## Techniques to try

- C2-DPO logarithmic-constraint regularizer: add `λ(log(π_θ(y_w|x)/π_ref(y_w|x)) + log(π_θ(y_l|x)/π_ref(y_l|x)))²` to the standard DPO loss ("these log probabilities are already computed in DPO, which makes the implementation of the corresponding C2-DPO algorithm more efficient" — Section 5.1). No extra forward/backward passes needed; drop-in for any DPO-stage training loop.
- Tuned once and reused across variants: "we used the hyper-parameter λ=2×10⁻⁴ for all 4 implementations and did not tune it for each of the 4 implementations separately" (Section 6.1) — a reasonable starting point if this is tried.
- The paper's own framing: "By hedging against the displacement, C2-DPO provides practical improvements over vanilla DPO when aligning several language models using standard preference datasets" (Abstract).

## Baselines & datasets used

- UltraFeedback Binarized (64k prompts), Zephyr-7B-SFT and Olmo-13B-SFT as base models.
- Reddit TL;DR summarization preference data, GPT-J-6B (SFT'd one epoch, then DPO/C2-DPO).
- Compared against vanilla DPO, IPO, CDPO, DPOP, Cal-DPO, SPPO; holistic eval via MT-Bench (GPT-4 judge, 8 categories).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Core negative result about plain DPO: "under the optimal solution to this new problem, both preferred and rejected responses tend to decrease in probability" (Abstract) — this is not a training bug, it is proven as a property of the optimum (Lemma 3.2): under a mild condition, "any optimal solution θ to the optimization problem (5) satisfies π_θ(y|x) ≤ e⁻¹ π_ref(y|x)" for in-sample responses, including the winner.
- Appendix C gives a concrete numeric example where both winner and loser probabilities "can decrease arbitrarily and even collapse to 0" while the DPO loss is still exactly zero — the standard DPO optimization problem is under-constrained, so nothing in the loss stops this collapse.
- This generalizes beyond DPO: "similar to DPO, none of these algorithms applies any direct guardrailing on out-of-sample responses" — IPO, CDPO, and other DPO-style losses share the same lack of control over where probability mass ends up.

## Metric / eval choices

- Head-to-head win rate judged by an external LLM: "We compute the win-rate by asking Anthropic's Claude Sonnet-3.5-v2 which response is more helpful" (Section 6.1) — used only for evaluation, not as a training reward.
- MT-Bench (GPT-4-judged, 8 categories: Writing, Roleplay, Reasoning, Math, Coding, Extraction, STEM, Humanities) as the holistic quality check after alignment.
- KL divergence between π_θ and π_ref estimated by sampling N=32 responses per test prompt and averaging log-ratios — a cheap post-hoc guardrail-adherence check that could be reused to sanity-check any DPO run here.

## Open questions for our project

- The malformed-tool-return DPO pairs in our candidate ladder are exactly the setting where likelihood displacement could bite: if the "flag" (winner) response's probability collapses along with the "assert as if valid" (loser) response, first-attempt schema validity or flag rate could degrade even while the DPO loss looks fine. Worth tracking chosen/rejected log-probs during our own DPO runs the way Figure 1 (right) does here.
- All experiments are at 6B–13B scale (GPT-J-6B, Zephyr-7B, Olmo-13B); nothing is shown at ~1.2B, and none of the preference data is tool-call or structured-output specific — unclear whether the same displacement magnitude, or the same λ=2×10⁻⁴, transfers to LFM2.5-1.2B or to a schema-validity-flavored preference set.
- The paper only demonstrates the log-space constraint (Section 5.1) with real experiments; the identity-constraint variant (Section 5.2) is derived but its experimental results are folded into the same aggregate win-rate figures without being clearly isolated — so its practical benefit over the log variant is not separately established here.
