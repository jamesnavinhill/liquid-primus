# 2307.05972v1 — Self-Distilled Quantization: Achieving High Compression Rates in Transformer-Based Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Offers a concrete quantization-aware-distillation technique and a "degrades sharply below 8-bit" pitfall relevant to the project's QAT/distillation step, but all results are on ~125M-parameter encoder-only classification models (XLM-R/InfoXLM), not decoder-only generative or tool-calling tasks, so nothing here settles a decision for a 1.2B GGUF chat/tool model.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes Self-Distilled Quantization (SDQ): during quantization-aware training, distill not just the teacher's output logits (KLD loss) but also the teacher's intermediate self-attention block outputs, because they find quantization error accumulates most at the *output* of self-attention blocks. Applied to INT-8 quantization of XLM-R/InfoXLM (encoder-only, ~1.1GB FP32 multilingual models) on the XGLUE benchmark, this attention-distillation loss beats plain KLD-based distillation and plain QAT/PTQ baselines, but all methods degrade steeply once pushed to 4-bit or 2-bit.

## Techniques to try

- Distill from intermediate self-attention outputs (not just final logits) during quantization-aware training, on top of a standard KLD distillation loss: "we further reduce the compounding effect of quantization errors in the network" by adding "$\ell_{\text{Attention}}$ computes the loss between the student and teacher outputs of each self-attention block in $L$ layers and $H$ attention heads per layer" (Section 3, Eq. 2).
- Locate where quantization error concentrates before designing the distillation loss: "the output layer contains the largest mean absolute error across each layer and highest error variance. In contrast, query, key, value (QKV) parameters have much smaller error" (Section 4). Worth checking whether an analogous layer-wise error profile exists for LFM2.5's hybrid conv/GQA blocks before assuming attention-output distillation is the right target.
- Progressive layer-wise quantization during training (bottom-to-top) combined with distillation, as in their iPQ+SDQ variant: "the upper layers are finetuned while the lower layers are progressively being quantized" (Section 3, "Iterative Product Distilled Quantization").

## Baselines & datasets used

- XGLUE benchmark (9 cross-lingual tasks: XNLI, NC, NER, PAWSX, POS, QAM, QADSM, WPR) with XLM-R_Base and InfoXLM_Base (12 layers, 768 hidden, ~1.1GB FP32 each) — well below the project's target scale but the only quant/distill comparison point in the paper.
- Compared against plain post-training dynamic quantization (PTQ), QuantNoise QAT (QNAT), and iPQ (iterative product quantization), all at INT-8, with fake-quantization sweeps down to 4-bit and 2-bit for a compression-vs-performance curve.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Sharp degradation below 8-bit even with their best method: "performance is generally well maintained up until 8 bits. However, performance significantly degrades for all quantization methods for 4 and 2 bit weights" (Section 4, "Performance versus Compression Rate") — a caution against assuming any distillation trick alone gets a model to 4-bit without larger losses, though this is fake (simulated) low-bit quantization on a 125M-class encoder model, not a real 4-bit GGUF format on a 1.2B decoder.
- Zero-shot cross-lingual generalization suffers disproportionately under quantization: "largest performance drops correspond to languages that [have] less pretraining data and morphologically rich [structure] (Swahili, Urdu, Arabic)" (Section 4) — an analogue for this project's concern that quantization may damage some capability (e.g., structured output) more than others, though here it's cross-lingual generalization rather than tool-calling/instruction-following.
- Attention-output distillation requires two forward passes and cannot use cached teacher logits: "since intermediate layer outputs of the teacher network are required for self-attention distillation, we have to perform two forward passes during training... this is not an option with self-attention outputs as the storage required offline scales with the number of self-attention heads, number of layers and the size of the training data" (Section 6, "Methodological Limitations") — a real compute-cost caveat if adapting this to a QAT/distillation stage.
- Needs an already-fine-tuned, same-task teacher model: "Our method requires a teacher model that is already trained on the downstream task... This is limiting when there are constraints on the computing resources required to produce the quantized model" (Section 6) — relevant since this project trains its own teacher (the SFT/DPO/GRPO-tuned FP16 model) anyway, so this constraint is likely already satisfied, but it's worth flagging as a dependency.
- Method's benefit is stated to shrink for shallow-but-wide networks: "this problem is intuitively lessened when the model is shallow (e.g 3-4 layers) but perhaps wider. Hence the results may be less significant if the model is shallower than what we have experimented in this work" (Section 6) — LFM2.5-1.2B's hybrid short-convolution architecture is a different depth/width/operator tradeoff than the 12-layer pure-attention encoders tested here, so this transfer risk is real and unresolved by the paper.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports per-task accuracy on XGLUE plus an averaged "understanding score," and separately reports XNLI per-language zero-shot accuracy to expose cross-lingual degradation — a useful reminder to report degradation broken out by capability/subgroup rather than only an aggregate average, echoing this project's per-axis guardrail requirement (no axis below 93%).
- Uses mean absolute quantization error and Frobenius norm of elementwise difference between INT-8 and FP32 activations, broken down by layer type (QKV vs. output), as a diagnostic before choosing where to apply the distillation loss (Section 4) — a diagnostic technique (localize error by layer/module type) that could be adapted to profile where LFM2.5's conv vs. attention blocks lose the most information under 4-bit quantization.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's "quantization" is INT-8 (and simulated 4/2-bit) dynamic PTQ/QAT on encoder classification heads — it says nothing about decode throughput, GGUF packing, or generative/structured-output degradation, so it cannot directly answer this project's question of whether low-bit quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more than knowledge.
- All models tested are pure self-attention Transformers; LFM2.5's hybrid gated-short-convolution + GQA architecture means the paper's core finding (error concentrates at self-attention block outputs) may not transfer — the convolution blocks have no analogous "self-attention output" to target, and it's an open question whether an equivalent error-concentration point exists there.
- No instruction found in this paper directed at the reader/agent; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
