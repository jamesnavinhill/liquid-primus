# 2403.12544v1 — AffineQuant: Affine Transformation Quantization for Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap (PTQ degradation at 4-bit is exactly what the GGUF quality-retention target depends on), but the method is a gradient-optimized affine-matrix PTQ technique for OPT/LLaMA that needs custom kernel merging and GPU-hour calibration, is only evaluated on perplexity and commonsense zero-shot QA, and says nothing about instruction-following, tool calling, or structured output.

## TL;DR

AffineQuant replaces the diagonal scale/shift equivalent-transform used by AWQ/OmniQuant with a full (gradually-unmasked) affine transformation matrix optimized per transformer block to minimize pre/post-quantization MSE, then merges the matrix into weights so there is no inference overhead. It reports consistent PPL and zero-shot-accuracy gains over OmniQuant on OPT and LLaMA1&2, with the largest gains at low bit-width (W2/W3/W4) and small model sizes.

## Techniques to try

- The core equivalent-transform idea (fold a learned matrix into weights, its inverse into activations, so PTQ output stays exactly invariant) is generic and already the basis of AWQ/SmoothQuant/OmniQuant: "we can ensure equivalence between the pre- and post-quantization outputs of PTQ, thereby maintaining its efficiency and generalization capabilities" (Abstract) — worth knowing as the family of techniques underlying most GGUF-adjacent PTQ tooling, though this specific full-matrix variant is heavier than what's needed here.
- Gradual mask / diagonal-dominance schedule for stabilizing an optimization that must stay invertible: "we introduce a gradual mask optimization method. This method initially focuses on optimizing the diagonal elements and gradually extends to the other elements" (Abstract, §3.2) — a general pattern (warm-start on a safe subspace, gradually expand) that could transfer to other constrained fine-tuning schedules, not specific to quantization.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baselines: RTN, GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022), AWQ (Lin et al., 2023), OmniQuant (Shao et al., 2023), SmoothQuant, FlexRound.
- Models: OPT (125M–30B) and LLaMA1&2 (7B–30B) — no model at or near the project's 1.2B scale is tested directly, though OPT-125M/1.3B results are the closest small-model data points.
- Datasets/metrics: WikiText2, PTB, C4 perplexity; 6 zero-shot commonsense tasks (PIQA, ARC-e, WinoGrande, BoolQ, ARC-c, HellaSwag). No instruction-following, tool-use, or structured-output benchmark anywhere in the paper.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Weight-activation W4A4 quantization causes large accuracy loss even with the best PTQ method here: LLaMA-7B FP16 zero-shot avg 64.09% drops to 52.65% (OmniQuant) / 53.42% (AffineQuant) — roughly an 11-point absolute drop at 4/4-bit ("Table 2"). This is weight+activation quantization, not the weight-only regime GGUF uses, but it is a useful upper bound on how bad naive low-bit PTQ can get.
- Without the gradual mask, optimization can fail outright: "when we remove the gradual approach, the OPT-125M and LLaMA-7B models exhibit poor performance or fail to complete training" — LLaMA-7B w2a16 WikiText2 PPL goes to NaN without gradual masking ("Table 6", §4.3).
- Stability factor α must be tuned per model/bit-width or training collapses: "a larger stability factor does not guarantee the strictly diagonal dominance of the transformation matrix, which can lead to training collapse" (§4.3) — at 2-bit, LLaMA-7B with α=1e0 or 1e-1 gives NaN PPL ("Table 5").
- Double-precision optimization for the affine matrix inverse is markedly more expensive than float: OPT-6.7B w4a16 calibration takes 16.7h (double) vs 8.65h (float) on a single A100, for only a marginal PPL difference (11.91 vs 11.90) — "this improvement is not significant for larger-scale models" (§4.3, Table 4). Compute cost of this class of PTQ method is nontrivial even before considering it's calibration-only, not fine-tuning.
- The method explicitly excludes the affine transform between the two MLP linear layers because "the optimization of the large transformation matrix in inflated dimensions is challenging" and the activation function breaks equivalence there (§4.1) — a concrete scope limitation of the affine-transform approach.

## Metric / eval choices

- Perplexity (WikiText2/PTB/C4) and 6-task zero-shot commonsense accuracy are the only metrics used; both are knowledge/fluency proxies, not instruction-following or schema-validity proxies, so this paper cannot speak to the project's core question of whether quantization damages structured output more than knowledge.

## Open questions for our project

- No data here on whether affine-transform-style PTQ (or PTQ generally) disproportionately harms instruction-following or JSON/tool-call formatting versus raw perplexity/QA accuracy — this remains untested by the paper and is exactly the open question the project needs to answer itself.
- The paper's weight-only W4A16 results (Table 1) are a better proxy for GGUF-style quantization than its headline W4A4 numbers; if this paper is revisited, use those weight-only rows, not the weight-activation headline numbers, when estimating an expected degradation floor.
- Method requires custom matrix-merge into weights/LayerNorm and calibration-time backprop through transformer blocks with A100-hours; not something to adopt as-is for a GGUF/llama.cpp pipeline without significant reimplementation, so treat as background context only, not a candidate technique.
