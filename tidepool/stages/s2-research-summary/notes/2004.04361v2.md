# 2004.04361v2 — Calibrating Structured Output Predictors for Natural Language Processing

**Relevance:** low
Real overlap on the topic of "confidence-based flagging of structured output," but the method is built for BERT-style encoder sequence-labelling/span-extraction (NER, POS, QA), needs a held-out validation set and 10 extra MC-Dropout forward passes per input to train/run, and never touches decoder-only generation, tool calls, or quantization — none of which transfers cleanly to post-training a 1.2B decoder model under a throughput budget.

## TL;DR

The paper calibrates BERT/DistilBERT confidence scores for NER, POS, and extractive QA by defining a small "event of interest" set (top-k MAP predictions plus MC-Dropout uncertainty and rank features) and training a gradient-boosted-tree forecaster on held-out data; the calibrated scores also double as a free re-ranking signal that gives small accuracy gains. It's a solid general lesson that raw softmax/MAP confidence is badly miscalibrated and that rank/variance features fix most of it, but the mechanism (post-hoc forecaster + repeated dropout sampling) doesn't map onto generating and flagging whole tool calls from a decoder LLM.

## Techniques to try

- The core idea — use MC-Dropout variance plus prediction rank as extra features rather than raw probability alone — is a cheap conceptual pattern for any "should I trust this output" decision: "an additional input of rank k leads to a substantial gain in performance for all models in POS" (Section 4, Discussion). Not directly implementable without paying the MC-Dropout inference cost, but worth remembering as a "confidence needs more than P(y|x)" heuristic when designing the malformed-tool-return flagging signal.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Penn Treebank (POS), CoNLL 2003 and MADE 1.0 (NER), SQuAD1.1 and EMRQA (QA), plus an out-of-domain QA set built by applying EMRQA's question-generation scheme to MADE 1.0 named entities/relations — all BERT-base / BERT+CRF / DistilBERT / bioBERT scale, not decoder LLMs.
- Baselines are Platt scaling and a "Calibrated Mean" GBDT trained on the same length-normalized, MC-Dropout-averaged probability Platt uses.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Re-scoring with the calibrated confidence gave no measurable benefit when the base task was already near-saturated: "In POS no appreciable gain or drop in performance was observed for k = 2. We believe this may be due to the already high token level accuracy (above 97%) on Penn Treebank data" (Section 4, Discussion) — a caution that confidence-based re-ranking/flagging may add nothing on tasks the base model already does well, so it should be validated per-subtask rather than assumed universally useful.
- Including top-k samples in forecaster training without also including the rank feature made calibration worse, not better: "we see large increases in calibration error when the top-k prediction samples are included in forecaster training without including the rank information in tasks such as CoNLL NER and MADE 1.0 QA" (Section 4, Discussion) — a concrete "this ablation hurt" result.
- Out-of-domain calibration stays badly miscalibrated even after the method's in-domain gains, and needs an out-of-domain validation set to fix — which usually doesn't exist: "there is still a large gap between the calibration error for within domain and out-of-domain testing" and "a validation dataset is often not available in the real world. Mitigating the loss in calibration and sharpness induced by out-of-domain evaluation is an important avenue for future research" (Section 4, Discussion, "Out-of-domain Performance"). Directly relevant caution for this project's own guardrail-metric-under-domain-shift concerns, even though the underlying models differ.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses Expected Calibration Error (ECE) with N=20 bins as the calibration metric, and separately reports task accuracy/F1/EM to show the re-ranking step doesn't hurt (and sometimes helps) task performance — a useful reminder to report calibration/flagging metrics and task-quality metrics as a pair rather than one alone, echoing this project's own paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate design.

## Open questions for our project

- No decoder-only or instruction-following/tool-calling setting is discussed anywhere in the paper; it is entirely encoder classification/span-extraction. It offers no evidence on whether MC-Dropout-style uncertainty estimation is even compatible with autoregressive tool-call generation, or what it would cost in latency at 1.2B scale — this would need to be established from scratch if pursued.
- The forecaster requires a dedicated held-out validation split per task and 10 MC-Dropout forward passes at inference; both add cost that is in tension with this project's 5%-throughput-floor constraint and its preference for signals with no extra training/data cost.
