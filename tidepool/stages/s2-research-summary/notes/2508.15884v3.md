# 2508.15884v3 — Jet-Nemotron: Efficient Language Model with Post Neural Architecture Search

**Relevance:** low
This is an architecture-search paper for building new hybrid full-/linear-attention pretrained models from scratch on 32 H100s; it has no post-training, quantization, tool-calling, or structured-output content and does not touch a model we could actually modify, but it does explain why hybrid architectures like LFM2.5's (a few full/GQA layers plus linear-recurrent blocks) preserve accuracy at all.

## TL;DR

Jet-Nemotron uses "Post Neural Architecture Search" (PostNAS) — starting from a pretrained full-attention model, freezing its MLPs, and searching where to place full-attention vs. linear-attention (and a new block, JetBlock) layers — to build 2B/4B hybrid models that match full-attention accuracy at up to 53.6x decode throughput. It's a pretraining/architecture-design paper (NVIDIA-scale compute), not a post-training or quantization paper, so it has little direct bearing on fine-tuning or shipping the already-fixed LFM2.5-1.2B architecture.

## Techniques to try

- None directly applicable — the "techniques" here are architecture-search methods (super-network training + beam search for attention-layer placement, linear-attention block selection, hardware-aware hyperparameter search) that require retraining/searching the base architecture, which is out of scope for a fixed pre-trained LFM2.5 checkpoint being post-trained via LoRA/DPO/GRPO.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Evaluates against Qwen2.5, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Gemma3, Llama3.2, MobileLLM, Smollm2, Mamba2, RWKV7, Zamba2, Hymba, and MoE models (DeepSeek-V3-Small, Moonlight) on MMLU(-Pro), BBH, math (GSM8K, MATH, MathQA, GPQA), commonsense (ARC, PIQA, Winogrande, OBQA, BoolQ, TruthfulQA), retrieval (FDA, SWDE, Squad), coding (EvalPlus, CRUXEval), and LongBench — all useful as a general efficient-small-LM benchmark landscape, but none at our project's exact size (1.2B) or task (tool-calling/schema validity).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Pure linear-attention/SSM models with no or minimal full attention degrade badly on precision-sensitive tasks: e.g. Mamba2-2.7B scores only 14.0 avg on coding (Table 7) and 10.3 avg on long-context (Table 8), vs. 59.5 and 41.1 for Jet-Nemotron-2B — a caution that fully removing attention hurts exactly the kind of structured/precise output our project cares about (LFM2.5 keeps GQA blocks for this reason, which this paper's motivation supports).
- "RWKV7 exhibits significantly lower training throughput compared to other linear attention blocks, possibly due to suboptimal kernel implementation" — an infra/kernel-maturity caveat about linear-attention variants, not something we'd hit since we aren't training a new linear-attention block.
- The full PostNAS + training pipeline cost ~18,000 H100-GPU-hours on 32 GPUs (Table 12) for a 2B model — this scale of experimentation is unavailable to us and not the right comparison point for a LoRA/DPO-scale post-training project.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses generation throughput and KV-cache size (not parameter count) as the efficiency target: "the decoding stage is typically memory-bandwidth-bound rather than compute-bound... Reducing its size decreases memory transfer time per decoding step and enables a larger batch size, thereby improving the generation throughput." This reinforces (but doesn't newly justify) our project's own throughput-floor framing, though our floor is decode tokens/s within 5% of base rather than KV-cache size.
- Finds that "multiple-choice tasks mainly rely on the pattern-matching property of the softmax operation to route the knowledge of answers to their options," which is why they keep sliding-window attention layers for MMLU-style tasks — a mechanistic note on why softmax attention layers matter for certain answer-routing tasks, tangentially relevant to why we shouldn't expect LoRA to touch attention layers that matter for such routing.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's final Jet-Nemotron models use a 151,643-token vocabulary, more than double LFM2.5's 65,536 — it offers no data on vocabulary-size effects on tokenization efficiency for JSON/tool-call syntax, so it can't answer our open question about whether LFM2.5's smaller vocab handicaps structured output.
- No content on quantization, GGUF, LoRA/DPO/GRPO, schema validity, or malformed-tool-call detection — none of our pre-registered claims or sub-criteria are addressed by this paper.
