# 2603.07211v2 — CompassDPO: Dynamics-Controlled Direct Preference Optimization for Robust Safety Alignment

**Relevance:** medium
Not about structured output, quantization, or tool-calling, but it's a directly relevant robustness patch for the DPO stage of our candidate ladder if our generated preference pairs (including synthetic malformed-tool-return pairs) turn out noisy or heterogeneous in quality.

## TL;DR

The paper shows that vanilla DPO training can be silently dominated by a small subset of "high-loss" or "wrong-direction" preference pairs within a mini-batch, and that this — not just label noise per se — degrades alignment quality. CompassDPO adds two lightweight, reward-free training-time controls (sparse directional loss-mixing and adaptive soft loss-capping) computed only from the DPO margin already available during training, and shows these controls make DPO noticeably more robust to synthetic label-flip noise across four backbones (2.8B–8B) on safety alignment.

## Techniques to try

- Diagnose DPO batch dynamics before trusting a DPO run: track "tail energy share" (fraction of gradient energy from the top 30% of samples) and the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index of per-sample gradient energy; both metrics are backbone/domain-agnostic and cheap to log ("(i) tail energy share, the fraction of gradient energy contributed by the top $30\%$ energy samples within each mini-batch; (ii) the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI)" — Section 3.1). Could be applied directly to our own DPO run on malformed-tool-return preference pairs to check for gradient dominance before spending eval budget.
- If generated preference pairs are suspected to contain noisy/wrong-direction labels (plausible for synthetically constructed malformed-tool-return pairs), a cheap patch is "sparse, budgeted, and warm-up delayed loss mixing to attenuate update components that conflict with the emerging preference direction" (Abstract) rather than discarding or relabeling data.
- Soft-cap high-loss "tail" pairs instead of dropping them: "it softly winsorizes the excessive part of tail losses, limiting their influence while preserving their gradients" (Section 3.3) — the ablation (Appendix E.4) shows hard truncation of tail losses is worse for general capability and over-refusal than soft capping, even though it looks slightly better on the safety metric alone.
- Delay any directional/robustness correction with a warm-up period — the paper found no-warm-up directional mixing actively hurts training because early-training margins are unstable: "removing warm-up ($\alpha=0$) noticeably hurts performance... CompassDPO can underperform standard DPO when $\alpha=0$" (Appendix E.2). They use $\alpha=0.3$ (30% of training steps).
- Reported to add negligible compute: "CompassDPO introduces little additional overhead compared with vanilla DPO" — 21:34±12s vs 21:36±8s on Llama-3.2-3B, 46:47±11s vs 46:35±15s on Llama-3-8B (Table 6, Appendix E.6).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Dataset: PKU-SafeRLHF-30K (26,874 train / 2,989 test preference pairs, safety-labeled).
- OOD generalization benchmarks: Do-Not-Answer, HarmBench, HH-RLHF (red-teaming split), Salad Bench — all evaluated as Attack Success Rate via two separate judge models (MD-Judge-v0.2-internlm2_7b and Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Safety-Guard-8B-v3).
- Baselines compared: DPO, IPO, cDPO, rDPO, Dr.DPO (the strongest baseline throughout).
- Backbones: Pythia-2.8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B — all larger than our 1.2B target; smallest tested backbone is 2.8B, so scale-down behavior to 1.2B is untested.
- General-capability/over-refusal auxiliary evaluation: MMLU-Pro, MT-Bench, OR-Bench (Hard-1K and Toxic subsets).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Vanilla DPO degrades sharply and monotonically as synthetic label-flip rate rises from 0% to 30% on Pythia-2.8B (Table 3), confirming that noisy/generated preference pairs are a real risk for a DPO stage, not just a theoretical concern.
- "these approaches typically apply a unified correction rule, offering limited control over how different samples shape the direction and magnitude of the batch-level update" — global corrections like cDPO and rDPO only partially fix the problem; cDPO in particular sometimes underperforms plain DPO out-of-distribution (Table 2, Llama-3-8B: cDPO AVG ASR 30.7 vs DPO 22.85).
- Hard truncation of tail-loss samples (an obvious simpler alternative) "degrades both MMLU-Pro and MT-Bench, and increases over-refusal on OR-Hard-1K" relative to soft capping (Appendix E.4) — a caution against naively filtering out "bad" preference pairs from a preference dataset rather than down-weighting them.
- Explicitly flagged as an open limitation: "CompassDPO does not specifically target over-refusal" — and separately, "all aligned methods exhibit high over-refusal rates on OR-Hard-1K, indicating that benign but challenging queries remain difficult after safety alignment" (Appendix E.3). This is directly analogous to our flag/false-flag tension: better "refuse/flag on bad input" behavior did not come for free and over-triggering on benign-but-hard inputs remained a problem for every method tested, including the proposed one.
- Robustness gains shrink at larger model scale within their tested range: "the improvements on Pythia-2.8B are larger than those on Llama-3.2-3B... On Llama-3.2-3B, strong baselines already achieve low ASR, which limits the attainable improvement" (Appendix E.1) — suggests the value of this kind of dynamics control may be inversely related to model capability/scale, which is a relevant hint (untested) for a 1.2B model being even more sensitive.
- Method depends on reasonably reliable batch-level statistics: "very small batches may provide less reliable estimates" (Limitations).

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses GPT-4.1 mini as an LLM judge for pairwise Win Rate and for MT-Bench/OR-Bench scoring, and two separate open-weight safety-classifier judges (MD-Judge, Nemotron Safety Guard) for Attack Success Rate — i.e., three different model judges used purely for *evaluation*, not as a training reward, which is consistent with our project's "no model-scored stored reward reused as a training signal" constraint (their training signal is only the implicit DPO margin, never a judge score).
- Uses two independent judges for the same ASR metric specifically to avoid single-judge bias — a pattern worth copying for our malformed-tool-return flag/false-flag eval, which currently seems to rely on a single scoring path.

## Open questions for our project

- Everything here is safety-preference alignment on general chat responses at 2.8B+ scale; it is untested whether "gradient dominance" from a small subset of samples is also the dominant failure mode for a much narrower, more structured preference task like malformed-tool-return flagging at 1.2B, or whether structured-output DPO has different dynamics (e.g., near-binary correctness rather than graded preference).
- If we use DPO with generated malformed-tool-return pairs and see instability or poor generalization, the diagnostic metrics (tail energy share, HHI) and the directional/magnitude control mechanisms here are cheap to try as a drop-in fix before concluding "DPO doesn't work for this signal" — but this has not been validated on anything resembling our task or model scale, so it should be treated as a candidate mitigation, not a proven one.
- No instruction-like text directed at a reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
