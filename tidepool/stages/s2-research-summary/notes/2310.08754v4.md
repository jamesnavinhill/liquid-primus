# 2310.08754v4 — Tokenizer Choice For LLM Training: Negligible or Crucial?

**Relevance:** low
The paper studies tokenizer *training/selection during pretraining* (algorithm, library, vocab size) for 2.6B models built from scratch, whereas our project post-trains a fixed model with an already-baked 65,536-token vocabulary and tokenizer we cannot retrain or swap — so its actionable levers do not transfer, though its vocab-size/downstream-performance findings offer weak indirect context for the "does 65K vocab handicap tool-call/JSON tokenization" question.

## TL;DR

The authors train 24 mono-/multilingual BPE and Unigram tokenizers (33k-100k vocab, HuggingFace vs SentencePiece libraries) and a matched 2.6B decoder-only model for each, to isolate the tokenizer's effect on downstream zero-shot accuracy and training compute. They find tokenizer choice measurably moves downstream accuracy and cost, but the common intrinsic proxies (fertility, parity) do not reliably predict which tokenizer will win downstream.

## Techniques to try

- None directly applicable: the paper's lever is training a new tokenizer before pretraining a model from scratch, not something available when post-training a released model with a fixed tokenizer like LFM2.5.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Downstream eval suite spans NLI (XNLI, MNLI, RTE, WNLI, CB), QA (X-CSQA, XStoryCloze, PubMedQA), reading comprehension (BoolQ, LAMBADA, RACE, MRPC), commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC, XCOPA, XCODAH, WSC, COPA), and classification (PAWS-X, GNAD10, SST, WIC, PIQA) — all general NLU/commonsense, none touch structured output, tool-calling, or code/JSON.
- Baseline comparator is the pretrained GPT-2 tokenizer/vocab, trained the same way as the ablated tokenizers.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "the common tokenizer evaluation metrics fertility and parity are not always predictive of model downstream performance, rendering these metrics a questionable proxy for the model's downstream performance" (Abstract) — a caution against trusting intrinsic tokenizer stats (like fertility on JSON/tool-call strings) as a stand-in for actual task performance.
- "we find that there is no distinct correlation across all tasks and languages, demanding a more granular analysis" (Section 6) — correlation between fertility/parity and downstream accuracy is inconsistent and task-specific, sometimes even negative.
- "using a pre-trained GPT-2 tokenizer to pre-train and fine-tune multilingual models should be omitted" (Section 5.2, Multilingual Tokenizer) — an English-centric tokenizer applied to multilingual training degrades performance and raises training cost by up to 68%.
- "we did not investigate whether the results obtained could be extrapolated to larger model sizes" (Section 8, Limitations) — all downstream numbers are from 2.6B-parameter models, one scale point, with no hyperparameter tuning per tokenizer and no seed-variation check.

## Metric / eval choices

- Fertility: "the average number of tokens that are required to represent a word or document," computed via whitespace-split word counts on a 10,000-document held-out set (Section 3.4).
- Parity (from Petrov et al. 2023): ratio of tokenized-sequence lengths between a non-English language and English on FLORES-200 parallel sentences, testing whether a tokenizer treats languages equitably.
- Compute-cost formula per training step, C = 96Bslh²(1 + s/6h + V/16lh), used to translate vocabulary size directly into training FLOPs (Eq. 1, Section 3.4) — a reusable way to reason about how vocab size trades off against fertility for total compute, relevant background if we ever need to justify LFM2.5's 65K vocab compute footprint.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper only measures zero-shot NLU/commonsense accuracy; it gives no signal on how tokenizer/vocabulary choice affects tool-call schema validity, JSON well-formedness, or code tokenization — exactly the axis our project cares about for the 65K vs 128K vocabulary question. That gap is not answered here and would need a different source.
- All findings are from tokenizers trained jointly with pretraining; nothing here addresses whether a fixed, already-trained tokenizer (our situation) can be partially mitigated at the post-training stage (e.g., via data mix or special tokens) when its vocabulary is suboptimal for a target domain.
