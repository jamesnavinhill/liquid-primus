# 2501.17994v1 — InnerThoughts: Disentangling Representations and Predictions in Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
The method is a cheap frozen-backbone alternative to LoRA, but it only targets closed-set multiple-choice classification on a 70B model, not open-ended structured/tool-call generation on a 1.2B model, so nothing here settles a decision for this project.

## TL;DR

The paper trains a small frozen-backbone neural network ("InnerThoughts") on the hidden states from *every* transformer layer at the last token position to pick the answer letter in multiple-choice QA, using Llama3 70B, and shows it recovers part of QLoRA's accuracy gain at a fraction of the compute. It is a calibration/classification-head technique, not a recipe for generating well-formed tool calls or JSON, so it doesn't map onto this project's agent-turn prediction unit.

## Techniques to try

- A cheap frozen-backbone probe (single forward pass, no backward pass through the LLM) is conceivably relevant to the malformed-tool-return flag/no-flag sub-task, which is itself a small classification problem: "we propose to attach to the LLM a module that makes predictions about the answer label from the hidden states produced by every layer at the final token position... training requires no backward passes through the LLM. Only a single forward pass is required to initially extract the hidden states, keeping the training costs to a minimum." (Section 1) — but this would mean shipping an extra module alongside the quantized GGUF, which cuts against the "single 4-bit GGUF" deployment target.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Datasets: AQuA, CosmosQA, MMLU, MedMCQA, LogiQA, HellaSwag — all closed-set multiple-choice QA, none of them structured-output/tool-calling benchmarks (Section 4.1).
- Baselines compared: Direct softmax-the-logits querying, Calibrate-before-use (Zhao et al.), Linear Probe Calibration / "Logistic on logits" (Abbas et al.), and QLoRA fine-tuning (rank r=1) as an upper bound, all on **Llama3 70B** — well above this project's 1.2B target and above the brief's 8B preference threshold.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Gains are wildly uneven across datasets and near zero on some: "Gain in accuracy... MMLU +00.20, MedMCQA +00.39" versus "AQuA +10.75, HellaSwag +05.04" (Table 4) — the method mainly helps where the base model's top-2 answer confidence margin is already small, not universally.
- An architecture variant trained on layer-wise additive contributions instead of raw hidden states ("DiffHiddenThoughts") "does not seem to make much of a difference" versus the plain hidden-state version (Appendix D).
- A self-attention-based predictor variant "results are slightly worse" than the MLP-Mixer-style predictor used in the main method (Appendix E).
- Statistical (Brier-score) calibration is not reliably improved: "the method does not appear to change the amount of statistical calibration in a noticeable pattern, with only MMLU and LogiQA offering any big change, in opposite directions" (Appendix C).
- Explicitly scoped to closed-set classification only: "One limitation of the current work is that we restricted ourselves to multiple-choice question answering problems" (Section 6) — the authors themselves flag that extending to open-ended/numeric answers (e.g. GSM8k-style) would require turning the predictor into a regression model, and they never attempt it.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports accuracy with bootstrapped 95% confidence interval widths and a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (p<0.05 threshold) to certify a method beats the Direct baseline, rather than relying on point-estimate deltas alone (Table 3).
- Also reports Brier score as a distinct "statistical calibration" metric, explicitly separated from the "calibration" literature's bias-correction sense used elsewhere in the paper (Appendix C) — a useful terminological distinction if this project's notes ever use "calibration" loosely.

## Open questions for our project

- The technique's benefit is tied to the base model hesitating between its top-2 discrete label choices; the agent-turn prediction unit here is open-ended (tool call JSON, natural-language answer, or flag), so it's unclear the same probe-on-frozen-hidden-states idea would transfer at all, let alone at 1.2B scale.
- All results are on a 70B model with 4x V100 32GB GPUs; no evidence at anything near 1.2B, so compute/accuracy ratios reported here (roughly "35% of the gains of QLoRA at 16% of the computational costs") cannot be assumed to hold at this project's scale.
- No experiments involve quantization, GGUF export, or throughput — irrelevant to the 4-bit deployment constraint.
