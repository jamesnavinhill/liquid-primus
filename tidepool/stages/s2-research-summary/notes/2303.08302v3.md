# 2303.08302v3 — ZeroQuant-V2: Exploring Post-training Quantization in LLMs from Comprehensive Study to Low Rank Compensation

**Relevance:** high
Directly pressure-tests this project's "≥97% full-precision retention, no axis below 93%" target for the 4-bit GGUF build by showing INT4 *weight-only* quantization is empirically **harder to recover for smaller models**, and offers an adoptable low-rank correction technique (LoRC) to close that gap.

## TL;DR

A systematic PTQ study (RTN, GPTQ, ZeroQuant-Global/Local) across OPT and BLOOM (125M–176B), measured by perplexity, finds that smaller models suffer *more* PPL degradation under INT4 weight-only quantization than larger ones, and that no existing PTQ method fully closes the gap to FP16. The paper proposes LoRC — a low-rank factorization of the quantization error matrix — as a cheap add-on that recovers most of the remaining quality loss with negligible size overhead.

## Techniques to try

- Fine-grained (block-wise) weight quantization instead of per-row/per-tensor scaling: "One potential solution to this issue is the implementation of finer-grained quantization schemes [5], where every k elements possess their own scaling factor and/or zero point." (Section 4.1) — block sizes of 64–256 are recommended; smaller models needed block size down to 32 before gains saturated.
- LoRC: factor the quantization error `E := W − Ŵ` with two low-rank matrices via SVD and add it back: "LoRC approximates the error E with Eˆ = UˆVˆ by using two low-rank matrices Uˆ and Vˆ." (Section 5) — a rank as low as m=8 was sufficient ("the enhancements achieved through LoRC begin to plateau as the dimension m surpasses 4"), and the two correction matrices can themselves be stored in INT8 with no measurable loss (Table 8), so the overhead ratio is "3m/2h ≤ 0.016" of model size.
- Recommended settings by model scale (their own take-away): "(3) For smaller models (<1B), per-row W8A8 (INT8 weight and INT8 activation) RTN is enough based on [36]" — i.e. for a model near our 1.2B scale, INT4 weight+INT8 activation RTN alone is explicitly *not* their recommendation; they'd push toward INT8, not INT4, unless fine-grained + LoRC is layered on.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Model families: OPT (125M–66B) and BLOOM (560M–176B); quantization methods compared: RTN, GPTQ, ZeroQuant-Global, ZeroQuant-Local, all with asymmetric and symmetric variants.
- Datasets: perplexity on WikiText-2, PTB, and C4 only — no downstream/task benchmarks. Calibration data for GPTQ/ZeroQuant: "we use C4 dataset to randomly select 128 sentences for training and each of them has 2048 tokens."

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Counter-intuitive size trend for weight quantization: "INT4 weight-only quantization for small models results in substantial accuracy degradation (Class-3), but this effect lessens as the model size increases (Class-2)" (Findings 1) — i.e. the smaller the model, the worse INT4 weight quantization tends to hurt it, which is a direct warning sign for a 1.2B target.
- Even with the best PTQ optimization methods, "none of the current methods can achieve the original model quality for quantization with either INT4-weight or INT4-weight-and-INT8-activation (W4A8)" and for W4A8 specifically: "all of the results fall into either Class-2 or Class-3. This suggests that for certain applications, it might be more beneficial to use smaller models with fewer parameters rather than larger, quantized models."
- Practical kernel support is still missing: "Although we use fine-grained quantization schemes in the paper, the real implementation is missing. Moreover, how to efficiently implement odd bit precision is challenging." (Future Opportunity) — the fine-grained/LoRC gains shown here are simulated in PPL evaluation, not yet a shipped inference kernel, in 2023.
- LoRC's benefit direction flips between PTQ and QAT: a PTQ-tuned low-rank dimension (m≈8) saturates quickly, but "when the two low-rank matrices are updated during QAT, we arrive at contrasting conclusions" — larger LoRC rank keeps helping under QAT. Any attempt to reuse LoRC in our quantization-aware training/distillation stage should not assume the PTQ-tuned rank transfers.
- Explicitly scoped as PPL-only: "our study was constrained by our computing resources... caution should be exercised when generalizing these findings to tasks that are dissimilar to those covered in this study" — no evidence here about instruction-following, tool-call schema validity, or any structured-output metric.

## Metric / eval choices

- Quantization sensitivity is bucketed into three classes by PPL degradation: Class-1 ≤0.1, Class-2 >0.1 & ≤0.5, Class-3 >0.5 PPL points, justified as "PPL is significantly correlated to zero-shot/few-shot accuracy measurement." No task-specific accuracy, BFCL-style, or JSON/schema-validity metric is used anywhere in the paper.

## Open questions for our project

- All tested architectures are dense decoder-only transformers (OPT, BLOOM); nothing here tests a hybrid short-convolution + GQA architecture like LFM2, so it's unclear whether the "smaller models degrade more under INT4 weight quantization" trend even applies to our architecture family.
- The paper never measures structured-output/tool-calling degradation under quantization — it cannot directly answer this project's question "does low-bit quantization damage structured output and instruction-following more than it damages knowledge?" It only establishes that knowledge-proxy PPL itself degrades more for small models, which is a lower bound on the risk, not an answer.
- Would LoRC (or a similar low-rank error-compensation step) be compatible with a 65,536-vocab embedding/output layer at 1.2B scale, and would it survive a subsequent quantization-aware distillation pass, given the paper's own caveat that PTQ-tuned rank does not transfer to QAT?
- No instruction-like text addressed to the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research report throughout.
