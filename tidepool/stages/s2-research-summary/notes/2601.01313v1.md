# 2601.01313v1 — Spectral-Window Hybrid (SWH)

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap (hybrid architectures for efficient sequence modeling, the same general family as LFM2.5's design) but this paper is about training a new 125M-scale architecture from scratch, not about post-training, quantizing, or specializing an existing instruct model for tool calling — nothing here is actionable for this project's plan.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes SWH, a new sequence-model architecture that runs a global causal spectral convolution (FFT-based, O(T log T)) in parallel with local chunked sliding-window attention, then sums the two branches. It is trained from scratch at 125M parameters on FineWeb-Edu and on synthetic algorithmic tasks, showing lower perplexity than a matched Transformer baseline and much better long-context efficiency, but it says nothing about post-training, quantization, or tool-calling/structured-output behavior.

- (Not applicable to this project — SWH is a from-scratch pretraining architecture change, not a post-training, quantization, or decoding-time technique that could be applied to an already-trained 1.2B model like LFM2.5.)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Real-data comparison: "We trained a 125M parameter class model on the FineWeb-Edu dataset (HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu sample-10BT" — Section 3.2, with baseline and SWH both at L=12, D=768, H=12, block size 1024, trained on two Kaggle T4 GPUs.
- Synthetic diagnostic suite of five tasks (Associative Recall, Induction Head, Sorting, Length Generalization, Needle-in-a-Haystack) at D=128, L=2, H=4, 3,000 training steps — Section 3.1 / Appendix A.2.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- The baseline Transformer "fails completely on the Needle task (0.00 accuracy) when the sequence length increases beyond training limits" (Table 2), i.e. standard attention has zero length-generalization on needle-in-haystack in this small-scale setup — relevant only as a general reminder that base architecture choice affects long-context robustness, not directly transferable to LFM2.5 post-training.
- Even SWH's own length-generalization numbers are weak in absolute terms (Needle accuracy only 0.05, LenGen 0.05 vs baseline's 0.02), so the "superior generalization" claim is a small-sample, small-scale result, not a strong positive one.

## Metric / eval choices

- Efficiency reported as inference latency and VRAM vs. sequence length T ∈ [512, 1024, 2048, 4096], not throughput/quantization-related metrics: "At T=4096, SWH reduces latency by approximately 60% (0.6s) and maintains constant memory usage" — Section 3.3. No BFCL, IFEval, MMLU-Pro, or any instruction-following/tool-use benchmark appears anywhere in the paper.
