# 2403.08370v3 — SMART: Submodular Data Mixture Strategy for Instruction Tuning

**Relevance:** medium
Offers a concrete, reusable technique for the "data-mix ablation" step on the candidate ladder (and for the general-vs-specialized data balance question), but every number is measured at 7B on generic FLAN/MMLU/BBH benchmarks with no tool-calling, JSON, or structured-output evaluation, and no quantization angle.

## TL;DR

SMART picks instruction-tuning data mixtures with a two-stage submodular optimization instead of manual/intuition-based weighting: first it scores and selects a weighted subset of tasks from a huge multitask pool, then it picks non-redundant instances within each selected task's budget. Across three 7B models on FLAN 2022, SMART mixtures beat both proportional and equal-mixing baselines at every training-budget size tested, and a small subset of ~16 well-chosen tasks can recover most of the performance of using all 1840 tasks — which is directly relevant to how one would build a balanced tool-calling + general-instruction SFT mix without eroding guardrail metrics.

## Techniques to try

- Two-stage submodular data mixture selection: Stage-1 picks a weighted subset of tasks with a submodular function $f_1$ over task embeddings (average prompt embeddings), converting greedy value-gains into per-task instance budgets via a Taylor-softmax; Stage-2 picks the actual instances per task with a (possibly different) submodular function $f_2$ over prompt-embedding similarity. ("SMART models the above problem as a sequence of two cardinality-constrained submodular maximization problems." — Section 3)
- Grid search found Graph Cut best for task-subset selection and Facility Location best for instance selection when using the full task set; but the right instance-selection function is budget-dependent: representation (Facility Location) matters more when many tasks share a small budget, diversity (Graph Cut/Log-Determinant) matters more when each task gets a generous budget. ("For higher $M^{\prime}$s, ... the need for representation dominates the need for diversity; however when there is sufficient enough budget for each task, i.e., for lower $M^{\prime}$s, the need for diversity takes over." — Section 4.8)
- Cheap, model-agnostic task/prompt embeddings suffice: a 340M sentence encoder (GTE-large) computed once and reused across LLMs, rather than model-derived task embeddings. ("we encode prompts in the collection with GTE-large (Li et al., 2023b), a light-weight (340M parameters) BERT-based effective sentence encoder" — Section 4.1)
- Task pruning as a budget-allocation lever: rather than always adding more tasks, allocate the budget to a small representative subset. ("close to 97% of performance achievable by using the entire FLAN collection can be attained with just a subset of 16 representative tasks alone with $N^{\prime}=200000$." — Section 4.6)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Ground set: FLAN 2022 (Longpre et al. 2023 / Chung et al. 2022) — 1840 tasks, ~17.5M (prompt, response) pairs, combining FLAN 2021, T0, NIV2, CoT, and Dialog sub-mixtures.
- Baselines compared: Examples Proportional Mixture (sample in proportion to each task's size, i.e. random pooling), Equal Mixture (equal budget per task), and the FLANv2-mix fixed sub-mixture weights (40/32/20/5/3%) from Longpre et al. (2023).
- Models: Llama-2-7B, Falcon-7B, Mistral-7B, each fine-tuned 1 epoch, batch size 64, 8×A100-80GB with Flash Attention.
- Evaluation sets: MMLU (57 tasks, zero-shot exact match) and BBH (23 tasks, zero-shot exact match), scored as a weighted average — both are knowledge/reasoning benchmarks, not tool-calling or structured-output benchmarks.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Falcon-7B produced BBH exact-match of exactly 0 even after instruction tuning on every mixture tested: "For Falcon, we only report MMLU-zero-shot since Falcon gets an exact match of 0.0 for BBH-zero-shot even after fine-tuning." (Section 4.9) — a reminder that some architectures/scales can totally fail a structured/challenging eval regardless of data mixture, which is a caution when judging whether a metric floor is being missed for data-mix reasons versus model-capability reasons.
- SMART's task-scaling curve is non-monotonic and can regress: after an optimal point (~16 tasks in their setup) adding more tasks to the mix *reduces* performance, unlike the baselines which improve monotonically with more tasks. This means naively adding more instruction-tuning task diversity is not always safe.
- The method gives no principled way to find that optimal task-pruning point in advance: "it doesn't say anything on how to find the optimal point as it may depend on language model, the total budget and most importantly the underlying dataset." (Section 7, Limitations)
- The approach is explicitly not model-specific — task/instance selection is computed once from generic sentence embeddings and reused across LLMs, meaning it does not adapt to a particular target model's weaknesses (e.g., its own structured-output failure modes) without further work per the authors' own stated future work.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports Exact Match on MMLU and BBH after greedy decoding with light post-processing (lower-casing, punctuation removal) — a simple string-match protocol, not a JSON/schema validity check, so it does not transfer directly to this project's schema-validity or flag-rate metrics.
- Aggregates as a single "weighted average" of MMLU exact matches (57 tasks) and BBH exact matches (23 tasks) for headline comparisons — useful pattern for reporting one number from many sub-tasks, though the specific weighting isn't spelled out beyond "weighted average."

## Open questions for our project

- Could Stage-1/Stage-2 submodular selection be applied at a much smaller scale (task pool = {tool-calling formats, JSON schema variants, general instruction data, guardrail-preserving data}) to build the data-mix ablation step's training set, instead of hand-picking ratios? The paper's machinery (sentence-encoder embeddings + SUBMODLIB) is lightweight enough to try even for 1.2B-scale SFT.
- Does the "task pruning can help more than task scaling" finding generalize to a mix that must simultaneously preserve five separate guardrail metrics (IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond) — i.e., would submodular selection of a small set of representative tasks risk sacrificing one of the guardrail axes even while raising the headline weighted average, the way BBH-Algorithmic actually dropped for SMART at some budgets in Table 2 (e.g. 20.1 vs EPM's 26.36 at $N'=100000$) even as MMLU+BBH weighted average rose?
- No content in this paper reads as an instruction addressed to the reader; the acknowledgements note ChatGPT was used only for paraphrasing/summarizing prose, which is a disclosure, not a directive, and is otherwise irrelevant to this project.
