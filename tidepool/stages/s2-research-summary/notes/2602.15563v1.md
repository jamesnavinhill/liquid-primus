# 2602.15563v1 — 1-Bit Wonder: Improving QAT Performance in the Low-Bit Regime through K-Means Quantization

**Relevance:** medium
Directly relevant to the GGUF quantization decision (nonlinear/codebook quantization beats uniform int at low bit-widths, and perplexity is a misleading quality proxy), but all experiments are QAT-from-scratch pretraining runs on general LM benchmarks at 0.8B–31B scale, not post-hoc quantization of an existing small instruct model, and never touch tool-calling/structured-output metrics.

## TL;DR

The paper trains Llama-style models from scratch with quantization-aware training and compares uniform integer weight quantization against a learned nonlinear k-means (centroid/codebook) format. It finds k-means quantization strictly dominates uniform integer quantization below ~4.25 bits/weight, and that under a fixed inference memory budget it's better to have more, lower-precision parameters than fewer, higher-precision ones — down to a 31B 1-bit model beating a memory-matched 12B 4-bit model and a 4B bf16 baseline on most downstream generative benchmarks.

## Techniques to try

- Prefer a nonlinear/codebook (k-means-style) low-bit quantizer over plain uniform-integer quantization when picking the 4-bit GGUF format, since the paper finds "k-means strictly dominates uniform quantization" at low bit-widths and remains stable at 1-bit "without requiring mean-shifting or other stabilization tricks" ("Importantly, we find that this nonlinear format remains stable even in the 1-bit regime, without requiring mean-shifting or other stabilization tricks." — Section 2.2, Nonlinear k-means quantization). This maps onto llama.cpp's importance-matrix/codebook GGUF types (IQ-series) as the closer analogue to test against plain Q4_0-style uniform int.
- Keep embeddings and the output projection in higher precision and only quantize the backbone: "All models are trained with backbone-only weight quantization; embeddings and output projections remain in bf16." (Section 3, intro paragraph) — worth checking whether the target GGUF build already does this for the 65,536-token vocabulary, since embedding/LM-head weight count is a larger fraction of a 1.2B model than of the 4B–31B models studied here.
- Warm up QAT for ~1,000 steps in full precision before turning quantization on, and freeze k-means centroids once learned at that point, rather than quantizing from step zero: "For k-means quantization, the centroids are learned at the onset of QAT and then frozen." (Section 2.2, Quantization-aware training schedule) — relevant if the candidate ladder reaches quantization-aware distillation/training on the LFM2.5 checkpoint.
- Do not trust perplexity/logprob evals alone to pick a quantization scheme or checkpoint: "in our experiments these pretraining metrics are not an accurate predictor for downstream generative model performance" (Appendix A.7) — reinforces evaluating the 4-bit build directly on BFCLv3/IFEval/schema-validity rather than on loss.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Uniform (symmetric) integer quantization is the baseline format compared against k-means, both with block size 64 and 16-bit block scales (Section 2.2).
- Pretraining/scaling-law runs on Nemotron-CC; longer runs use a curriculum mixing Nemotron-CC, Starcoder-V2, and FineMath-3+/4+, followed by SFT on the Tulu 3 SFT mixture (Section 3.1, 3.2, Appendix A.2/A.5).
- Downstream generative evals: MMLU, HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-C/E (logprob), HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MMLU-Pro-COT, IFEval, and AidanBench (coherence scored by GPT-4-mini as judge) (Section 3.2).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Pretraining loss/logprob evaluations gave the opposite ranking from downstream generative evaluations: the 12B 4-bit model was "consistently on-par with or better than" the 31B 1-bit model on logprob evals during training, but the 31B 1-bit model was on par with or better on the actual downstream generative benchmark table (Appendix A.7 vs. Table 1) — a direct warning against using perplexity as a quantization quality gate.
- Uniform integer quantization at ≤1-bit is unstable: "For the 1-bit format, we observe severe optimization instabilities that frequently lead to NaNs early in training," requiring BitNet-style mean-subtraction to fix (Section 2.2, Uniform integer quantization) — a pitfall specific to uniform int formats that k-means avoids.
- Normalization choice (absmean vs. absmax) matters a lot for uniform quantization at low bit-widths but barely at all for k-means: "This design choice has a substantial impact on uniform linear quantization at very low bit-widths... In contrast, the k-means quantizer is largely insensitive to the normalization choice" (Appendix A.6).
- Efficiency gains are batch-size-limited: the custom Triton LUT kernel gets near-optimal 4-bit speedup only at small batch sizes, with "no speedup observed by batch size 256," and the paper's own limitations section flags this as a concern for "deployment scenarios, including those with substantial prefill compute" (Section 3.3, Section 5 Limitations) — batch size 1 (this project's single-GPU serving case) is the favorable regime, but it's worth noting the speedup evaporates outside that regime.
- The 31B 1-bit model, despite winning on quality, is slower than the 4-bit model in tokens/s (60.7 vs. 120.0 tok/s) and much slower than bf16 in relative terms once model size differs — precision and parameter count are not independently controllable in their setup, unlike this project's fixed-architecture case.

## Metric / eval choices

- The paper explicitly critiques prior quantization literature for relying on "training or validation loss, or token-level log-likelihood evaluations" and argues for generative downstream benchmarks instead (Introduction) — same philosophy as this project's BFCLv3/IFEval-based gating, reinforcing that choice.
- AidanBench uses an LLM-judge (GPT-4-mini) for a coherence score, which is a model-scored metric the reader brief's standing constraints would rule out as a training signal, though the paper only uses it for evaluation, not as a reward.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the experiments quantize an already-trained instruction-tuned checkpoint (all QAT is from-scratch pretraining) — it's unclear whether the "k-means beats uniform int at low bit" and "1-bit is stable without tricks" findings transfer to quantizing/fine-tuning an existing 1.2B hybrid-architecture model rather than training one from scratch under QAT the whole time.
- No result at or near 1.2B parameters (smallest studied is 0.8B, but only in the scaling-law grid, not the downstream-benchmark table); no evidence on how a 65,536-token vocabulary (small relative to the 128,256 Llama 3 vocabulary used here) interacts with quantizing embedding/output layers.
- No tool-calling, JSON/schema, or structured-output evaluation anywhere in the paper — cannot directly answer whether low-bit quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more than knowledge, which is one of this project's central open questions.
- The k-means/LUT kernel work targets an inference-focused GPU (L40S) with a custom Triton kernel and a modified Marlin kernel, not the GGUF/llama.cpp CPU-and-consumer-GPU path this project needs; adopting the quantization philosophy (nonlinear codebook over uniform int) is plausible, but the specific kernel engineering here does not transfer.
