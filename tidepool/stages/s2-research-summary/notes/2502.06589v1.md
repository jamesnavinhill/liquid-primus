# 2502.06589v1 — Hephaestus: Improving Fundamental Agent Capabilities of Large Language Models Through Continual Pre-Training

**Relevance:** medium
Offers useful context on preserving general capabilities while specializing a model for agentic/function-calling tasks, but its core method (103B-token continual pre-training corpus, 8B-scale model, huge compute budget) doesn't transfer to a 1.2B model post-trained via the project's LoRA/SFT/DPO/GRPO ladder on managed hardware.

## TL;DR

The paper builds a 103B-token agent-specific pre-training corpus (API docs, function-calling trajectories, code, text) and continually pre-trains an 8B LLaMA-3 base on it in two stages before instruction fine-tuning, arguing pre-training (not IFT alone) is what actually instills durable function-calling and reasoning capability. It reports a scaling law for the optimal agent/text/code data mix and shows the resulting model beats similarly-sized instruction-tuned baselines on BFCL and AgentBench while roughly preserving general capabilities (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval).

## Techniques to try

- Two-stage continual pre-training to counter the "stability gap": inject general+agent data broadly first (Stage I), then narrow to high-quality seed agent data (Stage II) — "Stage II pre-training is essential for effectively bridging the gap between the pre-training corpus and the instruction fine-tuning data, thereby enhancing overall model performance." (Section 6.1/7.4) — could inform how we structure our own SFT data curriculum (broad-then-narrow) even without full continual pre-training.
- Scaling-law-derived data mix as a general planning tool: "the optimal mixture of agent data within the entire pre-training corpus is approximately 36%... the proportion of agent data, text data, and code data should be roughly 1:1:1" (Section 5) — a reference ratio worth sanity-checking our own SFT/DPO data mix against if we worry about eroding general ability.

## Baselines & datasets used

- BFCL-v2 and BFCL-v3 (Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard) used as the primary function-calling accuracy benchmarks — same benchmark family (BFCLv3) our project's pre-registered claim targets, though at 8B rather than 1.2B scale.
- AgentBench (OS, DB, HH, KG, WB, WS environments) for intrinsic reasoning/environment-feedback adaptation.
- General-capability preservation battery: MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval/HumanEval+, BBH, IFEval, HellaSwag — IFEval overlaps directly with one of our guardrail metrics.
- IFT data blend: ShareGPT, ToolACE, Agent-FLAN.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Degrading the retrieved data to unfiltered, low-quality data or removing it entirely negatively impacts overall performance" and removing retrieved data can *help* some narrow tasks (HH, WS) while hurting others — i.e., a narrower/cleaner data mix can look better on some slices of an eval while quietly overfitting to seed-data patterns (Section 7.4). Relevant caution for judging our own eval slice-by-slice rather than only on the aggregate.
- "Heavy fine-tuning can hinder generalization or even degrade performance in non-agent use cases, potentially suppressing the original base model capabilities" (Section 2, citing Ghosh et al. 2024) — a stated pitfall of IFT-heavy approaches, which is exactly the failure mode our guardrail metrics (IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond staying within 2 points) are designed to catch.
- Task-specialized fine-tuned baselines (Groq-8B-Tool-Use, AgentLM-7B, ToolACE-8B) show strong performance on their own training distribution's benchmark but large drops elsewhere (Table 4) — e.g. AgentLM-7B is -67.7% vs Hephaestus on BFCL-v3 despite being competitive on AgentBench — a concrete illustration of narrow fine-tuning failing to generalize across agent benchmarks, which bears on our "specialize to underlying technology, not one vendor's SDK" constraint.

## Metric / eval choices

- BFCL and AgentBench are both scored per-environment/per-scenario rather than as a single blended number in the paper's tables, which makes overfitting to one slice visible — a pattern worth mirroring when we report BFCLv3 sub-scores rather than only the overall number.
- The paper explicitly excludes eval benchmarks from its pre-training/IFT corpora and runs a string-matching contamination check (Ethical Statement) — a hygiene practice worth replicating given our BFCLv3 target is a specific pre-registered number.

## Open questions for our project

- Nothing here addresses quantization, GGUF conversion, or on-device throughput at all — this paper is silent on the sub-2B, 4-bit deployment questions central to our project.
- Nothing here addresses schema validity, malformed-tool-return flagging, or reward-hacking risk from a schema-based reward — none of our core sub-criteria are touched by this work.
- The paper's method (continual pre-training on a 103B-token custom corpus) is far outside our "managed hardware," LoRA-first candidate ladder — the finding that pre-training beats IFT for durable capability is suggestive but not directly actionable at 1.2B scale without a comparably large agent-specific pre-training corpus, which we don't have and aren't planning to build.
