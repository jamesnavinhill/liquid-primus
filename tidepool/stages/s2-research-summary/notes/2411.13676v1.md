# 2411.13676v1 — Hymba: A Hybrid-head Architecture for Small Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Mostly a pretraining-architecture paper (parallel attention+SSM fusion, meta tokens, KV sharing) that doesn't touch quantization or tool-call reliability, but its 1.5B-scale post-training recipe, DoRA (LoRA-style) result, and a BFCL eval-gaming pitfall are directly comparable in scale and topic to open questions in our project.

## TL;DR

Hymba fuses attention heads and SSM (Mamba) heads in parallel within the same layer (rather than stacking them sequentially like Jamba/Samba), adds learnable "meta tokens" prepended to every input as a substitute for attention-sink/BOS tokens, and combines cross-layer KV sharing with mostly-local attention to cut cache size. At 1.5B scale it beats same-size and larger (3B) baselines on commonsense/recall benchmarks and, after FFT+DPO post-training, gets best-in-class BFCLv2 function-calling and DoRA-based RoleBench results — useful as a comparably-sized reference point, but it never touches quantization, malformed-input handling, or reward-hacking-safe training signals, which are our project's core questions.

## Techniques to try

- Post-training recipe at ~1.5B: full-finetune then DPO, each for one epoch with a cosine scheduler, packed samples ("The learning rates are 5e-5, and 3e-6 for FFT and DPO, respectively." / "Both FFT and DPO training are carried out for one epoch with a cosine scheduler. The global batch size is set to 1024." — Sec. 3.4 / Appendix E) — a concrete comparable-scale hyperparameter starting point for our FFT/DPO rungs.
- DoRA (a LoRA variant) as the parameter-efficient rung for a ~1.5B model: "a DoRA [13]-finetuned version of Hymba-1.5B outperforms Llama3.1-8B-Instruct by 2.4% on RoleBench [14]" (Sec. 1) — evidence that PEFT can beat a much larger full model on a narrow downstream skill at this scale, relevant to our "LoRA or full fine-tuning at 1.2B?" question. Storage angle: "DoRA significantly reduces storage requirements for saving multiple downstream models, as it only requires storing the finetuned DoRA parameters, which constitute less than 10% of the original model's total parameters." (Sec. 3.4)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Pretraining mix: DCLM-Baseline-1.0, SmolLM-Corpus, plus a proprietary dataset ("We train Hymba-125M/350M/1.5B models using a mix of DCLM-Baseline-1.0 [36], SmoLM-Corpus [37], and a proprietary high-quality dataset" — Sec. 2.5); a public-data-only rerun is reported separately (Appendix A.2).
- Instruction-tuned baselines compared at similar size: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, OpenELM-1-1B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, SmolLM-1.7B-Instruct (Sec. 3.4).
- Function-calling eval: Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard v2 (BFCLv2), live categories only.
- Role-play PEFT eval: RoleBench, compared against RoleLlama-7B and other baselines from the RoleBench paper.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- BFCL eval gaming: models with no real function-calling ability can score artificially high once irrelevance-detection is included, so the authors dropped that slice: "We exclude live_irrelevance, since we found some baseline models without function calling abilities, could achieve high in the live_irrelevance category (where the model is not required to call function) and very low in other tasks, but still got high overall accuracy although these models are not helpful at all." (Appendix E) — a concrete example of a composite tool-use metric being gameable by a trivial degenerate policy (always/never flag), which is exactly the failure mode our 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate pairing is designed to guard against.
- Public-data-only version of their 1.5B model underperforms specifically on knowledge-heavy MMLU, not on reasoning tasks: "Hymba-1.5B trained on public data primarily suffers from performance drops on 5-shot MMLU compared to the version trained on all data... This suggests that the public data used may lack sufficient factual knowledge" (Appendix A.2) — a reminder that knowledge and instruction/reasoning ability can degrade independently, relevant to our guardrail-metric framing (MMLU-Pro/GPQA vs. IFEval/BFCL).
- Recurring architecture-level finding (not this paper's own result, cited from prior work) that pure SSMs have weak recall: "SSMs have been identified as having limited recall capabilities [4] and underperforming on in-context learning tasks [84]" (Sec. 4) — since LFM2.5 is itself hybrid (mostly gated conv blocks with a few GQA blocks), this is a reason to expect the small attention fraction of the network, not the conv blocks, to carry most of the burden for exact-schema/tool-argument recall.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses lm-evaluation-harness throughout for base-model benchmarking, and BFCLv2's official Gorilla-project evaluation code for function calling, restricted to the "live" categories (live_simple, live_multiple, live_parallel, live_parallel_multiple, live_relevance), explicitly excluding live_irrelevance for the gaming reason above.
- Reports throughput and KV-cache size as first-class efficiency metrics alongside accuracy (measured at 8k sequence length, batch 128, on A100) — not directly transferable to our RTX 2080 Super / 4-bit GGUF setting, but the practice of always pairing an accuracy number with a cache/throughput number is a useful reporting template.

## Open questions for our project

- If a hybrid architecture's recall capability is concentrated in its (few) attention layers, does post-training that targets tool-call/structured-output reliability need to weight those layers differently (e.g. higher LoRA rank on attention vs. conv blocks) in LFM2.5-1.2B? This paper doesn't test that, but its head-importance ablations (Appendix C: "removing one attention/SSM head results in a 0.46%/1.2% reduction in accuracy") suggest per-head/per-layer sensitivity is worth checking empirically on our own model rather than assumed uniform.
- The paper never discusses quantization at all (no INT/4-bit numbers anywhere), so it offers no evidence either way on whether hybrid attention+SSM/conv architectures are more or less quantization-fragile than pure transformers — this remains fully open for our own experiments.
- No instructions-to-the-agent or prompt-injection-style content was found in this paper; it is a standard research paper describing its own architecture and results.
