# 2504.14772v2 — Knowledge Distillation and Dataset Distillation of Large Language Models: Emerging Trends, Challenges, and Future Directions

**Relevance:** low
A broad academic survey of KD/DD methodology and applications (mostly at 7B+ or unspecified scale, healthcare/education/bioinformatics use cases) that never touches quantization, GGUF/on-device deployment, tool-calling, structured-output validity, or GRPO/DPO — the mechanisms and metrics this project actually needs a decision on.

## TL;DR

This is a survey/taxonomy paper cataloguing knowledge-distillation (KD) and dataset-distillation (DD) methods for LLMs — rationale-based KD, uncertainty-aware/Bayesian KD, multi-teacher KD, dynamic/adaptive KD, task-specific KD, plus DD via gradient/trajectory matching and synthetic data generation — and surveys their use in medical, education, and bioinformatics applications. It is organizational/background reading, not a source of a specific method, number, or pitfall directly transferable to post-training a 1.2B tool-calling model for 4-bit GGUF deployment.

## Techniques to try

- Rationale-based KD (train student to jointly predict the teacher's rationale and the final answer, not just the answer) is conceptually close to distilling "reasoning traces" for tool-call justification, but the paper gives no tool-calling application: "Instead of merely imitating the teacher's outputs, the student develops a deeper understanding of problem-solving, leading to better generalization and adaptability to new tasks." — Section 3.1
- Keypoint-based Progressive CoT Distillation (KPOD) weights per-step training loss by a difficulty score so the student learns reasoning steps easiest-to-hardest — a curriculum idea, not validated below 7B or on structured output. (Section 3.1, Eq. 5)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Reference hardware/training-cost table cites Alpaca-LoRA (LLaMA-7B → 6-layer student) trained with "LoRA/QLoRA (rank-8, 4-bit)" on a single RTX 4090 (≤8GB GPU RAM) in 6 hours — the closest thing in the paper to our hardware class, but at 7B, not 1.2B, and not evaluated on tool-calling or schema validity. (Table 7)
- Typical KD benchmarks cited are GLUE, SuperGLUE, MMLU, GSM8K, MATH — general capability benchmarks, not BFCL or any structured-output/tool-use benchmark.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Uncertainty quantification typically incurs significantly higher computational costs than standard prediction tasks, particularly when relying on large-scale sampling or Monte Carlo methods." — a caution against Bayesian/ensemble uncertainty methods as a route to "flag rather than assert," given this project's compute constraints. (Section 3.2)
- Distillation can cause "identity leakage" and "output homogenization," e.g. "DeepSeek-V3 achieving RSE >4.1 against GPT-4o" — a general robustness/diversity risk of distillation, not measured on tool-calling or at small scale. (Section 6.2)
- "differences in seemingly minor aspects, such as initialization schemes, optimizer settings, or data preprocessing, can lead to large variations in performance. This is particularly true for DD methods, where the sensitivity to hyperparameters and training schedules is often underreported." — reproducibility warning for anyone adopting DD techniques from this literature. (Section 8, "Reproducibility Challenges")

## Metric / eval choices

- Surveys generic robustness/calibration metrics that could be repurposed (not designed) for the malformed-tool-return probe: Attack Success Rate (ASR), Performance Drop Rate (PDR) under adversarial/perturbed prompts, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for confidence calibration. None are applied to tool-call or schema-validity settings in the paper. (Section 6.1, Eqs. 26–28)
- Reports generic performance-retention framing ("student models achieving 90–95% of teacher performance... while reducing model size by 5–10x") that is a useful sanity-check number for "how much capability loss is typical" but is not measured at 1.2B, not on BFCL, and not under 4-bit quantization. (Section 6.1)

## Open questions for our project

- The paper is silent on every one of this project's central open questions: quantization's effect on structured output vs. knowledge, GRPO/DPO as a training signal for "flag rather than assert," realistic flag/false-flag rates, throughput floors, vocabulary-size effects on tool-call tokenization, and reward hacking against a schema-check reward. None of these are addressed even indirectly.
- No content in the paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a conventional literature survey.
