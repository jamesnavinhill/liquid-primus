# 2605.17471v1 — WinQ: Accelerating Quantization-Aware Training of Language Models Around Saddle Points

**Relevance:** medium
It sits on the project's candidate ladder as the QAT-acceleration rung, but it is evaluated purely on perplexity and generic zero-shot QA (never tool-calling, JSON validity, or instruction-following), and its training recipe assumes a from-scratch-scale token budget that looks incompatible with a post-training-only project.

## TL;DR

WinQ shows that quantization-aware training (QAT) at 1–4 bits stalls because weights get stuck near flat saddle points in the loss Hessian, and it fixes this cheaply by periodically re-interpolating latent weights toward their quantized values and injecting Gaussian noise into weights before the backward pass. Across LLaMA (1B/3B) and Qwen (0.6B/1.7B) models trained for up to 240K steps (20B tokens) on FineWebEdu, this gives 1.5–4x faster convergence and up to 8.8% lower perplexity than the SOTA baseline ParetoQ at matched training cost, with under 1% extra compute/memory overhead. Critically, the gains are concentrated below 4-bit — at 4-bit weights, existing QAT already lands close to full precision, leaving "limited room for improvement," which is the precision regime this project actually ships at.

## Techniques to try

- Periodic weight re-initialization toward the quantization grid: "we reset the latent weights $W$ to the linear interpolation between $W$ and $Q(W)$... every $K$ steps" ("3.2 Algorithm Design") — could be worth trying only if a direct 4-bit GGUF quantization pass (PTQ or light QAT) fails to hit the 97%-retention floor and the team ends up doing real QAT rather than PTQ/quantization-aware distillation.
- Noise-injected gradients on latent weights ("computing gradients of noise-injected weights to regularize the Hessian," Abstract) as a near-free add-on to any STE-based QAT loop if one is run.

## Baselines & datasets used

- ParetoQ (Liu et al., 2025b) and QuEST (Panferov et al., 2025) as the SOTA QAT baselines; RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, SpinQuant as PTQ references.
- FineWebEdu for QAT pretraining continuation; WikiText2 for perplexity; eight zero-shot QA datasets (ARC-e/c, BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, OBQA, WinoGrande) for accuracy — none of these are instruction-following, tool-use, or structured-output benchmarks.
- Models: LLaMA-3-1B, LLaMA-3-3B, Qwen-3-1.7B, Qwen-3-0.6B (all ≤3B, in range for this project), plus one LLaMA-8B scaling check.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "At higher bit-widths, such as 4-bit, the performance of existing quantized models is close to that of the full-precision model, leaving limited room for improvement" — i.e., the paper's own framing is that 4-bit (this project's target precision) is the easy case where baseline QAT already nearly matches FP, and WinQ's biggest wins (up to 4x speedup, 8.8% PPL gain) are all reported at 1–3 bits, a regime this project doesn't need.
- Full QAT here is expensive even at small scale: "we train each model up to 20B tokens, typically in 240K steps" using 8 GPUs at batch size 64 per LLaMA-1B run, and 28.7 GPU-hours per run just for the base training (re-init/noise overhead is <1%). That is a from-scratch-style continued-pretraining budget, not a post-training/fine-tuning budget — a strong signal this method (and full QAT generally) may be out of reach for a project whose stated approach is LoRA/SFT/DPO/GRPO on top of an already-instruct-tuned 1.2B model.
- The embedding layer behaves differently from transformer layers: its Hessian eigenvalues "remain non-negative" (no saddle-point problem), and the paper's method targets only non-embedding weights — a reminder that vocabulary/embedding layers (relevant given LFM2.5's 65,536-token vocab) may need separate treatment in any quantization scheme.
- Optimal noise scale $\sigma$ and interpolation $\alpha$ are model- and bit-width-specific ("the optimal $\alpha$ can vary across bit-width settings and models"; $\sigma=0.001$ default for LLaMA vs. $\sigma=0.0002$ for Qwen) — not a plug-and-play hyperparameter-free method.

## Metric / eval choices

- Convergence measured by empirical Hessian eigenvalue spectrum via Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature (Hessian-vector products), not a metric this project would compute directly, but a useful diagnostic framing if debugging why a quantized model's quality won't move.
- Downstream quality reported only as WikiText2 perplexity and average zero-shot QA accuracy — no BFCL, IFEval, MMLU-Pro, or GPQA-style eval, so none of the paper's numbers can be carried over to this project's guardrail metrics directly.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the saddle-point/flat-curvature story generalize to a hybrid conv+GQA architecture like LFM2.5, or is it specific to standard transformer weight matrices (the paper only analyzes "transformer layers" of LLaMA/Qwen)?
- Given the paper's own admission that 4-bit QAT is already near full-precision, is the more relevant question for this project not "how do we accelerate QAT" but "does 4-bit PTQ/light QAT preserve structured-output and instruction-following specifically" — a question this paper never measures?
- If quantization-aware distillation/training is reached on the candidate ladder, is there a lightweight adaptation of WinQ's re-init step usable within a short post-training budget (thousands, not hundreds of thousands, of steps), or does the technique require the long training horizon shown here to pay off?
