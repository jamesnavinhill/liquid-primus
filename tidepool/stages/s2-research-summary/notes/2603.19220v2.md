# 2603.19220v2 — Nemotron-Cascade 2: Post-Training LLMs with Cascade RL and Multi-Domain On-Policy Distillation

**Relevance:** medium
The paper's RL post-training design choices (staging order, reward-hacking mitigation, on-policy distillation to recover regressions) are conceptually transferable, but every result is on a 30B-A3B MoE frontier model with multi-teacher pipelines (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B) far outside this project's 1.2B single-GPU, LoRA-first, no-external-teacher scope.

## TL;DR

Nemotron-Cascade 2 is a 30B MoE (3B active) model post-trained with a multi-stage "Cascade RL" pipeline (IF-RL → multi-domain RL → on-policy distillation → RLHF → long-context RL → code RL → SWE RL), reporting gold-medal IMO/IOI/ICPC results. The relevant parts for this project are the staging/interference lessons and reward-hacking safeguards, not the benchmark numbers themselves, which are all far off our 1.2B target scale.

## Techniques to try

- Dense token-level on-policy distillation to recover benchmark regressions between RL stages, using the same-tokenizer teacher checkpoints already sitting in the pipeline: "MOPD provides a dense token-level distillation advantage, whereas GRPO relies on a sparse sequence-level outcome reward that is shared across all generated tokens. This makes MOPD substantially more sample- and step-efficient in practice." (§4.4, "Training efficiency advantage")
- Dynamic filtering of RL batches (drop prompts where all rollouts are entirely correct or entirely incorrect) to keep every batch informative: "This technique filters out samples where all rollouts are either entirely correct or entirely incorrect. By ensuring that every prompt in a batch provides effective gradients, dynamic filtering stabilizes IF-RL training..." (§4.2.2)
- Overlong-response penalty (zero reward if generation doesn't finish within the max sequence length) to control token bloat during IF-RL. (§4.2.2)
- Prefer a strict binary reward over a learned/model-scored reward specifically to reduce reward hacking risk: "We adopt the strict binary reward function to avoid potential reward hacking and keep the whole training to be fully on-policy for stability." (§4.7.2, Code RL) — directly relevant to this project's "no model-scored stored reward" constraint.
- Curriculum-prune RL prompts by rollout pass rate: drop 100%-pass-rate (too easy) instances entirely and randomly discard 90% of 0%-pass-rate (too hard) instances, to keep gradient signal dense. (§4.8.2)

## Baselines & datasets used

- BFCL v4 (not v3, which this project targets) used for agentic tool-calling evaluation: "We follow the official BFCL V4 evaluation protocol and report scores across a combination of Agentic, multi-turn, live, and non-live categories. Models are evaluated in thinking mode, and we report pass@1 accuracy based on a single generation (avg@1)." (Appendix, BFCL description) — confirms BFCL is the standard agentic/tool-calling benchmark family, but no numbers at anywhere near 1.2B scale are reported.
- IFBench used to report state-of-the-art instruction-following accuracy (83.13%) — only at 30B-A3B scale, not usable as a calibration point for this project's IFEval/IFBench targets.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Cross-domain RL interference is real and order-dependent: "IF-RL can negatively impact human alignment capabilities (e.g., ArenaHard), while our subsequent generative-reward-model-based RLHF has a negligible impact on instruction following scores." — motivates doing instruction-following RL *before* preference RLHF, not after. (§4.2.2)
- Mixing thinking and non-thinking rollouts during RLHF traded away instruction-following gains that could not be recovered: "While incorporating both thinking and non-thinking modes can improve training convergence and yield slight gains on evaluation benchmarks, we observe a significant degradation in instruction-following performance. The resulting drop is substantial enough that the gains obtained in the earlier RLVR stage cannot be fully recovered." (§4.5.2)
- Even at 30B scale, agentic/knowledge-intensive capability lagged a same-size competitor: "It underperforms Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on knowledge-intensive and agentic benchmarks, highlighting the importance of stronger knowledge-intensive pretraining and agentic RL in future work." (§2) — a caution that agentic/tool-use reliability doesn't automatically follow from strong reasoning RL.
- Difficult RL prompts (all rollouts failing verification) were found to "degrade the stability and effectiveness" of training and had to be down-weighted/discarded (§4.8.1, §4.8.2) — relevant if this project's malformed-tool-return RL data turns out to be too hard for the base 1.2B model to get any reward signal from.

## Metric / eval choices

- BFCL v4, evaluated in thinking mode, pass@1/avg@1 over a single generation — differs from this project's BFCLv3 target and from a "no retry, no repair, no grammar enforcement, first-sample-parses" schema-validity metric; the paper does not report any first-attempt-parse-rate style metric.
- No quantization, GGUF, or throughput metrics anywhere in the paper — it does not touch the 4-bit retention or decode-throughput questions this project needs answered.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the Cascade RL / MOPD machinery has been shown to work without a pool of much-larger teacher/reward models (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B-A22B) generating data and judging rollouts — it's unclear whether any of it is reachable at 1.2B with no comparably strong same-family teacher.
- The paper never tests small (≤8B) models, so it gives no evidence either way on this project's central question of whether quantization or scale damages structured-output/instruction-following more than knowledge.
