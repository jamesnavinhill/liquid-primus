# 2508.12685v3 — ToolACE-MT: Non-Autoregressive Generation for Agentic Multi-Turn Interaction

**Relevance:** high
Offers a concrete, cheaper-than-multi-agent-simulation recipe for synthesizing multi-turn tool-use SFT data — including an explicit "tool call fails, assistant reflects and adjusts" injection type that maps directly onto the project's flag-rather-than-assert training-signal question — and reports a small-model scaling result (0.5B/1.5B vs 3B/7B) that is a direct pitfall warning for a 1.2B target.

## TL;DR

ToolACE-MT builds multi-turn, multi-step tool-use training dialogues via a three-stage non-autoregressive pipeline (coarse skeleton → iterative mask-and-fill refinement → rule+model offline verification) instead of costly turn-by-turn multi-agent simulation (MAS). Trained via LoRA on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, the resulting data beats MAS-generated data on BFCL-v3, τ-Bench and ACEBench multi-turn/agent metrics, at lower generation cost; but a size-scaling experiment shows the benefit shrinks sharply below 3B, which is the closest data point this paper has to the project's 1.2B target.

## Techniques to try

- Synthesize a matched pair of good/bad tool-call turns via explicit "error simulation" injection to train flag-rather-than-assert behavior: "Error simulation: tool call fails, resulting assistant reflecting and adjusting actions." — Section 3.4.1
- Use a hybrid rule-based + model-based offline verifier as a data filter (not a training reward) before SFT: "For rule-based, we evaluate several aspects, including dialogue and tool-calling format compliance, executability (when real tools are available), repetition, and identifiable hallucinations that can be detected with rules, such as references to special IDs that do not appear in the history." — Section 3.5
- Mask-and-fill iterative refinement with decaying re-selection probability to spread refinement across turns, plus an LLM judge that can reject a proposed rewrite and keep the original: "an LLM-based judger is used to determine whether to adopt the newly generated content or retain the original ones." — Section 3.4.2
- LoRA hyperparameters that worked for tool-use SFT at 8B (a starting point, not a guarantee at 1.2B): "we employ the parameter-efficient fine-tuning method LoRA ... with a rank of 16 and an alpha value of 32 ... global batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 1×10⁻⁴" — Section 4.1

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baseline: multi-agent simulation (MAS) data generation following ToolFlow (Wang et al. 2025), same tool pool, same generator LLM (GPT-4o-2024-11-20), same offline verification applied for fair comparison — Section 4.1
- Base model for main experiments: Llama3.1-8B-Instruct; generalization checked on Qwen2.5-Instruct (0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B) and Qwen3-8B (no-thinking) — Section 4.1
- Benchmarks: BFCL-v3 (multi-turn, single-turn, hallucination categories), τ-Bench (Airline, Retail), ACEBench (En) — Section 4.1
- Also compared against three open tool-use SFT datasets: ToolACE-10k, Toucan-1.5M (8k sampled), ToolDial-9k — Appendix D

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Small models benefit much less from this kind of multi-turn tool-use fine-tuning: "The smaller raw models (0.5B and 1.5B) exhibit little to no multi-turn capabilities, but fine-tuning with our dataset can enhance the corresponding performance. Notably, the improvements are more pronounced in the 3B and 7B models, suggesting that multi-turn function calling remains a relatively advanced ability that small models struggle to acquire." (Qwen2.5-Instruct series, BFCL-v3) — Section 4.5. This is the closest data point in the paper to the project's 1.2B scale and argues against assuming SFT data-mix improvements will transfer linearly down to 1.2B.
- Newer/better-pretrained base models show diminishing returns from the same fine-tuning recipe: "Both Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 were released after Llama3.1 and are likely to have incorporated improved agentic capabilities during their training. As a result, further fine-tuning on similar task formats may yield diminishing returns" — Section 4.5 (relevant since LFM2.5 is also a newer, already-instruction-tuned base).
- Using a weaker/cheaper generator LLM for the synthetic data materially hurts quality and downstream accuracy: with GPT-4o-mini as generator, offline-verification pass rate dropped to 48.7% (vs 72.3% with GPT-4o) and trained-model BFCL-v3 accuracy fell to 60.13% vs 65.41%; Llama3.1-8B-Inst itself "failed to produce valid instances in most of time" as a generator — Section 4.4. Matters if the project considers generating its own tech-stack-specific training data with a small/local model rather than a strong proprietary one.
- Parameter handling is the dominant failure mode even after this pipeline's refinement: rule-based verification failures were 51% "Parameter Hallucination"; model-based failures were 23% "Parameter Extraction" and 16% "Parameter Format Compliance" — Appendix E, Table 9. Directly relevant to the project's schema-validity and malformed-tool-return targets.
- τ-Bench Airline scores are noted as unreliable as an eval signal: "several instances define empty actions as the correct responses, assessing the assistant's ability to recognize unsolvable user requirements. When a model lacks sufficient capability and consistently fails to produce valid function calls, it may coincidentally align with these empty actions and receive positive rewards" — Section 4.3.2.

## Metric / eval choices

- BFCL-v3 multi-turn breakdown used: Base, Miss-Function, Miss-Parameter, Long-Context, plus single-turn (Non-Live/Live) and Hallucination (Relevance/Irrelevance) categories — Table 2, Section 4.3.1. Directly overlaps with the project's BFCLv3 target metric and its sub-parts.
- Data-quality metrics for synthetic dialogue before training: Semantic Similarity (SS) and Entailment Rate (EnR) for coherence, Shannon Entropy (H) and Distinct-3 for diversity — Section 4.2, Table 1. Could be reused to score the project's own tech-stack-specific synthetic data before spending a training run on it.
- ACEBench Agent category reports both End-to-End Accuracy (EA) and Process Accuracy (PA), the latter checking trajectory-level consistency with ground truth, not just final outcome — Section 4.3.2.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's data-generation pipeline is validated on general/simulated tool pools (real + synthetic APIs from prior work), not on a narrow, single-technology-stack tool set like ours (SQL dialects, JSON Schema, MCP, IPC) — unclear whether the "coarse skeleton + mask-and-fill" approach still helps once the tool space is much smaller and more repetitive.
- All experiments use GPT-4o as the trajectory-generation LLM; the paper explicitly reports that a weaker generator (GPT-4o-mini, or Llama3.1-8B-Inst itself) degrades data quality substantially — if we want to generate our own tech-stack-specific training data cheaply, this suggests we may need a strong external LLM as generator rather than bootstrapping from the target 1.2B model or its base checkpoint.
- The smallest model this paper tests (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B) is a similar size class to LFM2.5-1.2B but a different architecture (dense transformer vs. LFM2.5's hybrid conv+GQA blocks); the finding that multi-turn tool-calling "gains" shrink at small scale needs to be checked empirically on our actual model rather than assumed to transfer.
- No instructions-to-the-reader or suspicious embedded directives were found in the paper text; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
