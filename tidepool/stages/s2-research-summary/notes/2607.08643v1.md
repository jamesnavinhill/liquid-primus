# 2607.08643v1 — BiSCo-LLM: Lookup-Free Binary Spherical Coding for Extreme Low-Bit Large Language Model Compression

**Relevance:** medium
A 2-bit, codebook-free vector-coding method for 8B-scale models with custom neural decoders is a scale/precision/tooling mismatch for a 4-bit GGUF 1.2B target, but its recovery-distillation and sensitive-channel diagnostics are generalizable pitfalls worth carrying into the quantization stage.

## TL;DR

BiSCo-LLM compresses LLM weights to ~2 bits/weight by mapping weight chunks to binary codes on a unit hypersphere (no stored codebook), adding a second residual BSQ stage, protecting a small set of sensitive channels in 8-bit, and recovering accuracy with category-wise (not layer-wise) distillation. On Qwen3-8B it reaches WikiText-2 PPL 10.18 vs 9.73 FP16 and closes to within 1.87 points of full-precision average downstream accuracy — evaluated on general QA/commonsense benchmarks, not structured output or tool use.

## Techniques to try

- Category-wise (not layer-wise) recovery distillation: replace one module category at a time and distill with it active in the full model, rather than distilling each layer in isolation — motivated by a direct negative result (see Pitfalls) ("after compressing and replacing one module category, BiSCo-LLM performs category-wise recovery distillation before moving to the next category" — Sec. III-F).
- Small sensitive-channel protection pays for itself fast: 1% of channels stored at 8-bit captured most of the benefit of 3% protection ("increasing the ratio from 0% to 1% already captures 81.7% of the gain obtained by 3% protection on average" — Sec. IV-D3), suggesting a cheap, low-storage-overhead way to shore up the most fragile projections without full-precision fallback everywhere.
- MLP `down_proj` is consistently the most fragile category to quantize in their ablations (largest perplexity gains from both residual coding and channel protection), worth treating as a higher-risk module when deciding what LoRA ranks or protection to allocate per-module.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Models: Qwen3-8B and LLaMA3-8B (well above the project's 1.2B target scale).
- Recovery/distillation data mixture: Alpaca, OpenOrca, FineWeb-Edu, RACE, SciQ, LongAlpaca, LongAlign — a general instruction/reasoning/reading-comprehension/long-context mix, not tool-calling or structured-output data.
- Eval: WikiText-2 and C4 perplexity; BoolQ, RTE, WinoGrande, ARC-Easy/Challenge, OpenBookQA, PIQA, MMLU zero/few-shot accuracy. No BFCL, no JSON/schema, no tool-calling metric anywhere in the paper.
- Baselines compared against: GPTQ, SpinQuant, OSTQuant, QuIP, AWQ (scalar/group-wise); AQLM, VPTQ, QuIP#, LiftQuant, UniSVQ (vector/structured-code).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Layer-wise local recovery can fully converge while the assembled model still degrades badly: "the average downstream accuracy is 70.34% before activating recovered layers, reaches 70.71% at the best early prefix point, and decreases to 53.36% at the last evaluated prefix point" even though "all 36 block-level curves finish below their initial values" (Sec. III-F) — a concrete demonstration that local loss reduction is not evidence of global recovery, directly relevant to how this project should validate any layer-local or module-local fine-tuning step.
- Naively enlarging a single-stage binary code is not a reliable way to spend extra bit budget: a nominal-rate-matched granularity sweep on LLaMA-2-7B found "small and medium granularities remain stable... whereas the largest setting becomes unstable after multiple categories are compressed" (Sec. III-D) — a caution against assuming "more capacity in one place" beats "capacity split into stages/paths."
- Replacing the nonlinear decoder with a linear one "reduces the MLP average from 58.34 to 50.67, and increasing the number of linear residual stages from two to three does not recover the loss" (Sec. IV-D1) — decoder/reconstruction-function capacity matters more than raw stage count, a reminder that architecture choices in a compression pipeline aren't fungible with just adding more of the same.
- The method is explicitly not free at inference: "the decoded weights can be materialized and then used by standard matrix multiplication kernels, which simplifies integration but does not fully exploit the binary structure of the stored codes" (Sec. V) — i.e., no fused low-bit kernel exists yet, so any throughput benefit is not demonstrated, only storage savings are.
- The paper itself flags its own results as incomplete: HellaSwag numbers are missing for Qwen3-8B and the entire LLaMA3-8B BiSCo-LLM row is blank ("the BiSCo-LLM results on LLaMA3-8B remain to be filled in" — Sec. IV-C), so the headline comparison table should be read as preliminary, not final.

## Metric / eval choices

- Storage is accounted additively and explicitly: S_tot = S_code + S_dec + S_8bit + S_LoRA + S_meta (Sec. III-A) — a useful template for this project's "GGUF under 1.5 GB" budget, since it forces every auxiliary payload (adapters, protected channels, metadata) to be counted rather than only the nominal bits/weight.
- No structured-output, tool-calling, or instruction-following metric is used anywhere; all evaluation is perplexity plus general commonsense/QA accuracy, so this paper cannot directly answer the project's core question of whether quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more than knowledge.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's whole apparatus (learned BSQ codecs, per-category neural decoders, custom bit-packing) is built for research-grade 2-bit compression and has no compatibility path to standard GGUF quantization or llama.cpp kernels — does any piece of it (e.g., sensitive-channel protection, category-wise recovery ordering) survive translation into a GGUF/QAT-distillation workflow, or is it only the qualitative lessons (not the method) that transfer?
- Given the project's much smaller model (1.2B vs 8B) and much less aggressive target (4-bit vs 2-bit), is the "layer-wise recovery looks fine locally but fails globally" finding even reproducible at 4-bit, where per-layer error budgets are far smaller — or is this strictly an extreme-low-bit phenomenon?
