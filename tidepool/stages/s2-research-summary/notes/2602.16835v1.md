# 2602.16835v1 — NeST: Neuron Selective Tuning for LLM Safety

**Relevance:** medium
The core method (probe-detect a small set of behavior-relevant neurons, cluster them, restrict fine-tuning to cluster-level update vectors) is a general parameter-efficient adaptation idea worth knowing about for keeping general ability intact while training a narrow skill, but the paper's task (jailbreak refusal), datasets, and architecture assumptions (standard transformer FFN gate/up projections) don't map directly onto tool-calling reliability or LFM2.5's hybrid conv/attention architecture, and nothing here is validated at the quantized/GGUF stage this project cares about.

## TL;DR

NeST detects "safety neurons" in FFN gate_proj/up_proj layers via linear probes on benign-vs-harmful activations, clusters them by activation similarity (k-means, silhouette-selected k), then fine-tunes only a tiny per-cluster additive update vector while freezing everything else. Across 10 open models (1B–14B, including Llama-3.2-1B/3B and gemma-2b-it, close to this project's 1.2B scale), it cuts jailbreak attack success rate from 44.5%→4.36% average using only 0.44M trainable parameters, while nearly matching full fine-tuning's safety gains and beating LoRA at a fraction of the parameter budget.

## Techniques to try

- Neuron-selective, cluster-tied fine-tuning as a LoRA alternative for narrow-behavior specialization while freezing the rest of the model: "NeST performs targeted adaptation by first clustering functionally related safety components and then learning a compact set of shared update directions for each cluster, while freezing the remainder of the model." (Introduction, §1) — relevant to the project's "LoRA or full fine-tuning at 1.2B?" question as a third option worth at least being aware of.
- The authors explicitly claim the method generalizes beyond safety to any behavior with a detectable neuron signature: "By modifying the neuron detection procedure, NeST can be applied to harden or specialize models for other targeted behaviors... the same cluster-based adaptation mechanism enables lightweight, behavior-specific fine-tuning aligned with the model's internal structure." (§8, "Task-Specific and Structure-Aware Adaptation") — could in principle be repurposed to localize and specialize "tool-call schema compliance" neurons instead of safety-refusal neurons, though this would need to be built from scratch.
- Silhouette-score-driven adaptive clustering (k ∈ {2, k_max}, revert to a single cluster if silhouette < 0.1) is a concrete recipe for choosing how much parameter sharing to enforce, applicable to any neuron-clustering PEFT scheme: "We select the clustering with the highest silhouette score, preferring smaller $k$ in the case of ties. If the best silhouette score falls below a fixed threshold $\gamma$... we revert to a single-cluster assignment." (§3.4)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Compared against Full FT, LoRA (r=1, restricted to FNN projections for fair parameter-count comparison), and Circuit Breakers (representation-rerouting, LoRA-based) across 10 models: Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen2.5-7B/14B, Phi-4-mini/Phi-4, gemma-2b-it/7b-it, Qwen3-4B/14B.
- Training data: WildJailbreak (up to 10k harmful prompts) + Natural Reasoning (benign, matched count), 20k total, 95/5 split, 5 epochs.
- Safety-neuron detection data: CatHarmfulQA, HarmfulQA, LLM-LAT harmful dataset (harmful) vs. Natural Reasoning train split (benign).
- Utility eval: GSM8K, ARC, MMLU (standard accuracy).
- Multimodal stability eval: Gemma-3-12B/27B and Qwen3-VL-8B (Instruct/Thinking) across text/image and reasoning-on/off settings.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- LoRA (r=1, matched parameter budget) gave "inconsistent safety gains" and degraded on larger models — "ASR remains above 10% on larger models such as Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-14B, indicating limited robustness when adaptation capacity is constrained." Directly relevant to worries about whether small-rank LoRA reliably instills a targeted behavior.
- Circuit Breakers reproduced under the paper's unified eval protocol (rather than the original authors' protocol) "shows substantial variability across models... ASR remains high across most models" — a cautionary note that a safety/behavior-shaping method's reported numbers can collapse under a different, harder eval protocol; worth remembering when judging any single-paper number against this project's "matched base rerun" methodology.
- Weak clustering (one shared cluster per layer, maximally parameter-efficient) "collapses heterogeneous safety behaviors into a single update direction, leading to underfitting" — ASR rose to 11.2% average vs. 4.3% for the tuned setting — a concrete illustration that too much parameter sharing can underfit a narrow target behavior.
- Strong clustering (fully independent per-neuron updates, no sharing) only marginally improved over the tuned default (4.1% vs 4.3% ASR) "despite the significantly higher parameter cost," and even hurt on one model (Qwen3-14B) — a diminishing-returns/overfitting warning for maximizing adaptation flexibility.
- Utility trade-off is uneven across scale: while average utility drops were small (GSM8K -0.9pp, ARC -4.9pp, MMLU -3.7pp), "some smaller models, such as gemma-2b-it, show more noticeable declines" — suggesting narrow-behavior fine-tuning may cost proportionally more capability at smaller scale, a relevant caution for a 1.2B target model.
- The method is explicitly scoped to FFN gate_proj/up_proj neurons in a standard gated-FFN transformer block; it says nothing about how (or whether) an analogous neuron-localization approach would work on gated short-convolution blocks, which make up most of LFM2.5's layers.

## Metric / eval choices

- Attack success rate (ASR) computed via an independent LLM judge (Qwen3Guard) classifying responses unsafe, combined with a check for absence of explicit refusal: "A prompt is counted as a successful attack if the judge labels the response as unsafe and the output does not contain an explicit refusal." This is a model-scored eval metric (not used as a training reward), structurally similar in spirit to this project's paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate probe design, though here it's a single number rather than a paired precision/recall-style pair.
- Parameter efficiency reported as a first-class metric alongside safety (Table 3: trainable-parameter counts in millions), reinforcing the project's own emphasis on measuring adaptation cost, not just outcome quality.

## Open questions for our project

- Could a neuron-probing-and-clustering approach be adapted to localize "structured-output" or "tool-schema" neurons (analogous to safety neurons) rather than requiring full LoRA/SFT layers, as a way to protect general ability (MMLU-Pro, GPQA) while specializing tool-calling? The paper only asserts this generalizes; it does not demonstrate it on any non-safety behavior.
- LFM2.5-1.2B's hybrid short-convolution + GQA architecture is not a pure gated-FFN transformer stack in the way NeST assumes (detection/clustering is applied only to gate_proj/up_proj); it's unclear whether an equivalent neuron-localization signal exists in the convolution blocks or whether this restricts any adaptation to only the attention-block layers.
- No quantization interaction is studied anywhere in this paper — it offers no evidence either way on whether small, cluster-tied weight deltas survive 4-bit quantization better or worse than LoRA deltas or full fine-tuning deltas.
