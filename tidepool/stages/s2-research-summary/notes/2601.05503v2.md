# 2601.05503v2 — Over-Searching in Search-Augmented Large Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Domain and tool type differ (web/RAG search abstention vs. malformed structured tool-return flagging), but the paper's central finding — tool augmentation systematically erodes "know when not to commit" behavior, and prompting alone can't fully fix it — bears directly on the project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate sub-criterion and its choice of post-training over prompting.

## TL;DR

The paper shows that giving LLMs a search tool reliably improves accuracy on answerable questions but degrades their ability to abstain on unanswerable/false-premise/underspecified ones, and that this gets worse with more reasoning effort or model complexity. Prompt-level and corpus-level mitigations help only partially; the authors conclude the fix likely needs to happen at post-training/alignment time rather than through prompting.

## Techniques to try

- Query-level system-prompt mitigations for abstention/flagging, useful as a cheap eval baseline before training: "(1) Abstention-aware explicitly instruct models to consider abstention as a valid response when queries are unanswerable; (2) Few-shot learning provides examples of appropriate abstention behavior in the system prompt; and (3) Self-evaluation introduces a self-assessment stage where the model evaluates query answerability before answering." — Section 5.4
- Paired-metric evaluation design (avoids a single gameable number), directly analogous to the project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pairing: "Dual Accuracy separately reports answer accuracy on $\mathcal{A}$ ... and abstention accuracy on $\mathcal{U}$ ... By explicitly reporting two accuracies, we provide a symmetric, interpretable view of model behavior across both query types." — Appendix B.1
- Corpus/context augmentation with synthetic negative evidence, as a possible analog for injecting synthetic malformed-tool-return examples into training/context: "we generate synthetic negative evidence using GPT-4o-mini. Each generated document emphasizes one of ten angles to ensure diversity: (i) ambiguous and inconsistent information, (ii) data coverage gaps, ..." — Appendix F.2

## Baselines & datasets used

- OverSearchQA: a new abstention-focused benchmark, "a curated abstention-focused QA benchmark of 1,188 queries (balanced answerable/unanswerable)" spanning Answer Unknown, False Premise, and Underspecified Context categories, built from CoCoNot, FalseQA, QAQA, ALCUNA, MediQ, WorldSense, HotpotQA, NQ, and SimpleQA — Section 4 / Table 1.
- Models span 3B–235B; the only ones at or below 8B are Llama-3.2-3B and Hermes3-3B. For Llama-3.2-3B (Table 2, overall): without search Ans. 43.3 / Abst. 53.5 / TPC 241.0; with search Ans. 64.2 / Abst. 49.1 / TPC 734.6 — i.e. search added ~21 points of answer accuracy but cost ~4 points of abstention accuracy and 3x the token cost, at 3B scale.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Core negative result, aggregated across 9 models: search "boost[s] answer accuracy by an average of 24.0% while degrading abstention accuracy by 12.8%." — Section 5.1
- Reasoning effort makes it worse, not better (o4-mini, Low/Medium/High reasoning): Ans. Acc 74.1/74.3/74.6, Abst. Acc 46.6/46.2/45.4, TPC 517.1/1002.7/1492.2 — "TPC increases monotonically with reasoning effort, suggesting that deeper reasoning may encourage models to over-search." — Table 3, Section 5.1. Direct caution for this project's GRPO step on the candidate ladder: added reasoning/verification steps could plausibly increase false-assert rate on malformed tool returns unless explicitly trained against.
- Training-free mitigations only partially work and don't fix the underlying issue: "While both strategies can help mitigate over-searching to some extent, they do not resolve models' fundamental inability to search rationally." — Conclusion. Retrieval-level corpus augmentation specifically gave only "modest improvements (3.6% on average) in abstention accuracy," attributed to "(i) synthetic documents rank poorly in retrieval; (ii) negative evidence is diluted by numerous naturally-occurring positive documents." — Section 5.4
- Explicit call-out that prompting isn't enough: "addressing the inability of models to search rationally may require interventions at the post-training or alignment stage." — Section 7 (Limitations). Supports prioritizing this project's SFT/DPO/GRPO ladder over relying on system-prompt instructions to get the flag-rate target.
- "Negative evidence" (signal that should trigger abstention) is naturally rare in context: "negative documents comprise only 13-22% of retrieved content for unanswerable queries." Relevant to designing the malformed-tool-return training/eval set — don't assume the model will see enough naturally-occurring "this is broken" signal without deliberate oversampling.
- Multi-turn snowball effect: conversation history biases later-turn behavior — "a history of unanswerable questions encourages abstention, while a history of answerable questions encourages answer attempts." — Section 5.3. Worth checking whether the same holds for flag-vs-assert behavior across a multi-turn agent conversation with mixed well-formed/malformed tool returns.

## Metric / eval choices

- Tokens Per Correctness (TPC): "the expected compute cost per correct response," combining generated tokens, input/context tokens, and per-search-call cost with fixed coefficients (λ=0.25, μ=500) so it's comparable across models — Section 3.2. Not directly reusable (this project has no search-call cost axis), but the general pattern — a cost-normalized correctness metric — could be adapted if decode-latency/throughput needs to be traded off against flag accuracy.
- LLM-as-judge validated against humans at 84% agreement, with a known directional bias: "the overall agreement rate reaches 84%... 10/16 disagreements occurred in one direction: the LLM judge identified abstention in cases where the human annotator did not." — Appendix C.2. Useful caution if this project uses an LLM judge anywhere in eval (not as a training reward, per the standing constraint) — judges can over-credit abstention/flagging relative to human judgment.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the search-abstention failure mode (tool access biasing the model toward committing to an answer) transfer to the project's actual target behavior — flagging a malformed *tool return* rather than declining to answer an *unanswerable question*? The mechanism (tool integration erodes calibrated non-commitment) looks structurally similar but is not validated on structured/JSON tool-call settings in this paper.
- The paper only tested 3B–235B general models with prompting-only mitigations; it does not test whether SFT/DPO/GRPO training actually fixes the calibration gap it documents — that's an open question for this project's own ladder rather than something this paper settles.
- No instruction-like text addressed to a reader/agent was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
