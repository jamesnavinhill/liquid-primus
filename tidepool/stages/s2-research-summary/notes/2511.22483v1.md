# 2511.22483v1 — Enhancing Trustworthiness with Mixed Precision: Benchmarks, Opportunities, and Challenges

**Relevance:** medium
This paper studies how PTQ weight quantization affects instruction-following-adjacent behaviors (refusal rate, calibration, label consistency) beyond aggregate accuracy, which is directly the kind of failure mode our project must guard against in the 4-bit GGUF, but its core technique (multi-precision ensembling) is out of scope for us and its measurements are on 7B/13B LLaMA-2, not our 1.2B target or tool-calling/schema tasks.

## TL;DR

The authors quantize LLaMA-2-7B/13B-Chat with AWQ and GPTQ at 8/4/3-bit and find that while MMLU accuracy holds up well down to 4-bit, four "trustworthiness" metrics (adversarial robustness, fairness, machine ethics, OOD robustness) degrade unevenly and unpredictably, with elevated refusal rates and abrupt drops especially under 4-/3-bit GPTQ. They then propose "precision-ensemble voting" — running several quantized bit-widths of the same model in parallel and majority-voting the outputs — which recovers up to 5.8% over the 13B dense baseline on trustworthiness metrics but still falls short of the 7B dense baseline on some axes.

## Techniques to try

- Track refusal rate as its own axis alongside accuracy when evaluating the 4-bit GGUF, not just an aggregate score, since the paper shows accuracy can look stable while refusal/abstention behavior swings sharply: "quantized models are more vulnerable to high refusal rates and can suffer abrupt performance drops in specific scenarios" (Sec. IV-A).
- When comparing quantization methods or checkpoints, distinguish failures that manifest as explicit refusals from those that manifest as silent label-inconsistent/contradictory output, since these have different diagnostic implications: "low-bit GPTQ-quantized models frequently produce invalid or abstaining outputs under few-shot, yielding a higher refusal rate. This highlights the model's degradation of in-context learning capability." (Sec. III-C4)

## Baselines & datasets used

- LLaMA-2-7B-Chat and LLaMA-2-13B-Chat (dense) as baselines, quantized with AWQ and GPTQ to 8-, 4-, and 3-bit (Sec. III-A).
- Multi-domain accuracy via MMLU (57 tasks) (Sec. III-B).
- Trustworthiness via DecodingTrust's framework: AdvGLUE/AdvGLUE++ (adversarial robustness), the Adult dataset with demographic parity difference / equalized odds difference (fairness), the ETHICS dataset (machine ethics), and style-shifted/out-of-scope inputs (OOD robustness) (Sec. III-B1–B4).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "at lower precisions (4- and 3-bit), AWQ is more robust than GPTQ" and "low-precision GPTQ can substantially degrade few-shot adherence and in-context learning in the LLaMA-2 series" — the choice of PTQ method matters more at low bit-width than the bit-width alone (Sec. III-C6).
- Aggregate task accuracy is a poor proxy for structured-behavior stability: "quantization exhibits a different impact on trustworthiness metrics than on general-task accuracy" (Sec. III-C), and MMLU accuracy stayed near-baseline down to 4-bit while the trustworthiness metrics were "less stable, suffers from high refusal rates, and can exhibit abrupt failures" (Sec. I).
- The proposed fix (precision-ensemble voting) requires running multiple quantized copies of the model in parallel at inference time — incompatible with a single 8 GB GPU / single 4-bit GGUF deployment target, and the project's standing constraints already rule ensembling out of scope.
- Even the ensemble "fail[s] to match the 7B dense baseline" on some trustworthiness metrics, i.e., a smaller dense model can still beat a larger quantized-and-ensembled one on certain axes (Sec. IV-C3) — a caution against trusting quantized-model-vs-larger-model comparisons over matched-base-rerun comparisons.

## Metric / eval choices

- All four trustworthiness metrics are scored 0–100 and each carries its own refusal-rate figure, computed separately from the raw accuracy/score, because "in most trustworthiness metrics, refusals contribute to inaccuracy" but are conceptually distinct from wrong answers (Sec. III-B).
- For OOD robustness specifically, they separate "meaningful accuracy" (MACC, accuracy conditional on non-refusal) from raw accuracy — a pattern worth mirroring for our malformed-tool-return probe, where flag rate and false-flag rate likewise need to be reported as a pair rather than collapsed into one number.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the measurements are on tool-calling, JSON/schema output, or models near 1.2B parameters, so the direction (quantization hurts stability/calibration more than raw accuracy) is suggestive but not evidence we can size our 93%/97% thresholds against.
- The paper's own proposed mitigation is explicitly incompatible with our single-model, single-precision deployment constraint, so it offers a diagnostic lens (track refusal/abstention separately from accuracy) but no adoptable remedy for the 4-bit floor problem.
