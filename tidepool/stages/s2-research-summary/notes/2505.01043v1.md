# 2505.01043v1 — Low-Precision Training of Large Language Models: Methods, Challenges, and Opportunities

**Relevance:** medium
A survey of low-precision *training* (fixed-point/integer/FP8/custom formats, mostly for pretraining efficiency at scale) that explicitly excludes post-training quantization (PTQ) — the thing actually needed for the 4-bit GGUF deployment target — but its short QAT section (BitDistiller, LLM-QAT, EfficientQAT, QuEST) is directly relevant to the candidate ladder's last-resort "quantization-aware distillation / QAT" step.

## TL;DR

This is a categorization survey of techniques for training neural nets (mostly CNNs, plus some recent LLM work) in low numerical precision — fixed-point, integer, FP16/FP8/FP4, and custom formats like Microscaling — aimed at cutting training compute/memory, not at improving inference-time quantized model quality. It also has a short section on quantization-aware training (QAT) for LLM deployment, which is the part most useful to this project's plan to try QAT/quantization-aware distillation if lighter methods don't hit the ≥97% full-precision retention bar for the 4-bit GGUF.

## Techniques to try

- BitDistiller for sub-4-bit QAT with a full-precision teacher: "BitDistiller [157] leverages full-precision models as teachers and introduces a tailored asymmetric quantization and clipping strategy to minimize error in sub-4-bit regimes." (Section VI, "QAT with KD")
- LLM-QAT's data-free distillation recipe for 4-bit quantization when you don't want to depend on the original training data mix: "LLM-QAT [156] adopts data-free distillation, which generates synthetic data from pretrained models to guide the 4-bit quantization of LLaMA architectures without the need for original training data." (Section VI, "QAT with KD")
- EfficientQAT's two-stage scheme (block-wise train, then end-to-end quantization-parameter-only train) as a cheaper QAT recipe if full QAT is too costly on managed hardware: "It employs a two-stage training strategy. It first trains the model and quantization parameters block-by-block, followed by end-to-end training of the quantization parameters alone." (Section VI, "Other QAT methods")
- QuEST for stability at very low bit precision via Hadamard normalization plus a "trust estimation" gradient estimator: "QuEST [159] improves both accuracy and speed by introducing Hadamard normalization and MSE-optimal fitting, alongside a novel gradient estimator called trust estimation, which ensures stable training at 1-bit precision." (Section VI, "Other QAT methods")

## Baselines & datasets used

- This is a survey, not an experimental paper — no baselines/datasets of its own; cited works mostly evaluate on ImageNet-1K/CIFAR-10 CNNs (ResNet-50, VGG-16, AlexNet) or LLM pretraining at 7B–175B scale, not on instruction-following/tool-calling benchmarks.
- Notable exception close to this project's scale: "Q-GaLore ... enables training a LLaMA-7B model from scratch with only 16 GB of GPU memory," using INT4 gradient-projection quantization with INT8 weights (Section III-B1) — relevant as a data point for training-time memory limits on a single consumer-class GPU, though it's about pretraining memory, not inference quality retention.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- FP8 training is not free of accuracy loss even at moderate scale: "Early attempts at FP8 training show substantial accuracy degradation in popular architectures such as MobileNet and Transformers, primarily due to differing precision requirements between the forward and backward passes." (Section IV-B)
- FP8 instability specifically flagged for LLMs: "Fishman *et al.* [121] identify prolonged training instabilities in FP8 implementations and attribute these to activation function behaviors," and separately, "Lee *et al.* [113] investigate the robustness of reduced-precision training for LLMs, highlighting the instability of current FP8 methods." (Section IV-B) — a caution that even training-time float8 needs care; doesn't directly transfer to weight-only 4-bit GGUF PTQ, but signals general "low precision is not a free lunch" for LLM training pipelines the project runs (mixed-precision SFT/DPO/GRPO).
- The survey explicitly does not cover the technique class most relevant to shipping a 4-bit GGUF: "Although there is a large body of work on PTQ techniques for LLMs [145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 151, 152], these methods are beyond the scope of this survey, and thus will not be covered here." (Section VI) — this paper cannot settle the "does 4-bit quantization damage structured output more than knowledge" question for us.
- Field-wide gap flagged by the authors themselves: "The absence of consistent benchmarks and evaluation protocols makes it difficult to systematically compare different low-precision training methods across architectures and tasks." (Section VIII) — reinforces that our own BFCL/IFEval-based quantization eval plan isn't something we can borrow off-the-shelf from this literature.

## Metric / eval choices

- No task-specific eval metrics transfer (this survey's cited work evaluates classification accuracy or LM pretraining loss/perplexity, not tool-calling/schema-validity/instruction-following benchmarks).
- One general finding worth carrying into optimizer-state design during our SFT/DPO/GRPO runs: "while the first-order moments in Adam tolerate 4-bit quantization, the second-order moments require more careful handling, even at 8-bit precision" (Chitsaz et al., Section III-B2) — a caution if we ever consider quantized optimizer states to save memory on the managed hardware.

## Open questions for our project

- Nothing here answers whether low-bit *weight-only* quantization (our actual GGUF path) hurts structured-output/tool-calling more than general knowledge — the survey's scope (training-time, mostly full quantization of weights+activations+gradients) is a different problem from PTQ of a fully-trained 1.2B checkpoint. Would need to follow up on the PTQ survey it cites instead (Gong et al., "A survey of low-bit large language models," arXiv:2409.16694) or the specific PTQ papers it excludes.
- No content on 65K vs 128K vocabulary effects on tokenization/quantization sensitivity for tool-call/JSON generation.
- No content on reward design or "flag rather than assert" training signals — out of scope for this survey entirely.
- The paper contains no text that reads as an instruction addressed to an AI reader; nothing flagged under the untrusted-data rule.
