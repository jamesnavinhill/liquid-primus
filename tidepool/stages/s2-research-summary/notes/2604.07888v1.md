# 2604.07888v1 — Bit-by-Bit: Progressive QAT Strategy with Outlier Channel Splitting for Stable Low-Bit LLMs

**Relevance:** medium
The paper targets 2-bit QAT with custom CUDA kernels and block-wise progressive schedules, well beyond this project's 4-bit GGUF target, but its appendix directly measures whether quantization hurts instruction-following less than knowledge/reasoning — one of this project's open questions — and its calibration/training budget is small enough to be a plausible reference point if QAT is ever needed.

## TL;DR

Bit-by-Bit is a QAT recipe (progressive bit-width annealing 8→4→2 bit, "train once deploy any precision," and rounding-aware outlier channel splitting) aimed at making 2-bit weight and weight+activation quantization stable for LLaMA/Mistral-scale models. It is not about 4-bit GGUF export or tool-calling models, but its appendix ablation on IFEval/MMLU/GSM8k under w2a2 quantization is directly relevant to how instruction-following degrades relative to knowledge under aggressive quantization.

## Techniques to try

- Progressive precision annealing (quantize weights before activations, high-bit before low-bit) as a stability trick if quantization-aware training is attempted: "We found that reducing weight precision first, followed by activation bits, yields the most stable results" (Section 3.2).
- Per-group microscaling with FP8 (E4M3) scale factors instead of power-of-two (E8M0) scales for finer dynamic-range control at low bit-widths — relevant to how the GGUF 4-bit export chooses its quantization block/scale format: "We use E4M3 FP8 for group scales instead. This format provides sufficient mantissa precision for accurate step-size adjustment... resulting in a storage overhead of just 8/32=0.25 bits per weight" (Section 3.5).
- The training/calibration budget for QAT here is modest — a 4096-sample RedPajama subset for a few epochs — not a massive token budget: "our method (Bit-by-Bit) achieves a 3600× reduction in token requirements compared to ParetoQ" (Figure 2 caption, Section 1). This suggests QAT-style stabilization need not be prohibitively expensive if the candidate ladder reaches that rung.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baselines: PTQ (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, SmoothQuant, MatQuant, SpinQuant) and QAT (EfficientQAT, ParetoQ, BitDistiller), evaluated on LLaMA-2/3, LLaMA-3.2 (1B/3B), and Mistral-7B.
- Datasets: WikiText2 and C4 perplexity; zero-shot PIQA, ARC-Easy/Challenge, HellaSwag, Winogrande; appendix adds GSM8k, MathQA, MMLU, IFEval on Llama2-13B and Qwen2.5-7B/14B.
- All quantization/QAT training uses a 4096-sample RedPajama (or RedPajama+Alpaca) subset; PTQ baselines use 256-sample RedPajama calibration. All experiments on a single H800 GPU.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Group size matters a lot at ultra-low bit: "using group-128 saves only 0.04GB of memory but leads to a sharp degradation in performance where task accuracy falls from 45.18 to 38.60" (Section 4.4, Table 3) — group-32 clearly preferred over group-128 for w2a16 on LLaMA-3.2-1B.
- Newer/stronger base architectures are far more quantization-robust than older ones, and this gap widens under aggressive quantization: "While Llama2-13B suffers a severe performance drop in the w2a2 setting (e.g., GSM8k score halving from 0.22 to 0.11), the Qwen2.5-14B maintains near-lossless performance, dropping only from 0.84 to 0.81" (Appendix D). The paper explicitly flags this as unresolved: "We observe larger performance drops on the Qwen family, these models appear harder to quantize... a deeper analysis is left for future work" (Appendix A.3, Limitations) — note this contradicts the sentence right after it in the main text about "newer architecture is much more resilient," so the paper's own story about architecture and quantization difficulty is internally inconsistent and shouldn't be taken as a clean rule.
- Muon optimizer did not help in this QAT setting: "Muon did not yield consistent gains over AdamW: convergence speed and final perplexity were comparable or slightly worse" (Appendix B.5) — a negative result for anyone considering exotic optimizers for a QAT/distillation rung.
- Block-wise progressive training "is less friendly to distributed training than end-to-end schemes, requiring nontrivial load-balancing and communication engineering" (Appendix A.3) — an infrastructure cost to weigh if this recipe were ever adapted.
- Everything here is validated on 2-bit (w2a16, w2a2) settings on general-purpose base models with no tool-calling/structured-output evaluation; none of the custom CUDA kernel work (W2A2/W2A16 GEMV) is applicable to a 4-bit GGUF/llama.cpp deployment path.

## Metric / eval choices

- Appendix D's Table 5 is the one directly relevant data point for this project's quantization question: under w2a2 on Llama2-13B, IFEval drops only 0.18→0.16 (~11% relative) while GSM8k drops 0.22→0.11 (50% relative) and MMLU drops 0.52→0.40 (~23% relative) — i.e., in this experiment instruction-following degraded noticeably less than knowledge/reasoning under aggressive (2-bit) quantization. This is suggestive evidence for this project's question of whether low-bit quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more or less than knowledge, though the metric is IFEval (natural-language instruction following), not schema validity or tool-call correctness, and the quantization level (2-bit) is far more aggressive than the 4-bit target here.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the IFEval-vs-knowledge robustness gap seen here at 2-bit on Llama2-13B/Qwen2.5 hold at 4-bit on a 1.2B hybrid-architecture model, and does it extend to schema validity / tool-call correctness rather than just natural-language instruction following? This paper gives no direct signal on structured output or tool calling.
- No result in this paper is below ~1B parameters other than LLaMA-3.2-1B (used only for ablations, not the instruction-following table), so applicability to a 1.2B model is only partly supported.
- The paper's custom CUDA kernels and progressive-bit training loop assume full control over training infrastructure and are not compatible with a llama.cpp/GGUF export pipeline; nothing here informs the actual 4-bit GGUF packaging step this project needs.
