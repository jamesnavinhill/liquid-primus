# 2602.08813v2 — Robust Policy Optimization to Prevent Catastrophic Forgetting

**Relevance:** medium
Directly addresses "how is general ability kept from eroding while a narrow one is trained up" via a no-extra-compute GRPO modification tested at ≤8B scale, but the paper's setting is the reverse of ours (it makes the *upstream* RLHF stage robust to a *later, separate* downstream fine-tune we don't control) and it never tests quantization as the perturbation, only further SFT/RL.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes FRPO, a drop-in modification of GRPO that trains a policy to be robust within a KL-bounded neighborhood of itself, so that any later fine-tuning that stays inside that neighborhood does less damage to the behavior trained first (e.g., safety guardrails). It's derived as a max-min (worst-case-reward) objective that reduces to an entropic-risk term controlled by a single scalar λ, requires no extra forward/backward passes over vanilla GRPO, and empirically preserves safety guardrails and math accuracy much better than GRPO across SFT and RL downstream fine-tunes on 7B-class models (Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B).

## Techniques to try

- FRPO's core change to GRPO: replace the reward-weighted advantage with an exponential-tilted (entropic-risk) term $\hat Z_\lambda(x)=\frac{1}{G}\sum_i\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t e^{-A_{i,t}/\lambda}\lceil \pi_\theta/\pi_{old}\rceil^{1+\epsilon}$, plus a variance-reducing baseline and a jackknife bias correction for small group size ("FRPO, a policy gradient method that integrates seamlessly with the GRPO framework... recovers GRPO as λ→∞" — Introduction, Contributions).
- Practical λ-selection rule without sweeping: set λ ≈ std(reward) / √(2ρ), where ρ is the target KL-divergence budget ("According to Remark 3.3, λ is approximately optimal at std(r(x,y))/√(2ρ)" — Section 5.1.1, "Selecting λ").
- Empirically-grounded KL budget: per-token KL in [0.02, 0.3] is where downstream adaptation stays useful; beyond per-token KL ≈ 0.3 the model overfits and "helpfulness reward for all the models nearly collapses" — a concrete stopping/regularization heuristic for any fine-tuning stage that needs to avoid degrading held-out capability ("around per-token-KL=0.3, that helpfulness reward for all the models nearly collapses" — Section 2, "KL-bounded is bounded during fine-tuning").
- Training recipe used for the 7B models: LoRA r=64, α=64 (safety stage) or α=128 (math stage), group size G=8, advantages centered but *not* std-normalized (to avoid "difficulty bias"), rewards rescaled to [0,1] via sigmoid — a concrete GRPO hyperparameter template at a scale close to ours ("We use LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) with r=64 and α=64... group size G=8... do not normalize the advantages by the standard deviation to avoid the difficulty bias" — Appendix C.1, Section 2).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base models: Mistral-v0.1-Instruct (7B) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; a KL-vs-forgetting validation sweep also used Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B/32B.
- Safety dataset: 1000 harmful + 1000 harmless prompts, harmful from WildJailbreak / Aegis AI Content Safety 2.0 / RainbowTeaming, harmless from OR-Bench (to control over-refusal); reward = 1 − avg(OpenAI Moderation score, StrongREJECT judge score) for harmful prompts, an off-the-shelf helpfulness RM for harmless prompts.
- Downstream fine-tune tasks used to induce forgetting: SFT on Alpaca, SFT on GSM8K, RL (GRPO) on UltraFeedback for helpfulness; math continual-learning experiment: GRPO on MATH (levels 3–5) then LoRA SFT on "nvidia/OpenCodeInstruct" for code, measuring MATH500/MBPP+ retention.
- Baselines compared: vanilla GRPO, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3-TAR (adversarial fine-tuning defense), Llama-3-RR / Mistral-RR (representation-noising unlearning), Llama-3-derta, and GRPO+SAM (sharpness-aware minimization in parameter space).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Parameter-efficient methods, despite widespread adoption, do not reliably prevent forgetting—LoRA fine-tuning still degrades prior capabilities, sometimes comparably to full fine-tuning (Biderman et al., 2024)" — cited as a direct caution against assuming LoRA alone protects general ability during specialization (Appendix A).
- High-learning-rate SFT without LoRA badly damages general capability even when the KL-to-base metric looks only "slightly" larger: "the helpfulness score is highly compromised" at lr=1e-5 vs 1e-6, i.e. the same KL-ball heuristic can mask real overfitting at high LR (Appendix B.5).
- SAM (sharpness-aware minimization in *parameter* space, L2 ball) underperforms FRPO's KL-space approach for preserving safety after downstream SFT, and "the performance is fragile w.r.t the perturbation radius and degrades above the optimal ρ" — parameter-space flatness is not equivalent to output-distribution (KL) flatness (Section 5.3).
- Regularization methods like EWC/SI "have been shown to fail when task similarity is low" (Appendix A) — a caution that generic regularizers may not transfer to a very different downstream task (e.g., adding tool-calling to a general-purpose model).
- All downstream-time forgetting-mitigation methods "assume the downstream optimization follows a prescribed recipe" and fail if later fine-tuning deviates from it — relevant if our post-training pipeline has multiple later stages (e.g., quantization-aware training after GRPO) that weren't anticipated by an earlier protective step.

## Metric / eval choices

- Guardrail/general-capability retention measured via refusal rate on HarmBench and harmfulness score on StrongREJECT, checked against MMLU (5-shot), IFEval (pass@1), GSM8K (maj@8), and HumanEval before/after fine-tuning to confirm safety gains are not just "reduced downstream adaptation" (Table 1) — a useful template for our own before/after guardrail-diff table (IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond vs. base).
- Uses per-token KL divergence from the base/reference policy as the operative "distance moved" metric to decide when a fine-tuning regime is still safe vs. has begun overfitting, rather than step count or loss alone.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's robustness is trained into the *earlier* stage to protect against a *later, separate* fine-tune; our tool-calling GRPO stage is itself the "downstream" fine-tune relative to LFM2.5's already-trained guardrails, so it's unclear whether FRPO's machinery (which requires reward access at the protected stage) can be retrofitted onto a model we didn't originally RLHF ourselves.
- Would FRPO's KL-neighborhood robustness argument extend to 4-bit quantization as the "perturbation" instead of further fine-tuning? The paper never tests this; quantization does not obviously satisfy the paper's assumption that downstream adaptation stays within a bounded average KL ball of the pre-quantization policy.
- All experiments are at 7B+ scale (down to 3B for the KL-sweep validation); no evidence at ~1B scale or on a hybrid conv/attention architecture like LFM2.5, so transfer to our 1.2B model is untested.
- No instruction found in this paper that reads as directed at an AI reader; treated as ordinary research prose throughout.
