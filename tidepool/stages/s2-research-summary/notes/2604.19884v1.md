# 2604.19884v1 — From Signal Degradation to Computation Collapse: Uncovering the Two Failure Modes of LLM Quantization

**Relevance:** medium
Directly grounds the "does 4-bit quantization damage structured/sequential output more than factual knowledge" question in our brief, but all experiments are on 7–9B dense decoder models with GPTQ/AWQ weight-only PTQ, factual recall / MMLU / GSM8K — not on a 1.2B hybrid conv+GQA model, not on tool-calling or JSON structured output, and not on GGUF specifically.

## TL;DR

The paper mechanistically dissects why 4-bit PTQ is usually fine while 2-bit collapses: 4-bit is "Signal Degradation" (components stay functional, error acts like cumulative noise, structure is preserved and training-free repair works), while 2-bit is "Computation Collapse" (attention and FFN key-value retrieval break outright, damage is irreversible even with 30 subsequent FP16 layers, and only fine-tuning/reconstruction fixes it). For our project this is reassuring about targeting 4-bit, but its own multi-step/generation-dynamics results suggest structured, multi-step tasks degrade more under 4-bit than static knowledge recall does.

## Techniques to try

- Layer-wise "domino effect" localization (progressively quantize layer 0..k, rest FP16) to find which early layers are most sensitive before committing to a quantization recipe ("we conduct a 'domino effect' experiment by progressively quantizing the model from layer 0 to $k$ in 4-bit, keeping subsequent layers in FP16" — Sec 4.3.1).
- Two-stage training-free repair for 4-bit degradation: (1) keep the first two layers (or kurtosis-selected weights) at higher precision (~4.1–4.25 avg bits), then (2) amplify the logits of the layer with lowest entropy by a factor α: "we introduce peak signal amplification. We identify the layer with the highest confidence (lowest entropy) and amplify its output logits by a factor $\alpha>1$" (Sec 4.3.1) — recovered Llama3.1-8B from 0.00% to 75.19% and Mistral-7B to 81.26% on their Failure Subset. This is a candidate cheap post-quantization check/fix for our 4-bit GGUF build, though it was validated on factual-recall failure cases, not tool-call/JSON generation.
- Component-wise sensitivity probing (quantize one component at a time — QK proj, V proj, O proj, gate/up, down) to find which weights to protect first; for Llama/Mistral, "MLP modules are significantly more fragile than Attention modules... 'content generation' weights (down_proj, v_proj) are far more critical than the 'routing' weights" (Appendix C.1) — but note architecture-dependence (Qwen/Gemma showed uniform sensitivity instead), so this would need to be re-derived for LFM2.5's hybrid conv+GQA blocks rather than assumed.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Models: Llama-3.1-8B (primary), Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-it — all well above our 1.2B target scale.
- Quantization: GPTQ (primary, group size 128, calibrated on 128 C4 sequences of length 2048), with AWQ used only for a generalizability check (Appendix D). No GGUF/llama.cpp-style quantization tested.
- Tasks: Pararel factual recall (39 relation types, cloze-style), plus MMLU (5-shot, 1,066-sample subset) and GSM8K (5-shot, full set) for broader generalizability (Appendix E). No structured-output, tool-calling, or JSON-schema tasks.
- Compensation baseline compared against: EORA (Liu et al. 2024), an "advanced low-rank compensation method," which also failed to fix 2-bit Computation Collapse.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- 2-bit quantization is not fixable with any training-free method they tried: "the 2-bit collapse resists all such interventions" including both their own early-layer/kurtosis protection and EORA low-rank compensation (Sec 4.3.2) — reconstruction (fine-tuning) is required instead of compensation. Relevant if our quantization-aware-distillation/QAT fallback step is ever needed at very low bit-widths.
- Damage from 2-bit quantization is not just severe but irreversible even given perfect upstream signal: keeping the first k layers at 8-bit/4-bit and only quantizing later layers to 2-bit still causes cosine similarity to collapse immediately upon entering the 2-bit layers — "confirms 2-bit components are computationally non-functional, failing to sustain information even given perfect input" (Sec 4.3.2, Fig 12).
- On GSM8K specifically (closest proxy in this paper to structured, multi-step generation), 4-bit degrades more than on MMLU: 56.79%→51.33% (4-bit) vs MMLU 63.42%→61.44%, and at 2-bit the model "fails to execute fine-grained reasoning... generates chaotic content (e.g., meaningless numbers and repetitive loops), typically failing to halt until hitting the maximum generation length (with median generated tokens doubling from 77 in FP16 to 151 in 2-bit)" (Appendix E.3). This is a directly relevant warning sign: quantization can induce "does not stop / runs on" failures in generation, which is exactly the kind of failure mode our BFCLv3 / schema-validity metrics would penalize, even though this paper never tests tool-call or JSON output directly.
- Only weight-only PTQ is studied; the authors flag activation quantization as unstudied: "our investigation currently focuses on weight-only quantization... extending these findings to other paradigms, such as activation quantization, remains a direction for future work" (Limitations). Our GGUF target uses weight-only quantization too, so this is applicable, but any activation-quantization scheme would need separate validation.

## Metric / eval choices

- Answer-rank tracking (rank of the correct token in the full vocab distribution) as a finer-grained signal than accuracy — distinguishes "rank drop but still top-5" (4-bit) from "rank collapse to thousands" (2-bit) (Sec 3.2, Fig 2). Could be adapted as a diagnostic for our schema-token-level degradation, not just end-to-end pass/fail.
- Logit lens layer-wise probability/rank tracing to see where in the network a signal degrades vs. never forms (Sec 3.3).
- Causal activation patching (cross-model repair for sufficiency, zeroing ablation for necessity) borrowed from mechanistic-interpretability literature (Meng et al. 2022; Heimersheim & Nanda 2024) to test whether a pathway is merely noisy vs. structurally broken (Sec 3.4).
- Attention entropy + Jensen-Shannon divergence, FFN gate sign-flip-rate + Jaccard index on top-activated neurons, and CKA/SVD subspace-alignment metrics as component- and representation-level diagnostics (Sec 4.1–4.2) — these are generic and could in principle be run on LFM2.5's GQA attention blocks (its short-convolution blocks would need adaptation since they aren't standard attention/FFN).

## Open questions for our project

- None of this paper's tasks or metrics test structured output, tool-call JSON, or schema validity — the brief's core question ("does quantization damage structured output/instruction-following more than knowledge") is only indirectly supported here via the GSM8K generation-runaway result; a targeted probe on our own model's BFCL/tool-call outputs under 4-bit vs FP16 would still be needed.
- All findings are on 7–9B dense transformer decoders; it's unclear whether "4-bit = recoverable degradation, 2-bit = collapse" transfers to a 1.2B parameter budget, where redundancy is much lower and per-layer/per-component sensitivity could shift the degradation threshold to a higher bit-width than 4-bit.
- LFM2.5's architecture (gated short-convolution blocks + a few GQA blocks) is not a standard transformer stack; the paper's FFN key-value-memory and attention-entropy diagnostics assume standard self-attention/FFN blocks throughout, so applying its mechanistic toolkit to the convolution blocks would require new instrumentation.
- The paper's own repair recipe (early-layer 8-bit protection + logit amplification) was tuned per-model with a hand-picked α (3–9 depending on model); it is unclear whether such a recipe transfers to GGUF-style quantization (different grouping/scale scheme than GPTQ) or would need re-deriving for LFM2.5.
