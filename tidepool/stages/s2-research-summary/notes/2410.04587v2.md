# 2410.04587v2 — Hammer: Robust Function-Calling for On-Device Language Models via Function Masking

**Relevance:** high
Gives a concrete, small-scale (1.5B–7B) SFT data-augmentation recipe — function masking plus an irrelevance-augmented dataset — that targets exactly the "call vs. abstain" reliability problem this project must instill in LFM2.5-1.2B, with quantified BFCL numbers at 1.5B scale.

## TL;DR

Hammer fine-tunes small open models (1.5B/4B/7B) on an augmented xLAM-function-calling-60k dataset using two tricks: masking function/parameter names with random strings during training so the model must rely on descriptions, and adding 7,500 examples where the correct function is deliberately withheld (label = empty list) to teach irrelevance detection. This raises BFCL robustness across naming-convention shifts and gives it near-GPT-4 overall BFCL scores despite being 7B or smaller, with a 1.5B variant reported too.

## Techniques to try

- Function masking during SFT: replace function names, parameter names, and default values in the tool schema with random strings at training time (and correspondingly in the labels), forcing the model to ground calls in the natural-language descriptions rather than memorized names ("Function names in candidates are masked by replacing them with randomly generated strings during training... Parameter names in candidates are substituted with random strings as well" — Section 4.1).
- Irrelevance-augmented data: sample training instances where the correct function is removed from the candidate list and the label is replaced with an empty list, to counteract accuracy/irrelevance-detection trade-off ("this augmentation... incorporates 7,500 examples sampled from the original training set, in which the correct functions are deliberately excluded from the candidate list, and the labels are replaced with empty lists" — Section 4.2). Directly analogous machinery could be adapted to teach "flag rather than assert" on malformed tool *returns* rather than absent tools, though that is a different failure mode (see open questions).
- Tune the mask ratio rather than masking everything or nothing: a higher ratio slowed in-distribution (same-task) learning but improved cross-task/naming generalization, so the ratio is itself a lever for the "specialize to the underlying technology, not one vendor's naming" goal ("an excessively large mask ratio can impede the model's learning speed within the same task scenario... a larger mask ratio facilitates better generalization of the model across different scenarios" — Section 5.5).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base dataset: xlam-function-calling-60k (from Salesforce's APIGen), augmented with the 7.5k irrelevance set, open-sourced at `MadeAgents/XLAM-7.5k-Irrelevance`.
- Small base models fine-tuned with this recipe: Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen1.5-4B-Chat, Qwen2-7B-Instruct, Deepseek-Coder-1.3B-Instruct, Deepseek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct — the 1.5B/1.3B rows are the closest scale to this project's 1.2B target.
- Out-of-domain evaluation sets: BFCL v2, API-Bank, Tool-Alpaca, Seal-Tools, Nexus Raven API Evaluation — all treated as generalization tests, not just in-domain accuracy.
- At 1.5B scale: Hammer-1.5B reaches BFCL overall 73.04 (Irrelevance 72.18, Relevance 92.68) vs. its own base Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct's much lower unmasked AST overall of 46.90 (Irrelevance 22.91, Relevance 92.68) — a roughly +26-point overall gain and a +49-point irrelevance-detection gain from this SFT recipe alone at comparable parameter count (Table 7, Table 2).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Naively fine-tuning on function-selection data hurts irrelevance detection: "we identified a concerning inverse relationship between the model's ability to accurately execute function calls and its capacity for irrelevance detection" (Section 4.2) — this is the direct evidence that a schema/execution-only reward could suppress the "flag" behavior this project needs, unless explicitly counterbalanced.
- The irrelevance/function-calling trade-off persists even after augmentation and must be tuned via data ratio; the paper's own chosen ratio (~10% irrelevance-augmented data) is explicitly flagged as not necessarily transferable: "this proportion may require adjustment depending on the underlying model and training dataset; thus, the ratios presented herein are intended as a reference only" (Section 5.6).
- At the 1.5B scale, Hammer-1.5B's Executable "Simple" accuracy is unusually low (49.93, Table 4) compared to its own AST-Simple score (62.34) and to larger Hammer variants (91.86 at 7B, 67.79 at 4B) — a scale-dependent degradation the paper does not explain, worth treating as a caution that small-model exec-mode reliability doesn't scale smoothly down from 7B.
- No training hyperparameters (LoRA vs. full fine-tune, learning rate, epochs, compute budget) are reported anywhere in the paper, so the recipe's cost/feasibility at 1.2B cannot be directly estimated from this source.

## Metric / eval choices

- BFCL evaluates via two axes worth reusing as a design pattern: AST evaluation (structural/type correctness of the generated call against the function schema) and Executable Function Evaluation (actually running the call and checking output correctness) — directly parallel to this project's "first-attempt schema validity" vs. functional-correctness distinction.
- BFCL's separate "Irrelevance" and "Relevance" sub-scores (calibration when no tool applies, vs. when one does) are scored as a pair the way this project's flag-rate/false-flag-rate pair is meant to be — e.g., Hammer-7B posts Irrelevance 72.87 / Relevance 92.68 simultaneously, illustrating that one score alone is gameable (models can hit near-100 Relevance while cratering Irrelevance, e.g. mistral-large-2407 FC-Any at Irrelevance 0.34 / Relevance 100.00 in Appendix Table 6).
- Non-BFCL benchmarks (API-Bank, Tool-Alpaca, Seal-Tools, Nexus Raven) are scored with F1 on function-name match and F1 on function-name+arguments match, used as a stand-in for "did it call the exactly right tool with exactly right args" when no executable harness exists.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's "irrelevance" is about absent/no-matching-function scenarios, not malformed tool *return values*; it's unclear whether function-masking-style training or the empty-list augmentation transfers to teaching a model to flag garbage tool *outputs* (this project's actual 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate target), or whether a distinct augmentation is needed.
- All reported gains are on Qwen and Deepseek-Coder bases with larger vocabularies than LFM2.5's 65,536 tokens; it's untested whether name-masking (which relies on generating random-string identifiers as replacement tokens) behaves the same under a smaller/hybrid-architecture vocabulary.
- No quantization results are reported at all — nothing here bears on the project's 4-bit GGUF retention targets.
- The paper does not specify LoRA vs. full fine-tuning, so it cannot directly settle that open question for this project's candidate ladder, though it's evidence that plain SFT (of unspecified type) is sufficient to produce large gains at 1.5B–7B scale, before any DPO/GRPO stage.
