# 2411.03350v2 — A Comprehensive Survey of Small Language Models in the Era of Large Language Models: Techniques, Enhancements, Applications, Collaboration with LLMs, and Trustworthiness

**Relevance:** medium
A broad, ≤7B-parameter SLM survey with no direct BFCL/IFEval numbers or GGUF/4-bit throughput data for our exact setup, but it supplies useful background on architecture choices matching LFM2.5's hybrid design, a catalogue of quantization/fine-tuning methods, and a concrete pointer that quantization degrades trustworthiness — worth knowing even though it doesn't settle any pre-registered target.

## TL;DR

This is a survey (not original experiments) cataloguing how sub-7B "small language models" are built, compressed, fine-tuned, deployed on-device, and made to assist or be assisted by LLMs. For our post-training project it mostly offers taxonomy and pointers to primary sources rather than numbers we can adopt directly, though it does surface one directly relevant negative result about quantization and trustworthiness, and several tool-calling SLM precedents worth chasing down individually.

## Techniques to try

- LoRA, prefix-tuning, and adapter modules are the standard PEFT menu for SLMs; the survey frames LoRA as "avoiding significant alterations to pre-trained weights" ("$\Delta\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$" — Section 2.2.2), consistent with our candidate ladder's LoRA-first step.
- Domain-adapt teacher *and* student before distillation rather than distilling directly: "the 'Adapt-and-Distill' strategy—first involving domain adaptation of both the large teacher model and the small student model, followed by distillation—is the most effective compared to three other strategies" (Section 3.4, re: AdaLM/Yao et al. 2021) — relevant if we ever bring in a distillation step for the quantization-aware stage.
- Architecture-level precedent for our target model's hybrid design: "Attention heads in the Transformer facilitate high-resolution recall, while SSM heads in Mamba efficiently summarize context... Hymba integrates both attention and SSM heads within the same layer" (Section 2.1.3) — LFM2.5's gated-conv + GQA hybrid is in this same design family; Hymba-1.5B reportedly beats Llama-3.2-3B on commonsense reasoning with an 11.67x smaller cache and 3.49x speedup, though that's a different architecture, not ours.
- On-device tool-calling precedent worth reading directly: "Octopus (Chen et al., 2024a) uses a diverse dataset from over 30K APIs and curriculum learning... to improve API function accuracy" and "PhoneLM-1.5B-Call (Yi et al., 2024) is fine-tuned on DroidCall... and achieves comparable performance compared to GPT-4o-mini" (Section 4.1.5) — both are ~1-7B on-device function-calling SLMs, closely analogous to our task; the survey only summarizes them, so the primary papers would need separate review.
- Quantization method catalogue relevant to our 4-bit GGUF target: GPTQ, AWQ, QLoRA (NF4 + double quantization), and LLM-QAT (data-free distillation-based QAT achieving "accurate 4-bit quantization for weights and key-value caches, and 6-bit for activations") are all listed as candidates for the quantization-aware step (Sections 2.3.3, 3.5, Table 1).

## Baselines & datasets used

- None specific to our task; this is a survey aggregating results from other papers. Table 4 does show a directly relevant scale comparison: fine-tuned BioGPT-Large (1.5B) hits 81.0% zero-shot on PubMedQA vs. GPT-4's 74.4% few-shot on the same benchmark — an example of a <2B fine-tuned SLM beating GPT-4 on a narrow domain task, which is the same bet our project is making for tool-calling/structured-output.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Direct hit on one of our open questions ("How is reward hacking avoided..." / general degradation risk): "Hong et al. (2024) examines the effects of compression methods, including quantization and pruning, on the trustworthiness of language models. They find that pruning and extreme quantization significantly affect the trustworthiness of LMs" (Section 8) — this is reported as a finding of a cited paper, not something this survey measured itself, so the actual magnitude/axes would need to come from Hong et al. (2024) directly, but it's a warning sign for our "no axis below 93%" 4-bit retention target.
- Mamba-style SSM architectures "may compromise performance on tasks that require nuanced understanding, such as detailed sentiment analysis or complex entity recognition" and struggle with "multi-round tasks within a single query, such as interactive dialogue systems" (Section 2.1.2) — a caution about the conv/SSM half of LFM2.5's hybrid design for multi-turn agent conversations, though this is about pure-Mamba, not the attention-hybrid our target model uses.
- Instruction-tuned SLMs cloning teacher outputs ("behavior cloning") "struggles in reasoning-intensive QA tasks where accuracy is key, not style" (Section 4.1.1) — a caution that plain SFT-on-LLM-outputs may not transfer to correctness-critical tasks like ours (schema validity, execution correctness), motivating the later DPO/GRPO rungs on our candidate ladder.
- "Adapting both the teacher (LLMs) and the student (SLMs) models to the target domain can be time-consuming" (Section 3.4) — flagged as a cost/complexity caveat on the Adapt-and-Distill approach above.

## Metric / eval choices

- The survey explicitly flags an evaluation gap that matches our situation: "current evaluation tasks focus primarily on metrics like accuracy. Evaluating on-device SLMs involves balancing multiple factors, including overall capabilities, response times, storage and memory usage, power consumption, CPU utilization, additional fine-tuning requirements, and context window constraints" (Section 9.4) — supports our project's multi-axis eval design (BFCL + guardrail metrics + throughput + on-disk size) rather than a single accuracy number.
- No BFCL, IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF, MMLU-Pro, or GPQA numbers appear anywhere in the paper — this survey predates or simply doesn't cover the benchmark suite our project targets.

## Open questions for our project

- The survey doesn't quantify whether a 65,536-token vocabulary (our target model's size) meaningfully hurts JSON/tool-call tokenization versus 128K-vocab peers; it only notes generically that "embedding layers often constitute over 20% of a model's parameters" (Section 5.1.1), which is a parameter-budget point, not a tokenization-efficiency one. Our vocab-size question remains unanswered here.
- None of the flag-rate/false-flag-rate, first-attempt-schema-validity, or GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards questions from our brief are addressed in this survey; it is written at too high a level of abstraction (it's a survey of surveys' worth of methods, not an empirical study) to settle any of our pre-registered numeric targets.
- No instructions-to-the-reader or prompt-injection-like text was found in the paper; it reads as a standard academic survey throughout.
