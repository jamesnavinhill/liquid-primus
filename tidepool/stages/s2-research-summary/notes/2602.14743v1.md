# 2602.14743v1 — LLMStructBench: Benchmarking Large Language Model Structured Data Extraction

**Relevance:** medium
Useful metric-design ideas and a data point that structural JSON validity and semantic value correctness diverge even at small scale, but the paper is inference-only prompting research (no fine-tuning, no quantization, no tool-calling/BFCL setting), so it informs rather than settles our training decisions.

## TL;DR

The authors build a 995-example benchmark of email-style text-to-JSON extraction and test 22 open models (0.6B–70B, five families) plus GPT-4o across five prompting strategies that vary API-level JSON-mode enforcement vs. in-prompt schema/example guidance. Their headline finding is that prompting strategy and model-family compatibility matter as much as scale for getting parseable JSON, but "wrong value" errors (semantic mismatches) remain the dominant failure mode at every model size, including 70B and GPT-4o.

## Techniques to try

- Two prompting strategies dominated across the 22-model sweep: "P" (schema + example object only in the system/user prompt, no API-level format constraint) and "PJ+" (schema in both the prompt and the Ollama API `format` parameter as a full JSON-Schema object). Worth using as an evaluation-harness baseline before/alongside our fine-tuning: "P strategy often yields the best overall balance of accuracy and validity for well-aligned models but can fail catastrophically for some families (e.g., Phi)." while "PJ+ strategy is the safest choice for ensuring parseable outputs, especially for small or structurally unreliable models, but this comes at the cost of increased semantic errors." (Section VII, Conclusion)
- Splitting the eval into a key-presence F1 and a value-correctness F1, weighted so value correctness dominates, is a reusable idea for our own structured-output eval: "with a key weight of α=0.25" so that "a wrong value can silently corrupt downstream logic, whereas an extraneous or MK is often less severe." (Section IV-A, IV-B)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Dataset: "995 manually verified samples" across five synthetic email-style use cases (support tickets, sick leave, project extension, conference registration, loan request), each with 199 test cases, JSON schemas ranging from depth 2 (flat) to depth 4 (nested arrays of objects). Generated with GPT-4o then manually cleaned (Section III).
- Models: 22 variants across Qwen3 (0.6B–32B), Phi3/3.5/4 (3.8B–14B), Gemma3 (1B, 12B, 27B), Deepseek-R1 (1.5B–70B), Llama3.1/3.3 (70B), plus GPT-4o as a closed-weight reference excluded from scale-based analysis (Section V-A).
- Note for scale relevance: Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B are the closest data points to our 1.2B target model, but they are dense Qwen3 checkpoints (128K-class vocab), not the LFM2.5 hybrid conv/attention architecture with a 65,536 vocab, so the numbers are suggestive, not directly transferable.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Small models can look fine on per-field accuracy while being unusable at the document level: Gemma3-1B scored F1_micro = 0.89 but DOC_micro = 0.10, i.e. "the low count of flawless results (98 out of 955) ... to the bad performing DOC_micro-score (0.1). A perfect DOC_micro-Score can only be achieved when all JSON-Objects returned without any deviations to the GT." (Section VI-B, Table V)
- Prompt/model incompatibility can be catastrophic even for a "reasonable" family: Phi3-3.8B and Phi3.5-3.8B scored DOC_micro of 0.01 and 0.03 respectively under the P strategy — "the small Phi-family models generally do not perform good in this evaluation which is down to their incompatibility with the P-prompting strategy." (Section VI-B, Table V)
- Synthetic data generation broke down for deeper schemas: "We also experimented with use cases and schema that involved more nested JSON-objects, however, this did not result in even medium quality generated examples and have been therefore skipped. Often times the generation of the email was missing out on many crucial values from the ground-truth base object." (Section III-C) — a caution if we generate our own synthetic tool-call/JSON training data with deep nesting.
- The persistent bottleneck is semantic, not structural, and doesn't go away with scale: "WV errors remain the primary bottleneck across all configurations, suggesting that post-generation semantic validation or fine-tuning is needed for further gains" — even the 70B Deepseek-R1 model produced "at least one wrong field in roughly half of the test runs" (Section VI-C, Section VII).
- No monotonic scaling: "the leaderboard shows no strict monotonic relationship between parameter count and overall score" — e.g., Deepseek-R1-32B underperformed several smaller checkpoints under the P strategy (Section VI-B).

## Metric / eval choices

- Composite score: `Score = (1-λ)·F1_micro + λ·DOC_micro` with λ=0.5, combining token-level field accuracy with document-level "did it fully validate" success (Section IV-B, eq. 3).
- `DOC_micro = (#Correct/#Tests)·(1 - #Failed/#Tests)`, i.e., full-document exact-match rate tempered by outright failure rate (empty output, unparsable JSON, fatal schema violation) (Section IV-A, eq. 2) — conceptually close to our own first-attempt schema-validity metric, but scored as an aggregate rather than a per-sample first-attempt pass/fail.
- Error taxonomy used for failure analysis: Missing Key, Missing Value, and Wrong Value (further split into value deviation via Levenshtein distance, value error, coercible mismatch, and type error) — a usable template for classifying our own malformed-tool-return probe failures beyond a flat "bad JSON" bucket (Section IV).

## Open questions for our project

- The paper never fine-tunes or quantizes anything — it is entirely about inference-time prompting, so it gives no direct evidence on whether SFT/DPO/GRPO can close the "wrong value" gap that prompting alone could not, which is exactly the question our candidate ladder needs answered.
- The task (NL email → JSON extraction) is not agentic tool-calling (no multi-turn context, no tool schemas/results, no BFCL-style function selection), so transfer of the "match prompting strategy to model family" finding to our tool-call setting is unverified.
- No content in the paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a straightforward benchmarking report.
