# 2407.05591v1 — Convolution Augments Attention: Solving Associative Recall with One Layer

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap (theory for the short-convolution + attention hybrid family that LFM2.5's architecture belongs to, and why such hybrids solve associative recall), but the paper is pretraining/architecture-design research at ≤370M scale and says nothing about post-training, quantization, tool-calling, or structured output, so it settles none of this project's decisions.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes CAT, a layer that applies learnable 1D convolutional filters to the K/Q/V embeddings before attention, and proves a single CAT layer can exactly solve associative-recall (AR) and selective-copying tasks with guaranteed length generalization, unlike Mamba or plain attention. It validates this on synthetic AR/copying tasks and on a 370M-parameter Pythia-scale language model pretrained from scratch on 15B SlimPajama tokens, showing convolution improves perplexity and lets the model train stably even without positional encoding.

## Techniques to try

- None directly applicable — this is a from-scratch pretraining architecture proposal, not a post-training or quantization technique, and there is no ladder step in our project (LoRA/SFT/DPO/GRPO/quantization) that this paper's method plugs into.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Synthetic MQAR/MQNAR (multi-query associative recall) and selective-copying tasks, vocabulary size 8,092, sequence lengths 64–512, compared against Mamba, Based, linear attention, and plain attention baselines, all at embedding dims 32/64/128 ("we utilize convolution kernels with a width of $W=3$ and explore model embedding sizes of $d=32$, $64$, and $128$" — Sec. 6.1).
- Real language modeling: 370M-parameter Pythia backbone pretrained on 15B tokens of SlimPajama, evaluated zero-shot on Wikitext, LAMBADA, PIQA, HellaSwag, Winogrande, ARC-E, ARC-C, compared to Pythia, TF++/LLaMA-style transformer, Mamba, and GLA at matched scale ("We pretrain the modified 370M-parameter model on the SlimPajama soboleva2023slimpajama dataset, involving 15 billion tokens" — Sec. 6.2, Table 2).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Training a standard Pythia transformer with no positional encoding diverges outright: "training a Pythia model without positional encoding leads directly to divergence and extremely large losses during training" (Sec. 6.2) — relevant caution if we ever considered touching LFM2.5's positional scheme, though we are not planning to.
- The paper's own limitations section reports a null result for part of its method: "While K/Q convolution helps in theoretical constructions for N-gram AR, in real experiments, they don't provide noticeable performance benefits. We suspect that K/Q convolution might be *diluting* the attention scores" (Sec. 7, Discussion) — a case where a theoretically-motivated architectural change did not transfer to real-data gains, a useful general reminder to validate empirically rather than trust theory alone, but not tied to any of our target metrics.
- All results are pretraining-only at ≤370M parameters; the authors explicitly flag this as a limitation ("This work has a few shortcomings. We have only focused on pretraining" — Sec. 7), so nothing here has been shown to hold for instruction-tuned or fine-tuned models, let alone a 1.2B hybrid model already pretrained (our situation).

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses exact-match accuracy on synthetic AR/NAR/selective-copying tasks (100% accuracy is achievable and reported), plus standard zero-shot LM benchmarks (perplexity on Wikitext/LAMBADA, accuracy on PIQA/HellaSwag/Winogrande/ARC) — none of these are structured-output, tool-calling, or instruction-following metrics, so they don't inform our BFCL/IFEval/schema-validity metric choices.

## Open questions for our project

- LFM2.5 itself is described in this project as a hybrid of gated short-convolution blocks and GQA attention blocks — this paper's theory (short convolution on K embeddings gives exact, length-generalizing associative recall in a single layer) could in principle explain *why* such hybrids are chosen for on-device models, but the paper offers no guidance on how post-training, LoRA, or 4-bit quantization interacts with the convolution sub-blocks specifically, which is the actual open question for our quantization-robustness targets (does 4-bit hurt the conv paths' recall behavior more than the attention paths' knowledge?). This paper cannot answer that and would need architecture-level ablation on the actual LFM2.5 checkpoint to test.
- No instruction found in the paper that reads as directed at an AI reader; it is a standard academic paper throughout.
