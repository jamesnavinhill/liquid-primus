# 2407.11062v3 — EfficientQAT: Efficient Quantization-Aware Training for Large Language Models

**Relevance:** high
The candidate ladder explicitly ends in "quantization-aware distillation, then quantization-aware training if needed" for the 4-bit GGUF target, and this paper is a directly actionable QAT recipe (two-stage: block-wise full-parameter training then end-to-end step-size training) with concrete hyperparameters, data budgets, and quality-retention numbers at 2/3/4-bit — though all measured on 7B–70B Llama models, none at anything close to 1.2B.

## TL;DR

EfficientQAT quantizes a pretrained LLM in two cheap stages — block-wise training of all weights and quantization parameters (Block-AP), then end-to-end fine-tuning of just the step sizes (E2E-QP) — and beats prior PTQ/QAT/Q-PEFT methods at 2/3-bit while needing only ~4k calibration samples and a single GPU even for a 70B model. For our 4-bit GGUF target the gains over strong PTQ baselines (AWQ, GPTQ) are much smaller than at 2/3-bit, since 4-bit is already close to lossless for most PTQ methods.

## Techniques to try

- Two-phase QAT: first do full block-wise training of weights + step sizes + zero points ("Block-AP is the first to directly train all parameters during block-wise reconstruction"), then freeze quantized weights and train only step sizes end-to-end on the target dataset ("E2E-QP begins with $\mathbf{W}_{q}$ initialized via Block-AP and focuses solely on the training of quantization parameters ($s$ and $z$)") — this second phase is cheap enough to redo per target dataset/task, which matters if we want a tool-calling-specialized calibration set distinct from the SFT data.
- E2E-QP is described as directly adaptable to instruction tuning by swapping the calibration data: "Equipped with E2E-QP, EfficientQAT is adaptable to different scenarios by simply changing the training datasets, which includes applications such as continual pre-training and instruction-tuning" — suggests we could run E2E-QP with our own tool-call/JSON-heavy SFT data as the calibration set rather than a generic corpus.
- Group size of 64 is called out as the practical sweet spot for 2-bit weight-only quantization, trading a little extra metadata for accuracy: "a group size of 64 strikes an optimal balance for 2-bit quantization ... it slightly underperforms compared to a group size of 32, with a marginal difference of 0.09 in perplexity and 0.14% in accuracy" (Llama-2-7B, w2g64 vs w2g32/w2g128).
- Calibration data does not need to be domain-matched to get good general accuracy: using C4 or RedPajama (generic, diverse corpora) as calibration data works as well as or better than a narrow, matched dataset, per the training-dataset ablation (Appendix F, Table 13).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Compared against PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, AutoRound, QuIP#, AQLM), native QAT methods (LLM-QAT, BitDistiller, DB-LLM, BitNet b1.58), and Q-PEFT methods (QLoRA, QA-LoRA, PEQA, IR-QLoRA).
- Calibration/training data: 4096 samples from RedPajama for Block-AP (context 2048) and E2E-QP (context 4096); Alpaca dataset for the instruction-tuning experiments (5-shot MMLU eval).
- Model scales: Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, Llama-3 8B/70B, Llama-1 7B/13B, and LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B (vision-language). No result below 7B parameters — nothing at anything close to our 1.2B target model.
- Eval tasks: 5-shot MMLU, 5 zero-shot commonsense reasoning tasks (WinoGrande, PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge), WikiText2/C4 perplexity, and for LVLMs: MMBench, MME, MM-Vet, ScienceQA. No structured-output, tool-calling, or JSON-schema-validity evaluation anywhere in the paper.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "There remains a performance gap compared to full-precision (FP16) models, particularly in 2-bit settings" — the method does not fully close the quantization gap even with full QAT, and the paper's own limitations section flags this as unresolved.
- Method depends on data quantity and quality: "requiring 4096 samples for effective training in both the Block-AP and E2E-QP phases. The performance of the quantized models can vary significantly based on the size and distribution of the training data. This reliance may limit its effectiveness in data-scarce or domain-specific applications" — directly relevant if we'd want a small, highly specialized (SQL/JSON/MCP-schema) calibration set rather than a generic corpus.
- Naively training only the weights (ignoring step size/zero point) badly underperforms full Block-AP: "training only the weights results in a perplexity of 14.32, which is significantly higher than the 8.53 achieved by Block-AP" (Llama-2-7B, w2g64) — a specific ablation showing a plausible-looking shortcut that fails.
- At extreme low-bit (2-bit) the method still trails vector-quantization competitors like QuIP# and AQLM in raw accuracy, despite winning among uniform/weight-only methods — "EfficientQAT outperforms QuIP# in 3-bit quantization, but underperforms in 2-bit scenarios."
- Even the LVLM instruction-tuning ablation shows quantized fine-tuning can regress versus full-precision LoRA at 4-bit: "a slight performance decrease observed in the 4-bit EfficientQAT and 16-bit QLoRA compared to the 16-bit LoRA, indicating that further research is needed to optimize Q-PEFT within LVLMs."

## Metric / eval choices

- Zero-shot commonsense-reasoning accuracy (5 tasks averaged) and WikiText2/C4 perplexity are the primary quality metrics for the base-model quantization experiments; 5-shot MMLU accuracy is used for the instruction-tuning experiments. None of these measure instruction-following, schema validity, or agentic tool use — a direct gap relative to our BFCL/IFEval/structured-output targets.
- Training-vs-validation loss gap is used as an overfitting diagnostic when tuning calibration sample count (Figure 3), a useful pattern to reuse when tuning our own quantization calibration set size.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the Block-AP + E2E-QP recipe transfer down to a 1.2B hybrid-architecture model at all — the paper only tests 7B+ dense Llama/Llama-3/LLaVA models, and smaller models are noted elsewhere (Sec 4.1 discussion) to suffer larger absolute degradation from quantization, which argues our retention bar could be harder to hit than these results suggest.
- The paper never measures structured-output or tool-call fidelity, so it cannot answer our core question of whether low-bit quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more than general knowledge — this remains untested here and would need to be checked directly on our own eval suite.
- All speedup numbers are for BitBLAS/AWQ/Marlin/MLC-LLM kernels on A100 GPUs, not for GGUF/llama.cpp on a consumer GPU (our actual deployment target of an 8GB RTX 2080 Super) — inference-speed and format-compatibility claims do not transfer directly and would need separate validation.
- No text in the paper reads as an instruction addressed to an AI assistant; it is a standard research paper and was treated purely as descriptive source material.
