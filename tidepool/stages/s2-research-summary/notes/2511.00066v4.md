# 2511.00066v4 — Sharpness-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization via Probability Shaping

**Relevance:** medium
A concrete, low-overhead GRPO modification that could be dropped into the project's GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung, but every result is on reasoning/QA tasks at 3B–8B scale with accuracy rewards, not on schema/tool-call rewards or a model near 1.2B.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes GRPO-SG: reweight each token's GRPO update by a monotone function of that token's own selected-token logit (stop-gradient), so low-confidence tokens get downweighted and don't dominate the gradient norm. Motivated by a PAC-Bayes/sharpness generalization bound, it's a drop-in change to the GRPO surrogate loss that adds negligible compute and consistently beat plain GRPO across math, logic, and tool-augmented QA benchmarks at 3B–8B scale.

## Techniques to try

- Token-level confidence reweighting of the GRPO ratio: `w_{i,t} = clip(α·[σ(sg[h_{i,t}]/τ) − μ], L, U)` where `h_{i,t}` is the selected-token logit (stop-gradiented) — a nearly-free addition on top of an existing GRPO implementation since it reuses logits already computed ("these weights are derived directly from the model's own selected-token logits and therefore do not require any auxiliary extra forward passes" — Appendix C.2).
- Reported working hyperparameters (Qwen2.5-3B/7B, K&K logic task): α=2.0, μ=0.25, L=0.9, U=1.4, τ=9.0, with weights "centered near 1.0 after clipping" ("For token importance estimation (Eq. 20), the configuration uses the selected-token logit together with (α=2.0) and (μ=0.25), with clipping bounds (L=0.9) and (U=1.4), and the scaling parameter fixed to (τ=9.0)" — Appendix B.2).
- τ sensitivity matters more than the clip range: too-large τ flattens weights to near-uniform (no effect); too-small τ makes weights "overly sharp and highly disparate," which "amplify[s] per-token variance and destabilize[s] training" — best at a moderate τ≈9.0 ("As τ increases, the weighting function becomes flatter... In contrast, very small τ produces overly sharp and highly disparate weights" — Appendix C.1). The clip range (L,U) was tried over four settings and "achieves comparable performance" regardless — "the precise clipping range mainly acts as a safeguard against extreme values rather than a critical tuning factor" (Appendix C.1).
- The paper's own diagnostic (word-cloud analysis) is a reusable sanity check for any token-weighted RL scheme: high-probability tokens were mostly structural/syntactic (operators, brackets, formatting), while low-probability tokens were generic content words — i.e. standard GRPO's larger low-probability-token gradients don't obviously correspond to the tokens that matter for correctness ("high-probability tokens concentrate on mathematical and logical structure... where even a single error can break the entire solution, whereas low-probability tokens are mostly generic content words" — Appendix C.6). This is directly relevant to a JSON/tool-call setting where syntax tokens (braces, quotes, keys) are plausibly the high-confidence "structural" tokens that a schema-validity reward most needs preserved.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Math reasoning: Qwen2.5-7B, trained with DeepScaleR (DSR) and Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) recipes, evaluated on OlympiadBench, Minerva, MATH500, AMC, AIME.
- Agentic/tool-use QA: Search-R1/VerlTool setup ("VT-Search"), Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3-4B, retriever tool calls with intermediate tool-call tokens masked out of the policy loss; evaluated on NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA (general QA) and HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle (multi-hop QA), reward = exact-match on final answer.
- Logic: Knights & Knaves (K&K) puzzles, difficulty 3–7 people, reward checks both explicit `<think>`/`<answer>` format and correctness; tested across Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M, Qwen2.5-3B-Base, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct (Appendix C.5, "Cross-Model Validation").
- Compared against three other token-level GRPO variants: 80/20 (keep only high-entropy tokens), AR, and Lopti (both downweight/sequence low-probability tokens).
- All training on 8×NVIDIA H100 GPUs — not the project's managed/consumer-GPU-adjacent setup, but relevant mainly for training-time cost, not inference.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- GRPO-SG adds real training-time overhead, not just memory-free bookkeeping: "GRPO-SG does introduce a modest increase in training time per sample (e.g., 271.4 vs. 286.0 minutes on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, and 678.5 vs. 722.2 minutes on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct)" (Appendix C.2) — roughly a 5–6% wall-clock increase per sample, even though peak GPU memory is essentially unchanged (459.7 vs 460.2 GB reported for 3B, 581.2 vs 580.6 GB for 8B — note these memory figures look like aggregate/cluster totals, not per-GPU, given the scale).
- Author-stated limitation is exactly that overhead: "A key limitation of GRPO-SG is the extra compute from token sharpness control. Each update estimates and applies weights to all generated tokens, slightly increasing per-step cost over standard GRPO" (Appendix C.3).
- The "Reverse" ablation (flip the weighting rule to 2−w) tracks plain GRPO with no clear improvement — confirms the specific direction of the confidence-weighting (favor high-confidence tokens) is what matters, not just adding any token-level noise/heterogeneity to the update (Appendix C.7).
- On the agentic/tool-use benchmark, gains are inconsistent per-column even though the average improves: on Qwen3-4B, GRPO-SG loses to GRPO on HotpotQA (36.60 vs 35.54 baseline is a slight win actually, but check 2Wiki: GRPO-SG 30.89 vs GRPO 28.87, still a win) — actually all listed backbones improved on average, but "different methods win different retrieval-heavy QA columns," meaning no single token-weighting scheme dominates on every sub-metric — a caution against expecting uniform improvement across all guardrail axes simultaneously.
- Everything is validated on 3B+ models; there is no result at or below ~1.2B, and no test on schema-validity or execution-correctness rewards — the paper's own domains (math, logic, QA exact-match) are a mismatch to the project's structured-output reward signal, so the generalization-bound argument (which is domain-agnostic in theory) is untested empirically in this paper for that reward type.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports exact-match accuracy for QA tasks and puzzle-solved accuracy for K&K, plus an "Avg." and "Gain" (% relative improvement over the GRPO baseline) column per table — a useful reporting pattern (baseline, method, relative gain) that the project's own notes/comparisons could mirror.
- Uses gradient-norm trajectories (Figure 1) and training-reward trajectories (Appendix C.4) as secondary diagnostics beyond final accuracy, to argue the mechanism (reduced sharpness) is actually operating, not just correlated with better scores.

## Open questions for our project

- Does token-confidence reweighting (favoring high-logit/structural tokens) transfer to a schema-validity + execution-correctness reward, where the "structural" tokens are JSON punctuation/keys rather than math operators? The word-cloud finding (Appendix C.6) suggests it plausibly would, but this is speculative extrapolation, not something the paper tested.
- Would the ~5% training-time overhead reported here compound badly with the project's own 5% throughput-floor constraint, given that constraint is about inference (4-bit decode), not training — worth confirming this technique's cost is training-only and orthogonal to the shipped model's inference throughput.
- No instructions-to-the-reader content was found embedded in the paper; it reads as a standard NeurIPS-style submission with a checklist appendix.
