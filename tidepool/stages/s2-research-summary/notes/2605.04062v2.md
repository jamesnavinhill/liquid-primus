# 2605.04062v2 — EdgeRazor: A Lightweight Framework for Large Language Models via Mixed-Precision Quantization-Aware Distillation

**Relevance:** high
Directly instantiates the "quantization-aware distillation" step of our candidate ladder at comparable model scale (Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B, MobileLLM-350M), and its per-axis results empirically show that instruction-following (IFEval) and coding (HumanEval) degrade more from quantization than knowledge (MMLU) — exactly the tradeoff our 4-bit retention floor is meant to catch.

## TL;DR

EdgeRazor is a quantization-aware distillation (QAD) recipe with three pieces — a mixed-precision bit layout, a distillation-layer selector, and an adaptive forward/reverse KL mix — aimed at pushing weight/activation quantization down to 1.58–4 bit while keeping a full-precision teacher's behavior. It's evaluated mostly in the sub-4-bit regime (1.58/1.88/2.79-bit), but it also reports 4-bit numbers on Qwen3-0.6B that are the closest analog we have to our own 4-bit GGUF target, and those numbers show axis-level cracks (IFEval, HumanEval) that a single averaged score would hide.

## Techniques to try

- Layer-adaptive feature distillation (LAFD): instead of fixing which transformer layers get feature-distillation supervision, pick the k layers where consecutive-layer cosine similarity is lowest (i.e., where the teacher's representation changes most), per training batch/domain. "We select the $k$ layers with the lowest scores as the targets of feature distillation" (Section 3.2, k=3 in their configs). Motivation: "these transformation patterns are highly domain-dependent, which indicates the limitations of heuristic fixed-layer selection" (Section 3.2) — relevant if we do feature-level distillation from a larger LFM2.5 checkpoint or a teacher model rather than pure logit KD.
- Entropy-aware KL divergence (EAKLD) for logit distillation: mix forward and reverse KLD with a coefficient λ derived from the *teacher's output entropy* (not label-token confidence), so the mix works on both human-annotated and externally-distilled data without needing teacher-generated labels. "By adapting logit distillation exclusively from entropy, EAKLD captures the full shape of the teacher's uncertainty and avoids the need for internally distilled labels, supporting training corpora that comprise both human-annotated and externally distilled datasets." (Section 3.3) — directly applicable to our mixed data recipe (human-annotated tool-call examples + synthetic malformed-tool-return pairs).
- Ablations show each module has a measurable independent effect: e.g. "Reverting from EAKLD to standard forward KLD similarly degrades performance, with EdgeRazor$_{\text{SG+F+E}}$ exceeding EdgeRazor$_{\text{SG+F+F}}$ by 1.02 and 0.42 points at 2.19-bit, and by 0.57 and 0.54 points at 1.88-bit" (Section 4.3) — a plain forward-KLD-only distillation loss is a weaker baseline than a confidence/entropy-mixed one.
- For a 4-bit-only target (no mixed-precision needed), the two distillation losses (LAFD + EAKLD) are the transferable pieces; the mixed-precision "super-group" bit layout (SQMP) is only useful if we ever explore sub-4-bit or fractional-bit GGUF variants, which is outside our current 4-bit scope.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Training data mixture: BAAI/Infinity-Instruct (7M_domains + Gen + 7M_core subsets), allenai/tulu-v3.1-mix-preview-4096-OLMoE, a-m-team/AM-DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-1.4M (with chain-of-thought stripped for non-reasoning mode), plus commonsense-reasoning training splits (PIQA, ARC, HellaSwag, BoolQ, Winogrande, SIQA) — see Table 6.
- Compared against a large PTQ/QAT baseline suite: GPTQ, AWQ, AQLM, QTIP, Slim-LLM+, AutoRound, Q-Palette, EfficientQAT, ParetoQ, LQER, OmniQuant, ABQ-LLM, SpinQuant, FlatQuant (Section 4.1, "Baselines").
- Evaluated with lm-evaluation-harness v0.4.9.1 across commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, trustworthiness (Ethics), truthfulness (TruthfulQA2), knowledge (MMLU), instruction-following (IFEval), math (GSM8K, 5-shot), and coding (HumanEval, 0-shot pass@1) (Section 4.1, "Evaluation"; Table 7).
- Deployment benchmarking via llama.cpp (`b6300`) `llama-bench` on Apple M4 Pro and Intel i9-14900K, 100 repetitions (Appendix D.5).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- On Qwen3-0.6B at 4-bit weight-only (Table 10), the aggregate 14-task average actually rose slightly (BF16 47.35 → EdgeRazor 47.83, "+0.48" per Table 2), but this masks a real per-axis regression: IFEval dropped from 58.41 to 53.42 (≈8.5% relative loss, i.e. under a 93%-of-baseline floor) and HumanEval dropped from 37.20 to 34.15 (≈8.2% relative loss), while a large gain on Ethics (47.70→54.36) inflated the average. This is a concrete illustration of why an aggregate average can pass while an individual guardrail axis (like our IFEval requirement) fails.
- On the held-out-only benchmark subset (MMLU + IFEval + GSM8K + HumanEval, Appendix E, Table 22), even this same best-in-class 4-bit EdgeRazor config trails full precision by 2.08 points on Qwen3-0.6B (44.32 → 42.24) and by 4.77 on Qwen3-1.7B (64.61 → 59.84) — degradation persists even with a purpose-built QAD method, at a bit-width above (i.e., easier than) their main sub-4-bit focus.
- CAKLD (the BitDistiller forward/reverse KLD mixing baseline, which uses label-token confidence as the mixing signal) "leads to severe mismatch errors in both human-annotated and synthetic corpora" (Section 3.3) — the paper measured that on human-annotated math data, 16.78% of "high-confidence" (>0.60 threshold) tokens were actually mismatched with the teacher's own top-1 prediction, versus only 2.98% on purely teacher-distilled data (Appendix C, Table 5). Their conclusion: "This strict dataset dependency prevents quantized models from leveraging optimal and heterogeneous training recipes" — a caution if we plan to mix human-annotated tool-call transcripts with synthetic distilled data and use any confidence-based distillation weighting.
- At the 2-bit level (well below our target but instructive), "most 2-bit baselines collapse to near-zero scores on the reasoning-intensive GSM8K and code-generation HumanEval tasks" (Section 4.2) and in the held-out-only comparison, "the strongest 2-bit weight-activation baselines ABQ-LLM and FlatQuant collaps[e] to scores below 10 points" (Appendix E) — reinforces that generation/reasoning-style tasks are far more fragile to quantization than multiple-choice knowledge tasks, consistent with the IFEval/HumanEval-vs-MMLU pattern seen above at 4-bit.
- All headline speedup/compression numbers (15.16× decoding speedup, storage 1.11GB→0.19GB) are reported at 1.58-bit on Qwen3-0.6B via llama.cpp on Apple M4 Pro — not at 4-bit, and not on our target device (RTX 2080 Super). Their own 4-bit numbers (Table 21, Qwen3-0.6B, M4 Pro) show EdgeRazor's Q4_0 decode at 262.66 tok/s vs BF16's 20.91 tok/s (12.6×) and vs a standard llama.cpp Q4_K baseline at 239.55 tok/s — so at 4-bit the margin over a plain off-the-shelf quantization is much smaller than the headline sub-2-bit numbers suggest.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses lm-evaluation-harness (v0.4.9.1) with IFEval scored by "Prompt Strict Acc" — worth comparing against whatever exact IFEval scorer/config our own harness uses, since strict vs loose prompt-level accuracy can shift numbers by several points.
- Reports full per-task breakdowns (Tables 8–13) rather than only aggregate averages, which is what let us find the IFEval/HumanEval-specific degradation above — a good practice we should mirror in our own reporting so a single blended average doesn't hide a guardrail-axis failure.
- Deployment efficiency is measured with `llama-bench`, prompt length 512, generation length 512, batch size 4096, 100 repetitions (Appendix D.5) — a reasonable template for our own throughput comparisons, though we'll need single-stream (batch=1) numbers since our deployment target is a single on-device consumer GPU serving one request at a time, not a batched throughput scenario.

## Open questions for our project

- Their 4-bit numbers are all weight-only or symmetric weight-activation quantization via a from-scratch QAD training run (full retraining with a live full-precision teacher on 8×A100-80GB); it's unclear how much of LAFD/EAKLD's benefit would transfer to a lighter-weight quantization-aware fine-tuning pass on top of an already-SFT'd/DPO'd 1.2B checkpoint rather than a full QAD run from a base model, which is closer to our situation.
- The paper's data mixture and teacher models are all general-purpose (Infinity-Instruct, Tulu, DeepSeek-R1-distilled reasoning traces); none of it targets structured tool-calling or JSON-schema output specifically, so we cannot infer from this paper whether these distillation techniques help or hurt schema validity or malformed-tool-return flagging — that would need to be tested directly on our own eval suite.
- The paper is candidate to change our GGUF quantization plan (e.g., adopting LAFD/EAKLD-style QAD instead of vanilla PTQ, or budgeting for axis-level not just aggregate retention checks), but it does not itself answer whether a 5% throughput floor or 0.80/0.10 flag-rate targets are realistic — those remain open.
- No instruction-like text directed at a reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
