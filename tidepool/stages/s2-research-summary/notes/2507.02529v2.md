# 2507.02529v2 — RetrySQL: Text-to-SQL Training with Retry Data for Self-Correcting Query Generation

**Relevance:** medium
It doesn't touch tool-calling schemas, quantization, or JSON output directly, but its core technique — continued-pretraining with corrupted-then-corrected reasoning steps at 1.5B scale — is a concrete, cheap alternative mechanism worth weighing against DPO/GRPO for instilling "notice the mistake and correct/flag" behavior.

## TL;DR

RetrySQL continually pre-trains 1.5B coding LLMs (OpenCoder, Qwen2.5-Coder) on synthetic "retry data": correct CoT reasoning steps for SQL queries are randomly corrupted, followed by a special `[BACK]` token, then the correct step. This teaches the model to self-correct mid-generation and improves Execution Accuracy on BIRD/SPIDER, especially on the hardest examples, without any RL.

## Techniques to try

- Data-augmentation recipe for self-correction via plain continued pretraining, no RL: "we follow it with the [BACK] token and then with the correct $r_{i}$ itself" (Section 3.3) — corrupt a step, insert a delimiter token, then give the correct continuation. Directly analogous to a possible recipe for "flag rather than assert" on malformed tool returns: inject a bad-tool-result step, a flag/back token, then the correct handling.
- Of four corruption strategies tried, forward-single was the clear winner: "the FS variant proved to be the best overall" (Section 5.3), and best corruption probability was moderate, not aggressive: "The highest improvements in overall generation accuracy were observed for $p_{retry}$=0.2 and 0.3" (Section 5.3).
- Diagnostic for whether "self-correction" is a real learned behavior vs. superficial formatting: measure max softmax token confidence in a window around the correction token — "the mean of max confidence scores for predicted tokens differs between tokens preceding the [BACK] token and those after it" and variance across beam passes is much higher before correction than after (Section 5.4). This is a reusable eval technique for checking whether flag/repair behavior is "confident" rather than noise.
- Linear probing on frozen base-model weights to check for latent error-detection ability before any fine-tuning: "the linear probing model detected incorrect steps with average $balanced\_accuracy=82\%$" using only a classification head on frozen weights (Section 5.2), suggesting the capability partly pre-exists and training mainly "unlocks" it.

## Baselines & datasets used

- BIRD (9,135 training examples after removing one mislabeled schema) and SPIDER (8,659 examples), both text-to-SQL, dev sets of 1,534 and 1,034 examples respectively (Section 3.1, 4.3).
- Base models at comparable scale to this project's target: OpenCoder-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B, chosen over Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B because coding-oriented models zero-shot better (Section 4.1, 5.1).
- Compared against GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-pro/flash as proprietary references, and against LLM-based/embedding-based/heuristic schema-linking methods for the full pipeline (Table S4).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Not all corruption strategies help: "the FM, FBS and FBM variants are either worse than the FS variant, or worse than the error-free baseline altogether (see especially the EXchallenging metric)" (Appendix A.3) — the specific corruption design matters a great deal, naive corruption schemes can underperform even plain error-free continued pretraining.
- Gains are inconsistent across model/dataset pairs and sometimes marginal: "For Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B, the overall improvements were 0.4 p.p. and 3.93 p.p., on BIRD and SPIDER, respectively" (Section 5.3) — one combination barely moved.
- Method uses full-parameter continued pretraining (not LoRA), 5 epochs, effective batch size 128, on 2×A100 80GB, "approximately 4.47 GPU hours" per run (Appendix A.1) — cheap at 1.5B but the paper explicitly avoided scaling up: "full-parameter training of larger models is quite intensive in terms of compute resources" (Section 6, Limitations).
- Synthetic reasoning steps came from GPT-4o with only a 100-example manual spot check, not full verification: "we did not aim for the reasoning steps to be perfectly accurate" (Section 3.2) — a quality risk if this recipe is adapted with imperfect synthetic step generation.
- No examination of general-capability retention/forgetting anywhere in the paper — a real gap relative to this project's concern about guardrail metrics staying within 2 points of base.

## Metric / eval choices

- Execution Accuracy (EX): execute generated SQL and ground truth on the dev DB, compare returned row sets, average per difficulty band (simple/moderate/challenging for BIRD; easy/medium/hard/extra for SPIDER) (Section 4.3).
- Balanced accuracy / F1 from linear probing on frozen internal states, used purely as a pre-training diagnostic, not a training signal (Section 4.2, 5.2).
- Beam-search confidence statistics (mean/variance of max softmax) around the correction token as a mechanistic explainability check (Section 5.4).

## Open questions for our project

- Would the corrupt-then-correct continued-pretraining recipe transfer from "SQL reasoning step correctness" to "tool-call/JSON schema validity," and would it need a full-parameter run at 1.2B or could it work as a LoRA adaptation given this project's LoRA-first candidate ladder?
- Could the linear-probing method be reused pre-training to check whether LFM2.5-1.2B already has a latent ability to detect malformed tool returns, informing whether "flag rather than assert" is more a data problem or an elicitation problem?
- The paper frames self-correction as achievable via a standard autoregressive objective without RL — worth weighing as a cheaper rung before DPO/GRPO on the project's candidate ladder, though it would require synthesizing malformed-tool-return "retry" data analogous to the corrupted reasoning steps here.
