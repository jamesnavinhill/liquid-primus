# 2510.07737v1 — ToolExpander: Extending the Frontiers of Tool-Using Reinforcement Learning to Weak LLMs

**Relevance:** high
Directly targets GRPO training for tool-calling in ~1.5B-scale models — the closest scale in the literature to our 1.2B target — and reports concrete training-collapse and reward-design pitfalls that bear on the GRPO step of our candidate ladder.

## TL;DR

The paper trains Qwen2/Qwen2.5 at 1.5B and 7B for tool calling with GRPO and finds that vanilla GRPO reliably collapses at 1.5B scale because most samples become "hard" (no correct rollout in 10 tries). Their fix dynamically swaps unsolved hard samples for few-shot-guided variants of the same problem (with LR decay to curb oscillation), plus a modified GRPO ("Self-Exemplifying Thinking") that drops the KL penalty, uses DAPO-style clip-higher, and gives a tiny +0.01 reward for the model generating its own valid few-shot examples.

## Techniques to try

- Replace (not just add) unsolved "hard" samples with few-shot-guided versions each round, rather than discarding them or oversampling rollouts ("We replace the data that still fail to generate answer correctly in 10 rollouts with few-shot data... Once the model generates a correct rollout for this sample, its few-shot data won't be involved anymore." — Section 3.2)
- Use a plain conjunctive 0/1 reward (format correct AND result correct) rather than a complex composite reward for tool-call RL ("Our study adopted a simple 0-1 reward mechanism, which checks not only the correctness of the format but also the accuracy of the results." — Section 3.3)
- Drop the KL penalty term in GRPO for this setting; they found it added compute cost without helping ("The KL constraint appears to be of little necessity in our experimental setup... removing the KL constraint not only avoids unnecessary computational burdens but also does not compromise the model's learning efficacy" — Section 4.1)
- Apply exponential learning-rate decay specifically to counter oscillation caused by swapping training data composition each round ("To reduce the oscillation in the number of hard samples caused by forgetting phenomenon, we implemented exponential learning rate decay." — Section 3.2)
- If adding a self-generated-example bonus reward, require a minimum count (>3) of distinct examples before granting it, to prevent the model from padding with one trivial repeated example ("it is only eligible for the additional reward when it produces more than 3 distinct examples... In the absence of this small reward mechanism, the model tends to reiterate the answer within the ⟨examples⟩⟨/examples⟩ tags." — Section 4.2)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Training data: ToolACE and xLAM (via Tool-N1's preprocessing), single-turn tool-use only, adapted to Qwen's official system prompt format.
- Backbones: Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct and Qwen2-1.5B/7B-Instruct, compared against Tool-N1 and vanilla GRPO baselines.
- Benchmarks: BFCL (Non-Live and Live subsets: Simple/Multiple/Parallel/Parallel-Multiple), APIBank (Level-1, Level-2, Call vs Retrieve+Call), ACEBench (Normal category, Atom and Single-turn subsets).
- Compute: "8 NVIDIA A800 80GB GPUs" with the VeRL framework, batch size 1024 — far larger than our managed-hardware, single-8GB-GPU-deployment budget.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Vanilla GRPO training collapse at 1.5B scale: "The 1.5B model experiences training collapse when using the original GRPO training method." (Table 3 caption) — direct evidence GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards needs a stabilization strategy before trying it on a 1.2B model.
- Just raising rollout count is inefficient: "When the number of rollouts is directly increased from 10 to 32, the number of hard samples for the 1.5B model decreases by only 5%–8%, while the training time doubles."
- Naively excluding hard samples backfires: "Simply excluding hard samples from the dataset leads to a reduction in data volume, which in turn causes original non-hard samples to be transformed into hard samples. Consequently, the performance of the trained parameters degrades significantly."
- Training exclusively on few-shot-augmented data overfits to the crutch: "training exclusively with few-shot data causes the model to over-rely on few-shot examples, leading to degraded inference performance on datasets without few-shot guidance."
- Reward hacking appeared before their clip-higher/KL-removal fix: "such reward hacking-related issues were observed in the initial phase of our experiments without modification," manifesting as "producing examples through trivial and meaningless modifications... or generating instances that are nearly redundant or repetitive." Directly relevant to our "reward is a schema check" reward-hacking question, though their hack surface (self-generated examples) differs from ours (schema validity).
- Reasoning SFT damaged general/task capability relative to their RL approach at 7B: BFCL ACC 0.7611 and ACEBench 56.23 for "Reasoning SFT" vs. 0.86 / 81.1 for their self-exemplifying RL method (Table 8) — "this direct application of SFT severely impairs the original capabilities of the model."

## Metric / eval choices

- Result-correctness check parses the tool-call output into a dict/JSON structure and requires an exact match on tool name and all arguments against ground truth — same strict-match philosophy as our schema-validity metric, but here folded into the RL reward itself rather than reported standalone.
- ACEBench methodological caveat worth carrying over: "ACEBench does not use the system prompts adopted during training, which may lead to a certain deviation in evaluation and increase the impact of system prompts to a certain extent" — a reminder to control for prompt reuse when comparing our BFCL/eval numbers across training regimes.

## Open questions for our project

- All reported numbers are for correct/incorrect tool-call generation under RL; the paper never touches malformed-tool-return detection or "flag rather than assert" behavior, so it gives no direct evidence for our 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate targets.
- Their compute setup (8×A800 80GB, VeRL, batch size 1024, 10 epochs) is far above our managed-job budget; it's unclear which parts of the recipe (dynamic hard-sampling, LR decay, clip-higher) still work at much smaller batch sizes/GPU counts, or on a 1.2B hybrid-architecture model rather than a standard Transformer.
- The paper self-flags as unfinished: "As this constitutes our preliminary draft, certain issues regarding structural details may be present. We will further refine and revise it in subsequent stages." — treat exact numbers as provisional rather than final.
