# 2604.04269v2 — Beyond Fluency: Toward Reliable Trajectories in Agentic IR

**Relevance:** medium
It contributes no numbers, models, or datasets to ground our targets, but it names a reward-design idea (redistributing RL credit toward abstention) and an eval framing (abstention precision/recall) that map directly onto our "flag rather than assert" and false-flag-rate questions.

## TL;DR

This is an industry position paper (Google authors, SIGIR-style venue) that synthesizes other people's findings on why agentic LLM systems fail: fluent-sounding reasoning traces mask broken tool execution, and small early errors snowball across a multi-step trajectory. It has no experiments of its own — it proposes a taxonomy of failure modes and a set of verification-gate / abstention metrics practitioners "should" adopt, all backed by citations to other papers rather than original results.

## Techniques to try

- Validate/dry-run the tool call before committing to it, as an inference-time gate rather than a training intervention: "Trial-Before-Execution (Dry-Running): Convergent with our execution gates, performing schema validation or sandboxed simulation of an API call prior to commitment reduces execution-level hallucinations and provides a deterministic anchor for action correctness." (§4.3) — relevant to the gateway that consumes our model's output, not to the model itself.
- For the "flag rather than assert" reward-design question, the paper points at redistributing credit toward abstention rather than task completion: "industry practitioners should redistribute RL credit to reward agents for systematic abstention on unsolvable tasks, effectively training for 'solvability detection'." (§4.3, citing SPA-RL / Wang et al. 2025 — not evaluated in this paper itself, would need to be checked directly)

## Baselines & datasets used

- None. This is a synthesis/position paper with no experiments, models, or datasets of its own. All concrete artifacts mentioned (WebShop, WebArena, Mind2Web, SWE-bench, SPA-RL, InfiAgent, OdysseyArena, PRISM) are citations to other work, not things run or measured here.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Names a failure mode directly germane to our "flag vs assert" sub-criterion: RL optimization for fluency/helpfulness can train the model to paper over tool/environment failures instead of reporting them: "Instead of reporting the system error, the agent generates a plausible-looking reasoning trace that 'fills in the gaps' to maintain the conversation's flow." (§3.1)
- Cites a specific risk for any RL/GRPO stage in our ladder: reasoning-style RL training can amplify tool hallucination rather than reduce it — "Preliminary industrial observations suggest a 'Reasoning Trap' (Yin et al., 2025), where models optimized for long-form deliberation via RL show a propensity to prioritize linguistic rationalization over functional grounding." (§3.3) This is itself a citation to Yin et al. 2025, not this paper's own finding, so it should be treated as a pointer to go verify rather than a settled result.

## Metric / eval choices

- Proposes "Abstention Precision and Recall" as an evaluation pair, conceptually parallel to our own flag-rate / false-flag-rate target pairing: "Precision measures how often an agent's refusal is technically justified, while recall measures how many unsolvable 'likelihood traps' were successfully intercepted." (§4.2)
- Also proposes First-Error Position (index of first divergence in a trajectory), Rollback Recovery Rate, and "Weakest-Link Reliability" (minimum per-step confidence across a trajectory) as trajectory-level metrics — none of these are used or validated numerically in the paper itself, so treat as candidate framings rather than proven metrics.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper offers no evidence at any model scale (no parameter counts, no benchmark numbers, no ablations) — everything actionable here is a name to go chase in the underlying cited work (SPA-RL, PRISM, InfiAgent, Yin et al. 2025's "Reasoning Trap") rather than a result we can use directly.
- Does the "Reasoning Trap" (RL-for-reasoning amplifying tool hallucination) reported in Yin et al. 2025 apply to a 1.2B-scale model trained with GRPO on schema-validity/execution-correctness rewards, or is it specific to larger reasoning-tuned models? Worth checking that source directly before assuming it transfers.
- Would "redistribute RL credit to reward abstention" (SPA-RL-style) actually produce the 0.80 flag-rate / 0.10 false-flag-rate targets, or does it just shift the precision/recall trade-off without hitting both bars simultaneously? The paper doesn't say — it names the idea, not a demonstrated result.
