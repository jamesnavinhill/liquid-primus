# 2503.11701v1 — A Survey of Direct Preference Optimization

**Relevance:** medium
It is a broad taxonomy survey (no new experiments at our scale) but it directly names several DPO variants and pitfalls (KTO's binary-signal loss, ORPO's single-stage SFT+alignment, QDPO's quantization-aware divergence, alignment-tax mitigation via merging) that map onto our DPO rung, our "flag rather than assert" question, and our quantization-damage question — useful context and candidate leads to chase in their primary sources, not enough on its own to settle an implementation choice.

## TL;DR

This is a taxonomy-and-survey paper covering ~250 DPO variants organized by data strategy, learning framework, constraint mechanism, and model property, plus a small reproduced benchmark comparing 10 DPO-family losses on Mistral-7B and LLaMA-3-8B. For our project it is most useful as an index into specific techniques (KTO, ORPO, QDPO, PAFT) and named failure modes (alignment tax, length bias, likelihood collapse) rather than as a source of numbers we can use directly.

## Techniques to try

- KTO for binary desirable/undesirable feedback — plausibly the closest existing loss shape to "flag rather than assert" on malformed tool returns: ("Similarly, KTO [67] directly maximizes the utility of model generations using loss functions based on prospect theory rather than the log-likelihood of preferences. It requires only a binary signal indicating whether an output is desirable or undesirable for a given input." — Sec 3.1.2(a))
- ORPO as a reference-free, single-stage alternative to sequential SFT→DPO, argued to reduce the catastrophic-forgetting risk of doing SFT then a separate alignment pass: ("ORPO [117] proposes a monolithic framework that directly augments the standard negative log-likelihood loss with an odds ratio term comparing chosen and rejected responses, eliminating the need for a separate reference policy while preserving SFT's domain adaptation capabilities." — Sec 3.3.1(a)) — in the paper's own 7B/8B benchmark table, ORPO has the highest average score on both base models tested.
- QDPO — a DPO variant built specifically to counter quantization-induced errors, directly relevant to the quantization-aware training rung: ("QDPO [177] utilizes divergence between the quantized model and the full-precision model for preference optimization, effectively addressing the token-flipping issue. Token-flipping refers to the phenomenon where quantization errors skew token distributions, leading to incorrect token selection." — Sec 3.3.2(b))
- PAFT / online merging optimizers as a way to hold general capability while training a narrow objective, via parallel (not sequential) SFT + alignment merged by parameter fusion: ("PAFT [118] introduces a parallel training paradigm where SFT and preference alignment are performed concurrently on the same pre-trained model and then merged using parameter fusion techniques, avoiding the sequential pipeline that can lead to catastrophic forgetting." — Sec 3.4.2(b))

## Baselines & datasets used

- The survey's own empirical comparison (Table II) uses Mistral-7B-Base/Instruct and LLaMA-3-8B-Base/Instruct, SFT'd on UltraChat-200k and preference-tuned on UltraFeedback, evaluated on IFEval, BBH, MATH, GPQA, MUSR, MMLU-Pro — three of these (IFEval, GPQA, MMLU-Pro) are the same benchmark families used as our guardrail metrics, though absolute numbers are not comparable (different base model, different SFT data, ~7-8x our parameter count).
- Table I catalogs common DPO training datasets (UltraFeedback, SafeRLHF, HelpSteer, Anthropic-HH, etc.) with size, source (human/AI), and structure (point/pair/list) — useful reference if we need to benchmark our synthetic malformed-tool-return pairs against typical preference-dataset scale and structure.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Alignment tax: DPO-style alignment reliably degrades general capability, and is reported as worse than SFT under mixed data: ("Thakkar et al. [207] demonstrate the sensitivity of DPO to training data composition, showing significantly worse performance degradation than SFT when using mixed-preference datasets." — Sec 3.4.2(b))
- Length/verbosity bias is described as a systematic DPO artifact, not an edge case: ("DPO has a tendency to favor longer responses, a phenomenon known as verbosity, which can negatively impact performance and user experience." — Sec 3.4.1) — worth guarding against for structured-output tasks where verbosity could break strict schema formatting.
- Likelihood over-optimization / reward-hacking-adjacent finding: improving the DPO training objective does not guarantee real-task improvement, which bears on the "reward is a schema check" concern even though this is about implicit DPO rewards, not GRPO: ("Shi et al. [205] further investigates the problem of likelihood over-optimization in DPO, demonstrating that higher completion likelihoods do not necessarily correlate with better model performance and may even degrade it." — Sec 3.4.2(a))
- Reference-free DPO variants underperformed standard DPO in a direct comparison the survey cites: ("Despite these advancements, [168] argue that a reference model remains crucial. They compared two reference-free variants using posterior probabilities and likelihood functions as rewards, respectively, and found the original DPO consistently outperformed both." — Sec 3.3.1(a)) — relevant if we consider SimPO/ORPO-style reference-free training to save memory at 1.2B.
- Noisy preference annotations measurably hurt DPO and require explicit handling, which matters if we auto-generate malformed-tool-return preference pairs: ("standard DPO loss functions can yield biased estimates under noisy conditions" — Sec 3.1.1(c), summarizing rDPO's motivation).

## Metric / eval choices

- The survey's own benchmark reuses IFEval, MMLU-Pro, and GPQA (subset "Diamond" not specified) as generic alignment/capability probes at 7-8B scale, which cross-validates our choice of IFEval/MMLU-Pro/GPQA Diamond as guardrail metrics, though it gives no quantized or ≤2B-scale numbers to calibrate against.

## Open questions for our project

- The survey gives one-sentence summaries of ~250 methods with no implementation detail; KTO, ORPO, and QDPO would each need their primary papers read directly before being adopted, since nothing here specifies loss hyperparameters, data requirements, or compute cost at 1.2B.
- None of the reported experiments are below 7B parameters or in a tool-calling/structured-output setting, so it is unclear whether the described alignment-tax and length-bias effects transfer to a 1.2B hybrid-architecture model with a 65K vocabulary.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in this paper; it reads as a standard academic survey throughout.
