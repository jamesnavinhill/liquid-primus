# 2601.07878v1 — Sliced-Wasserstein Distribution Alignment Loss Improves the Ultra-Low-Bit Quantization of Large Language Models

**Relevance:** medium
Directly targets minimizing quantization-induced quality loss with a zero-inference-overhead calibration loss, but all experiments are on 6.7B–13B LLaMA/OPT decoder models with commonsense-QA and perplexity metrics — nothing at 1.2B scale, nothing on structured output, tool calling, or instruction following, so it informs but doesn't settle whether this helps LFM2.5-1.2B's GGUF quantization step.

## TL;DR

The paper adds a sliced-Wasserstein (SW) distance term to the block-wise calibration loss of two PTQ-with-retraining frameworks (OmniQuant, TesseraQ), aligning full-precision vs. quantized block *output distributions* under random projections rather than just matching them point-wise (MSE). This recovers a meaningful chunk of the accuracy lost to ultra-low-bit (2–4 bit) quantization, with no added inference cost, but the wins are demonstrated only on 7B–13B language models using perplexity and commonsense-reasoning accuracy, not on anything resembling structured output or tool-call fidelity.

## Techniques to try

- Add a sliced-Wasserstein loss term to the block reconstruction loss during quantization calibration, combined additively with MSE: "$\mathcal{L}_{\rm block}=(1-\textbf{sw_w})\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm MSE}\;+\;\textbf{sw_w}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm SW}$" (Section 3.4, Eq. 5) — applicable to "any post-training quantization framework that has a retraining component," which would cover a QAT-style step before final GGUF export.
- The loss adds negligible overhead: "as the loss is applied solely during training, it introduces no additional computational cost at inference time" (Section 3.4) — relevant given the project's 5%-of-base throughput floor for the 4-bit build, since this technique wouldn't touch decode speed at all.
- Table 8 shows near-zero training-time cost too: OmniQuant baseline vs. +SW loss training time differs by only ~0.02-0.03h with identical max GPU memory on LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-2-13B.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baselines: OmniQuant [Shao et al., 2024] and TesseraQ [Li and Panda, 2024], both PTQ frameworks with a retraining/calibration component, built on the LLMC quantization toolkit.
- Models: LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, OPT-6.7B — all well above the project's 1.2B target scale.
- Perplexity datasets: WikiText-2 and C4.
- Downstream accuracy: six zero-shot commonsense tasks via the LM evaluation harness — ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, BoolQ, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande. No instruction-following, tool-calling, or JSON/schema-validity benchmarks appear anywhere in the paper.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- A simpler distributional alternative (KL divergence on output token distributions) underperformed and was unstable: "the gains were modest and inconsistent across bit-widths and datasets. We observed sensitivity to temperature/label smoothing and to the calibration set composition. This sensitivity was such that small changes could swing results from slight improvements to regressions" (Section 3.2).
- At the most aggressive setting tested, W4A4 (weight+activation 4-bit) on OPT-6.7B, training failed outright regardless of the proposed loss: "The W4A4 configuration is excluded from the comparison due to gradient explosion during training, which prevented the model from producing meaningful results with or without the SW loss" (Section 4.1).
- A separate MSE-only calibration run on LLaMA-2-7B W3A16 hit numerical instability: "several iterations produced infinite parameter norms ($\|\mathbf{W}\|_{2}\to\infty$), revealing numerical instability under aggressive quantization" (Appendix C.2) — a concrete warning that low-bit calibration/retraining can diverge silently.
- Diminishing returns at larger scale: "larger models, with inherently smoother activations, leave less quantization-induced distortion for the SW term to mitigate" (Section 4.2) — the smallest model tested is still 6.7B, so it's unclear whether a 1.2B model behaves like the "larger, smoother" end or the opposite.
- Two other appendix ideas (hierarchical token+channel LET scaling, smarter LWC clipping initialization) were tried and gave mixed-to-negative results: percentile-based clipping init "resulted in slightly degraded perplexity compared to the default initialization" (Appendix B.2).

## Metric / eval choices

- Primary fidelity metrics are perplexity (WikiText-2, C4) plus six-task zero-shot commonsense accuracy — a proxy the paper itself argues is necessary because "perplexity alone cannot fully reflect subtle distortions in activation distributions and downstream reasoning" (Section 3.1), but the proxy chosen is still knowledge/reasoning QA, not structured output.
- The paper explicitly does not measure inference speed or memory at deployment time, since the loss changes training only: "we do not measure inference speed or memory usage, which remain unchanged from the baseline method" (Section 3.1) — so it offers no throughput data point for the project's 5% decode-throughput floor beyond the logical argument that inference is untouched.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper offers no evidence either way on this project's central question — whether low-bit quantization damages structured output/instruction-following more than general knowledge — since its eval suite is entirely commonsense QA and perplexity.
- Unclear whether OmniQuant/TesseraQ-style block-wise retraining calibration (needed to use this SW loss) is compatible with, or worth adding on top of, a standard llama.cpp/GGUF quantization pipeline, versus being a separate PTQ track that would need its own export path to GGUF.
- No results at anywhere near 1.2B scale or on a hybrid conv+GQA architecture like LFM2.5; the smallest model here (OPT-6.7B) is 5-6x larger, and the paper's own finding that gains shrink with model size raises the question of whether they would shrink further, or behave differently, at 1.2B.
- The two extra hyperparameters this method introduces (sw_w, n_proj) need per-configuration tuning ("the optimal combination of $w_{\text{SW}}$ and $n_{\text{proj}}$ may vary depending on the model architecture and quantization configuration" — Appendix E), adding search overhead the project's queued-job constraint would need to budget for.
