# 2407.08699v2 — Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in Language Transfer via Model Merging

**Relevance:** high
Directly answers the project's own open question of how to keep general ability from eroding while a narrow capability is trained up, with a concrete, empirically validated method (Branch-and-Merge) plus a negative result on LoRA that bears on the candidate ladder.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes Branch-and-Merge (BaM): split training data into N slices, fine-tune K copies of the current model in parallel on different slices, merge them (Slerp/Linear/Model Stock), and repeat — instead of one long fine-tuning run. On Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B adapted to Bulgarian/German, BaM matches or beats standard continued pretraining and instruction tuning on the new-language benchmarks while substantially reducing forgetting of English capability, at no extra data or compute cost. It also shows that LoRA and reduced learning rates trade away far more target-domain learning than BaM does for a similar amount of forgetting reduction.

## Techniques to try

- Branch-and-Merge: partition SFT/CPT data into N slices, train K models in parallel per iteration, merge with Slerp (c=0.5), then continue on the next slices with the merged model as the new base ("we choose a parallelism factor $K$ ... and train our current base model $f_{\theta}$ independently on $K$ of these data slices ... We merge the resulting models to obtain the base model for the next iteration" — Section 3, Implementation). This is a plausible way to run the full-SFT rung of the ladder while holding guardrail metrics (IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond) closer to the base model's matched-rerun scores.
- K=2 was the best learning/forgetting trade-off found; larger K reduces both further ("increasing $K$ reduces both learning and forgetting as we reduce weight change magnitude and improve task vector quality" — Section 6.3, Effect of the Parallelism Factor). If BaM is adopted, K=2 is the reported sweet spot, not the largest parallelism tried.
- Slerp and Linear merging performed almost identically and both beat Model Stock on downstream score, though Model Stock reduced forgetting most ("Slerp and Linear merging achieve almost identical results, with Model Stock reducing forgetting at the cost of reduced learning" — Section 6.3, Effect of the Merging Method).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base models: Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B; continued pretraining then instruction tuning, evaluated on English plus a target language (German or Bulgarian).
- Compared against: standard CPT/IFT on the full pooled data, halved-learning-rate CPT, and LoRA CPT, all under matched data/compute where possible.
- Approximate experience replay: an English mix of OpenWebText, Wikipedia, GitHub, and instruction datasets (OpenHermes-2.5, SlimOrca, MetaMathQA, CodeInstructions) used as a stand-in for the (unknown) original pretraining distribution, "17.1B tokens" (Section 4.1, Table 1) — analogous to needing a general-capability replay mix if this project wants to run this style of merging on LFM2.5.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- LoRA at 7-8B scale preserved English capability but crippled target-domain learning: "We indeed observe a better preservation of the English Negative Log Likelihood but also a significant reduction in learned Bulgarian capabilities" (Appendix A, Low-Rank Adaptation), consistent with "Biderman et al. (2024) show that LoRA reduces forgetting at the cost of reduced learning" (Section 7). Directly relevant to the project's "LoRA or full fine-tuning at 1.2B?" question — this paper's evidence (at 7-8B, not 1.2B) leans toward full fine-tuning being needed if the goal is real learning, not just forgetting avoidance.
- Lower target-language loss did not imply better downstream benchmark performance once forgetting is accounted for: "while standard CPT achieves a lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) in Bulgarian ... the increased forgetting of base model capabilities (higher English NLL) leads to worse or equal benchmark performance" (Section 6.1) — a caution against optimizing training loss alone as a proxy for the guardrail-preserving objective this project cares about.
- Scale limitation acknowledged by the authors themselves: "Our study focuses on language transfer to two languages ... and considers two models of up to 8 billion parameters. However, to establish the general applicability of our approach ... further experiments across a broader set of ... model architectures will be necessary" (Section 9, Limitations). None of BaM's results are demonstrated below 7B, so its behavior at 1.2B (this project's scale) is unverified.
- The method's benefit is data-mix dependent: it "is possible that the success of Branch-and-Merge depends on the composition of these datasets" (Section 9), and the ablations show a high-quality "approximate" replay mix was necessary — a "minimal" replay mix (raw web text only) caused BaM to help even more but everything, including standard training, degraded faster.

## Metric / eval choices

- Forgetting is measured primarily via held-out English document perplexity/NLL rather than English benchmark scores, because their instruction-replay mix could inflate English benchmark numbers directly: "we focus on held-out English document perplexity as a measure of forgetting" (Section 5.1, Evaluation Metrics). Worth considering an analogous perplexity/NLL-based forgetting probe for this project's guardrail suite, separate from the benchmark deltas already specified.
- Target-language learning is measured with both benchmark accuracy and held-out perplexity in the target language, tracking learning/forgetting as a joint pair rather than a single number — the same spirit as this project's pairing of BFCL gain with guardrail-metric drift.

## Open questions for our project

- Does BaM's learning/forgetting trade-off hold at 1.2B parameters, or does the smaller model's limited capacity make the "merge reduces noisy task-vector error" argument (Section 3, Intuition) break down? The paper only tests 7-8B models.
- BaM's branch phase needs K model copies trained in parallel per iteration (K=2 in most experiments) — feasible on managed/queued hardware at 1.2B scale, but worth checking against this project's job-queue constraints before adopting it wholesale for the full-SFT rung.
- The paper only validates BaM for continued pretraining and instruction tuning, not for DPO or GRPO — unclear whether iterative branch-and-merge composes with preference optimization or verifiable-reward RL, which are later rungs on this project's candidate ladder.
- No prompt-injection-style text or instructions directed at an AI reader were found in this paper.
