# 2508.07329v1 — Efficient Edge LLMs Deployment via Hessian-Aware Quantization and CPU–GPU Collaborative (Inference)

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap (edge quantization, outlier handling, low-bit deployment) but the method is built for Mixture-of-Experts CPU/GPU offloading and only validated at 8-bit PPL, neither of which transfers to a dense 1.2B model being pushed to 4-bit for structured-output quality.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes HAQ, a joint 8-bit activation+weight quantization scheme (adaptive AWQ-style smoothing factor found via grid search, plus GPTQ-style Hessian-based weight compensation) combined with an expert-level CPU–GPU offloading/caching scheme for MoE models like Mixtral-8x7B and OPT-6.7B/13B/30B. It reduces GPU memory ~60% and keeps perplexity near FP16, but the offloading contribution assumes an MoE architecture with many "cold" experts to shard across CPU/GPU — the target model here (LFM2.5-1.2B, dense hybrid conv+GQA) has no experts to offload and is meant to fit entirely on one 8 GB GPU.

## Techniques to try

- The adaptive smoothing-factor search itself is architecture-agnostic and could be tried as a component of a joint activation-weight quantization step, though the project's target precision is 4-bit, not the 8-bit this paper validates: "we perform a grid search for the scaling factor 𝑒 within the interval [0,1] to determine the optimal scaling parameter" — Section III.B.1.
- Hessian-based weight compensation (GPTQ-style) is already a known baseline technique likely worth comparing against for the quantization-aware distillation/QAT rungs of the candidate ladder, but this paper adds nothing beyond standard GPTQ for the weight side: "leveraging the sensitivity of model outputs to weight perturbations as captured by the Hessian matrix to guide quantization optimization" — Section III.B.2.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Perplexity (PPL) on Wikitext2 and C4, compared across FP16, RTN, SmoothQuant, AWQ, GPTQ, and the paper's HAQ, on OPT-6.7B/13B/30B and Mixtral-8x7B — all quantization done at 8-bit (W8A8), not 4-bit.
- Quantization/calibration done on 2x H20 GPUs (96GB each); inference benchmarked on a single 5090D GPU (32GB) — much larger hardware envelope than the project's 8GB RTX 2080 Super target.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- RTN (round-to-nearest) degrades sharply at scale and on harder data: "the PPL for OPT-30B on C4 rises from 11.442 to 28.017" (FP16 to RTN), underscoring that naive quantization is not safe even at 8-bit for larger models — a cautionary data point, though at a much larger scale than 1.2B.
- The Hessian-based weight-sorting ("Ranking 1"/"Ranking 2") variants tested did not help and sometimes hurt versus the unsorted Smooth+Hessian baseline: "the ranking strategies even result in reduced accuracy... whereas the Smooth+Hessian strategy achieves even lower PPLs" — Section IV.B.3. This is a genuine negative result but specific to their weight-sorting ablation, not to quantization in general.
- Frequency-based (global) expert placement gets the best average GPU cache hit rate but "a high inter-layer standard deviation of 11.9%... leading to highly polarized inference latency," i.e. optimizing mean hit rate alone can create severe tail-latency variance — a general lesson about not over-indexing on averaged throughput/latency metrics, but again MoE-specific machinery (expert caching) that doesn't map onto a dense model.

## Metric / eval choices

- All quantization quality is measured purely via language-modeling perplexity on Wikitext2/C4 — no instruction-following, tool-calling, or structured-output evaluation appears anywhere in the paper, so it gives no evidence on whether 8-bit or 4-bit quantization disproportionately harms structured output versus general knowledge (one of this project's open questions).
- Expert deployment quality is measured via GPU cache hit rate and its inter-layer standard deviation, plus wall-clock inference latency stability — a reasonable stability-vs-throughput framing in the abstract, but tied to expert-routing statistics that don't exist in a dense model.

## Open questions for our project

- Does the "adaptive smoothing factor via grid search" generalize usefully to 4-bit joint activation+weight quantization for a small dense model, or is its benefit specific to the 8-bit regime and large-model outlier patterns shown here (OPT/Mixtral, 6.7B+)?
- The paper's central engineering contribution (CPU-GPU expert offloading/caching) has no analog for a 1.2B dense model that fits fully on an 8GB GPU; confirm the project doesn't need any of this machinery before spending more time on it.
