# 2603.03305v1 — Draft-Conditioned Constrained Decoding (DCCD)

**Relevance:** medium
Directly quantifies how much grammar-based constrained decoding degrades semantic correctness at small scale — good grounding for the project's decision to exclude grammar enforcement from the schema-validity metric — but the paper's own fix (an unconstrained draft pass plus XGrammar) is training-free, doubles generation cost, and is not itself usable under this project's on-device throughput and "no constrained decoding" evaluation constraints.

## TL;DR

The paper shows that forcing a model to decode token-by-token inside a JSON/grammar constraint can badly damage answer correctness, especially in small (1B–1.5B) models, because low-entropy formatting tokens (braces, quotes, field names) often have very low probability under the base model, so renormalization drags the whole trajectory off course. Their fix, DCCD, generates a free-form "draft" answer first, then re-generates the structured output conditioned on that draft under constrained decoding — this makes the answer 1B model jump from 15.24% to 39.04% strict accuracy on GSM8K. It's an inference-time, training-free technique tested on 1B–14B general instruct models (Llama/Qwen), not on tool-calling per se.

## Techniques to try

- Interleave a short unconstrained reasoning/plan span before the structured output during training data construction, mirroring what DCCD achieves at inference: "Conditioning on $d$ changes the distribution over formatting tokens: $\pi_{\theta}(s_{t}\mid h_{t},d)\gg\pi_{\theta}(s_{t}\mid h_{t})$" (Section 4.1) — i.e., if the training data itself teaches the model to emit a brief plan immediately before the tool-call/JSON, the "feasible mass" for structural tokens may already be high at first-sample time, without needing a second inference pass or a grammar engine.
- Their draft-then-constrain composition uses a smaller "projector" model for the structured-realization stage since "this stage primarily performs structured realization rather than open-ended reasoning, it can often be carried out by ... a smaller projector model, improving parameter efficiency" (Section 4.2) — not directly applicable here (single 1.2B on-device model, no room for a second model), but the framing of "structured realization is easier than reasoning" is a useful mental model for where errors originate.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baselines: Constrained Prompting (CP, schema instructions only), Constrained Few-Shot (CF, k=3 in-context examples), Constrained Decoding (CD, XGrammar token masking integrated with vLLM).
- Datasets: GSM8K, MATH500, GSM-Symbolic, FOLIO/P-FOLIO (JSON schemas, expression grammars, and Prover9-checked logical forms) — general reasoning/math benchmarks, not tool-calling or BFCL-style datasets.
- Models: Llama 3.2 1B, Qwen 2.5 1.5B/3B/7B/14B, Llama 3.1 8B — general instruct models; the 1B/1.5B results are the closest scale match to this project's 1.2B target.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Recent works ... have documented performance degradation by 10-30% compared to unconstrained generation under hard structural constraints" (Section 2) — a citable quantification that grammar-enforced decoding is not free, worth keeping in mind if the eventual deployment stack considers adding a grammar engine as a safety net.
- The degradation from constrained decoding is worst exactly at the project's target scale: at 1B, CD only reaches 15.24% strict accuracy on GSM8K vs. 39.04% with the draft-conditioning fix, and CD is *near zero* on GSM-Symbolic at 1B ("CD | 15.24 | 0.00 | 6.00 | 19.70" in Table 2) — a concrete data point that small models suffer the most when forced through low-entropy schema tokens, i.e., grammar enforcement is not a reliable crutch for a 1.2B model's structural failures.
- The paper's own remedy is expensive: it requires generating two full passes (an unconstrained draft, then a conditioned constrained pass), which conflicts with this project's 5%-of-base throughput floor for the shipped 4-bit build and its ban on grammar enforcement for the first-attempt schema-validity metric — so DCCD itself is not deployable here, only its diagnostic framing is useful.

## Metric / eval choices

- "Strict structured accuracy": a response only counts as successful when it is both format-valid *and* semantically/answer-correct — analogous in spirit to this project's paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate and schema-validity metrics that refuse to let one axis be gamed alone.
- They also ran an LLM-as-judge pairwise win-rate evaluation (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct as judge) for non-verifiable summarization tasks — a reminder that model-scored judging is a real alternative eval channel, though this project's standing constraints explicitly forbid reusing any model-scored stored reward as a training signal (this paper only uses it for offline evaluation, not training).

## Open questions for our project

- Could training data be constructed so the model's own first-sampled tokens already carry the "draft-conditioning" effect (e.g., a short inline rationale immediately preceding the tool call), removing the need for any second inference pass while still raising first-attempt schema validity?
- The paper's degradation numbers are on general reasoning/math JSON schemas, not on tool-call signatures or MCP/JSON-Schema-shaped payloads — does the same collapse in "feasible mass" for low-entropy structural tokens (braces, field names, commas) apply equally to the tool-call formats this project targets, or are those schemas different enough (e.g., fewer free-text semantic slots) that the distortion is smaller to begin with?
