# 2604.27283v1 — Learning When to Remember: Risk-Sensitive Contextual Bandits for Abstention-Aware Memory Retrieval in LLM-Based Coding Agents

**Relevance:** medium
No LLM is trained or quantized here — it's a non-learned/lightweight contextual-bandit controller gating coding-agent memory retrieval — but its asymmetric false-positive-penalized reward design and paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate style metrics are a direct conceptual analog to this project's "flag rather than assert" abstention target.

## TL;DR

The paper builds a contextual bandit (not an LLM) that decides whether a coding agent should inject a retrieved memory, abstain, or ask for feedback, using a reward that penalizes false-positive memory injection more heavily than it rewards correct reuse. It reports that removing the abstention action is the single most damaging ablation (false-positive rate jumps from 0.0% to 17.5%, cumulative reward collapses), which is a useful cautionary data point for anyone designing an abstain/flag reward, even though the mechanism itself (bandit over 16 hand-built features) does not transfer to fine-tuning an LLM.

## Techniques to try

- Asymmetric reward with false-positive penalty strictly dominant over correct-action bonus, as a template for shaping a GRPO/DPO reward that must instill "flag rather than assert": the paper sets coefficients "(α,β,κ,γ,δ,η,λ)=(2.0,1.0,0.5,4.0,1.0,0.001,0.01)" with the explicit invariant "γ>α>β" (Section 2.6) — i.e., penalizing a false flag/injection roughly 2x the reward for a correct one.
- Treat abstention as a first-class action with its own reward term rather than a fallback/default, and evaluate it separately from task accuracy: "abstention and no_memory are first-class actions, not fallback states" (Section 1) — relevant framing for scoring the ~300-case malformed-tool-return probe as its own axis, not folded into schema-validity numbers.
- A closed-form "decision boundary" for when abstention should dominate expected reward over acting: "a non-injection action $a_{A}$ has greater expected reward than an injection action $a_{I}$ exactly when $\gamma p_{f}+\delta p_{r}+\kappa p_{c}>\alpha p_{v}+\beta p_{a}$" (Section 2.8) — a useful sanity-check equation when tuning reward coefficients for the flag-rate/false-flag-rate targets.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Compares against lexical/static retrieval baselines and bandit baselines (epsilon-greedy, UCB1, Thompson sampling, LinUCB, risk-sensitive Thompson) plus a non-deployable oracle upper bound (Section 3.3) — none of these are LLM baselines, so not directly reusable, but the practice of reporting an oracle upper bound alongside the deployed method is a good habit for this project's own eval tables.
- Uses hand-constructed hard-negative cases ("cases that look similar to stored memories but should not be injected", Section 3.5) and paraphrase-variant sets to stress-test whether a high-confidence-looking match is actually safe to act on — methodologically close to how this project's malformed-tool-return probe should be constructed (near-miss cases that look well-formed but aren't).
- All experiments run on a "deterministic," "local," "smoke-scale" synthetic benchmark (24 canonical queries, 32 hard negatives, 40 replay events per seed) explicitly flagged by the authors as proxy evidence, not a causal or production evaluation (Section 3.1, Section 4.8).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Removing the abstention action is the most damaging ablation reported: "minus_abstention raises the false-positive rate from 0.0% to 17.5% and decreases cumulative reward from -31.740 to -87.800" (Section 3.7) — a warning that if a training signal doesn't explicitly reward abstaining/flagging, the resulting policy will default toward asserting even on unsafe/ambiguous cases.
- The abstention policy is over-conservative in this setup: on a 56-case abstention benchmark, the system "correctly abstain[s] on 57.1%, and wrongly abstain[s] on 42.9%" under a conservative threshold (Section 3.5) — directly illustrates the flag-rate vs. false-flag-rate tension this project's own 0.80/0.10 targets are trying to navigate, and shows that a naive high-false-positive-penalty reward can overshoot into excessive refusal.
- The authors are explicit that reward-magnitude tuning may not change behavior unless it changes qualitative signs/categories consumed by the policy: "reward magnitude changes alter reported utility directly, but ... they may not always alter action selection unless they change the signs or categories consumed by the policy update" (Section 3.8) — a caution relevant to reward-hacking concerns when the reward is itself a hand-designed check.
- More retrieved context is not free: in the context-budget proxy, the largest-context option is worst on risk-adjusted utility ("Full trace ... Utility -72.7%", Table 8) because of token cost, latency, and false-positive influence, while "no_memory" wins on risk-adjusted utility despite lower raw expected success — an analog to this project's concern about token/latency budgets under quantization.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports false-positive rate and correct/wrong abstention rate as separate, paired metrics rather than a single accuracy number (Section 3.3, Table 5) — structurally the same idea as this project's paired flag-rate/false-flag-rate probe, worth citing as prior art for why the pairing (not either number alone) is the right way to score refusal behavior.
- Uses p95 decision latency as a first-class metric alongside safety metrics ("331.466 μs p95 decision latency", Abstract; Table 9) — though at a completely different scale (microsecond bandit inference vs. millisecond-to-second LLM decode), the practice of reporting p95 rather than mean latency is transferable.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the mechanism (bandit over 16 hand-engineered retrieval/uncertainty features, no LLM in the loop) is transferable to training LFM2.5-1.2B; the only reusable content is the reward-shaping philosophy and the paired-metric evaluation habit, so this note should not be cited as a source of a technique to implement, only as a design analogy.
- No instructions-to-the-reader or suspicious embedded directives were found in the paper text; it reads as a standard (if narrowly-scoped, single-author, synthetic-benchmark) research paper.
