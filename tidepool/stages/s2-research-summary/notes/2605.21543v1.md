# 2605.21543v1 — Provable Joint Decontamination for Benchmarking Multiple Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
The paper is a statistics-of-benchmark-auditing method (conformal p-values + BH procedure to detect whether pretraining data overlaps a benchmark across multiple audited models); it never touches tool-calling, structured output, quantization, or post-training, so nothing here is directly actionable for this project.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes Joint Envelope Conformal Selection (JECS), a statistical procedure to pick a single benchmark subset that is provably "decontaminated" (not memorized in training data) across several audited language models at once, using conformal p-values and an adaptive Benjamini-Hochberg step. It is a benchmark-integrity/auditing tool for researchers comparing multiple pretrained models, tested on membership-inference datasets (WikiMIA, ArXivTection, MIMIR) with Pythia, GPT-NeoX-20B, and LLaMA models up to 30B.

## Techniques to try

- None. This is a benchmark-contamination-detection method for auditing pretraining corpora against existing eval sets; it does not propose a fine-tuning, quantization, or reward-design technique applicable to post-training LFM2.5-1.2B.

## Baselines & datasets used

- Membership-inference/contamination benchmarks: WikiMIA and ArXivTection (main experiments, K=16 audited models), plus five MIMIR subsets (HackerNews, DMMath, GitHub, Pile-CC, PubMed) in appendix experiments.
- Audited model families: Pythia (1.4B/2.8B/6.9B/12B), GPT-NeoX-20B, and LLaMA (7B/13B/30B) — all base pretrained models, not instruction-tuned or tool-use models, and mostly above our 1.2B scale of interest.
- Detection scores compared: "Perplexity (Carlini et al., 2021), Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024), Min-K%++ (Zhang et al., 2025b), and Modified Entropy (Song and Mittal, 2021)."

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Naive combination of per-model contamination decisions fails outright: "Union $\cup_{k}\mathcal{S}_{k}$" and "Intersection $\cap_{k}\mathcal{S}_{k}$" both violate the target contamination-rate control (e.g. GCP of 0.763 and 0.366 respectively at α=0.1 on synthetic data), showing that naive ensembling of per-model decisions is unsound — a generally useful caution if this project ever needs to combine multiple detectors/judges rather than a single scored signal.
- The simpler valid baseline (JMCS, max-p aggregation) is "substantially conservative" due to what the paper calls "the super-uniformity tax," losing power especially as the number of audited models K grows.

## Metric / eval choices

- Defines "global contamination rate" (GCR) — expected fraction of contaminated items in a selected benchmark subset — and "Power" — fraction of truly pure items correctly selected — as the two axes procedures are judged on; not applicable to BFCL/IFEval-style capability metrics used in this project.

## Open questions for our project

- None specific to this project's decisions; the paper's scope (multi-model pretraining-data contamination auditing) doesn't intersect with tool-calling reliability, quantization, or reward design for LFM2.5-1.2B.
