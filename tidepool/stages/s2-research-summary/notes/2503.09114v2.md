# 2503.09114v2 — Sometimes Painful but Promising: Feasibility and Trade-offs of On-Device Language Model Inference

**Relevance:** low
This is a hardware-benchmarking study of edge SBCs (Raspberry Pi 5, Jetson Orin Nano) across latency/energy/cost, using generic commonsense/QA benchmarks — it never touches structured output, tool calling, or fine-tuning, and its deployment targets (battery-powered SBCs) are a different hardware class than the project's RTX 2080 Super GPU + managed-job setup.

## TL;DR

The paper benchmarks 11 decoder-only/encoder-decoder LMs (0.5B–9.2B params) on CPU-based Raspberry Pi 5 and GPU-accelerated Jetson Orin Nano, measuring memory, latency/throughput, energy, and cost under Q4_0 vs Q4_K_M GGUF quantization via llama.cpp. It's a systems/feasibility study, not a training or structured-output paper, so it mostly offers background color rather than actionable methods for post-training LFM2.5-1.2B.

## Techniques to try

- (None — the paper is purely an empirical measurement study of existing quantized models; it proposes no training, prompting, or fine-tuning technique applicable to this project.)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Model quality was assessed with "WikiText-2 test dataset" perplexity plus four zero-shot downstream benchmarks: "HellaSwag ... Winogrande ... AI2 Reasoning Challenge (ARC) ... divided into easy and challenge subsets, and ... TruthfulQA" (Section 4) — all generic commonsense/QA tasks, none testing structured output, tool calling, or instruction following.
- Quantization compared two llama.cpp GGUF schemes, "Q4_0 and Q4_K_M (k-quants)" (Section 4), chosen because "quantizing below 4 bits in blocking-based post-training quantization results in a significant drop in model quality."

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Quantization scheme is not uniformly good: "Q4_0 quantization exhibited higher perplexity than Q4_K_M quantization for all models, except for Yi 1.5 6B and the Gemma models" (Section 5.4), even though Q4_0 wins on CPU speed/energy — a reminder that "smaller/faster GGUF" and "best quality" are different axes and the choice may be model-dependent.
- Larger context windows destabilize quantized on-device inference: "Even with a moderate context size of 1028, the largest model began to encounter performance issues. During CPU inference, constant page faults led to sharp performance declines. For GPU inference, the system experienced device crashes and restarts" (Section 5.1) — a caution about testing GGUF builds only at short context and assuming it generalizes.
- Meta's claimed Arm optimization for Llama 3.2 didn't show up in the paper's own measurements: "this advantage was not present in CPU inference, contradicting Meta's claim that Llama 3.2 models are optimized for Arm processors" (Section 5.2) — a general lesson that vendor claims about a model's on-device efficiency should be independently verified rather than assumed.

## Metric / eval choices

- The paper explicitly separates perplexity from downstream task accuracy and finds them diverge under quantization: "on the down-stream task benchmarks the observed accuracy drop was almost in all cases negligible, showing the effectiveness of quantization as a model compression technique" while perplexity rose for all models (Section 5.4), consistent with prior work that "4-bit quantization of LMs impacts model quality on downstream tasks only modestly, while perplexity was found to be a more sensitive metric for evaluating quantization effects." This is a generic (non-structured-output) data point suggesting perplexity alone may overstate quantization damage — but it says nothing about whether structured-output/tool-call validity behaves like the "downstream task" curve or the "perplexity" curve.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the paper's benchmarks probe tool calling, JSON/schema validity, or instruction following, so it cannot directly answer this project's core question of whether 4-bit quantization disproportionately damages structured output versus general knowledge — at best it offers a weak prior (from unrelated commonsense/QA tasks) that downstream accuracy is more robust to 4-bit quantization than perplexity is.
- No text in the paper reads as an instruction directed at the reader; it is a standard empirical systems paper.
