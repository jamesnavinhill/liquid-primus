# 2608.20210v1 — Daedalus-150M: A Convolution–Attention Hybrid Designed for CPU Inference

**Relevance:** medium
Same architectural family as LFM2.5 (short-conv blocks + a minority of GQA attention layers) and directly measures 4-bit quantization quality cost and a QAT failure, but the paper is a from-scratch 150M pretraining ablation, not a post-training/tool-calling study, so it informs rather than settles any of our decisions.

## TL;DR

The paper trains a 150M-parameter hybrid (12 short-convolution blocks + 6 GQA attention blocks) from scratch and shows it matches an all-attention twin on quality while decoding up to 1.76x faster on CPU as context grows, because the convolution blocks carry a constant-size state instead of a growing KV cache. It's architecturally the same family as LFM2.5, and it reports concrete, unresolved costs from 4-bit quantization and a failed quantization-aware training (QAT) attempt that are directly relevant to our own 4-bit GGUF quality-retention target.

## Techniques to try

- Verify a quantized artefact's tokenizer/vocab round-trips exactly rather than assuming it: "We did verify the tokeniser rather than assume it: reading token ids directly out of the quantised file gave zero mismatches against the reference tokeniser." (Section IX-D) — cheap sanity check worth adding to our own 4-bit GGUF export pipeline.
- Re-score comparison/baseline models on your own harness rather than trusting published numbers, since normalization and task subsets differ: "peer models are re-scored on the same harness rather than compared against published figures... Comparing a locally measured number against a published one would manufacture roughly a point of spurious advantage." (Section XI-A) — supports our project's own requirement to use a "matched rerun of the base model" rather than the published BFCLv3/IFEval numbers.
- Pre-register the win condition and margin floor before scoring an ablation, to prevent post-hoc rationalization: "The pre-registered metric was validation bits-per-byte over a 645 M-token held-out set with a 0.5 % margin floor fixed in advance." (Section VII) — a process pattern applicable to our own candidate-ladder decisions (LoRA vs full SFT vs DPO vs GRPO).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Pretraining corpus is a 10-source, 16.93B-unique-token English mixture (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM-baseline, Stack-Edu, FinePDFs-Edu, FinePhrase, Cosmopedia-v2, FineMath-3+, InfiWebMath-3+, FineWiki-en, Everyday-conversations) — not directly reusable for our tool-calling/structured-output specialization task, since none of these sources target tool schemas, SQL, MCP, or JSON.
- Quality baselines are HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, PIQA, OpenBookQA, WinoGrande (5-task mean) plus GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, OPT-125M, GPT-neo-125M, MobileLLM-125M — general-knowledge/commonsense benchmarks, not structured-output or tool-use benchmarks, so not directly transferable to BFCLv3/IFEval-style evaluation.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Quantization-aware training failed outright and was abandoned: "It produced a non-finite loss on its first step and was disabled; the run finished without it... We did not diagnose the failure." (Section IX-C) — a concrete warning that the candidate ladder's "quantization-aware distillation, then quantization-aware training if needed" step can fail non-obviously and may need debugging budget set aside.
- Post-training 4-bit quantization (Q4_0, no QAT) cost was substantial and inconsistent across scales: "Quantising to 4 bits costs roughly 6 % perplexity (9.18 at half precision against 9.75 at Q4_0...) That is higher than the 2.5 % measured at 5 B tokens." (Section VIII) — suggests the quantization penalty is not a fixed constant and can grow with more training/capability, relevant to our ≥97%-of-full-precision retention target.
- Structural pruning of "dead" channels (47.9% of convolution channels contribute nothing to output) could not be exploited because the reference runtime shape-checks tensors at load time and rejects narrowed weights: "a real artefact narrowed from 768 to 640 is rejected with check_tensor_dims: expected 3,768, got 3,640." (Section X) — a caution that architecture-level efficiency gains can be blocked by fixed inference-runtime/export-format constraints, similar to our own constraint of shipping a stock 4-bit GGUF.
- An oversized vocabulary (49,152 for a 150M model) was inherited from an unrelated earlier plan and never revisited, wasting ~13M parameters: "Scaling laws put the optimum for a 150 M model nearer 24–32 k... A smaller vocabulary is the first thing to change in a successor." (Section IX-D) — relevant background (not a direct answer) to our own open question about whether LFM2.5's 65,536-token vocabulary handicaps tool-call/JSON tokenization; this paper's concern is parameter-efficiency, not tokenization efficiency for structured text, so it doesn't settle that question.
- Single-seed results: the reported 0.81% architecture-ablation win is explicitly not treated as robust: "The 0.81 % ablation margin is not a confidence interval... one seed cannot resolve a quarter of a standard deviation." (Section IX-E)

## Metric / eval choices

- Validation bits-per-byte (not bits-per-token) is used specifically so scores are comparable across different tokenizers/vocabularies: "Bits per byte, rather than per token, is used so that the figure is comparable across tokenisers." (Section XI-A)
- Decode throughput is measured only for generation (not prompt processing), with arms alternating within a single timed pass rather than run back-to-back, because a non-alternating run produced a spurious 1.29x figure that did not reproduce: "an earlier back-to-back measurement in this project produced a 1.29x figure that did not reproduce, because background load drifted between the two halves." (Section XI-B)

## Open questions for our project

- Does the ~6% (post-hoc, non-QAT) 4-bit perplexity cost this paper reports for a 150M hybrid scale up or down for a 1.2B instruct model, and does it hit structured-output/tool-call tokens disproportionately versus prose — the paper only measures aggregate perplexity on encyclopedic text, not schema-validity or JSON-token accuracy specifically.
- If we attempt quantization-aware training or distillation in our candidate ladder, should we budget explicit time to diagnose numerical-instability failure modes, given this paper's QAT run produced non-finite loss on its very first step and was never root-caused?
- The paper's architecture (short-conv + GQA hybrid, cache-free majority of layers) is structurally the same family as LFM2.5's own design — could its "decode speed grows with context depth, near-zero gain at empty context" result help us reason about how our post-training changes (extra tool schemas/history in the prompt) will interact with the base model's own KV-cache-light layers during agent-turn inference on the 8GB RTX 2080 Super target?
