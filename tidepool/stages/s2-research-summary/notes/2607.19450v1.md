# 2607.19450v1 — REGEN: Replay-recycling for Expert-to-Generalist distillation with Offline Reinforcement Learning

**Relevance:** high
Runs a direct ablation of training objectives (DPO vs. off-policy GRPO vs. TOPR vs. their offline-RL method) at 0.5B–1.5B scale, close to our 1.2B target, and its "eliminating online supervision sacrifices exact-match instruction-following" finding bears directly on our first-attempt schema-validity requirement.

## TL;DR

REGEN trains domain-expert teachers with online GRPO, then recycles their discarded rollout replay buffers (not the teachers themselves) into a single offline dataset, training one generalist student on it with a truncated-importance-sampling objective plus class-balanced positive/negative sampling. On Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B-Instruct across math, code, and instruction-following, this matches multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) accuracy at much lower training cost, but with a specific weakness on exact-match-style tasks.

## Techniques to try

- Truncated importance sampling (TIS) that clips only the importance weight modulating the gradient, not the policy-gradient term itself, so more of the offline replay buffer stays useful: "TOPR (Roux et al., 2025) adopts Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS), which clips only the importance weight that modulates the gradient while leaving the underlying policy gradient term intact." (§3.2)
- Class-Balanced Sampling (CBS) for constructing contrastive pairs from accumulated rollouts: keep only queries that have at least one positive and one negative response, then downsample the majority class per query so positives and negatives are equal — "we retain only the *valid* queries, i.e., those that have at least one positive response and one negative response." (§3.2, "Class-balanced sampling")
- Record the negative log-likelihood of the generating (behavior) policy alongside each stored rollout so the true sampling probability μ(y|x) can be recovered later for off-policy correction, even across different model snapshots: "we record the Negative Log Likelihood (NLL) ... and therefore the replay buffer is augmented by the NLL" (§3.2, "Augmented experience recording")
- Per-query, per-domain advantage normalization (subtract empirical mean, divide by empirical std + ε) rather than raw reward, so heterogeneous reward scales across domains/tasks don't distort the objective (Eq. 6, §3.2).

## Baselines & datasets used

- Base/student models: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (main) and Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (capacity-mismatch experiment) — both ≤ our 1.2B target's neighborhood.
- Domains/datasets: GSM8K + MATH (math), KodCode-Light-RL-10K (code), an IFEval-style instruction-following set (Hugging Face H4) — evaluated on GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, IFEval (loose-match and strict-match).
- Baselines compared: plain Behavior Cloning, Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD), and an ablation grid crossing {CBS, Uniform, Positives-only} sampling with {REGEN/TIS, TOPR, off-policy GRPO, DPO, SFT} objectives (Table 2).

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Plain DPO was the weakest offline objective in their own ablation, consistently beaten by REGEN's TIS objective, TOPR, and even off-policy GRPO on GSM8K/MATH/HumanEval/MBPP/IFEval (e.g. IFEval: DPO 56.9 vs. REGEN 71.5, CBS sampling; Table 2) — a caution against relying on plain DPO as the offline training signal if used.
- REGEN loses more ground to MOPD under stricter, exact-match evaluation: IFEval loose-match gap is −1.7 points but strict-match gap widens to −3.5 points (Table 4), attributed to losing "fine-grained supervisory signal that is particularly valuable for exact instruction following" once online per-token teacher supervision is removed (§4.2, Appendix A) — directly relevant since our first-attempt schema-validity metric is an exact-parse criterion.
- Uniform sampling of accumulated rollouts "repeatedly draws highly similar positive samples, reducing sample diversity, collapsing the advantage signal and driving the model to overfit the concentrated positive mode" — plain replay reuse without balancing degrades training (§3.2).
- On code generation specifically, positives-only SFT beat CBS+REGEN on MBPP, because "an error typically propagates globally rather than remaining localized" in code, making a fully correct program a cleaner imitation target than contrasting with a broken one (§4.3, RQ1) — suggests contrastive/negative-sample objectives may not transfer uniformly to all structured-output tasks.
- Capacity mismatch (1.5B-teacher replay used to train a 0.5B student) still leaves REGEN "within 1 to 3 accuracy points of MOPD," i.e. some but not catastrophic degradation when the collection and training model sizes differ (Table 3, §4.3 RQ2) — relevant if replay/rollout data were ever generated at one scale and reused at another.
- Distilled/offline students (both REGEN and MOPD) "remain noticeably behind the code teacher on HumanEval," attributed to a train/eval task-format mismatch (training on generation-style code tasks, evaluating on completion-style HumanEval) rather than a method failure — a reminder that distillation inherits the training distribution's blind spots.

## Metric / eval choices

- Primary metric is pass@1 across all benchmarks; Appendix A additionally reports majority@4 (math), pass@10 (code), and strict-match (IFEval) as robustness checks — worth noting that pass@10 flips the ranking: "the RL teacher no longer exhibits an advantage and even falls below the pretrained student in some cases" once multiple decoding attempts are allowed, consistent with claims that online RL "primarily improves first-sample decoding rather than fundamentally expanding the model's reasoning capacity" (Appendix A, citing Chen et al., 2026).

## Open questions for our project

- Our candidate ladder includes GRPO with verifiable rewards; if we run it, would recycling its rollout replay buffer (à la REGEN, with TIS + class-balanced sampling) for a cheap follow-up offline pass be worth trying to reinforce "flag rather than assert" behavior on malformed tool returns, versus a second full GRPO pass?
- Given REGEN's exact-match degradation under offline objectives, would our first-attempt schema-validity metric (also an unforgiving exact-parse criterion) suffer a similar gap if we substitute an offline/replay-based objective for on-policy GRPO — and is that a reason to keep the "flag malformed tool return" reward on-policy rather than distilled?
- The paper's per-query class-balanced positive/negative sampling looks directly reusable for constructing our malformed-vs-well-formed tool-return contrastive pairs (DPO stage), independent of whether we ever adopt full REGEN.
- No instruction-like text addressed to the reader was found in the paper; nothing to flag under the untrusted-data rule.
