# 2512.21651v3 — Rethinking Output Alignment For 1-bit Post-Training Quantization of Large Language Models

**Relevance:** low
Real overlap in topic (why output-driven PTQ degrades LLM behavior via attention/geometry distortion) but the method and evaluation target 1-bit (~1.06–1.11 bit) binarization of base OPT/LLaMA/Qwen models on perplexity and QA accuracy, which the paper itself scopes away from the 2/4/8-bit regime this project needs, and nothing here touches structured output, tool calling, or instruct models.

## TL;DR

The paper diagnoses why naive output-driven 1-bit PTQ (matching quantized-model outputs to full-precision targets) underperforms weight-driven PTQ: quantization error accumulates layer-to-layer and MSE-based output matching anisotropically distorts token representation geometry, degrading attention patterns. It fixes this with an error-aware reconstruction objective plus an "Attention Matrix Preservation" (AMP) masking rule that only accepts closed-form weight updates when they don't increase distortion of the token-similarity (attention) structure, evaluated on OPT/LLaMA/Qwen base models via perplexity and zero-shot QA accuracy.

## Techniques to try

- None directly transferable: the closed-form update derivations for `B`, `α_r`, `α_c` and the AMP mask are specific to binary (±1) weight quantization with per-layer analytic solutions — the paper explicitly says so: "In higher-bit quantization (e.g., 2-, 4-, or 8-bit), not all quantization variables admit analytic solutions; as a result, existing approaches in these settings typically rely on heuristic rounding schemes or gradient-based optimization. Such optimization procedures fall outside the scope of this work." (Section 1)

## Baselines & datasets used

- Baselines: PB-LLM, BiLLM, ARB-RC/ARB-X, DBellQuant — all 1-bit PTQ methods, none applicable at 4-bit.
- Calibration: "128 samples from the C4 dataset with a sequence length of 2048" (Section 5.1), block size 128, 15 optimization iterations — standard GPTQ-style calibration recipe, could be a reference point for calibration-set sizing conventions even at higher bit-widths.
- Eval datasets: WikiText2 and C4 perplexity, plus seven zero-shot QA benchmarks (ARC-Easy/Challenge, PIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, OBQA, LAMBADA) — none measure instruction-following, tool-calling, or schema validity, so not usable as-is for this project's BFCL/IFEval/schema metrics.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- "Surprisingly, naive output-driven approaches often perform even worse in the 1-bit regime" than simple weight-driven quantization — a caution that a more "principled" output-matching objective is not automatically better and can actively hurt at extreme low bit-widths (Abstract). Worth keeping in mind if quantization-aware distillation/training is later explored for the 4-bit GGUF step, even though this paper's regime (1-bit) is far more extreme.
- Reported one anomaly where their own method regressed: "We note one exception on Qwen2-1.5B evaluated on WikiText2, where our method produces slightly higher perplexity. We attribute this to the mismatch between the calibration corpus and evaluation dataset, which may have a larger impact on smaller-capacity quantized models." (Section 5.2) — relevant caution given this project's target model is also small (1.2B): calibration-set/eval-domain mismatch effects may be amplified at small scale.

## Metric / eval choices

- Uses perplexity (WikiText2, C4) and zero-shot QA accuracy as sole quality metrics; no structured-output, JSON-validity, or agentic/tool-use evaluation appears anywhere in the paper, so it offers no metric this project can reuse directly.

## Open questions for our project

- The paper's core mechanistic claim — that output-driven quantization objectives distort attention/token geometry via magnitude-biased MSE — is plausible but only demonstrated on base language-modeling behavior (perplexity, QA), not on structured output or tool-call generation. It's an open question whether the same anisotropic-distortion failure mode is a contributor to this project's concern about 4-bit quantization damaging structured output/instruction-following more than knowledge, or whether that's a distinct phenomenon at the much milder 4-bit level.
- No instruction-tuned or agentic models are evaluated anywhere in this paper, so it gives no evidence on whether these findings would even qualitatively carry over to instruct-tuned small models like LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct.
