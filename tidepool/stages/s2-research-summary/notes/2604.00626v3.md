# 2604.00626v3 — A Survey of On-Policy Distillation for Large Language Models

**Relevance:** high
Directly relevant to the last rungs of the candidate ladder (quantization-aware distillation, GRPO) and to the "flag rather than assert" calibration question, with concrete pitfalls specific to structured/tool-call outputs.

## TL;DR

This is a survey (not a new method) unifying on-policy distillation (OPD) — where a teacher scores/corrects the *student's own* generations instead of static teacher text — as f-divergence minimization over student-sampled trajectories. It catalogs 100+ methods along objective choice, signal source, and stabilization, and consolidates known failure modes (flawed prefix trap, self-play saturation, diversity collapse, a calibration-capability gap) that bear directly on this project's reliability and quantization goals.

## Techniques to try

- Use the fine-tuned full-precision model as an on-policy "teacher" for the quantized student during the quantization-aware step, matching DeepSeek-V4's recipe: "integrates FP4 quantization-aware training during the OPD phase itself, quantizing MoE expert weights and attention QK activations so that the distilled model is natively adapted to deployment-time precision from the start rather than requiring post-hoc quantization" (Section 8.3).
- At 1.2B scale, prefer dense logit-level supervision over reward-only signals: "Smaller students (<7B) often benefit from dense logit supervision (GKD, DistiLLM) to bootstrap competence" (Section 7.4).
- For tool-integrated reasoning specifically, weight/attenuate supervision at the step level rather than per-token, since "erroneous tool calls inject corrupted observations into the reasoning context, causing student-teacher divergence to accelerate sharply at subsequent steps rather than drift gradually as in text-only reasoning" — SOD reweights per reasoning step to handle this (Section 6.1).
- Cap reward-extrapolation strength (λ) on structured-output/JSON training rather than pushing student beyond teacher confidence, per the ListOPD extrapolation-cliff analysis (Section 7.2) — see Pitfalls below.

## Baselines & datasets used

- ListOPD: 1.7B Qwen3 student vs. 8B-SFT baseline on "calibrated listwise JSON tasks (Amazon Fashion)" — the closest scale/task match to this project's structured-output setting (Section 7.2).
- SOD: Qwen3-4B teacher, Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B students on tool-integrated-reasoning math/science/code benchmarks (Section 6.1).
- General small-scale reference: GKD used T5-XL (3B) teacher → T5-Small/Base students on XSum/WMT; DistiLLM used GPT-2 XL (1.5B) → GPT-2 (124M) (Table 2) — both well under 8B, useful precedent for tiny-student OPD.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- **Calibration-capability gap (directly relevant to the flag/false-flag targets):** "CaOPD ... identifies a 'Scaling Law of Miscalibration'. While OPD reliably improves task accuracy, the authors report that it tends to leave models in a state of severe overconfidence" because "the deployed model must report confidence using only its deployment-time context," concluding "the distilled model becomes more capable but less aware of its own uncertainty boundaries" (Section 7.2). This is a direct warning that pushing tool-call/schema accuracy up via distillation-style training can quietly wreck the "flag rather than assert" behavior this project needs.
- **Structured-output extrapolation cliff:** "ListOPD ... identifies a sharp failure boundary specific to reward-extrapolation OPD (λ>1) on structured-output tasks... Above λ*, the extrapolated fixed point exits the clip-safe region, switching training from format-preserving to format-collapsing" (Section 7.2).
- **Invalid JSON breaks teacher evaluation:** "Standard GKD-style OPD struggles in this domain because student outputs are often invalid JSON structures that the teacher cannot meaningfully evaluate" (Section 8.2, re: GUI agent distillation) — a structural risk for any teacher-scored training signal on this project's tool-call outputs.
- **Flawed prefix trap:** "when the student generates an erroneous prefix, the teacher's conditional distribution over subsequent tokens is poorly calibrated because the teacher was never trained on such out-of-distribution inputs. Naively matching this corrupted signal propagates errors instead of correcting them" (Section 6.1) — relevant to any scheme that has a stronger/base model score a fine-tuned model's malformed-tool-return recovery attempts.
- **Training instability from naive on-policy matching:** "Unconditional token-level matching with periodic hard-copy teacher resets leads to abrupt output collapse (KL divergence dropping from 2.637 to 0.343 at a single teacher-reset event)" (Section 2.2).
- **Diversity/format collapse under aggressive mode-seeking:** "Aggressive Reverse KL distillation produces a student that scores highly on Pass@1 (precision) but catastrophically on Pass@k (recall), because the student has collapsed onto a single reasoning strategy per prompt" (Section 7.2) — a caution against over-tuning toward one "correct" tool-call format at the expense of robustness.
- **Compute cost:** "OPD's compute overhead (4–5× over off-policy SFT...)" (Limitations/Broader impact) — relevant given this project's constraint that everything runs as queued jobs on managed hardware.
- **Fair-comparison problem:** "Methods are evaluated on different base models, different training compute budgets, different benchmark versions... making cross-method comparison unreliable from published numbers alone" (Limitations) — a reason to discount absolute percentage gains quoted from this literature rather than treat them as directly transferable targets.

## Metric / eval choices

- No BFCL, IFEval, or MMLU-Pro results reported anywhere in the survey; its benchmarks are math/code/agentic reasoning (AIME, MATH, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench, ScreenSpot) and one JSON/listwise-ranking task (ListOPD/Amazon Fashion), so it cannot ground this project's specific pre-registered numbers, only the qualitative risks above.
- The survey explicitly flags cross-paper benchmark incomparability and calls for "standardized OPD benchmarking protocols that fix the base model, compute budget, and evaluation suite" — worth keeping in mind when citing any single number from this space.

## Open questions for our project

- The candidate ladder's final rung ("quantization-aware distillation") is essentially white-box OPD with the full-precision fine-tuned model as teacher and the 4-bit model as student — should we adopt an explicit on-policy (student-rollout) distillation loss here rather than plain QAT, given DeepSeek-V4's reported use of exactly this combination?
- Given the calibration-capability gap finding, should the malformed-tool-return flag/false-flag targets be evaluated *after every* training stage (not just at the end), since later stages (GRPO, quantization) could independently erode calibration even while schema-validity and BFCL numbers improve?
- The paper's own scope note says it excludes quantization as a topic ("we exclude ... compression techniques orthogonal to the training regime such as pruning and quantization") except where OPD and quantization-aware training are explicitly combined (DeepSeek-V4) — so it is a survey lens on OPD, not a quantization survey; treat its quantization mentions as incidental rather than comprehensive.
- No instruction-like text addressed to the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard academic survey throughout.
