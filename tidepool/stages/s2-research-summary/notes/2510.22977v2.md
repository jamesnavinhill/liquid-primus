# 2510.22977v2 — The Reasoning Trap: How Enhancing LLM Reasoning Amplifies Tool Hallucination

**Relevance:** high
Directly bears on the project's "flag rather than assert" objective and the GRPO-with-verifiable-rewards rung of the candidate ladder: it shows that RL/reasoning training aimed at improving tool-use competence can simultaneously and substantially increase fabricated tool calls, and that the paper's own DPO mitigation trades away capability to buy honesty — exactly the tension the project's guardrail metrics (flag rate vs. false-flag rate, ≤2-point guardrail drift) are designed to police.

## TL;DR

The authors build SimpleToolHalluBench, a diagnostic that checks whether an agent abstains (rather than fabricates a tool call) when no suitable tool exists or only a distractor tool is available. Across GRPO tool-RL, GRPO math-only RL, distillation, and toggleable "thinking" modes, reasoning enhancement consistently raises tool hallucination rates even while task-utility metrics improve; DPO on honest-vs-hallucinated preference pairs reduces hallucination but measurably degrades tool-use utility, and prompt engineering barely helps at all.

## Techniques to try

- The DPO preference-pair recipe used to instill "flag rather than assert" behavior: "chosen = honest abstention when required tools are missing; or correct tool usage when available. rejected = fabricated tool calls/outputs; or needless refusal when capable. This teaches abstention *and* preserves competence when tools are present." (Appendix C.2, "Our Preference Construction.") — this is nearly identical in spirit to the project's planned DPO stage on malformed-tool-return pairs; the paper's inclusion of a needless-refusal-as-rejected case is a useful detail for avoiding an over-cautious model.
- Ablating the `<think>` step in an otherwise identical GRPO setup isolates reasoning (not RL per se) as the driver of hallucination: "removing the reasoning step yields only a moderate increase in hallucination ($R_{NTA}$: 34.8 $\rightarrow$ 41.4), whereas enforcing think-then-act RL produces a dramatic jump to 90.2." (§4.4.1) — worth replicating as a cheap diagnostic before committing to a think-then-act format for LFM2.5's tool-calling SFT/GRPO stages.
- SimpleToolHalluBench's two probe types (No-Tool-Available and Distractor-Tool) are a template directly reusable for the project's own "~300 malformed tool returns" flag/false-flag eval — the paper's construction method (explicit-invocation queries with no tools provided; implicit-requirement queries with only an irrelevant distractor tool) maps cleanly onto testing "flag rather than assert."

## Baselines & datasets used

- SimpleToolHalluBench: 296 tools selected from AgentSafetyBench, two query types generated by ChatGPT-4o and manually quality-controlled by two annotators plus a third for disagreements (Appendix A.1).
- ReCall (GRPO-style agentic RL, Chen et al. 2025) reproduced on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, trained only on the SynTool synthetic multi-tool split (Appendix D).
- GSM8K used as a non-tool GRPO reasoning task to isolate reasoning-per-se effects (§4.2).
- Instruction-following/tool-calling comparators: IFEval, ComplexBench, and BFCL Multi-Turn (base subset) (§4.4.2).
- Cross-family/scale checks: Qwen2.5-7B vs. R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Llama3.1-8B vs. its R1-Distill, Qwen3-8B/32B/4B/235B thinking on/off, DeepSeek-V3/R1 (671B), Kimi-K2 Instruct/Thinking (1T) — all consistent with the core effect, but note none of these runs are below 7B, so the effect at a 1.2B scale like LFM2.5 is untested here.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Reasoning-focused RL raises tool hallucination even when trained on tasks with *no tools at all*: GRPO on GSM8K alone still drove up $R_{NTA}$ and $R_{DT}$ on SimpleToolHalluBench (§4.2) — the effect is not simple overfitting to tool-use data, it looks like a general "fill in the gap with a confident answer" tendency induced by reasoning-style RL.
- Improved tool-calling benchmark scores can mask a hallucination regression: after ReCall GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, "tool-calling competence on BFCL Multi-Turn actually *improves* substantially ($+9.9\%$). Yet tool hallucination on SimpleToolHalluBench simultaneously surges ($R_{NTA}$: $34.8\%\rightarrow 90.2\%$; $R_{DT}$: $54.7\%\rightarrow 100.0\%$)" (§4.4.2) — a strong caution that BFCL-style metrics alone will not catch degraded "flag rather than assert" behavior, which is exactly this project's blind-spot risk with its BFCLv3 target.
- Prompt engineering is nearly ineffective against RL-induced hallucination: "+Prompt Eng." only moved $R_{NTA}$ from 90.2 to 87.5 and $R_{DT}$ from 100.0 to 98.9 on ReCall-7B (Table 4); the paper concludes "the model largely ignores the directive, suggesting that the behavior learned during RL is too deeply ingrained to be corrected by a simple prompt."
- DPO mitigation is a reliability/capability trade-off, not a free fix: DPO cut $R_{NTA}$ 90.2→55.8 and $R_{DT}$ 100.0→71.4, but "the model's validation reward on SynTool ... sees a significant drop" (0.45→0.34, Table 4) — directly relevant to whether the project's DPO rung can hit its flag-rate targets without a matching cost to BFCLv3/task utility.
- Representation-level evidence that RL-induced hallucination is not localized: layer-wise CKA shows tool-related representations "collapse dramatically, with CKA scores plummeting below 0.75 in early and middle layers" after GRPO, even though in-distribution (training-task) representations stay stable (CKA > 0.9) (§5.1) — suggests reward-shaping for one capability can silently destabilize an unrelated behavior (abstention) without any metric on the training task flagging it.

## Metric / eval choices

- Hallucination rate is judged by an LLM-as-judge (DeepSeek-R1) rather than a programmatic check: "We employ DeepSeek-R1 ... as the judge to determine whether a model response exhibits tool hallucination" (Appendix A.4) — the project's "no model-scored stored reward may be reused as a training signal" constraint means this judge-based scoring approach can inform *eval* design here but could not itself be reused as a GRPO reward without violating that constraint.
- Discrimination score for mechanistic analysis: linear-probe classification accuracy minus 0.5 chance baseline, applied per-layer/per-component (attn_out, mlp_out, resid_mid, resid_post) to localize where correct vs. hallucinated activations diverge (§5.2, Eq. 2) — residual stream late layers show the strongest separability (>0.14 vs. <0.04 for attention/MLP), a possible probe-based diagnostic if the project wants a cheap internal check on flag-vs-assert behavior beyond the BFCL/probe eval.

## Open questions for our project

- All quantitative results are at 7B+ (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B/32B, and larger). It's unclear whether the reasoning-trap effect holds, is weaker, or is stronger at LFM2.5's 1.2B scale — this is a gap the project may need to test directly rather than assume transfers.
- If the project's GRPO stage rewards schema validity and execution correctness, this paper's finding that reward-optimized reasoning RL can silently erode an orthogonal reliability behavior (here, abstention; for the project, "flag rather than assert" on malformed returns) suggests building the flag/false-flag probe into every GRPO checkpoint eval, not just a final check — mirroring how this paper caught the effect only because it evaluated SimpleToolHalluBench alongside SynTool reward at every checkpoint.
- The paper's DPO trade-off (hallucination down, task reward down) raises the concrete question of whether the project's own DPO stage should mirror this paper's "needless refusal as rejected" pairing (Appendix C.2) to avoid overshooting into excessive flagging, given the project's own false-flag-rate ceiling of 0.10.
- No embedded instructions directed at a reader/agent were found in the paper; it reads as a standard academic paper throughout.
