# 2601.22230v1 — DaJ: Data-Reweighted LLM Judge for Test-Time Scaling in Code Generation

**Relevance:** low
Real topical overlap with our candidate ladder (LoRA + GRPO with verifiable rewards on small backbones) but the task is training a separate Best-of-N judge for code-candidate selection, not making a single 1.2B model itself emit reliable structured tool calls — nothing here settles a decision about schema validity, quantization, or flag-vs-assert behavior.

## TL;DR

DaJ trains an LLM-as-a-judge to pick the best of several sampled code solutions (Best-of-N test-time scaling), using a bi-level optimization scheme that learns per-domain or per-instance data weights against a held-out "meta" set that mimics the target distribution, then trains the judge with GRPO or preference optimization on verifiable (execution-checked) rewards. It reports state-of-the-art Best-of-N results on LiveCodeBench/BigCodeBench, including with a judge backbone as small as Qwen3-1.7B.

## Techniques to try

- LoRA config used for small-backbone judge fine-tuning is concrete and transferable as a starting point: "we adopt LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, with rank $r=32$, scaling factor $\alpha=64$, and dropout rate set to $0.0$. LoRA adapters are applied to the query, key, value, and output projection layers, as well as the gate, up, and down projection layers of the transformer." — Appendix B.
- Verifiable rewards for judge training are binary/graded on execution correctness rather than a model-scored signal: "The reward $\mathcal{R}$ evaluates selection correctness rather than the quality of the candidate solutions themselves. This formulation follows the reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) paradigm" — Section 3. This is the same "no model-scored stored reward" spirit our project is constrained to, but applied to selecting among code candidates rather than to schema validity.
- Ablation shows GRPO beating DPO/KTO/ORPO for this task: "Among the training objectives, GRPO achieves the best overall performance, while KTO performs competitively even without paired preference data." — Section 5.4. Weak supporting data point for our ladder's GRPO step, though on a different task and backbone.

## Baselines & datasets used

- LiveCodeBench (temporal split: train on problems before 2025-02-01, test on 2025-02-01 to 2025-05-01) and BigCodeBench (difficulty split, hardest 20% held out) — both far from our BFCLv3/IFEval/tool-calling evaluation surface.
- Backbones: Qwen2.5-Coder-14B (preference optimization) and Qwen3-1.7B (GRPO/RL) as judge models; Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B, o4-mini (high), DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale as policy/candidate-generation models. Nothing at or near 1.2B, and no on-device or quantized setting is evaluated.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- The paper itself flags "severe distribution shifts" as the core failure mode for judge training: "imbalances between easy and hard problems, mismatches between training tasks and evaluation benchmarks, and trajectory mismatch arising from training data generated by cheaper models whose behavior differs from that of inference-time models" (Abstract). Analogous to a risk in our project: if malformed-tool-return training examples are synthesized by a different (cheaper) generator than what the shipped model sees at inference, the "flag rather than assert" behavior may not transfer — this paper's trajectory-mismatch framing is a useful lens even though its fix (bi-level reweighting) is out of scope for our candidate ladder.
- All experiments run in bf16 on A100s with no quantization or throughput measurement at all, so it offers no evidence either way on whether structured-output/judgment quality degrades under 4-bit quantization.

## Metric / eval choices

- Reports pass@1 percentages on LiveCodeBench/BigCodeBench broken out by difficulty (Easy/Medium/Hard), not schema validity, flag rate, or false-flag rate — no direct analogue to our sub-criteria metrics.

## Open questions for our project

- None of the paper's reweighting machinery (bi-level optimization over domain/instance weights against a meta set) maps onto our fixed candidate ladder (LoRA → SFT → DPO → GRPO → quantization), so it's not something to adopt, only a citation for "GRPO with verifiable rewards beats preference optimization" in a different domain.
- No instruction-like text directed at the reader was found in the paper; it reads as a standard research paper throughout.
