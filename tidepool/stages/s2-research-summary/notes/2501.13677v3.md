# 2501.13677v3 — HumorReject: Decoupling LLM Safety from Refusal Prefix via A Little Humor

**Relevance:** low
The paper is about defending 7-8B chat models against jailbreak/prefix-injection attacks using humor-based preference training; it shares only surface machinery (ORPO, LoRA rank 8, small hand-built preference datasets) with a project whose target behaviors are schema validity, tool-call reliability, and quantization robustness.

## TL;DR

HumorReject fine-tunes LLaMA3-8B-instruct and Mistral-7B-instruct-v0.1 with ORPO on a 400-sample preference dataset (200 harmful + 200 benign prompts) so that harmful requests get a harmless, humorous non-refusal instead of a "Sure, here is..." style prefix, making the model far more robust to prefix-injection jailbreaks. It is a safety/jailbreak-robustness paper, not a tool-calling, structured-output, or quantization paper, so it does not bear directly on this project's BFCLv3/IFEval/GGUF targets.

## Techniques to try

- Small, targeted preference datasets can produce large behavioral shifts with modest compute: "By applying existing alignment algorithm (hong2024orpo) with just 10 epochs of fine-tuning on this dataset, we effectively enhance model safety" using only 400 total samples ("§3.1", "§3.2") — relevant as a data-efficiency data point if this project ever needs a small DPO/ORPO-style preference set for the "flag rather than assert" behavior on malformed tool returns, though the domain (humor/safety) is unrelated.
- Training config used: "We adopt Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (hu2021lora) with rank 8 for efficient parameter-efficient fine-tuning" with "a batch size of 4 and a learning rate of 5e-5, utilizing a cosine learning rate scheduler" over "10 epochs" ("§3.2") — a candidate LoRA hyperparameter starting point, but validated only on 7-8B general chat models, not the 1.2B hybrid-architecture LFM2.5 target.
- Their ablation isolates the training *signal* from the algorithm by holding the algorithm, base model, and harmful-instruction set fixed and varying only the chosen-response style (humorous vs. "I'm sorry, but I cannot fulfill that request."), which cleanly demonstrates that response content, not just the alignment algorithm, drives the safety outcome ("Appendix B.1") — methodologically relevant as a pattern for isolating what a training signal actually teaches (echoes this project's own question of "which training signal actually instils 'flag rather than assert'").

## Baselines & datasets used

- AdvBench (200 harmful prompts) and Alpaca (200 benign prompts) for training; a 100-instruction test set drawn from AdvBench + HarmBench for evaluation; XSTEST (250 safe instructions) for over-refusal; MMLU (500 QA pairs) for general capability; HEx-PHI (11 harmful categories) for a broader ablation — all are safety/jailbreak benchmarks, none overlap with BFCLv3, IFEval, IFBench, Multi-IF, MMLU-Pro, or GPQA Diamond used in this project.
- Baseline defenses compared against: Circuit Breaker, "DeepAug" (qi2024safetyalignmentjusttokens's augmentation), and DeRTa — all jailbreak-defense methods, not applicable to structured-output or tool-calling training.

## Pitfalls / negative results

- Over-defense / capability collateral damage from other safety methods is documented and quantified: on Mistral, "Circuit Breaker dropping to 0%" on MMLU-with-harmful-context, and "DeRTa and DeepAug show substantially higher over-rejection tendencies" on XSTEST (e.g., DeRTa's XSTEST compliance falls to 72.4% on Llama3 and 25.6% on Mistral vs. ~95-97% for the vanilla model) — a concrete illustration that safety/behavior-shaping fine-tuning can silently wreck unrelated capability or benign-request handling, a risk analogous to this project's concern about "how is general ability kept from eroding while a narrow one is trained up," even though the specific numbers are from a different task domain.
- A failure case is explicitly documented: "we present a failure case of CodeAttack that succeeds in bypassing the humor-based defense mechanism" ("Appendix A.5"), and the authors' own proposed fix is just scaling up training data ("increasing the size of training samples to build more robust associations"), i.e., no principled fix, just "add more data."
- The method requires an external LLM judge (Claude-3.5-Sonnet, chosen over GPT-4o after a human-agreement check) to label training data and score evaluation outputs — this project's standing constraint that "No model-scored stored reward may be reused as a training signal" would rule out reusing this paper's exact recipe as-is if the model-judge scores were to be cached and reused as reward.

## Metric / eval choices

- They validate their LLM-judge choice with a small human-agreement study before trusting it: "we collected responses... Among the 200 responses, we identified 9 cases where GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-sonnet had divergent safety judgments. Six human evaluators were invited to assess these 9 conversations" ("§4, Evaluation Metrics") — a reasonable pattern for validating any LLM-judge-based metric (e.g., a schema-validity or "malformed-return flag" judge) before relying on it, though it is not itself a schema/tool-calling metric.

## Open questions for our project

- None of this paper's core techniques (humor-based indirect refusal, jailbreak/prefix-injection defense) map onto structured tool-call output, schema validity, or quantization — this note is included in the corpus but is unlikely to change any project decision.
